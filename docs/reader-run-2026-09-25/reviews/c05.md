## amano-shrimp

1. Page verdicts:
- 00 Hub: A. Everything a first-week buyer needs, numbers link to sources, buy list is real.
- 01 Encyclopedia: A-. The Stimpson/fire/renaming story is genuinely interesting, not filler history.
- 02 Cost guide: A. Real listings, honest about why amano beats cherry shrimp on price floor, not just "it costs more."
- 03 Handling guide: A-. Netting and drip acclimation both tie back to molt failure, not generic advice.
- 04 Health issues guide: A. The copper study and the eggs-that-never-hatch FAQ are the two most useful things a new owner reads.
- 05 Tank setup guide: A. Corrects the "gentle filter" myth directly instead of repeating it.
- 06 Feeding guide: A. States plainly that the reputation oversells the animal, then gives the fix.
- 07 Enrichment guide: B. Useful welfare framing, but copper, molting cover, and feeding cadence all repeat guides 04-06 nearly verbatim (filler-adjacent, though the sentience citation is new).

2. Conflicts:
- GH target: the tank setup guide gives amano a working range of "4 to 15 dGH, with many keepers targeting 6 to 8 dGH." The sidebar Shrimp Molting guide's table gives "Caridina species GH 4 to 6" as the number to hold for a clean molt, and amano is Caridina multidentata. A reader who follows the molting guide's number is landing half a point below the species page's own target.
- Quarantine length: the cost guide, handling guide, and hub all say "two to four weeks" for a new amano. The sidebar Fish Quarantine and Treatment guide's key takeaway says "Quarantine every new fish for at least 30 days." The species pages never say why wild-caught, disease-prone amano get a shorter window than the site's own general standard.

3. Gaps (after sidebar check):
- The cost guide only prices a 10-gallon quarantine setup ($44-75), but the handling guide and hub buy list recommend "10 to 20 gallons." Nobody prices the top end.
- Tankmate compatibility beyond cherry shrimp is thin: the stocking sidebar's amano row just says "anything that eats shrimp," which is true but not actionable.

4. Trust: Nothing reads as an ad or as overstated once you finish the set. The hub tagline ("never overruns it") is accurate given the species can't breed in freshwater, and every hard number (copper LC50, GH range) traces to a named study or source.

5. Set grade: B+. Deep, well-sourced content undercut by two real number disagreements between the species pages and the shared sidebar guides that a careful reader would actually hit. First fixes: reconcile the GH target (6-8 or 4-6, pick one and say why the other number exists), and either match the 30-day quarantine standard or explain on-page why amano get less.

## cherry-shrimp

1. Page verdicts:
- 00 Hub: A. Dense but organized, and the "don't release them" invasive-species warning is placed where a buyer will see it.
- 01 Encyclopedia: A-. The taxonomic history (davidi to denticulata to heteropoda and back) is a real hook.
- 02 Cost guide: A. The FWS invasion-risk citation and the "why cherry shrimp is cheap" argument are both substantive, not padding.
- 03 Handling guide: A. The TDS-shift number (30-40 ppm) and the 2023 epibiont study give this real teeth beyond generic acclimation advice.
- 04 Health issues guide: A. Specific, sourced, and the copper ranking (most toxic of five metals tested) sticks.
- 05 Tank setup guide: A. Actual product doses (Salty Shrimp, Seachem Equilibrium) make this genuinely usable, not just a range of numbers.
- 06 Feeding guide: A-. Clear portion sizes and the overfeeding-toward-shrimp-not-fish framing land well.
- 07 Enrichment guide: A-. Rare honesty here: it flags that the sentience review's evidence was weaker for penaeid shrimps and that dwarf shrimp weren't the strongest case, instead of overclaiming the citation.

2. Conflicts:
- Quarantine length: the cost guide and handling guide say "at least two weeks" for new cherry shrimp. The sidebar Fish Quarantine and Treatment guide's key takeaway says "at least 30 days" as the sitewide standard. Same gap as the other two species in this set: no page explains why shrimp get roughly half the general quarantine window.

3. Gaps (after sidebar check):
- The cost guide's own fun fact frames the real risk as "ten turns into fifty," but nothing in the set says what tank density or gallon count that colony actually needs once it's fifty, or when to rehome. The set covers starting a colony, not managing one that succeeded.
- The handling guide's osmotic-shock threshold is stated in TDS ppm, but a TDS meter never appears on the hub's buy list, only a GH/KH kit. A reader can't act on the number they were just given.

4. Trust: Nothing here reads as an ad. The tagline oversells slightly ("turns one bag of shrimp into a colony") but the invasion-risk section immediately after corrects the tone rather than hiding it.

5. Set grade: A-. The strongest of the three sets: specific doses, honest caveats, real citations. First fixes: resolve the quarantine number against the sitewide 30-day standard, and add a line (plus a TDS meter to the buy list) on managing a colony once it outgrows its starting tank.

## ghost-shrimp

1. Page verdicts:
- 00 Hub: A. The "most start life as feeder stock" framing up top sets expectations no other page in the set walks back.
- 01 Encyclopedia: A. The Palaemonetes-to-Palaemon renaming and "still wild-collected, not hatchery-raised" detail are genuinely uncommon facts.
- 02 Cost guide: A. The ghost-vs-whisker-shrimp identification section (rostrum teeth count, claw length) is the standout page in this entire assignment; it changes what the reader should do in the store.
- 03 Handling guide: A-. Feeder-tank stress as a distinct, named cause of early loss is a real addition over generic acclimation advice.
- 04 Health issues guide: A. The full copper-remediation protocol (move shrimp, CupriSorb dose, two-week tail after a clean test) is actionable in a way most health pages aren't.
- 05 Tank setup guide: A. The compatibility section is direct about which direction the risk runs (shrimp to betta, ghost shrimp to cherry shrimp).
- 06 Feeding guide: A. States the cannibalism risk plainly instead of burying it in an FAQ.
- 07 Enrichment guide: B+. Good natural history, but "the species is solitary... does not school or need a group" sits oddly next to the rest of the set.

2. Conflicts:
- Solitary vs. colony: the enrichment guide states ghost shrimp are "solitary" and don't need a group, while the hub and tank setup guide describe "a small group" or "a colony" as the normal way to keep them, and the sidebar stocking table lists ghost shrimp stocking as "Groups." No page reconciles "solitary animal" with "keep a colony."
- Quarantine length: species pages say "2 to 4 weeks." The sidebar Fish Quarantine and Treatment guide says "at least 30 days." Same pattern as the other two species.

3. Gaps (after sidebar check):
- The cost guide gives a careful visual test for ghost shrimp vs. whisker shrimp at purchase, but nothing tells a buyer what to do if a "ghost shrimp" they already added starts showing whisker-shrimp traits weeks later, after it's already living with other shrimp.
- "Buy from a source selling shrimp as pets, not a feeder tank" is repeated twice but never given a concrete tell (separate tank, individually bagged, priced per shrimp vs. per dozen) for spotting one at the store.

4. Trust: Nothing reads as an ad. The set is unusually candid about the species' feeder-stock origin instead of glossing over it, which is the opposite of oversold.

5. Set grade: A-. Excellent identification and remediation detail, undercut by the solitary/colony contradiction and the same unexplained quarantine gap as the other two sets. First fixes: reconcile "solitary" against the colony-keeping advice used everywhere else in the set, and match or explain the quarantine number against the sitewide 30-day standard.
