## angelfish

**Page grades**
- 00 hub: A. Every first-week number links to its source article, buy list and FAQ are actionable.
- 01 encyclopedia: A-. Short by design but the history section (Schultze's 1823 marine-dory misfile, the 2022 student genome) earns its space.
- 02 cost guide: A. Clear fish-vs-tank split, vet cost comparison is genuinely useful.
- 03 handling guide: A. The predation-risk and pair-bonding sections are the kind of thing a buyer needs before, not after.
- 04 health guide: A. Names which parasites need a vet and why, doesn't just list symptoms.
- 05 tank setup guide: A. Height-over-length case is made with real numbers (20-gallon-long trap).
- 06 feeding guide: A. Life-stage schedule plus the sunken-belly warning tied back into it.
- 07 enrichment guide: A. Cites the actual study and states its null result honestly.

**Conflicts:** None found.

**Gaps (after sidebar check):**
- No footprint dimensions (length x width in inches) for the recommended tanks, only gallons and a height figure. A buyer comparing tanks at a store still has to guess.
- Nothing on introducing a second angelfish, or a replacement, into a tank an existing pair already holds. The "add last" advice only covers initial stocking.

**Trust:** The encyclopedia's claim that "a high school student named Indeever Madireddy sequenced and assembled it on a community lab bench and published the result in October 2022" is stated as flat fact with no hedge. It reads like a trivia item repeated because it's a good story, not because it was checked. Nothing else here sounds like an ad or overstatement.

**Set grade: A-.** Tightly cross-linked, no filler, one real internal conflict-free set. First two changes: add tank footprint dimensions next to the height numbers, and add a short section on adding a fish to an already-paired tank.

## discus

**Page grades**
- 00 hub: A. Honestly flags the acclimation-method disagreement in the first-week table itself.
- 01 encyclopedia: A. Good breeder history (Wattley, Schmidt-Focke).
- 02 cost guide: A. RO/DI unit cost called out as a real, easy-to-miss add-on.
- 03 handling guide: A. Rare for a care site to say two named sources disagree and give both, rather than picking one.
- 04 health guide: A-. Strong use of the 2019 calcium/phosphorus study to push back on hobby folklore, but see the conflict below.
- 05 tank setup guide: A. The grow-out section with the 2024 trial numbers is a genuine cut above.
- 06 feeding guide: A. The "discus milk" fun fact is backed by a real 2010 study, not folklore dressed up.
- 07 enrichment guide: A. Explains why group size logic differs from the angelfish case instead of just asserting it.

**Conflicts:** The species' own health guide (04-health-issues-guide.txt) and the hub's emergency card both say ich treatment for discus should go "toward roughly 90°F rather than the 82 to 86°F this species already lives at, since discus have less headroom left before hitting ich's own upper survival limit." The shared sidebar guide (sidebar/08-aquarium-ich-treatment-guide.txt) says the opposite in its table: "Discus | 82 to 86°F is its normal range | Yes, it lives there already," and its body text says "For a species that lives at 86 already, the discus tank setup guide covers the two-heater setup that keeps it there." One says the normal range is enough, the other says push 4 degrees past it. A reader who opens both pages gets two different target temperatures.

**Gaps (after sidebar check):**
- No quarantine duration on the discus hub itself (just "never shared with the display"); every other species set in this batch states "at least 30 days" on its own hub row. The number exists in the shared quarantine guide, but a discus reader has to go find it.
- No floor-load guidance for the 75-plus gallon tank this species needs, despite the oscar set (same weight class) covering it in detail.

**Trust:** Nothing reads as an ad. The filtration section is unusually candid about a real disagreement among sources rather than picking a side, which is a point in its favor, not against it.

**Set grade: A-.** Strong writing and honest about disagreement everywhere except the one place it matters most for a health emergency. First two changes: fix the ich-temperature conflict between the species page and the shared guide, and add the 30-to-60-day quarantine number to the hub's first-week table.

## oscar

**Page grades**
- 00 hub: A. The floor-load warning surfacing in the setup section is the standout.
- 01 encyclopedia: A. Good on the Florida invasive-population detail.
- 02 cost guide: A-. Solid, but the "$1,000-plus" total is asserted without the tank itself ever getting a line-item price.
- 03 handling guide: A. The catch-as-trap technique and wet-hands/no-knotted-net rules are the kind of specific, checkable advice this format should have more of.
- 04 health guide: A. Doesn't oversell hole-in-the-head as solved; names five suspected causes and says none is proven.
- 05 tank setup guide: A. The floor-load math (785 to 1,400 lbs) with IRC design-load numbers is genuinely rare in this genre.
- 06 feeding guide: A. Vitamin C deficiency section is backed by an actual controlled trial with real numbers.
- 07 enrichment guide: A. Honest that there's no oscar-specific research and says so plainly.

**Conflicts:** None found.

**Gaps (after sidebar check):**
- No hospital tank size scaled to an adult oscar. The shared quarantine guide's default is "a 10-to-20-gallon tank, sized to whatever you're actually quarantining," but nothing in the oscar set says what that means once the fish is past 10 inches; the hedge is there but unused.
- The handling guide's list of moving-day risks never mentions the bite itself, only that "they do bite" appears once, in the enrichment guide's closing line, with no elaboration.

**Trust:** The cost guide's claim that upfront cost "easily exceeds $1,000" is plausible but not shown: the cost table prices the filter and heater but leaves the tank itself as "budget for adult size from day one" with no number. It reads a little like a headline the math doesn't quite show its work for.

**Set grade: A-.** The floor-load and vitamin-C sections are genuinely above the category standard. First two changes: put an actual price range on the 75-gallon tank in the cost table, and state what hospital tank size an adult oscar actually needs.

## corydoras-catfish

**Page grades**
- 00 hub: A. Concise, and correctly foregrounds the algae-eater myth and the group-size requirement.
- 01 encyclopedia: A. The 2024 genus-revision history (Osteogaster) is a nice, current detail.
- 02 cost guide: A. "Buy the school, not the fish" framing is the right way to price this species.
- 03 handling guide: A. The spine/venom and bag-foam-toxin sections are specific and actionable, better than most sites bother with for a small fish.
- 04 health guide: A. Honest that barbel erosion has no established cause, and still gives a concrete home-treatment path.
- 05 tank setup guide: A. The sand-vacuuming technique (crimp, mow in rows, work in thirds) is genuinely practical.
- 06 feeding guide: A. Seven reasons for appetite loss, each distinct and checkable.
- 07 enrichment guide: A. Cites two real studies and uses them to correct, not just support, the standard barbel-erosion story.

**Conflicts:** None found.

**Gaps (after sidebar check):**
- No pygmy/dwarf-specific feeding guidance. The handling guide flags that Corydoras pygmaeus is a different animal (midwater, much smaller), but the feeding guide's wafer-and-pellet sizing is written for standard cories only, with nothing on whether a pygmy's mouth can even take the same food.
- No guidance on choosing tankmates by feeding speed, despite the feeding guide identifying "faster eaters taking everything" as a named cause of appetite loss. The reader is told the risk but not how to avoid it when stocking.

**Trust:** Nothing here oversells or reads as promotional. The set is unusually willing to say "no study has pinned this down" rather than presenting a folk explanation as settled fact, which is the opposite of the usual failure mode.

**Set grade: A.** The strongest of the four sets: consistent, well-sourced, and honest about uncertainty. First two changes: add pygmy-specific feeding notes, and add a stocking note on matching tankmate feeding speed to a cory school.
