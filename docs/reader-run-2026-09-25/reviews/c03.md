## guppy

1. Page by page
- 00 Care guide hub: Would finish it. Every first-week number links to a real article and the buy list is usable as-is. Grade A.
- 01 Encyclopedia: Short, but the taxonomic-history paragraph (three scientific names, the honorific that lost) and the Endler predation experiment are real, not filler. Grade B+.
- 02 Cost guide: Real price bands, an honest "vet costs are rare" section, and the fry-glut cost called out before the buy list matters. Grade A.
- 03 Population/handling guide: Sexing by the anal fin, brood timing, three fry-rearing options with real tradeoffs. Grade A.
- 04 Health issues guide: Six conditions with distinct causes and treatments, including Camallanus, which most sites skip. Grade A.
- 05 Tank setup guide: Concrete numbers for size, heater wattage, pH, filter type. Grade A.
- 06 Feeding guide: Gives a range instead of faking precision, and states plainly when sources disagree. Grade A-.
- 07 Enrichment guide: Cites actual harassment research and turns it into a stocking rule and priority list. Grade A.

2. Conflicts: None found. Checked feeding frequency (hub's "once to three times a day" is a fair compression of the feeding guide's wider range, not a contradiction), water-change thresholds (hub and the sidebar water-changes guide both use 20 ppm / 40 ppm as the same trigger points), quarantine length (hub and the sidebar quarantine guide both give 30 days as the floor, 30 to 60 for a fish you want to be sure of), and the ich heat ceiling (health guide's 82°F matches the sidebar ich guide's table entry for guppy/molly/swordtail).

3. Gaps (after the sidebar check):
- No positive list of tankmates. The set repeats "not bettas, not goldfish" three times but never names a fish that does work; the stocking sidebar guide doesn't mention guppies either.
- No guidance on introducing a males-only group's own pecking order or fin-nipping between males, only male-female harassment is covered.
- No specific note on what to do with a heavily pregnant female during a water change, only during a power outage or move.

4. Trust: Nothing reads as an ad, and the cost guide's "$200 Dragon guppy and a $3 wild-type guppy are the same species" line undercuts rather than inflates the premium strains, which is a rare honest note for a page that could easily have upsold them. One line to flag rather than distrust: the feeding guide's vet-sourced "contact a vet after one day of reduced appetite" is immediately softened as "notably stricter than hobbyist consensus", so a reader could reasonably skip a real warning sign. That's a judgment call, not dishonesty.

5. Set grade: A-. Consistent numbers across every page, real citations in the enrichment guide, and gaps that are minor rather than structural. The two changes I'd make first: name two or three actual compatible tankmates instead of only naming the two that don't work, and add one line to the health guide on when a water change during pregnancy is or isn't safe.

## molly

1. Page by page
- 00 Care guide hub: Would finish it. Numbers are specific and every one links out. Grade A.
- 01 Encyclopedia: The genus-name history (Mollienesia, the contested black-molly origin) is real research, not filler, even at this length. Grade B+.
- 02 Cost guide: Real price tiers, a $22-for-434-gallons salt calculation that actually helps a buyer decide, honest about vet costs. Grade A.
- 03 Breeding/handling guide: Sexing, gestation, and a direct guppy comparison that earns its place instead of padding. Grade A.
- 04 Health issues guide: Names the actual parasite and bacteria species, and gives the salt debate a real, sourced answer instead of picking a side quietly. Grade A.
- 05 Tank setup guide: Specific and honest about the salt question, ties the shimmy trigger to the temperature-swing number. Grade A.
- 06 Feeding guide: Clear on why mollies need more vegetable content than other livebearers, with a real reason, their wild diet. Grade A-.
- 07 Enrichment guide: The grazing-surface argument is genuinely species-specific rather than boilerplate copied from the guppy page. Grade A.

2. Conflicts:
- Quarantine length. The hub and health guide both say "2 to 3 weeks," but the sidebar's quarantine guide says "at least 30 days," calling that "the floor, not the target," citing two veterinary sources. Guppy's own pages correctly use 30 days; molly is the outlier at under half that.
- Cycling time. The hub and tank setup guide both say the bacteria colony "takes 2 to 4 weeks," but the sidebar cycling guide says fishless cycles typically finish in "four to six weeks," a number that does not vary by fish species. Molly understates it by roughly half.

3. Gaps (after the sidebar check):
- No positive tankmate list, the same absence as the guppy set.
- The enrichment guide notes mollies "get sold beside guppies and platies as if they were the same fish" and are "fussier about water," but no molly page states outright that mixing mollies with guppies means compromising on pH for one of them (molly wants 7.5 to 8.5, guppy 7.0 or above). A reader building the classic mixed livebearer tank never gets told this directly.
- No mention of Camallanus or other internal parasites, which the guppy health guide covers for the same fish family.

4. Trust: The cost guide links a specific Walmart product mid-paragraph, a 65-ounce container that "runs about $22 and treats up to 434 gallons," with a live price. It is disclosed as an affiliate link at the top of the page, so it is not deceptive, just more product-specific than anywhere in the guppy set. Otherwise the set is unusually candid: it names a specific hobbyist, Dr. Joanne Norton, taking the anti-salt side rather than presenting "some people say" anonymously, and the health guide's dropsy and ich sections name the actual pathogens instead of hand-waving.

5. Set grade: B+. The research and specificity match the guppy set, but the quarantine and cycling numbers are real, checkable disagreements with this site's own shared guides, not stylistic differences. The two changes I'd make first: fix the quarantine and cycling numbers to match the sidebar guides, or add a stated reason mollies genuinely differ if one exists, and add one line clarifying that a mixed livebearer community tank means splitting the difference on water hardness.

## platy

1. Page by page
- 00 Care guide hub: Would finish it. The tank-footprint detail (a 10-gallon's 20x10 base misses the 24x12 minimum a 20-long meets) is the kind of specific, checkable fact most hub pages skip. Grade A.
- 01 Encyclopedia: The Gordon-Kosswig cross and Xmrk gene history is real science writing, not filler. Grade A-.
- 02 Cost guide: Same solid format as guppy and molly, plus actual linked sources for price and lifespan claims rather than bare assertions. Grade A.
- 03 Breeding/handling guide: The rehoming section (shop, aquarium society, another keeper, never a waterway) with the Florida and Hawaii invasive-population note is the most useful population-management writing in any of the three sets so far. Grade A.
- 04 Health issues guide: The melanoma section is careful in both directions, cites a 2024 journal review by name, and correctly does not recommend heat-raising for ich the way the guppy and molly pages do, matching platy's lower heat ceiling. Grade A.
- 05 Tank setup guide: Concrete, and honest about a real limitation (soft acidic water) instead of pretending platies tolerate everything. Grade A.
- 06 Feeding guide: Clear on why platies need less vegetable content than mollies, with the stringy-waste tell as a genuinely useful visual sign. Grade A.
- 07 Enrichment guide: "Tolerance is not evidence the tank is fine" is the sharpest framing across all three species sets. Grade A.

2. Conflicts:
- Quarantine length. The hub and health issues guide both give "2 to 3 weeks," while the sidebar quarantine guide states "at least 30 days... the floor, not the target," the same gap found in the molly set.
- Cycling time. The hub and tank setup guide both say the bacteria colony "takes 2 to 4 weeks with a fishless cycle," while the sidebar cycling guide says fishless cycles typically finish in "four to six weeks." Same understatement as molly's set, suggesting this is a site-wide pattern across the livebearer pages rather than a one-off.

3. Gaps (after the sidebar check):
- No positive tankmate list, the same gap as guppy and molly.
- The tank setup guide never states what water hardness a platy needs relative to a guppy sharing the same tank (platy 10 to 30 dGH hard water, guppy tolerates softer with a supplement), which matters for anyone building a mixed livebearer tank, same gap flagged for molly.
- No explicit note on how long after the gonopodium appears a young male is actually fertile, only that it can appear "anywhere from about two to six and a half months." A keeper sorting fry by sex needs to know how much margin the sorting age has to leave.

4. Trust: This is the most transparently sourced of the three sets so far, with live links to thatpetplace.com for pricing and aquariumcoop.com for lifespan, both under an affiliate disclosure. Nothing reads as an ad. The invasive-species claim about Oahu damselfly decline is stated as fact with no citation attached in the text, worth a source note even though it is plausible and consistent with known platy introductions.

5. Set grade: A-. Strongest writing of the three sets on population management and the tumor topic specifically, let down only by the same quarantine and cycling discrepancies found in the molly set. The two changes I'd make first: fix the quarantine and cycling numbers against the shared guides, and add a one-line hardness note for anyone mixing platies with guppies or other softer-water livebearers.

## swordtail

1. Page by page
- 00 Care guide hub: Would finish it. "Not two males" as the lead stocking rule, ahead of tank size, is the right call for this species. Grade A.
- 01 Encyclopedia: The Xiphophorus stock center history, from Myron Gordon's 1930 expeditions to the collection's move to Texas in 1992, is genuine research depth. Grade A-.
- 02 Cost guide: Live-linked prices from two actual LiveAquaria listings, plus a real cited 1990 mate-choice study. The most transparently sourced cost guide of the four sets reviewed today. Grade A.
- 03 Handling/breeding guide: Covers acclimation, sexing, the female-to-male sex reversal question honestly (disputed, not settled), and gives a clear reason ratio is a "handling" decision here specifically. Grade A.
- 04 Health issues guide: Treats an unbalanced sex ratio as a genuine health topic with a stated mechanism (stress lowers immune response), not just a behavior note. Grade A.
- 05 Tank setup guide: The jumping-risk section and the three-plant list (Vallisneria Tiger, hornwort, Anubias nana) with real light, CO2, and substrate specifics is the single most actionable page in this whole four-species assignment. Grade A.
- 06 Feeding guide: Clear, direct comparison to the molly's diet with a stated reason (wild diet balance). Grade A-.
- 07 Enrichment guide: "Two males is the number that causes trouble" is a sharp, correct, non-obvious rule stated up front. Grade A.

2. Conflicts:
- pH range, within this set. The hub and tank setup guide both give "pH 7.0 to 8.4," but the handling guide says "Swordtails live in hard, alkaline water, pH 7.0 to 8.0." The upper bound disagrees by 0.4 pH between two pages of the same species.
- Quarantine length and cycling time repeat the same site-wide pattern found in the molly and platy sets: this set's own pages say quarantine "2 to 4 weeks" and cycling "2 to 4 weeks," while the shared sidebar guides give 30 days as the quarantine floor and 4 to 6 weeks as the typical cycle. Swordtail is at least closer on quarantine than molly or platy were.

3. Gaps (after the sidebar check):
- No positive tankmate list, the same gap running through all four species.
- "Several males in a large, well-planted tank" is given as an alternative to one male, but no gallon number or male count is attached to "several" or "large," unlike almost every other number in this set, which is specific.
- No guidance on what to do with hybrid platy-swordtail fry if a mixed tank already happened before the owner read the cost guide's warning.

4. Trust: Nothing reads as an ad despite the direct product links; the cost guide's use of two different LiveAquaria listings to show that the same species gets a 20-gallon minimum on one product page and a 30-gallon recommendation on another is an unusually candid way to handle conflicting retail advice instead of picking whichever number is more convenient.

5. Set grade: A-. The best-sourced and most specific set of the four, with one real internal contradiction (the pH upper bound) that would take one edit to fix. The two changes I'd make first: reconcile the 8.0 vs 8.4 pH figure between the handling guide and the rest of the set, and put a number on what counts as a "large" tank and "several" males for the multi-male stocking option.
