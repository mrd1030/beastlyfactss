## ball-python

**Pages**
- 00 care guide hub: Finish it. A. Front-loads every first-week number with a source link, an emergency card, and a buy list. Nothing to act on beyond "read the linked page," but that's the job of a hub.
- 01 encyclopedia: Finish it. B. Good wild-range and export history, but the lifespan field is broken (see Conflicts) and it mostly restates numbers the hub already gave.
- 02 cost guide: Finish it. A. Real price bands by category, dated sourcing, and the spider-morph wobble-syndrome warning is a genuinely useful buying flag most cost pages skip.
- 03 handling guide: Finish it. A. The 48-72 hour and no-shed rules are specific, and the escape-search protocol is a real step-by-step, not filler.
- 04 health issues guide: Finish it. A. Cause, signs, and action for every entry, with a clear vet-now list at the end.
- 05 tank setup guide: Finish it. A. Full gradient, humidity range, substrate, and a real cleaning schedule with contact time for disinfectant.
- 06 feeding guide: Finish it. A. The strongest page in the set: thaw method, warming without overheating, freezer burn, and Salmonella handling, each sourced (Merck, FDA, CDC).
- 07 enrichment guide: Finish it. A. Built on one real housing study and honest about where it stops being evidence and starts being reasoning (the scent section).
- 08 vs. boa constrictor: Finish it. B+. Useful if size is the deciding factor, but roughly half the table just restates numbers the hub already gave.
- 09 vs. corn snake: Finish it. B+. Same value and same restating problem as 08.

**Conflicts**
The encyclopedia's captivity lifespan does not match the rest of the set. 01-encyclopedia.txt, under a field literally labeled "Wild Lifespan," says "About 10 years on average in the wild; around 20 years in captivity, with zoo reports approaching 50." The hub, cost guide, and both comparison pages all say 20 to 30 years in captivity, up to 48 recorded (00-care-guide-hub.txt: "Lifespan: 20 to 30 years, up to 48 recorded in captivity"; 08-vs-boa-constrictor-guide.txt: "A ball python commonly lives 20 to 30 years in captivity"). "Around 20 years" undersells the typical range by up to a decade against every other page in the set.

**Gaps**
Checked sidebar for quarantine, heating, and brumation coverage before listing these; none of the three closes them.
- No page gives a single pre-purchase checklist (weight check, vent, eye caps, mite specks) to look for at the store; the pieces exist scattered across the health and cost guides but never as one list.
- Increasing weigh-ins during an off-food stretch is only in the sidebar brumation guide ("weighed every one to two weeks"), not in the feeding or health guide where an owner worried about appetite loss would actually land.
- The state-by-state legal guide is linked from nearly every page but isn't in this set, so a reader in New Jersey or Minnesota gets one line and no detail on what the permit actually requires.
- "Found before you need one" is the entire guidance on choosing a reptile vet; no page says what to look for in one.

**Trust**
The encyclopedia's lifespan field is the one thing that reads wrong rather than just thin, since it's mislabeled ("Wild Lifespan") and gives a lower captivity number than the rest of the site uses. Nothing else reads as oversold or ad-like; the cost guide names specific retailers (Zen Habitats, Josh's Frogs, PetSmart) but ties them to a dated price check under an existing affiliate disclosure, not a pitch.

**Set grade: A-.** The care mechanics are specific, sourced, and consistent with each other everywhere except one lifespan number, and the enrichment and feeding guides in particular go well past the genre's usual filler. First fix: correct and relabel the encyclopedia lifespan field to match the rest of the set. Second: fold a short pre-purchase checklist into the cost guide, since that's where a reader is standing when they need it.

## corn-snake

**Pages**
- 00 care guide hub: Finish it. A. Same strong pattern as the ball python hub, and unusually honest about the humidity range being a genuine disagreement rather than a settled number.
- 01 encyclopedia: Finish it. B. The Bechtel 1961 breeding history is a real, specific detail most care sites skip, though it's thin as care content and mostly a link list otherwise.
- 02 cost guide: Finish it. A. Monthly, annual, and decade-long tables that actually show the math (mouse cost per unit, kWh math for the heat mat), not just totals.
- 03 handling guide: Finish it. A. The five-point escape-proofing checklist (door locks, lid clamps, snout-test every gap) is genuinely usable, not generic advice.
- 04 health issues guide: Finish it. A. The Cryptosporidiosis section is specific enough to actually distinguish it from routine regurgitation, which matters.
- 05 tank setup guide: Finish it. A. Clear on the one non-negotiable (thermostat) and honest about the humidity debate, but see Conflicts on its basking temperature.
- 06 feeding guide: Finish it. A. The best page in the set: age-and-weight-keyed schedule, and it flags a real disagreement between specialist and vet sources instead of hiding it. See Conflicts for where its own numbers slip.
- 07 enrichment guide: Finish it. A. Built on named, dated studies (Hoehfurtner 2021, Almli and Burghardt), with a real preference test behind its top recommendation.
- 08 vs. hognose snake: Finish it. B+. Useful and specific (the venom question, the size gap), not just a restated table.

**Conflicts**
Two numeric slips. First, the warm-side target disagrees with itself: 00-care-guide-hub.txt and 05-corn-snake-tank-setup-guide.txt both set the warm/basking side at "85 to 90°F," but 06-corn-snake-feeding-guide.txt says a stalled eater needs "roughly 82 to 85°F on the warm side to digest food safely," a range that barely overlaps the setup guide's own target. Second, brumation duration: 06-corn-snake-feeding-guide.txt gives "2 to 4 months without food" for a cooled snake, while sidebar/01-snake-brumation-guide.txt, the shared guide it doesn't link to for this number, gives "Typically 60-90 days," about a month short of the feeding guide's upper end.

**Gaps**
Checked the sidebar for quarantine, heating, brumation, and shedding coverage first; the two gaps below survive that check.
- No page gives a single pre-purchase or health checklist to look at before buying, though pieces sit in the cost and health guides.
- The state-by-state legal guide (Georgia's native-species ban, New Jersey and Illinois morph restrictions) is linked from every page in the set but isn't included here, so a reader in one of those states gets one line and nothing else.

**Trust**
Nothing reads as an ad or overstated. The feeding and setup guides are unusually candid about where sources genuinely disagree (feeding frequency, humidity), which is the opposite of a trust problem, it's the set showing its work. The one place that reads wrong is the digestion-temperature figure under Conflicts, which undercuts the setup guide's own stated range rather than just disagreeing with an outside source.

**Set grade: A-.** Same strength as the ball python set: sourced, specific, and willing to name real disagreements instead of papering over them. The warm-side and brumation-duration numbers are the two loose threads. First fix: reconcile the 82-85°F digestion figure with the 85-90°F basking target, or explain why they're different measurements. Second: bring the feeding guide's brumation duration in line with the sidebar guide it should be deferring to.

## milk-snake

**Pages**
- 00 care guide hub: Finish it. A. Same strong pattern as the other hubs, though its own FAQ repeats a corn snake enclosure-size finding as if it answered the milk snake question, without the caveat the enrichment article insists on.
- 01 encyclopedia: Finish it. A-. Genuinely more interesting than a typical encyclopedia page, it offers a real competing explanation for the banding (motion-dazzle camouflage, not just mimicry), but that undercuts how confidently other pages in the set state mimicry as settled fact.
- 02 cost guide: Finish it. A. The "choosing the snake behind the price" section is an actual pre-purchase checklist (clear eyes, clean vent, feeding record), which the other two sets in this batch don't have. One small internal number slip, see Conflicts.
- 03 handling guide: Finish it. A. Treats musking and nipping as normal juvenile behavior rather than a problem to fix, and handles the coral snake question with real nuance instead of just the rhyme.
- 04 health issues guide: Finish it. A. The "one moisture dial causes three conditions" framing is genuinely useful and something a reader will actually remember.
- 05 tank setup guide: Finish it. A. The halogen-vs-ceramic explanation (light versus heat-only) is a level of specificity most setup guides skip entirely.
- 06 feeding guide: Finish it. A. Direct about cannibalism as the reason for solo housing, and the two sizing rules are simple enough to actually use.
- 07 enrichment guide: Finish it. A. The most honest page in this whole review: it says outright there is no research on this species and repeatedly labels its corn snake evidence a "borrow," warning against over-reading it.
- 08 vs. corn snake: Finish it. B+. Refreshingly admits the two species' day-to-day care is nearly identical instead of manufacturing a bigger contrast than exists.

**Conflicts**
The coral snake mimicry claim isn't handled consistently. 00-care-guide-hub.txt's fun fact calls it "a survival trick called Batesian mimicry," and 02-cost-guide.txt calls it "a textbook case of Batesian mimicry," both stated as settled fact. But 01-encyclopedia.txt's own history section says milk snakes "live across a range that includes large areas where no coral snake occurs at all, which is difficult to square with mimicry as the whole explanation," and offers a competing camouflage explanation instead. The set states the same claim as fact in three places and as contested in a fourth.

Smaller: 02-cost-guide.txt headlines "The Snake: $60 to $500," but the cheapest subspecies it actually lists, a Pueblan, is "$70 to $100." The $60 floor doesn't match anything in the guide's own table.

**Gaps**
Checked the sidebar for quarantine, heating, and shedding coverage first; the legal-guide gap survives that check, same as in the other two sets.
- The state-by-state legal guide is linked from every page in the set but isn't included here, so a reader can't check whether their state is one of the restricted ones.
- No page says how to verify which subspecies you're actually being sold (Pueblan vs. Sinaloan vs. Honduran) beyond adult size; at hatchling size that's not visually obvious, and price scales with the answer.

**Trust**
The mimicry overclaim under Conflicts is the one real issue, it's the kind of thing that reads as more settled than the site's own research section supports. Everything else earns trust rather than losing it: the enrichment guide's repeated "this is a borrow, not a finding" caveat is the kind of honesty this genre usually skips, and the cost guide's buying advice (ask for a feeding record, walk away from a seller who won't show the snake) reads as genuinely protective rather than promotional.

**Set grade: A.** The strongest of the three sets so far, largely because of how often it admits uncertainty instead of papering over it. First fix: soften the "textbook case" mimicry language in the hub and cost guide to match the nuance the encyclopedia already has. Second: correct the cost guide's $60 floor or show what actually costs that little.

## california-kingsnake

**Pages**
- 00 care guide hub: Finish it. B+. Strong overall, but its own fun fact overstates the one thing the rest of the page gets right, see Conflicts.
- 01 encyclopedia: Finish it. A. The best encyclopedia page in this whole review: it separates wild and captive lifespan correctly, and the jungle-corn-hybrid and California-permit details are specific enough to actually matter to a buyer.
- 02 cost guide: Finish it. A. The buying-well section (feeding records, ask to see both parents, name the permit) is a real screening tool, not generic advice.
- 03 handling guide: Finish it. A. Unusually rigorous for the genre, it names its sources inline (PetMD, RSPCA, Reptiles Magazine, California Herps) instead of presenting borrowed advice as its own.
- 04 health issues guide: Finish it. A. The soaking-as-early-mite-sign detail and the 3-to-4-week refusal threshold are both specific enough to act on.
- 05 tank setup guide: Finish it. A. The three-stage enclosure table, keyed to the snake's own length rather than its age, is more precise than most setup guides bother to be.
- 06 feeding guide: Finish it. A. Cross-links the milk snake's cannibalism rule instead of restating it, and the prey-sizing logic is identical and clear.
- 07 enrichment guide: Finish it. A. Same honest labeling as the corn snake and milk snake enrichment guides: no kingsnake study exists, and it says so repeatedly instead of hiding the borrow.

**Conflicts**
The hub contradicts itself on venom resistance. Its own Fun Fact says kingsnakes "are immune to the venom of rattlesnakes, copperheads, and cottonmouths." Its own FAQ, two sections later, says "Partly. Serum research on the genus found meaningful resistance to rattlesnake venom, and how much varies by population." The encyclopedia agrees with the FAQ, not the fun fact: "That resistance is strong, not total, and it varies between kingsnake species and populations." Three places on the same page and site disagree on whether "immune" or "partly resistant" is the accurate word, and the page leads with the wrong one.

**Gaps**
Checked the sidebar for quarantine, heating, and shedding coverage first; both items below survive that check.
- The state-by-state legal guide is linked from every page but isn't in this set, and it matters more here than for the other species: the hub notes Oregon and Nevada regulate by color pattern rather than species, but nothing says which patterns.
- The cost guide flags that a hatchling buyer needs two or three enclosures as it grows, not one, but never puts a number on what the extra enclosures cost, the one place in an otherwise fully-priced guide that names a real cost and then skips the figure.

**Trust**
The "immune" claim under Conflicts is the one thing on this page that reads as flatly wrong rather than just simplified, and it's wrong in the more dangerous direction: a keeper who took it literally would treat a rattlesnake-resistant myth as more reassuring than it is. Nothing else in the set oversells. The handling guide's named citations are a genuine trust builder rare in this genre.

**Set grade: A-.** Everything past the hub is as rigorous as the milk snake set, with sourcing that's unusually easy to check. The one overstated claim sits in the highest-visibility spot on the page, which is why it costs more than a single line should. First fix: change "immune" to "partially resistant" in the hub's fun fact so it agrees with its own FAQ. Second: bring the missing legal guide into this set, or at minimum name the Oregon and Nevada color rules in the hub instead of just flagging that they exist.
