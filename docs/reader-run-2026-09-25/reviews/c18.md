## savannah-monitor

1. Page grades
- 00 care guide hub: A. Every first-week number links to its source article, buy list is concrete, emergency card is short and real.
- 01 encyclopedia: B+. No action items by design, but the CITES trade numbers (Ghana, Togo, Benin exports vs US imports) are genuinely useful context for why this species is cheap and often wild-caught.
- 02 cost guide: A. Honest that the 8x2x2 enclosure sold online is a partial match, not the real 8x4x4 an adult needs.
- 03 handling guide: A. Concrete warning signs, sourced to LafeberVet and Tree of Life, and the nail-trim crew size gives a real benchmark.
- 04 health issues guide: A. Body condition scoring (1 to 9, target 5) is something a reader can act on the same day.
- 05 tank setup guide: A. Answers the enclosure, heat, humidity, substrate and water numbers a buyer needs before ordering anything.
- 06 feeding guide: A. Schedule by age plus the reasoning for why rodents stay occasional.
- 07 enrichment guide: B+. Honest that the puzzle-tube study is genus level, not species level, and the obesity warning on food-based enrichment is the right call.

2. Conflicts: None found. Quarantine duration (3 to 6 months), lifespan (10 to 15, 15 to 20 achievable), monthly cost ($30 to $50), and enclosure cleaning interval (once or twice a month for loose substrate, confirmed in sidebar 03-reptile-salmonella-hygiene-guide.txt) all match across every page that repeats them.

3. Gaps, after the sidebar check:
- No dimensions or gallon figure for the water basin, only "big enough to submerge the whole body." A buyer shopping for a stock tank needs a number.
- No sexing method (visual or probe) anywhere in the species pages or sidebar.
- No first-aid guidance for a bite that does not break the skin, only "a deep one gets a doctor."
- No nail-trim how-to beyond "takes two or three people," which is a safety note, not instructions.

4. Trust: Nothing overstated or ad-like. The cost guide is unusually candid that the enclosure it prices is a partial match ("it's actually 8x2x2ft") rather than pretending it meets the standard, which is the opposite of a sales pitch.

5. Set grade: A-. The husbandry numbers are consistent everywhere they repeat and the enrichment guide is honest about its evidence gap, but a reader still cannot buy a correctly sized water basin or handle a first bite without going elsewhere. First two changes: give the water basin a size, and add a short sexing and bite-care note to the handling guide.

## green-iguana

1. Page grades
- 00 care guide hub: A. Dense first-week table, and the emergency card list is long because the species' problem list actually is long.
- 01 encyclopedia: B+. The CITES trade history (peak imports over 800,000 in 1995) explains why this species is everywhere and cheap.
- 02 cost guide: B. Good numbers throughout, but see the enclosure-size conflict below.
- 03 handling guide: A. The breeding-season castration study (Lock, Bennett and Gross) with a specific surgery-timing takeaway is the standout.
- 04 health issues guide: A. Seven conditions, each with signs and cause, no padding.
- 05 tank setup guide: A. Clear on why this is a room build, not a tank, and gives exact UVB mounting distances by bulb strength.
- 06 feeding guide: A. Separates oxalates from goitrogens instead of lumping them into one vague warning, which most care sheets get wrong.
- 07 enrichment guide: A. The 1977 Science study on hatchling sociality is real evidence, not a genus-level borrow, and the piece is upfront that it says nothing about adults.

2. Conflicts: The cost guide and the tank setup guide disagree on what "minimum" means. The cost guide says an adult "needs a 6 by 3 by 6 foot arboreal enclosure at a floor, with 10 by 5 by 6 as the working minimum" (02-cost-guide.txt), naming two different sizes as two different kinds of minimum in the same sentence. The tank setup guide only recognizes one: "A single adult needs a minimum of 10 feet long by 5 feet wide by 6 feet tall, some vet and husbandry sources recommend 12 by 6 by 6" (05-tank-setup-guide.txt), with no mention of 6x3x6 as adequate at all. A reader pricing a 6x3x6 box off the cost guide could believe it meets a real minimum; the setup guide says it does not.

3. Gaps, after the sidebar check:
- No sexing method for a hatchling or juvenile iguana anywhere in the set or sidebar.
- No nail-trim guidance, despite claws being listed as one of the three real physical risks in handling.
- No bite first-aid, only that claws and tail cause "deep scratches" and "welts."

4. Trust: The tank setup guide recommends a specific commercial product by name inside an affiliate-disclosed article: "a higher-end multi-nozzle system like the MistKing Starter System covers the space far more evenly than a single fogger" (05-tank-setup-guide.txt). It may be good advice, but naming one product this way inside husbandry copy reads closer to a placement than a neutral recommendation.

5. Set grade: B+. The husbandry content is some of the best-sourced material in the set, but the 6x3x6 vs 10x5x6 confusion in the cost guide is the kind of number a buyer acts on with real money, and the sexing and bite-care gaps sit next to a handling guide that specifically names claws and tail-whipping as real risks. First two changes: fix the cost guide's enclosure-size sentence so it does not imply 6x3x6 is any kind of adequate minimum, and add sexing plus basic nail and bite care to the handling guide.

## veiled-chameleon

1. Page grades
- 00 care guide hub: A. The first-week table correctly puts the laying bin next to the diet numbers, which is exactly where a new keeper needs it.
- 01 encyclopedia: B+. Short and to the point on the trade history and the infertile-clutch trait.
- 02 cost guide: A. Splits male and female lifespan cost implications clearly, and the egg-binding surgery number ($800 to $1,500+) is the one that actually changes a buying decision.
- 03 handling guide: A. The five-step taming ladder is genuinely usable, and the tarsal-spur sexing method is simple and correctly placed in both the body and the FAQ.
- 04 health issues guide: A. The post-lay checklist (dig up clutch, refill bin, taper diet) is the kind of concrete instruction a first-time female owner needs.
- 05 tank setup guide: A. States the dripper and mister timing (20 to 30 minutes, 3 to 5 minutes) as real settings, not vague advice.
- 06 feeding guide: A. Resolves its own apparent tension (no portion table gap) by giving one clean schedule by age plus the reasoning for the twice-monthly D3 and vitamin A cap.
- 07 enrichment guide: A. States plainly what the isolation study does and does not support, which is the harder and more honest thing to write.

2. Conflicts: None found. Dripper (20 to 30 minutes) and misting (3 to 5 minutes, 2 to 4 times daily) settings match exactly between the enclosure guide and the sidebar hydration guide. Vet cost figures in the hub and cost guide match. Egg-binding signs are identical across the hub, cost guide, and health guide.

3. Gaps, after the sidebar check:
- The cost guide raises spaying as a way to extend a female's life but gives no price, leaving a reader who wants to act on it with nowhere to go for a number.
- No guidance on what to do after a bite, even a minor one, though the handling guide does describe biting as a real (if last-resort) outcome.

4. Trust: The claim that "spaying a female can meaningfully extend her life" (02-cost-guide.txt) is stated as settled fact with no source or vet citation, unlike most of the numeric claims elsewhere in this set, which do cite a study or a vet sheet. It is plausible but reads as more confident than it is backed up.

5. Set grade: A. This is the tightest set of the four: no numeric disagreements, and the two chameleon-specific sidebar guides reinforce rather than contradict the species pages. First two changes: add a price range for spaying next to where it is first raised, and cite a source for that claim.

## jacksons-chameleon

1. Page grades
- 00 care guide hub: A. Puts the temperature difference from the veiled chameleon front and center, which is the single fact most likely to get a buyer in trouble if missed.
- 01 encyclopedia: B+. The Hawaii-as-source-population history explains the cost guide's warning about wild-caught animals cleanly.
- 02 cost guide: A. Explains exactly why a cheap Hawaiian animal is a red flag rather than a bargain, with the export law and the founder-population inbreeding both cited.
- 03 handling guide: A. Correctly separates "calm" from "content," and the horn-based sexing note is simple and accurate.
- 04 health issues guide: A. The staged description of respiratory infection (nose-up, then open-mouth, then eyes shut) and the full dystocia section are the strongest health writeups across all four sets.
- 05 tank setup guide: A. Gives distances for three different UVB bulb strengths through mesh and without, not just one number.
- 06 feeding guide: A. Explicitly reconciles what looks like a conflict (three insects vs a five-to-seven ceiling) inside the article itself rather than leaving two numbers sitting unexplained.
- 07 enrichment guide: A. Honest that there is no species-specific research and states the veiled chameleon borrow plainly, then correctly leads with temperature over any enrichment item.

2. Conflicts: None found. The one place two different numbers appear for the same thing (three insects per feeding vs a five-to-seven ceiling) is explained within the feeding article itself as two different rules for two different situations, not a contradiction. Quarantine duration, UVB bulb type, and vet-emergency signs all match across the hub, the health guide, and the sidebar.

3. Gaps, after the sidebar check:
- No guidance on finding a chameleon-experienced exotics vet before bringing one home, despite the health guide listing several conditions (dystocia, edema, gout) that need one urgently. Savannah monitor and green iguana both state this directly; this set does not.
- No bite first-aid, though minor given how rarely it is mentioned as a risk here.

4. Trust: Nothing overstated. Claims are consistently tied to a named source (LafeberVet, Chameleon Academy, VCA) rather than stated flatly, which is the strongest sourcing pattern of the four sets.

5. Set grade: A. The best-executed set here: it resolves its own apparent numeric conflict, is honest about its research gap, and the dystocia and baby-care sections are genuinely complete. First two changes: add a line on finding an exotics vet experienced with chameleons before the animal comes home, and a short note on what to do after a bite.
