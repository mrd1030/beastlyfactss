## hognose-snake

1. Page grades.
Care guide hub: A. Every number links to its source article, the emergency card is usable, and the buy list is realistic.
Encyclopedia: B+. Short by design, identity and history only, not meant to be actionable, and it says so honestly.
Cost guide: A. Real tables, a dated price check, and vet cost ranges you can plan around.
Handling guide: A. The bite mechanics and the 48 hour rule are concrete, and the venom section is honest about the one documented case.
Health issues guide: A. Every issue names a cause, a sign, and a fix, and the closing pattern section ties them together well.
Tank setup guide: A. States the heat mat versus halogen disagreement openly instead of picking a side and hiding the debate.
Feeding guide: A. The gram based prey chart is exactly what a new owner needs, and it names its sources.
Enrichment guide: A. Built on a named 2022 study, not vague advice, with a clear priority order.

2. Conflicts.
The tank setup guide says to replace hognose substrate in full "about every three months even if it looks clean." The sidebar's Salmonella and Reptile Hygiene guide gives the general reptile rule as loose sand or soil "spot cleaned daily and replaced completely once or twice a month." A reader who only reads the species page will run the substrate four to six times longer than the general hygiene guide recommends for the same material.

3. Gaps.
The hub and encyclopedia both link to "Is It Legal to Own a Hognose Snake?" for the state by state answer, but that article was not part of this read, so a reader following the site cannot yet confirm California's status, which the hub itself flags as unresolved.
The health issues guide makes several clinical claims (respiratory infection timelines, impaction thresholds) without naming a source, unlike the feeding guide, which lists three.

4. Trust.
Nothing sounds like an ad or an overstatement. Retailer names in the cost guide (Josh's Frogs, PetSmart) are disclosed as an affiliate price check, not a pitch. The substrate interval conflict above is the one place a reader could be misled into worse husbandry by trusting only one page.

5. Set grade: A-. The set is thorough, internally cross-linked, and honest about disagreements in the hobby. Fix the substrate replacement interval so the species page and the general hygiene page agree, and add citations to the health issues guide to match the feeding guide's standard.

## garter-snake

1. Page grades.
Care guide hub: A. Balances the legal complexity, the diet variety, and the swim feature clearly in one page.
Encyclopedia: B+. Identity and research history only, appropriately short.
Cost guide: A. Breaks the three diet types into separate cost paths instead of one misleading average.
Handling guide: A. Leads with musk instead of bite risk, which matches what a new owner will actually experience first.
Health issues guide: A. Thiamine deficiency section is specific about signs and cause, not generic.
Tank setup guide: A. The swimmable water feature is treated as core equipment, not an afterthought.
Feeding guide: A. The thiaminase warning is detailed and actionable, with named safe and unsafe fish and worms.
Enrichment guide: A. Opens by correcting its own past misattribution of a ratsnake study to garter snakes, a rare and trustworthy move.

2. Conflicts.
None found. Numbers for enclosure size, temperature, humidity, and quarantine length match across the hub, cost guide, and tank setup guide.

3. Gaps.
The hub promises a state by state legal guide (New York permit requirement is the teaser), but that article was not part of this read, so the legal picture stays unconfirmed here.
No page gives a full substrate replacement interval; the tank setup guide only says "spot-clean regularly." The sidebar hygiene guide gives a general interval for bark and mulch, but the species page never points to it or states its own number.
The cost guide actively encourages catching a wild garter snake, but no page gives quarantine or handling specifics for that exact scenario beyond the generic quarantine link everyone gets.

4. Trust.
Nothing overstated. The specific retailer price citation (Backwater Reptiles, $49.99) is disclosed and treated as a snapshot, not a quote. The enrichment guide's self correction is a genuine trust builder rather than a red flag.

5. Set grade: A. This is the most self critical set of the four, correcting its own past error and being upfront about dietary unpredictability instead of oversimplifying. Fix: state a real substrate change interval on the tank setup page, and add a line for the wild caught path since the cost guide specifically invites it.

## rosy-boa

1. Page grades.
Care guide hub: A. The "opposite of every other boa" humidity framing is clear and gets repeated consistently everywhere it matters.
Encyclopedia: B+. Identity and history only, appropriately short.
Cost guide: A. Shows its math (mouse cost per unit, aspen bags per change, heat mat wattage to kWh), which is unusually rigorous.
Handling guide: A. The balling response and the dawn/dusk activity timing are both concrete and specific to this species rather than generic snake advice.
Health issues guide: A. The retained shed section explicitly resolves the seeming contradiction with the dry baseline, which is exactly the kind of confusion a new keeper would have.
Tank setup guide: A. Substrate depth is given by enclosure size in cubic feet, genuinely useful for buying the right amount.
Feeding guide: A. Clear on why overfeeding, not underfeeding, is the real risk for this species.
Enrichment guide: A. Honest that no research exists on this species and clearly labels which parts are borrowed from hognose and corn snake studies.

2. Conflicts.
None found. Quarantine wording ("proximity with a lid on it") is quoted identically between the hub and the sidebar quarantine guide, and UVB zone guidance matches the sidebar's Ferguson Zone system.

3. Gaps.
The hub's legal teaser (NYC ban, Colorado's 2026 exemption change, New Jersey, Hawaii) was not confirmed against a dedicated legal article, since that page was not part of this read.
No page states how often to change the small water dish; garter snake's guide gives a weekly minimum for its water feature, but rosy boa gets no interval at all.
The health issues guide mentions buying captive-bred to prevent inclusion body disease but gives no vetting checklist, while the boa constrictor set has a full one for the same disease risk in the same family.

4. Trust.
Nothing overstated. The fun fact boxes match the body text rather than exaggerating it, and the vet price citation is sourced to a named, dated price list rather than a vague range.

5. Set grade: A. Consistently accurate, well cross-referenced, and unusually transparent about where evidence is borrowed from other species. Fix: add a captive-bred sourcing checklist to the health issues guide matching the boa constrictor page's rigor, and state a water dish change interval.

## boa-constrictor

1. Page grades.
Care guide hub: A. The second-handler rule and the "never loop around the neck" warning are given the prominence a genuine safety issue needs.
Encyclopedia: B+. Identity and trade history only, appropriately short, and the Cayos Cochinos history is a strong, specific detail.
Cost guide: B+ (thin ongoing cost section). The setup and vet cost tables are solid, but the "Ongoing Costs: Modest" section has no dollar figures at all, unlike the matching sections in all three other species guides in this set.
Handling guide: A. The body language checklist (tongue flicks, S-posture, musking) is specific and actionable, with named sources.
Health issues guide: A. The breeder vetting checklist for inclusion body disease is the strongest buying advice in the whole set.
Tank setup guide: A. Height plus floor space rule is explained rather than just asserted, and the semi-arboreal reasoning is clear.
Feeding guide: A. Good on why an adult refusing food is usually benign, with a clear step by step check-list.
Enrichment guide: A. Honest that no boa-specific research exists and explains why the ball python study transfers imperfectly (boas climb more).

2. Conflicts.
None found in the facts given. The one inconsistency is internal to the cost guide: it gives precise dollar figures for the snake, setup, and vet care, then drops to a single vague sentence for monthly food, substrate, and electricity, the exact category every other species guide in this set quantifies.

3. Gaps.
No monthly ongoing-cost number exists anywhere in the set for a boa constrictor, so a buyer weighing this species against a hognose or rosy boa can compare setup and vet costs but not the actual month to month bill.
The hub's legal teaser (federal withdrawal, Hawaii and NYC bans, state permits) was not confirmed against a dedicated legal article, since that page was not part of this read.
The detailed breeder vetting checklist lives only in the health issues guide; the cost guide, where a buyer is actually deciding who to pay, does not link to it.

4. Trust.
Nothing overstated. The common-boa-sold-as-red-tail warning is a genuinely useful anti overpay flag rather than a sales pitch, and it is repeated consistently across the cost, handling, and health pages.

5. Set grade: A-. Strong and safety-focused, let down only by the one thin section. Fix: add a real ongoing-cost table with numbers to the cost guide, and link the breeder vetting checklist from that page instead of leaving it only in health issues.
