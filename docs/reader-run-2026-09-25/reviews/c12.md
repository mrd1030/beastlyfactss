## hamster

1. Page grades
- 00 Care Guide Hub: A. Every first-week number is correct against the deep dives, the emergency card is real, nothing padded.
- 01 Encyclopedia: B. Good origin story (the single 1930 female, the 91-year gap) that actually explains why Syrians must live alone, but it is not meant to carry care advice on its own.
- 02 Cost Guide: A. Dated prices, a real cited study for lifespan, honest that setup costs more than the animal.
- 03 Handling Guide: A. The best page in the set: species-by-species temperament, correct scoop technique, sexing, and a clear-eyed dwarf-pairing protocol with an exit plan.
- 04 Health Issues Guide (thin on one point): B. Wet tail coverage is excellent and correctly framed as a same-day emergency, but it never mentions the antibiotic risk that a sidebar guide names for hamsters specifically.
- 05 Tank Setup Guide: A. Real numbers, cites the bedding-depth study, no filler.
- 06 Feeding Guide: A. Openly flags a genuine disagreement between sources on schedule instead of pretending it's settled.
- 07 Enrichment Guide: A-. Adds real value from the Hauzenberger study, though a chunk of it repeats the tank setup guide's bedding numbers.
- 08 vs. Guinea Pig: B+. Useful decision table, though the opening claim about return/rehoming rates has no source.

2. Conflicts
Comfortable temperature ceiling. 05-tank-setup-guide.txt: "65 to 75°F is the comfortable range, kept stable... Above 80°F risks heat stress." Sidebar 02-small-mammal-temperature-heat-stress-guide.txt lists Hamster "Comfortable range" as "65-80°F (18-27°C), per VCA rodent guidance." One page's comfort zone ends at 75, the other's at 80.

3. Gaps (after the sidebar check)
What to do during a multi-day owner absence (boarding, pet sitter): never covered; the vet-travel sidebar only covers trips to the vet.
No potty/litter guidance for the cage, a common early question.
No day-to-day management plan for a diagnosed diabetic dwarf beyond "vet-guided low-sugar diet."
Nothing on introducing a hamster safely around resident cats or dogs.

4. Trust
"A carrier built specifically for the job, like the Petsfit small-animal carrier, checks these boxes out of the box" (sidebar vet-travel guide) reads as product placement dropped into otherwise sourced advice.
"Both get returned or rehomed at a fairly high rate by people who picked based on which one looked cuter" (08-vs-guinea-pig-guide.txt) is stated flatly with no source anywhere in the set.
The vet-travel sidebar's "Bring the Cage Mate" section is listed as hamster reading, but it is written for bonded rabbit/guinea pig pairs. Taken literally for a Syrian it directly contradicts the hub's own solitary-housing rule.

5. Set grade: A-. Tight, well-sourced, and internally consistent almost everywhere, with one real content gap (the antibiotic risk is invisible unless a reader detours into a shared page) and one numeric mismatch (75°F vs 80°F comfort ceiling). First fix: pull the antibiotic-enterotoxemia risk into the health issues guide since it is hamster-specific. Second: reconcile the comfortable-range number between the tank setup guide and the shared temperature guide.

## gerbil

1. Page grades
- 00 Care Guide Hub: A. Numbers check out against every deep dive; the emergency card correctly treats diarrhea as urgent and tail injury as a same-day vet matter.
- 01 Encyclopedia: B. Short by design, but it plants the social-versus-solitary and burrowing facts that the rest of the set builds on.
- 02 Cost Guide: A. The sexing/accidental-litter section is a genuine standout, real numbers on how fast a mixed pair compounds into cost.
- 03 Handling Guide: A. The declanning section (warning signs in escalating order, who's the victim vs. aggressor) is the kind of detail most care sites skip entirely.
- 04 Health Issues Guide: A. Tyzzer's, cholesteatoma, tail slip and its after-amputation care are all handled with real specificity and consistent urgency framing.
- 05 Tank Setup Guide: A. Correctly foregrounds depth and humidity, cites real numbers for both.
- 06 Feeding Guide: A. The "bag says gerbil, formulated for hamster" point is a genuinely useful, specific warning.
- 07 Enrichment Guide: A. The Wiedenmayer study (tunnel-and-chamber beats sand, cage size doesn't matter) is used correctly and changes the buying priority in a way most enrichment pages don't.
- 08 vs. Guinea Pig: B+. Solid, standard comparison, no major issues.
- 09 vs. Hamster: A-. Clear on the two real differences (social needs, activity pattern) without padding.

2. Conflicts: None found. Enclosure size, bedding depth, humidity thresholds, protein/fat targets, portion sizes, cost figures, and lifespan all match exactly across every page that repeats them.

3. Gaps (after the sidebar check)
No heat-stress danger threshold specific to gerbils: the shared temperature guide's table covers guinea pig, rabbit, chinchilla, and hamster, but gerbil is not a row in it, and the species' own tank setup guide gives a comfortable range (65-75°F) without a stated upper danger line.
What to do during a multi-day owner absence (boarding, pet sitter) is never addressed, since the shared vet-travel guide only covers trips to the vet.
No guidance on introducing a gerbil pair to resident cats, dogs, or other pets in the home.
No day-to-day scent-gland maintenance for gerbils; the shared grooming guide covers only the rabbit's and guinea pig's glands, so gerbil owners get tumor-watch advice (health issues guide) but no routine care.

4. Trust
"Check the guaranteed analysis panel on the bag, Oxbow's Garden Select among them" (feeding guide) is a specific product name dropped into otherwise general sourcing.
The same "Bring the Cage Mate" and Petsfit-carrier language flagged in the hamster set's shared vet-travel article also sits in this species' Health and More list; it's less contradictory here since gerbils are properly paired, but it's still written entirely around rabbits and guinea pigs and never actually mentions a gerbil.

5. Set grade: A. This is the strongest of the two sets so far: no internal conflicts, specific and well-cited advice throughout, and real research used correctly rather than just cited. First fix: add a gerbil row to the shared temperature guide's table so there's an actual danger threshold, not just a comfortable range. Second: a short line in the grooming guide on checking a gerbil's scent gland during handling, since the health issues guide already treats a lump there as a real risk but no page says to look regularly.

## mouse

1. Page grades
- 00 Care Guide Hub: A. Tight numbers throughout, correctly flags that the "hamster and gerbil" tub on the shelf is the wrong protein target for a mouse.
- 01 Encyclopedia: A-. The Abbie Lathrop history (a failed poultry farmer whose mouse-breeding barn became the root of modern lab mouse genetics) is genuinely interesting and unusually deep for this page type.
- 02 Cost Guide: A. Honest about the sex-ratio cost trap and transparent that it deliberately skips a specific wheel product link until it has a verified-safe one.
- 03 Handling Guide: A. The 13-inch vertical jump and 24-inch escape figures actually change how a reader would set up a handling session, not just trivia.
- 04 Health Issues Guide: A. The mouse-versus-rat mammary tumor malignancy difference is a genuinely useful, specific fact most sites blur together.
- 05 Tank Setup Guide: A. The wire-cage-not-tank logic is argued clearly and explicitly contrasted with gerbil housing so a reader who read both sets won't get confused.
- 06 Feeding Guide: A. Correct and specific on the protein gap versus hamsters/gerbils, and the coprophagy explanation is accurate and reassuring rather than glossed over.
- 07 Enrichment Guide: A. The nesting-material-as-thermostat framing (6 to 10 grams) is a real, well-sourced finding used correctly.

2. Conflicts: None found. Cage size, bar spacing, bedding depth, wheel size, temperature/humidity range, protein target, and nesting-material amount all match exactly everywhere they repeat.

3. Gaps (after the sidebar check)
No nail-care guidance for mice anywhere in the set: the shared grooming guide's nail-wear table explicitly names hamsters, gerbils, rats and degus as species that wear their own nails down, but never mentions mice at all.
No heat-stress warning signs specific to mice: the shared temperature guide's species table and its "order of signs" section cover guinea pig, rabbit, chinchilla and hamster, but mice are absent from both, even though the mouse's own tank setup guide gives a comfortable range with no danger threshold or symptom list.
No vet-visit or anesthesia guidance actually written for mice: the shared vet-travel guide is written entirely around rabbits and guinea pigs and never names a mouse.
No protocol for introducing a new mouse into an existing female group later in life, only the male-specific littermate/desexing rule is covered in that detail.

4. Trust
"One widely cited mouse-cost guide notes that many owners skip routine vet visits for mice entirely given the cost relative to the animal's value" (02-cost-guide.txt) is caveated as not an endorsement, but it still normalizes skipping care in a way worth reading carefully rather than taking as guidance.
No product placement found in this set; the enrichment guide even states it deliberately omits a wheel product link until a verified-safe one exists, which is the opposite problem from what showed up in the hamster and gerbil sets.

5. Set grade: A. The strongest set so far: internally consistent, specific, and honest about the couple of places its numbers are estimates rather than settled fact. First fix: add mice to the shared grooming guide's nail-wear line, since every other rodent on the site gets that one sentence and mice don't. Second: give mice an explicit heat-stress danger threshold and symptom list, either in their own tank setup guide or by adding them to the shared temperature guide's table.

## rat

1. Page grades
- 00 Care Guide Hub: A. Correct, specific numbers throughout and an emergency card that matches the health issues guide exactly.
- 01 Encyclopedia: A-. The lab-line-versus-pet-line history (Wistar Institute, the National Fancy Rat Society's 1976 founding) is substantive enough to be worth reading on its own.
- 02 Cost Guide: A. The neuter/spay economics section, tumor-recurrence risk, and the honest note that spaying alongside a tumor removal doesn't prevent new ones, are real decision-relevant detail most cost guides skip.
- 03 Handling Guide: A. The allogrooming-versus-biting distinction and the two-week quarantine-before-introduction protocol are both genuinely useful and specific.
- 04 Health Issues Guide: A. Clear-eyed about a 95%-prevalence condition without being alarmist, and covers pituitary tumors, kidney disease, Zymbal's gland tumors, and bumblefoot with real specificity.
- 05 Tank Setup Guide: A. Explains the wire-cage-versus-tank logic mechanistically rather than just asserting it.
- 06 Feeding Guide: A. The label comparison (rat food's protein/fiber numbers against the same maker's hamster-and-gerbil formula) is a concrete, checkable fact.
- 07 Enrichment Guide: A. Cites a real 2026 pet-rat keeper survey rather than only lab literature, and is honest that the wheel is one category of seven, not the centerpiece.

2. Conflicts: None found. Cage size, bar spacing, temperature range, wheel size, bedding schedule, vet cost figures, and lifespan all match exactly across every page that repeats them.

3. Gaps (after the sidebar check)
No mention anywhere in the set of whether rats are nocturnal, crepuscular, or day-active, an activity-pattern detail every other species in this reading assignment states plainly and rats do not.
No vet-visit or anesthesia guidance actually written for rats: the shared vet-travel guide the hub links to for "Vet trips" is written entirely around rabbits and guinea pigs and never names a rat, despite the hub sending readers there specifically.
No mention of barbering (fur or whisker pulling between cage-mates), a well-documented rat social behavior that a health-conscious owner would want flagged alongside the declanning-style warning signs the gerbil set covers for its own species.

4. Trust
The cost guide names specific commercial products more than any other page in this reading assignment: "a basic multi-level starter cage like the Prevue rolling cage runs around $200" and "a spacious option like the Critter Nation double unit... runs $280 to $360," repeated with brand detail (the MidWest cage line sold under different branding for ferrets) that reads closer to a buying guide than the sourced-figure tables the other cost guides use.

5. Set grade: A. Extremely strong: specific, mechanistic explanations instead of bare assertions, and no internal contradictions. First fix: state the species' actual activity pattern somewhere, since a reader finishes this whole set not knowing when a rat is awake. Second: either give rats their own line in the shared vet-travel guide or add a short rat-specific anesthesia note to the health issues guide, since the hub promises vet-trip guidance the linked page doesn't actually deliver for this species.
