## african-grey

1. Page grades:
- 00 care guide hub: A. Complete first-week numbers, an emergency card built on real signs, and a buy list you could shop from today.
- 01 encyclopedia: A-. Short, but earns its slot: origin, the 2016 CITES Appendix I vote, real quick facts.
- 02 cost guide: A. Real price ranges, a closed-leg-band explainer that actually protects a buyer from a fake CITES pitch, and a lifespan section that reframes the whole budget.
- 03 handling guide: A. Separates fear-biting from aggression, gives an actual step-up protocol, and is honest that N'kisi's word count is owner-reported rather than studied.
- 04 health issues guide: A. Hypocalcemia, plucking, aspergillosis, psittacosis, PBFD, plus a full first-exam workup list.
- 05 cage setup guide: A-. Specific numbers throughout (bar spacing, UVB distance and duration, temperature range), and ties the aspergillosis risk directly to a cleaning habit.
- 06 feeding guide: A. Clear ratios, a real toxin list, and explains why grit is actively dangerous instead of just banning it.
- 07 enrichment guide: A. The strongest page in the set. Names the actual studies (Lumeij and Hommers, Meehan et al.) and is honest about where the evidence is thinner (established adult pluckers versus prevention in young birds).
- 08 vs. cockatoo guide: A-. A real comparison table, not marketing copy for either bird.

2. Conflicts: One soft tension. The health issues guide (04) says a grey needs a vet visit "at least once a year, with many avian vets advising twice." The sidebar quarantine guide says "after that first visit, avian vets commonly recommend checkups at least twice a year," treating twice as the norm rather than the more cautious minority. Not a hard contradiction, but the two pages don't agree on which number is the baseline. No other conflicts found.

3. Gaps (after the sidebar check):
- Nothing on whether a grey benefits from a second bird for company, or how to introduce one; the whole set assumes a single grey and one owner providing 4 to 5 hours a day.
- Nothing on arranging pet-sitting or boarding for a routine absence. Succession planning (sidebar) only covers permanent absence, not a two-week trip.
- The "Is It Legal to Own an African Grey Parrot? CITES Appendix I Explained" deep dive is referenced repeatedly but wasn't part of this read set, so anyone actually planning international travel with the bird has no answer here beyond the domestic leg-band explainer in the cost guide.

4. Trust: Nothing reads as an ad or an overstatement. The set is careful about sourcing, flags the N'kisi word-count claim as anecdotal rather than studied, and is specific about which numbers are "commonly cited" versus actual veterinary consensus (cage size, vet frequency).

5. Set grade: A-. The strongest of the three sets: consistent numbers, real citations, honest hedging where the evidence is thin. First fix: pick one number for vet-visit cadence instead of two slightly different framings. Second: add a line on single-bird versus companion-bird welfare, since 4 to 5 hours a day of human time is the headline ask and the set never says whether a second grey helps or complicates it.

## cockatoo

1. Page grades:
- 00 care guide hub: A. Excellent first-week numbers and an emergency card built on real signs (a glossy, powder-less beak; over-preening to bare skin).
- 01 encyclopedia: B+. Solid CITES and import history, but "Cacatua alba" and umbrella-cockatoo specifics stand in for "cockatoo" generically without flagging that.
- 02 cost guide: A-. Thorough and itemized, with real per-visit vet pricing, but its lifespan line doesn't match a sidebar FAQ (see conflicts).
- 03 handling guide: A. The over-bonding section and the crest and eye body-language breakdown are specific and actionable, not generic parrot advice.
- 04 health issues guide: A. Covers lipomas, fatty liver, cloacal prolapse, and PBFD, and ties each one to a husbandry cause.
- 05 cage setup guide: B+. Good numbers throughout, but names specific commercial cage products by brand and model (see Trust).
- 06 feeding guide: A. A real portioning method (weigh what's left, not what goes in) instead of a vague "watch the bowl."
- 07 enrichment guide: A. Unusually honest that the plucking research is thin, and refuses to repeat an uncited "training beats the others" claim that circulates elsewhere.
- 08 screaming/plucking guide: A. The over-bonding mechanics and the concrete warning-sign list are the best behavioral content in the set.

2. Conflicts: Lifespan. The hub, the cost guide, and the sidebar's cockatiel-vs-cockatoo guide all say cockatoos "commonly live 40 to 60 years," with some individuals reaching the 70s or 90s. The sidebar's Choosing a Pet Bird guide gives a different typical range for the same species: "a cockatoo's [lifespan is] 25 to 45 years, with the larger species living 70 to 80 years or more" (attributed to VCA). Twenty-five to 45 and 40 to 60 can't both be the normal case, and this is exactly the number a buyer uses to size up the commitment. No other conflicts found.

3. Gaps:
- The health issues guide discusses PBFD screening and a pre-purchase exam but doesn't link to or restate the baseline first-exam timing and bloodwork that the sidebar quarantine guide covers in detail. Covered only in the quarantine sidebar guide; the cockatoo pages give no reason to open it.
- Nothing on how to actually vet a rescue organization or what to ask one, despite adoption being recommended twice in the set.

4. Trust: The cage setup guide names two specific commercial products by brand and model, "the Prevue Pet Products Imperial Extra Large Stainless Steel Bird Cage" and "the Prevue Pet Products Empire X-Large Bird Cage," on a page that carries an affiliate disclosure. That reads like a sponsored placement rather than neutral advice, and it sits oddly next to the enrichment guide's refusal, two pages later, to repeat an unverifiable claim.

5. Set grade: B+. Strong, specific, well-sourced behavioral content, undercut by an internal lifespan conflict a buyer would actually use, and one page that drifts into naming products. First fix: reconcile the 25-45 versus 40-60 year lifespan claim. Second: pull the specific cage brand names out of the setup guide or label them clearly as recommendations rather than facts.

## conure

1. Page grades:
- 00 care guide hub: A. Clear numbers, and honest about expectations: "buying one expecting a budgie with more color is how conures end up rehomed."
- 01 encyclopedia: C+. Short, and its claim that green cheeks bond "without requiring as intensive a daily time commitment" as larger parrots undersells the rest of the set (see conflicts).
- 02 cost guide: A. Real breeder-versus-pet-store price gap, morph pricing, and an honest note that finding a vet can be harder than affording one.
- 03 handling guide: A. "The beak is a tool, not a weapon" framing and the DNA-sexing explanation are genuinely useful rather than filler.
- 04 health issues guide: A-. Complete, ties each condition to a husbandry cause, and correctly notes hypocalcemia is less common here than in larger parrots like African greys.
- 05 cage setup guide: A. Specific bar spacing and floor advice, and honest that wing-clipping is the keeper's call rather than picking a side.
- 06 feeding guide: A. The "four failures of an all-seed diet" structure is the clearest diet-risk explanation across all nine files reviewed today, and it names its sources.
- 07 enrichment guide: A. Upfront that there is no conure-specific research and that it's borrowing from grey and Amazon studies, which is the right way to handle a real evidence gap.

2. Conflicts: The encyclopedia (01) says green cheeks "bond closely with keepers without requiring as intensive a daily time commitment" than larger parrots. Every other page in the set says the opposite in practice: the cage guide calls "several hours of supervised free time daily" non-optional, the enrichment guide specifies "three to four hours of supervised out-of-cage time every day," and the handling guide says housing one without "meaningful daily human time" reliably causes problems. A reader who only skims the encyclopedia would conclude this is a lower-commitment bird than the rest of the set describes.

3. Gaps:
- Nothing on chronic egg laying or egg binding in a single conure hen. The shared sidebar guide on that topic is scoped explicitly to budgies, cockatiels, and lovebirds and never mentions conures, so a lone hen owner has nowhere to go on this species.
- Nothing beyond one line (housing with other Pyrrhura species) on whether to get a second conure or a bonded pair; someone deciding between one bird and two gets no real guidance.

4. Trust: Nothing reads as an ad. The feeding guide's named foraging-format brands (Nutri-Berries, Avi-Cakes, Pellet-Berries) are presented as a nutritional-equivalence claim with a stated basis, not a sales pitch, and the page lists its actual sources (VCA, Merck, Lafeber) at the bottom, which the other two sets don't do.

5. Set grade: B+. Very strong individual pages let down by one encyclopedia line that contradicts the daily-commitment message the rest of the set is built around. First fix: rewrite the encyclopedia's time-commitment claim so it doesn't undercut the hub, handling, and enrichment guides. Second: add or link something on single-hen egg laying, since the shared sidebar guide explicitly excludes this species.
