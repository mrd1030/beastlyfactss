## budgie

**1. Page by page**
- 00 Care guide hub: A. Every number links to its source article, emergency card is clear, buy list is practical.
- 01 Encyclopedia: B+. Good history and origin detail, no action items, but that is what this page type is for.
- 02 Cost guide: A-. Real dollar ranges throughout, honest about avian vets being scarce and pricier.
- 03 Handling guide: A. Concrete taming ladder, ages the bird by eye and forehead barring, tells you what not to do.
- 04 Health issues guide: A. Ties the leg-lameness-means-tumor detail together well, gives a same-day symptom list.
- 05 Cage setup guide: A. Width-over-height reasoning, bar spacing as a hard rule, cleaning routine spelled out.
- 06 Feeding guide: A. The iodine/goiter section is the best thing in the set, specific and sourced.
- 07 Enrichment guide: B+. Solid but more generic than the feeding or cage pages.
- 08 Cere color guide: A-. Narrow topic done well, flags the mutation exception most sites miss.
- 09 vs. Cockatiel guide: B+. Useful comparison table, light on hard numbers beyond size and lifespan.

**2. Conflicts**
None found.

**3. Gaps**
- No steps anywhere in the set for introducing a second budgie to an existing one, despite repeatedly recommending a companion bird. Cockatiel, lovebird, and parrotlet sets all give an intro protocol; budgie's does not, and the sidebar folder has nothing bird-specific either (only the general colony/aviary guide, which covers groups, not a two-bird introduction).
- No weaning age or minimum age to bring a budgie home. The set gives juvenile-vs-adult eye and forehead cues but never a number, unlike the cockatiel feeding guide, which states 42 to 52 days.

**4. Trust**
The hub's Fun Fact states "The Guinness World Record holder was a budgie named Puck who knew 1,728 words, more than any other bird on record!" as flat fact with no citation, while the feeding guide two clicks away sources every claim to named vets and hospitals. The disparity is worth noticing.
The cage setup guide ends with "[card: the printable care package for this species, with its price and a link to the store]", a paid-product plug sitting inside an otherwise sourced care article.

**5. Set grade: A-.** Consistent numbers, real sourcing on the technical pages, and no contradictions across nine articles is a genuinely strong result. First fix: write the missing two-budgie introduction steps. Second: either source the Puck claim properly or move it out of the unqualified "Fun Fact" slot.

## cockatiel

**1. Page by page**
- 00 Care guide hub: A. Clean, well-linked, emergency card is specific.
- 01 Encyclopedia: B+. Good breeding-mutation history, no action items expected.
- 02 Cost guide: B-. Real numbers for the bird and monthly food, but the cage row just says "Often the single biggest expense" with no range.
- 03 Handling guide: A. The full two-cockatiel introduction protocol is the best of the four sets.
- 04 Health issues guide: A. Ties nutrition, reproduction, and the kitchen hazard together clearly.
- 05 Cage setup guide: A-. Vertical-bar reasoning and the powder-down cleaning note are good detail; the earthquake anecdote is unsourced.
- 06 Feeding guide: A. Honest about not inventing a "hours without food" number, gives real warning signs instead.
- 07 Enrichment guide (Two Studies): B. Interesting framing, but the two named studies carry no author, journal, or year detail beyond "2020" and "2025."
- 08 vs. Cockatoo guide: A-. Strong comparison, the "velcro bird" section is genuinely useful for a buyer.

**2. Conflicts**
None found.

**3. Gaps**
- The cost guide never puts a dollar figure on the single largest line item, the cage itself, unlike every other species' cost guide in this batch, which all give a cage range. A buyer reading only this page cannot budget the biggest expense.
- The cage setup guide never directly tells a single-bird owner to skip nest-like hideouts. That advice exists only in the shared chronic-egg-laying sidebar guide, reached through a link buried in the health issues article's reproductive-disease section, not the cage guide itself.

**4. Trust**
The cage setup guide states: "Avian vets in earthquake-prone California have noticed that cockatiels, more than other species, turn up injured the morning after a tremor, and a clinic in Melbourne saw the same after two minor quakes there." No source is named for either claim, unlike the feeding and enrichment articles, which cite specific studies or institutions throughout.

**5. Set grade: A-.** Deep, well-sourced pages let down slightly by a cost guide that dodges its most important number and one unsourced anecdote. First fix: put an actual price range on the cage in the cost guide. Second: source or cut the earthquake claim.

## lovebird

**1. Page by page**
- 00 Care guide hub: A. Leads with the one-bird-or-two decision, which is exactly right for this species.
- 01 Encyclopedia: B+. Good trade-history detail, no action items expected.
- 02 Cost guide: B+. Thorough, but the cage price contradicts itself within the same page (see Trust).
- 03 Handling guide: A. Honest about the name being misleading, the two-lovebird introduction steps are excellent.
- 04 Health issues guide: A. The best-sourced page in any of the four sets, cites VCA, LafeberVet, PetMD, Merck, and a university source by name.
- 05 Cage setup guide: A. Bar spacing framed as a safety issue rather than a preference, materials section is specific.
- 06 Feeding guide: A. The "dusting seed does nothing" section is a genuinely useful correction to common advice.
- 07 Enrichment guide: A-. Cites an actual Japan-wide parrot survey rather than a vague "research shows."
- 08 vs. Budgie guide: B+. Clear on bonding-intensity as the deciding factor, good comparison table.

**2. Conflicts**
None found between pages. Within the cost guide itself: the table lists the cage as "$80 - $150," while the paragraph directly below it says "A quality stainless or powder-coated option runs $80 to $200."

**3. Gaps**
- The enrichment guide states plainly that no study has put a number of hours on a lone lovebird's daily needs, which is honest, but it leaves a new owner with no concrete daily-time anchor at all, unlike the cockatiel enrichment guide's "an hour or more."
- No lovebird-sized grooming or nail-trim instructions; the shared grooming guide in the sidebar is explicitly sized to a cockatiel, so a lovebird owner has to extrapolate down.

**4. Trust**
The cage setup guide ends with the same "[card: the printable care package for this species, with its price and a link to the store]" plug as the budgie cage guide, a paid-product mention inside an otherwise clean, well-sourced article.
The cage price mismatch above ($80-$150 vs $80-$200) sits in the same article and could send a buyer shopping with the wrong number.

**5. Set grade: A.** The most honestly written set of the four, willing to say "we don't know" rather than invent a number, and the health page's sourcing is a standout. First fix: reconcile the $80-$150 and $80-$200 cage figures in the cost guide. Second: add a rough daily-time floor for a solo lovebird even if it is framed as a judgment call rather than a research finding.

## parrotlet

**1. Page by page**
- 00 Care guide hub: A. Dense but well organized, the emergency card is the most detailed of the four species.
- 01 Encyclopedia: B+. Good regulatory history (Wild Bird Conservation Act), no action items expected.
- 02 Cost guide: B+. Good breakdowns, but the monthly total contradicts itself between the body and the FAQ (see Trust).
- 03 Handling guide: A. The "warning nip" section and the direct comparison to a budgie's more forgiving temperament are the strongest writing in the set.
- 04 Health issues guide: A-. Clear on the lower PBFD risk with a real geographic explanation, good same-day symptom list.
- 05 Cage setup guide: A. Quarter-inch bar spacing justified by head size rather than asserted, good detail on locking latches.
- 06 Feeding guide: A. Specific on why this bird needs no grit, thorough toxic-food list.
- 07 Enrichment guide: B+. Honest that no parrotlet-specific research exists and borrows from African grey and cockatoo studies instead, which is transparent but thin.

**2. Conflicts**
None found between pages. Within the cost guide itself: the body says "Ongoing Costs: Roughly $21 to $33 a Month," repeated again in the text, while the FAQ directly below states "Roughly $20 to $40 a month" for the same ongoing costs.

**3. Gaps**
- No weaning age or minimum acquisition age is given anywhere in the set, the same gap as the budgie set.
- Parrotlet is the only one of the four species here without its own standalone comparison deep dive. Budgie, cockatiel, and lovebird each have a top-level "X vs. Y" article; the parrotlet's only such piece is a shared sidebar overview against zebra finches, not a dedicated parrotlet-vs-budgie or parrotlet-vs-lovebird page, even though the handling and feeding guides compare it to a budgie repeatedly in the body text.

**4. Trust**
The cost guide's ongoing-cost contradiction above ($21-$33 vs $20-$40) is a real inconsistency a reader would hit within one page.

**5. Set grade: B+.** Strong, honest writing throughout, especially on temperament, but the monthly-cost contradiction and the missing comparison page are the two things worth fixing first. First fix: reconcile the $21-$33 and $20-$40 monthly figures in the cost guide. Second: write a standalone parrotlet vs. budgie deep dive to match what the other three species get.
