## rabbit

Page grades:
- 00 Care guide hub: A. Every first-week number links to its source article, the emergency card is genuinely useful, and I'd print it.
- 01 Encyclopedia: B+. Short by design, but the Pope Gregory myth correction is the kind of detail worth reading even without a rabbit yet.
- 02 Cost guide: A. Real dollar ranges, dated, and the giant-breed feeding math alone justifies the page.
- 03 Handling guide: A. The trancing section is the standout, it debunks a widespread myth with actual physiology data instead of just saying "don't."
- 04 Health issues guide: A-. Covers GI stasis, dental disease, flystrike, RHDV2 and uterine cancer with clear vet triggers, though it never mentions the antibiotic risk that's one click away in the sidebar.
- 05 Housing guide: A. Scales the space standard for giant breeds instead of stopping at "one size fits all," and the litter training trick is genuinely actionable.
- 06 Feeding guide: A. The life-stage table plus the weight-scaled portion table for large breeds is the most complete feeding page in the set.
- 07 Enrichment guide: A. Cites actual welfare studies (behavioral diversity, fecal corticosterone, friendship index) instead of the usual toy-aisle advice, and ranks priorities honestly.
- 08 GI stasis guide: A-. The dedicated emergency page, thorough on triggers and treatment, but a lot of it repeats the hub, health guide, and feeding guide's "why rabbits stop eating" section almost verbatim. Still worth its own page since it's the top killer.

Conflicts: None found on numbers I could directly compare. Nail trim interval is phrased two ways, "roughly every 6 to 8 weeks" (hub and sidebar grooming guide) versus "every one to two months" (handling guide). The ranges overlap, so it's a phrasing inconsistency rather than a true contradiction.

Gaps (after sidebar check):
- Nothing on quarantining or slowly introducing a new rabbit to a resident one before bonding. Grepping the sidebar for quarantine or isolation returned nothing, and the enrichment guide's bonding section jumps straight to short supervised sessions with no pre-bonding health check or separation period.
- No settling-in guidance for the first 24 to 48 hours after bringing a rabbit home, when it's most likely to hide or refuse food from stress alone.
- No signs that a bonding attempt is failing or how to reset it, only that bonding "takes weeks."
- The antibiotic-associated enterotoxemia risk is covered only in the sidebar Enterotoxemia guide. The rabbit health issues guide gives no reason to open it, it doesn't mention antibiotics at all despite listing several other emergencies.

Trust: The sidebar vet-visit guide's carrier section reads like sponsored copy: "A carrier built specifically for the job, like the Petsfit small-animal carrier, checks these boxes out of the box." That's a named product dropped into otherwise sourced, disclosure-labeled content. Separately, the feeding guide's claim that "an adult Flemish Giant can drink as much in a day as a 25-pound dog" has no source attached, unlike nearly every other figure in the set, which cites PetMD, Merck, or the House Rabbit Society by name.

Set grade: A-. This is the most thorough set structurally, sourced numbers, a real emergency card, and enrichment backed by actual studies rather than generic tips. The two things I'd fix first: add a short "bringing your rabbit home" or introduction/quarantine section since it's a real gap the sidebar doesn't cover either, and drop the named carrier product from the sidebar guide or move it into a clearly separate "what we recommend" box so it doesn't sit inside sourced veterinary advice.

## guinea-pig

Page grades:
- 00 Care guide hub: A. First-week numbers are dense and sourced, print-ready emergency card, but it repeats a temperature figure that its own linked sidebar guide contradicts (see Conflicts).
- 01 Encyclopedia: B+. Short, but the sixteenth-century skeleton dating is a genuinely interesting hook.
- 02 Cost guide: A. Breaks the budget down honestly for a pair rather than one animal, and explains which pairings need surgery and which don't.
- 03 Handling guide: A. The full quarantine-then-neutral-ground introduction protocol, with a real step list and named warning signs, is the strongest single section in the set.
- 04 Health issues guide: A. Covers respiratory infection, scurvy, dental disease, bladder stones, mites, and ovarian cysts, with an actual differential table between cysts and mites that most care sites skip.
- 05 Housing guide: A-. Solid, but restates the 80°F heatstroke line as flat fact instead of the sidebar's own more cautious 75°F starting point for this species.
- 06 Feeding guide: A. Clear life-stage schedule and a full toxic-food list with reasoning, not just a bare list.
- 07 Enrichment guide: A-. Good foraging detail and warning signs, though the "2025 survey of 1,181 guinea pig keepers" stat has no source named, unlike nearly everything else on the page.
- 08 Scurvy guide: A. The best page in the set: explains why fortified pellets alone fail, gives per-kilogram dosing plus the everyday equivalent, and covers overdose risk too.

Conflicts: The care guide hub and the tank-setup guide both state "Heatstroke becomes a real risk above 80°F" for guinea pigs, with the hub calling the 79-to-80°F gap "a single degree" of margin. But the sidebar's own Heat Stress guide, linked from both pages, gives guinea pigs a lower, separate line: "Guinea pigs can run into heat trouble from 75°F (24°C), and rabbits, chinchillas and hamsters from about 80°F," repeating 75°F again in its power-outage section. The species pages promise a five-degree cushion past 79°F that the linked source says does not exist.

Gaps (after sidebar check):
- No guidance on what a new guinea pig's 2-to-3-week quarantine should actually include, such as a vet check or mite screening, only that it prevents introducing illness to the herd.
- No aftercare instructions for spay/neuter recovery, incision monitoring, activity restriction, when to worry, beyond the general anesthesia notes in the sidebar vet-visit guide.
- No routine cage-cleaning schedule (spot cleaning versus full bedding changes), which matters for a solid-floor cage getting used by two or more animals.

Trust: The enrichment guide's "2025 survey of 1,181 guinea pig keepers" stands out because it names no publication or source, while the rest of the page and set consistently cite Merck, PetMD, or named studies. The cost guide's mention of "the MidWest Guinea Habitat, an 8 sq ft option at around $100 to $109" reads closer to a product citation than an ad, since it comes with dated pricing context like the rest of the page, so it is a lower-priority flag than a similar carrier mention in the rabbit set's sidebar.

Set grade: A-. This is a strong, well-sourced set with a genuinely useful introduction protocol and the best single-condition explainer (scurvy) of anything reviewed. Fix the 75°F versus 80°F heat threshold conflict first, since it's a real safety number and both hub and tank-setup guide currently give a false margin. Second, add a short quarantine checklist and a spay/neuter aftercare paragraph, since surgery and new-pig introduction are both treated as routine events but neither gets a real "what happens after" section.

## chinchilla

Page grades:
- 00 Care guide hub: A. Dense, sourced first-week numbers, and the antibiotic warning gets its own line item here instead of being buried in the sidebar.
- 01 Encyclopedia: B+. The 500,000-skins-a-year ranching history is a strong, specific hook.
- 02 Cost guide: A. The air-conditioning line item and the rescue-versus-breeder pre-purchase checklist (eyes, teeth, weaning age, temperament) are both things a buyer would actually use at pickup.
- 03 Handling guide: A. The clearest introduction protocol reviewed so far: health check and parasite test first, side-by-side cages for a week, then neutral ground, in that order.
- 04 Health issues guide: A. The 150 rule gets repeated cleanly, and tracking weight in grams by life stage is a genuinely actionable early-warning tool most care sites skip.
- 05 Housing guide: A. Includes an actual daily-versus-weekly cleaning schedule, something the guinea pig set's housing page never gives.
- 06 Feeding guide: A. Flags disputed items (apple, carrot, lettuce) openly instead of presenting a single list as settled, which is more honest than most feeding guides.
- 07 Enrichment guide: A. Built around a real Scientific Reports study on cage flooring and fur-chewing, not generic tips.
- 08 Chinchilla vs. Guinea Pig: A-. Direct, honest comparison table with a clear bottom line for each type of household.
- 09 Chinchilla vs. Hamster: A-. Same strength, centered honestly on the lifespan gap as the real deciding factor.

Conflicts: The species pages consistently give dust bath length as 10 to 30 minutes (hub, and the housing guide's own table: "Dust bath length | 10 to 30 minutes"). But the sidebar Grooming, Nails, and Molting guide's FAQ states: "The Merck Veterinary Manual recommends a dust bath 2 to 3 times a week for up to 15 minutes at a time." A reader following the hub could leave a chinchilla in dust for twice as long as the source the sidebar cites actually recommends.

Gaps (after sidebar check): Nail trimming is never addressed for chinchillas anywhere in this set or the sidebar grooming guide, which despite its "Small Mammal" title is written almost entirely around rabbit and guinea pig nail care, with chinchillas mentioned only for dust and matted fur. Rabbit and guinea pig owners get an explicit 6-to-8-week trim interval; a chinchilla owner gets nothing, not even a note that chinchilla nails typically need less attention. No aftercare guidance exists either for a sick or injured chinchilla coming home from a vet visit (recovery environment, when to resume normal dust bathing).

Trust: The sidebar vet-visit guide, linked from this hub too, contains the same named-product line seen in the other sets: "A carrier built specifically for the job, like the Petsfit small-animal carrier, checks these boxes out of the box." It reads as product placement inside otherwise sourced, disclosure-labeled advice, and it appears verbatim across every species' shared sidebar folder rather than being chinchilla-specific.

Set grade: A. This is the strongest set of the three reviewed today: real citations for the enrichment claims, an honest disputed-foods list instead of a false consensus, and the best-explained introduction protocol. Fix the dust bath duration conflict first, since it's a specific number a new owner would follow literally. Second, add a short nail-care note for chinchillas, either in the housing guide or by actually covering the species in the shared grooming guide, since right now that guide's title promises coverage it doesn't deliver for this animal.

## degu

Page grades:
- 00 Care guide hub: A-. Otherwise excellent and dense, but see Trust: one first-week line item names the wrong species.
- 01 Encyclopedia: A-. The named researchers, the 1964 MIT collection trace, and the 2022 inbreeding-drift study make this the most substantial encyclopedia page of the four sets reviewed today.
- 02 Cost guide: A. Correctly frames the per-animal price as the wrong number to budget from, and prices dental and diabetes management as expected costs rather than remote risks.
- 03 Handling guide: A. The male-versus-female sexing instructions (the 6 to 10 mm gap test) are a level of specificity most care sites skip entirely, and the adult introduction protocol is detailed and staged.
- 04 Health issues guide: A. Diabetes framed clearly as the one condition that should shape the whole diet, with orange-versus-white teeth as a genuinely useful home check.
- 05 Enclosure guide: A. The solid-roof detail (degus fear movement overhead because of avian predators) is a specific, well-reasoned point most housing guides miss entirely.
- 06 Feeding guide: A. Sugar is treated as a hard metabolic limit rather than a moderation guideline, and the guide is honest that it's the single most consequential mistake owners make.
- 07 Enrichment guide: A. Grounded in real dust-bathing communication research, and reframes the dust tray as a shared scent board instead of hygiene, which changes the practical advice (don't sterilize it every time).
- 08 Degu vs. Chinchilla: A-. Clear, honest comparison, consistent with both species' own pages on lifespan and temperature.

Conflicts: None found on numbers between pages in this set.

Gaps (after sidebar check):
- The adult introduction protocol (scent-swap, then neutral ground) never includes a health check or parasite screen before two adults are introduced, unlike the chinchilla set's version of the same protocol, which explicitly puts a vet exam and parasite test first.
- No neuter cost or procedure information anywhere, even though a neutered male living with females is presented as a valid pairing option. The cost guide has no line for it.
- Digestive bloating (tympany) is named as a cause of appetite loss in the feeding guide but gets one sentence, with no dedicated emergency guide the way rabbits get one for GI stasis, despite being described as "genuinely" appetite-suppressing.

Trust: The care guide hub's first-week list includes this line under "Antibiotics": "Certain ordinary antibiotics can wipe out a chinchilla's gut bacteria and let toxin-producing Clostridium take over." This is the degu hub page, about a degu's antibiotic risk, naming the wrong species. Separately, the species pages state degu temperature limits with flat confidence, "never toward 77°F," "kept below about 77°F," while the sidebar Heat Stress guide's own FAQ admits: "We couldn't verify a specific degu temperature range from a veterinary source, though degus are... generally kept in the same range as a precaution." The number itself is probably reasonable, but the species pages present a precautionary estimate as an established veterinary figure.

Set grade: A-. The best-researched set of the four for its handling and enrichment sections, with genuine science behind both the sexing method and the dust-bath-as-communication framing. Fix the chinchilla/degu species mix-up in the hub first, it's a one-line factual error on the page most new owners read first. Second, add the missing pre-introduction health check to the handling guide's adult pairing protocol, since the chinchilla set already shows what that step should look like.
