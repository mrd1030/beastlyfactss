## pacman-frog

**1. Page by page**
- 00 Care guide hub: A. Every first-week number is real and links out; the emergency card and buy list are both usable as-is.
- 01 Encyclopedia: B. Short but earns its place, the Darwin/Bell history is a nice touch, nothing actionable though.
- 02 Cost guide: A. Real price ranges, a real vet-cost number, honest about impaction being the actual financial risk.
- 03 Handling guide: A. Clear rule (never hand-feed), clear technique, useful sexing detail most guides skip.
- 04 Health issues guide: A. Symptom lists are specific enough to act on, and it correctly separates normal aestivation from the under-a-year emergency version.
- 05 Tank setup guide: A. The heat-mat-never-underneath rule and the substrate rule are both stated with the reasoning, not just as fiat.
- 06 Feeding guide: A. The fasting-tolerance numbers come with an honest "rough consensus, not clinical fact" caveat, which is rare and welcome.
- 07 Enrichment guide: A (borderline filler). The body-condition-scoring and glass-surfing research is genuinely useful, but the "buried is not bored" point is now the third time this set has made it (hub, health, enrichment), and this page spends a lot of words re-arguing it.

**2. Conflicts**
None found. Quarantine length (hub: "6 to 8 weeks") matches the sidebar's Merck-sourced 6-to-8-week figure exactly, and the hygiene/Salmonella rules match the sidebar hygiene guide word for word on the under-5 and four-inch-rule points.

**3. Gaps**
- The health guide says a mild impaction "sometimes resolve[s] with warm soaks" but never gives a water temperature, depth, or how long to try before calling it a failure. Someone trying this at home is guessing.
- Nothing tells a new owner how to actually locate an amphibian-competent exotic vet before they need one; every page just says "find one," which is sound advice with no next step attached.

**4. Trust**
The bite-force fun fact box states: "the largest living horned frogs should reach close to 500 [newtons] at mid-jaw, the range of medium-sized mammalian carnivores." This is an extrapolation from one study on a smaller relative, not a measurement, and the confident framing ("should reach") oversells a projection as a known fact. Nothing else reads as advertising; the affiliate-linked buy lists stay generic (type of item, not brand).

**5. Set grade: A-**
Reason: numbers are consistent across every page and the sidebar, the emergency/normal distinctions (aestivation, shedding, fasting) are handled with real nuance, and nothing contradicts itself. The two changes: give the warm-soak advice an actual temperature and time limit, and cut the "buried is not bored" restatement down to one page instead of three.

## whites-tree-frog

**1. Page by page**
- 00 Care guide hub: A. Same strong format as the pacman hub, numbers are specific and every row links out correctly.
- 01 Encyclopedia: A. The John White/Rana caerulea naming history is a genuinely good hook, not filler.
- 02 Cost guide: A. Clean tables, and it's honest that the terrarium, not the frog, is the real expense.
- 03 Handling guide: A. The "you're the danger to the frog, not the other way around" framing is correct and well argued, breeding info is a bonus most care sites skip.
- 04 Health issues guide: A. Chytrid, red-leg, and the shed-skin tell are all specific and actionable.
- 05 Tank setup guide: A (see conflict). Otherwise clear and complete on gradient, humidity cycling, and substrate.
- 06 Feeding guide: A. The ridge test is the standout: a concrete, checkable obesity signal instead of vague "watch its weight" advice.
- 07 Enrichment guide: A. Built on one real study rather than padding, and it says so plainly.

**2. Conflicts**
The enclosure setup guide and the cost guide both say the UVB bulb needs replacing "every 9 to 12 months." The sidebar's UVB lighting guide says the opposite for this class: "Amphibian guidance is shorter: replace those every 6 to 8 months or per the manufacturer." A keeper who only reads the species pages will run a bulb two to four months past the site's own general standard.

**3. Gaps**
- Nothing on setting up a secure quarantine container for a species that jumps and climbs; the shared quarantine guide assumes a generic tub and doesn't flag that an arboreal frog needs a taller, tightly lidded one.
- The tank setup guide punts bulb distance detail to "the complete UVB lighting guide," but a reader who doesn't click through is left with only "at least 6 inches" and no sense that distance varies by bulb type, which the linked guide implies but this page doesn't.

**4. Trust**
The 9-12 vs. 6-8 month UVB figure above isn't just a conflict, it's a trust problem: the more specific, species-level page gives the more lenient number, which is the direction that actually risks under-dosing UVB. Everything else holds up; the price tables read as snapshots, not pitches, and the lifespan and bite/handling comparisons to the Pacman frog are consistent everywhere they appear.

**5. Set grade: A-**
Reason: well-researched, well-linked, and honest about what the evidence does and doesn't support, but the UVB replacement conflict is exactly the kind of number a new keeper would rely on. Two changes: fix the 9-12 vs 6-8 month bulb figure to one number, and add a line to the quarantine guide (or this species' setup page) about containing a climber during quarantine.
