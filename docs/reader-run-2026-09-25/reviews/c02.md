## betta-fish

1. Page grades
- 00 care guide hub: real numbers up front, buy list, emergency card. Would finish and act on it. A
- 01 encyclopedia: short but earns its slot with real history (Cantor, Regan, the 1840 diplomatic gift) the other pages don't repeat. B+
- 02 cost guide: full price ladder plus a "how to pick a healthy fish" checklist you can use at the store. A
- 03 handling guide: clear on the one thing people get wrong (bare hands), good tankmate and stress-sign detail. A
- 04 health issues guide: the standout. Separates fin rot from normal regrowth and from age-related slowdown, cites actual regeneration studies, and flags that heat cures ich but can kill a fish with columnaris. A
- 05 tank setup guide: heater, filter, cycling, lighting hours, all with reasons attached. A
- 06 feeding guide: honest that sources disagree on frequency, clear on portion size and the "why won't it eat" ladder. A
- 07 enrichment guide: cites three real studies, is blunt that a mirror is a stress test, not a toy. A
- 08 vs-goldfish guide: solid comparison table but mostly repackages numbers the other pages already gave. B+
- 09 water parameters guide: clear target table, explains the nitrogen cycle instead of just saying "change water." A

2. Conflicts
The hub's emergency card says: "raise the tank slowly to 86°F with an air stone, inside this species' range, and hold it two weeks" for ich. But the tank setup guide says the target is "76 to 82°F, with 78 to 80°F the sweet spot," and the water parameters guide gives the same 78-80°F range and calls a betta "tropical fish" needing that band. 86°F is a treatment ceiling the sidebar ich guide confirms a betta can tolerate ("Betta | 76 to 82°F | Yes, raised slowly, for the course only"), not a temperature "inside this species' range" for normal keeping. The hub's wording overstates it as normal.

3. Gaps (after the sidebar check)
- The hub's buy list includes a sponge filter but no air pump, and a sponge filter needs one to run. The quarantine sidebar guide assumes an air pump is on hand; the species pages never say to buy one.
- The health issues guide treats fin rot with "clean water and aquarium salt" but gives no dose. The quarantine sidebar guide explicitly warns against "aquarium salt... at a dose borrowed from somewhere else," which makes the missing fin rot dose a real hole, not just a style choice.
- No page addresses tank shape (long and shallow vs. tall) even though several pages stress that a betta needs reliable surface access for its labyrinth organ.

4. Trust
The hub's "inside this species' range" line for an 86°F ich treatment (quoted above) is the one claim that reads as wrong against this set's own numbers elsewhere. Nothing else in the set reads as an ad for a product or outside site; the affiliate disclosures are separate from the claims themselves.

5. Set grade: A-. The health issues and enrichment guides in particular do real, cited work instead of generic filler, and the numbers agree with each other everywhere except the one temperature claim above.
Two changes first: fix the hub's ich temperature line so it doesn't call 86°F "inside this species' range," and add an air pump to the buy list (or say the sponge filter needs one).

## goldfish

1. Page grades
- 00 care guide hub: strong first-week numbers by variety, honest emergency card. A
- 01 encyclopedia: short, but the domestication history (Song dynasty ponds, the Ming-era move indoors) is real content, not filler. B+
- 02 cost guide: the feeder-goldfish section is the best part of this set, it tells you to judge the tank, not just the fish, and cites a real pathogen survey. A
- 03 handling guide: excellent, uses actual acclimation-tolerance research to explain why a fast temperature swing (shop to pond, pond to indoor tank) is the real risk. A
- 04 health issues guide: the strongest health page in either fish set so far. It flags its own numbers conflict on anchor worm dosing and tells you which one to trust, and says outright there's no safe home antibiotic dose for fin rot or dropsy. A
- 05 tank setup guide: clear on filtration turnover math and the substrate choking hazard most sites skip. A
- 06 feeding guide: thorough on life stage, pellet vs. gel vs. flake, and pond dormancy. A
- 07 enrichment guide: cites real space-use and substrate research, and calls out "using plants to excuse weak filtration" by name. A
- 08 bowl myth: the set's centerpiece, directly debunks both the "grows to fit the tank" and "3-second memory" myths with real numbers. A

2. Conflicts
None found. Tank sizes, water change percentages, ammonia/nitrite targets, and quarantine length all match across the hub, cost guide, tank setup guide, and bowl myth page. The one place two numbers disagree, anchor worm dosing (0.066 mg/L species-specific vs. 0.03 mg/L Merck's general figure), is flagged and resolved in the health issues guide itself, so it reads as a disclosed judgment call, not a mistake.

3. Gaps (after the sidebar check)
- No goldfish page gives a pH number. It only shows up in the sidebar's Freshwater pH, GH, KH guide (6.5 to 9.0, goldfish do well on the harder end), and none of the goldfish pages link to it or mention pH at all, so a reader has no reason to know it exists.
- The buy list recommends one canister or hang-on-back filter, but the tank setup guide says running two filters is "common practice among experienced keepers." A first-time buyer following the buy list alone would under-filter by the tank setup guide's own standard.
- Outdoor pond overwintering gets only scattered mentions (feeding dormancy, moving fish between pond and tank); there's no single page on ice safety or de-icers for someone actually planning a pond.

4. Trust
Nothing reads as overstated or like an ad. The set is unusually careful about not inventing precision it doesn't have, for example explicitly saying dropsy and fin rot have no safe home dose rather than giving one anyway.

5. Set grade: A. This is the most internally consistent and best-sourced set of the two so far, and the health issues guide in particular does the rare thing of admitting where its own sources disagree.
Two changes first: add a pH target to the tank setup guide (or link the pH/GH/KH sidebar guide from it), and make the buy list's filtration line match the two-filter recommendation in the tank setup guide.

## koi

1. Page grades
- 00 care guide hub: pond volume, depth, and temperature numbers up front, and the emergency card is specific about which parasites and which KHV signs matter. A
- 01 encyclopedia: the Taisho Exposition and Hirohito history earns its place, not just filler bio text. B+
- 02 cost guide: excellent, and it's the one page in this whole read that catches and corrects its own folklore (the 226-year-old Hanako claim, flagged as unverified in the same breath it's mentioned). A
- 03 handling guide: strong, with the goldfish-into-koi-pond quarantine risk and the Chagoi personality note both genuinely useful. A
- 04 health issues guide: the most complete health page across all four sets, walking through KHV confirmation, mandatory reporting, and the depopulation decision instead of stopping at symptoms. A
- 05 pond setup guide: real engineering, turnover tables, delivered-flow-vs-rated-flow math, and the liner sizing formula worked with actual numbers. A
- 06 feeding guide: clear temperature-banded schedule and an honest "not corroborated elsewhere" flag on the 48-hour vet-contact number. A
- 07 enrichment guide: admits there's no controlled koi enrichment study and builds the guide on physical requirements instead of dressing that up as research. A
- 08 vs-goldfish guide: the hybrid-sterility detail and the KHV cross-species warning make this more than a size comparison. A

2. Conflicts
None found. Quarantine length (30 days at 75°F, "4 to 6 weeks" as the common phrasing for the same window), temperature range (64-75°F comfortable, 59-77°F outer limits), and pond size numbers (250 gallons per adult, 1,000-gallon practical minimum) all match across the hub, pond setup guide, handling guide, and health issues guide.

3. Gaps (after the sidebar check)
- No koi page gives a pH number; it's only in the sidebar's Freshwater pH, GH, KH guide (6.5 to 9.0, koi/goldfish do well on the harder end), and no koi page links to it.
- The health issues guide says parasites are "manageable at home with salt treatments" but gives no dose. The aquarium-scale dose used elsewhere on the site (a tablespoon per 5 gallons) doesn't translate to a pond measured in thousands of gallons, and nothing in this set gives a pond-scale salt dosing number.
- No page states a de-icer wattage or sizing rule for the "keep a hole open in winter ice" instruction; it's told to do it but not how to size the equipment.

4. Trust
Nothing reads as an ad or overstated. If anything this set undersells itself by flagging its own weakest citation (the Hanako age claim) rather than letting it stand.

5. Set grade: A. This is the deepest and most self-critical set of the four, with real math for pond and filtration sizing and a health guide that treats KHV as seriously as it deserves.
Two changes first: add a pond-scale salt dose (or say plainly that salt isn't practical at pond volume and name the alternative), and give a pH target in the pond setup guide instead of leaving it only in the shared sidebar article.

## zebra-danio

1. Page grades
- 00 care guide hub: clear first-week numbers, and it's upfront that this is one of the rare fish that can skip a heater. A
- 01 encyclopedia: genuinely excellent for a short page, the Streisinger cloning history earns its place and isn't repeated anywhere else. A-
- 02 cost guide: the best cost page across all four sets, it prices out the quarantine tank as its own line item and explains why GloFish cost more for identical care. A
- 03 handling guide: the trap-and-guide netting technique and the jump-height warning are both genuinely actionable, not generic filler. A
- 04 health issues guide: excellent, and honest that mycobacteriosis has no cure and can infect a keeper through a cut. A
- 05 tank setup guide: clear on the one real decision (long tank, optional heater) with real sourced numbers throughout. A
- 06 feeding guide: handles the accidental-spawn risk from live food better than most sites bother to. A
- 07 enrichment guide: cites real survivorship data, not just behavior, and is blunt that "hardy" doesn't mean "indifferent to a bare tank." A

2. Conflicts
None found. Temperature range (64-77°F), school size guidance (6 minimum, 8-10 better, attributed to different named sources each time), and quarantine length (2 to 4 weeks) all agree across the hub, tank setup, handling, and health issues guides.

3. Gaps (after the sidebar check)
- The hub's buy list includes a sponge filter option but no air pump to run it, the same gap as the betta set.
- The health issues guide calls quarantine "the single habit that matters most" for this species because mycobacteriosis has no cure, but the hub's main "what to buy" list never includes a quarantine tank or kit. It only gets priced out inside the cost guide, so a reader who skips straight to the buy list could miss it entirely.

4. Trust
Nothing reads as overstated or promotional. The set is careful to separate what a danio can survive (up to 107°F in a lab stress test) from what it needs (64-77°F), which is the opposite of overselling a number.

5. Set grade: A. Sourcing is consistently named and specific (Seriously Fish, Aquarium Co-Op, ZFIN, named journal studies) rather than vague "experts say," and the set is honest about where recommendations differ between sources instead of hiding it.
Two changes first: add an air pump to the buy list next to the sponge filter option, and move a basic quarantine kit onto the main buy list instead of leaving it only in the cost guide.
