## russian-tortoise

**1. Pages**
- 00 Care guide hub: A. Every first-week number links to its source article, the buy list and emergency card are printable and specific.
- 01 Encyclopedia: B+. Good natural history and trade context (CITES II, wild-caught vs captive-bred mortality), but it is reference material, not something you act on.
- 02 Cost guide: A. Itemized setup table, dated pricing, and a real lifetime-cost figure that changes how you think about the purchase.
- 03 Handling guide: A. Direct about this being an observation pet, with a real escape-proofing checklist and pairing risks spelled out.
- 04 Health issues guide: A. Sourced to VCA, a 2018 German study, and a 2021 Japanese survey, with clear vet-now triggers.
- 05 Enclosure setup guide: A. States the 8 sq ft minimum plainly as a floor, not a target, and is honest that sources disagree on the real number.
- 06 Feeding guide: A-. Clear on the calcium ceiling and the overweight test, with a real source list.
- 07 Enrichment guide: B+. Grounded in the wild activity pattern, useful and specific, but the least essential page of the seven.

**2. Conflicts**
Soak depth for juveniles disagrees between the species pages and the site's own soaking guide. The hub says: "in lukewarm water no deeper than the elbows, and never unattended" (00-care-guide-hub.txt), and the feeding guide repeats "no deeper than its elbows" for a juvenile. But the dedicated soaking guide's table gives: "Russian juvenile | Twice a week | 10 to 15 minutes | Lukewarm, no deeper than the knees" (sidebar/04-tortoise-soaking-guide.txt). Elbows and knees are not the same depth for a juvenile tortoise, and the soaking guide is the more detailed, cited source on this exact question.

Lifespan ceiling is stated two ways. The hub and cost guide say "40 years or more typical, some individuals reaching 50-plus." The encyclopedia says "the widely quoted 40 to 50 years, with some individuals reported past 60." Minor, but a reader comparing the two pages gets a different upper bound.

**3. Gaps**
The hub's first-week checklist has no quarantine line at all, unlike the sulcata hub's explicit "six months minimum." The topic is covered only in the shared herpesvirus/quarantine sidebar guide (6 months to a year, EAZWV), which the health guide links to, but a reader working only from the hub's numbered list would never learn a new tortoise needs months of isolation before joining another chelonian.

No page gives a screen-top mesh size or wall height number to stop the climbing the handling guide warns about; "solid and smooth" has no dimension attached.

**4. Trust**
The enclosure setup guide ends with a mid-article product pitch: "[card: the printable care package for this species, with its price and a link to the store]." It reads as a store plug dropped into care content rather than information, right after a genuinely useful UVB and brumation section.

**5. Set grade: B+.** This is a well-sourced, honestly-hedged set (it says outright when experts disagree on pyramiding) that loses points on one real numeric conflict and one missing safety number. First change: fix the elbows-vs-knees soak depth so the hub and feeding guide match the dedicated soaking guide. Second change: add a quarantine duration line to the hub's first-week list, matching the format the sulcata hub already uses.

## sulcata-tortoise

**1. Pages**
- 00 Care guide hub: A. Leads with the one real decision (do you have the outdoor space and multi-generational plan), and the numbers back that framing up.
- 01 Encyclopedia: B. Solid context on the Sahel origin and export history, but its own cited lifespan source undercuts the headline figure used elsewhere (see Conflicts).
- 02 Cost guide: A. The $4,000 shell-repair case and the bladder-stone surgery example make the emergency-fund advice concrete rather than generic.
- 03 Handling guide: A. Covers the size problem, safe lifting technique for an 80-plus pound animal, and reliable sexing cues at the right age.
- 04 Health issues guide: A-. Honest that the two pyramiding studies disagree, with a clear outdoor-hazard list.
- 05 Enclosure setup guide: A. The "indoor is a phase" framing is reinforced with real numbers at each life stage.
- 06 Feeding guide: A. The eight-item "why they stop eating" list is the most useful troubleshooting content in the set.
- 07 Enrichment guide: B+ (thin on species-specific evidence). Admits upfront that no research exists on this species and borrows from red-footed tortoise cognition studies; interesting but speculative for training a hundred-pound animal.
- 08 Vs. Russian tortoise guide: A-. A genuinely useful decision table, though it compresses Russian tortoise lifespan to "40-50 years" where the Russian tortoise's own pages emphasize routinely exceeding 50.

**2. Conflicts**
The 70-years-or-more figure is used everywhere as the headline number: the hub says "Lifespan: 70 years or more, potentially 100," and the cost guide and comparison guide repeat it. But the encyclopedia's own cited source says something narrower: "Animal Diversity Web puts average captive longevity at 54.3 years, and 70 years or more is achievable with good care." An achievable ceiling is being used as the typical figure throughout the set, while the page that actually cites a source gives a lower average.

**3. Gaps**
None of the nine top-level pages give the hatchling soaking schedule. The feeding guide only says "juveniles benefit from regular soaking," no numbers. The shared soaking guide is specific and urgent about this exact species: hatchlings soaked daily at 85 to 95°F for 20 to 30 minutes, because "the best case for chronic dehydration is a pyramided shell, the worst case is death." A reader working only from the sulcata pages would never see that number or the stakes attached to it.

**4. Trust**
The hub states: "The real rule is the federal import ban since 2000." The site's own federal exotic pet laws guide, which catalogs exactly six federal wildlife laws reaching private ownership in detail, names nothing matching this: no sulcata-specific import ban, no year 2000. CITES (a border/trade rule, not a possession or import ban per that same guide) is the closest real mechanism. This specific claim looks unsupported by the site's own reference material.

**5. Set grade: B+.** Strong, consistent messaging around the one decision that matters (space and decades), backed by real cost examples. It loses points for repeating an achievable lifespan ceiling as though it were typical, and for an apparently unsupported legal claim in the hub. First change: source or correct the "federal import ban since 2000" line. Second change: pull the hatchling soaking schedule out of the sidebar and into the feeding or enclosure guide, given how directly it connects to pyramiding risk.
