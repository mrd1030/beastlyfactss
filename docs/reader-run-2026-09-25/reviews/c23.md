## axolotl

**Pages**

00 Care guide hub: A. Every first-week number is here with a source link, plus a buy list and emergency card. I'd finish it and act on it immediately.
01 Encyclopedia: B+. Short but earns its length, real history (the Dumeril lineage, the albino cross) instead of padding.
02 Cost guide: A. Real price bands, a vet-cost benchmark, morph genetics explained instead of just listed.
03 Handling guide: A. Answers the actual question (why not to handle) and gives the submerged-container method in enough detail to use.
04 Health issues guide: B+. Solid symptom list and a clear vet-now list, but "salt baths can help" has no dose and no link to the guide that has one.
05 Tank setup guide: A. The chiller-sizing math (rated volume vs. actual room heat) is the best thing in the set.
06 Feeding guide: A. Life-stage schedule, portion sizing, and the foods-to-avoid list are all concrete.
07 Enrichment guide: A. Cites an actual 2017 study rather than generic "add some hides" filler.

**Conflicts**

Whether to keep two axolotls together is inconsistent across the set. The hub and tank setup guide size a tank for a pair as a normal option: "A 20-gallon long tank is the minimum for one axolotl, with a 40-gallon breeder better if keeping two" (00-care-guide-hub.txt, 05-tank-setup-guide.txt). The enrichment guide treats that same setup as a real hazard: "Cohousing isn't enrichment for this species and carries real risk, axolotls will bite at limbs and gills, especially around feeding time or if sizes are mismatched. Housing separately is the safer default" (07-enrichment-guide.txt). A reader who only reads the hub and tank setup page would buy a 40-gallon breeder for two animals with no warning that the site itself later calls single housing the safer default.

**Gaps**

Salt bath dosing for a sick axolotl is real and specific (checked the sidebar: Tubbing, Cooling, and Salt Baths for Sick Axolotls has the actual concentration, duration, and a caution that it's a vet-directed figure, not DIY), but the health issues guide only says "salt baths can help" with no number and no link to it. Covered only in that sidebar article.
No day-to-day lighting duration is given, only "keep lighting dim." An owner still needs a photoperiod, not just a brightness level.
No guidance on what a normal shed or skin-sloughing looks like versus the fungal growth described in the health guide, which could be confused for one another.

**Trust**

The JBJ Arctica Titanium chiller is named as "the frequently-recommended premium pick, quieter and more consistent than budget compressor units" in both the cost guide and the tank setup guide, word for word close enough to read as a placed product mention rather than independent advice repeated twice. Nothing else oversells; the Virginia legality myth is corrected with a named source (Virginia DWR, August 2021), and the vet cost benchmark is honestly attributed to one named Colorado hospital rather than presented as a universal number.

**Set grade: A-**

This is the strongest-built set of the three so far: real numbers, real sources, and cross-links that go somewhere useful. It loses the plus for the unresolved single-vs-pair housing conflict and for burying the one health fact (salt bath dosing) that actually needed a link.

Two changes first: add one sentence to the tank setup guide and hub flagging the bite risk before someone buys a 40-gallon breeder for two animals. Then link the salt bath sidebar guide from the health issues page instead of leaving "salt baths can help" as an unsupported, unlinked claim.

## tiger-salamander

**Pages**

00 Care guide hub: A. First-week numbers, an emergency card, and a legal check up front. Actionable immediately.
01 Encyclopedia: A-. The California hybridization history (Fitzpatrick and Shaffer, PNAS 2007) is real substance, not filler.
02 Cost guide: A. Names two vendors and a date for its prices, breaks down the cost of keeping a second animal honestly.
03 Handling guide: A-. Clear on the "why" and the wet-glove method, though it repeats material the other guides already cover.
04 Health issues guide: A. Concrete signs for each condition and a real vet-now list; the "appetite loss is a red flag here" framing is well earned.
05 Enclosure setup guide: A. The substrate depth argument and the larva-to-adult changeover warning are both genuinely useful, not generic.
06 Feeding guide: A. The "why it stopped eating" section (winter slowdown, cold, shed, metamorphosis, new arrival) is the best troubleshooting content in the set.
07 Enrichment guide: A-. Honest that almost no research exists for this species, then still gives a specific, usable priority list.
08 Vs. axolotl guide: B. Useful comparison, but contains a factual self-contradiction on axolotl temperature (see Conflicts).

**Conflicts**

The vs-axolotl guide gives two different numbers for axolotl water temperature in the same article. Its comparison table says "60-64°F, stresses above 72°F" for the axolotl, then the body text later says "An axolotl's water needs to stay even tighter, 60 to 68 degrees F, with real stress and immune problems appearing above 72°F" (08-vs-axolotl-guide.txt). The axolotl's own care pages consistently give 60 to 64°F, so the 68°F figure here is simply wrong.

Whether two tiger salamanders can share an enclosure is answered two different ways. The enclosure setup guide allows it conditionally: "Two adults can share only when they are close in size, and never in less floor space than the single-adult minimum above" (05-tank-setup-guide.txt). The enrichment guide states it flatly in its rules list: "Do not house two together" (07-enrichment-guide.txt), and its FAQ adds "Better not... Solo is the safe default." A reader gets permission on one page and a flat no on another for the same question.

**Gaps**

No guidance anywhere in the set, or in the sidebar quarantine and gut-loading articles, on preventing mold or pests in a deep substrate bed that has to stay damp for months, or on how often to replace it.
The sidebar quarantine setup assumes bare-bottom or paper towel, which fits an aquatic animal in a tub; nothing says what a quarantine tub should look like for a burrowing land species that wants substrate to dig into.

**Trust**

The vet cost line hedges without a source: "$50 to $135, sometimes cited higher, $150 to $300 for a thorough workup" (02-cost-guide.txt) names no origin for the higher figure, unlike the guide's clearly sourced purchase price and dated retailer check.

**Set grade: B+**

The life-stage and feeding-refusal content in this set is the best writing of the three, but the axolotl-temperature error in the comparison article and the flat contradiction on cohousing are the kind of mistakes that cost a careful reader's trust in cross-linked pages.

Two changes first: fix the 60 to 68°F axolotl figure in the vs-axolotl guide to match the rest of the site's 60 to 64°F. Then make the enclosure and enrichment guides agree on cohousing, either both conditional or both solo-only.

## fire-bellied-toad

**Pages**

00 Care guide hub: A. Every number, the emergency card, and the buy list are here and match the deep-dive pages.
01 Encyclopedia: A. The chytrid-origin history (O'Hanlon et al., Science 2018, tracing the fungus to Korea through the pet trade) is real, specific, and directly relevant to a buyer.
02 Cost guide: A. Honest that most stock is wild-caught and that breeding isn't profitable at this price, which most cost guides would leave out.
03 Toxic skin/handling guide: A. The staged unken reflex description and the breeding section are both concrete and go beyond the obvious "don't touch it" advice.
04 Health issues guide: A. Water quality as the throughline for nearly every condition is a genuinely useful organizing idea, not just a list.
05 Enclosure setup guide: A. The API conditioner dosing math and the aquarium turnover numbers are the kind of specific, checkable detail most care sites skip.
06 Feeding guide: A. The "can't flick its tongue so everything must move" framing explains every other rule on the page.
07 Enrichment guide: A-. Honest that no research exists on Bombina, then reasons carefully from adjacent frog studies to a usable priority list.

**Conflicts**

None found. Temperature (65 to 82°F), quarantine length (6 to 8 weeks), group housing, and lifespan (10 to 15 years, 20 recorded, 30 max) all match across every page and against the sidebar guides checked.

**Gaps**

The cost and health guides both flag, more than once, that most trade stock is wild-caught and carries a real parasite and stress risk, but nothing on the site gives a buyer a way to actually spot a wild-caught or stressed toad at the store, the way the axolotl cost guide has a "picking a healthy axolotl" checklist. The advice stops at "ask the seller where it came from."
No substrate depth number is given for the land side of the enclosure, unlike the tiger salamander's explicit 3 to 4 inches; the enrichment guide has the toad using leaf litter and land-side cover but the setup guide never says how deep to make it.

**Trust**

The water-dosing detail is oddly specific for something that varies by location: "5 mL per 30 gallons where it uses chloramine, which the water utility's annual report states" (05-tank-setup-guide.txt) cites one unnamed utility's report as if the ratio holds everywhere, when chloramine and chlorine levels differ by city. It's genuinely useful information, but stated with more universal confidence than a single local report supports.

**Set grade: A**

This is the cleanest set of the three: no numeric conflicts found anywhere, and the water-quality throughline actually helps a reader understand why the rules exist instead of just listing them.

Two changes first: add a short "signs of a wild-caught or stressed toad" checklist to the cost guide, since the risk is mentioned repeatedly but never made actionable. Then give the land-side substrate a depth number instead of leaving it as "coconut fiber or soil" with no amount.
