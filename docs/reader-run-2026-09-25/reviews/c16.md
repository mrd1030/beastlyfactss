## bearded-dragon

**Page verdicts**

00 Care guide hub: A. Finish it, act on it immediately, every first-week number links to its source article.
01 Encyclopedia: B. Finish it, short but a real natural history and import-ban backstory, not just filler.
02 Cost guide: A. Finish it, clear budget with a range for every line item.
03 Handling guide: A. Finish it, tells you when to start, how long, and who should not touch the animal.
04 Health issues guide: A. Finish it, symptom by symptom with a clear vet-now list.
05 Tank setup guide: A. Finish it, full temperature table and UVB specifics, though see the UVI conflict below.
06 Feeding guide: A. Finish it, and it is honest about where its own sources disagree.
07 Enrichment guide: B. Finish it, useful priority order, though a chunk of the "what not to do" section restates points made elsewhere.
08 Brumation guide: A. Finish it, the two worked examples make the timeline concrete.
09 Eggs and egg binding guide: A. Finish it, the normal-vs-dystocia table is the best single thing in the set.
10 Growth and weight guide: A. Finish it, the two worked weigh-in examples do real work.
11 Safe foods guide: A. Finish it, tiered tables answer almost any single-food question fast.
12 Shopping list: B. Finish it, though it repeats most of the cost guide's line items rather than adding new ones.
13 Vs. leopard gecko: B. Finish it, a fair comparison that does not tilt the scale for either animal.

**Conflicts**

The tank setup guide sets the basking-spot target as "a UVI gradient from 0 up to 4.0-4.5" for bearded dragons (05-tank-setup-guide.txt). The sidebar's UVB Lighting: Why It Matters More Than You Think guide places bearded dragons in Ferguson Zone 3, defined as "UVI 1.1 to 3.0, from moderate UVB" (sidebar/13-uvb-lighting-complete-guide.txt). A reader following the species page would buy and aim for a fixture strong enough to hit 4.0 to 4.5, a full point past the ceiling the shared lighting guide gives for that same zone. Nothing on either page flags the gap.

No other numeric conflict turned up. Quarantine length (3 to 6 months), the 65°F/60°F power-outage line, and the gut-loading window (both pages land on 24 to 72 hours, with 48 most common) all match between the species pages and the matching sidebar guides.

**Gaps**

The eggs guide tells every female owner to have a lay box ready, plastic storage bin plus 6 to 12 inches of substrate, but neither the cost guide nor the shopping list prices a bin or the substrate mix, so a first-time owner budgeting from those two pages alone will be caught short.

The eggs guide also raises spaying (ovariosalpingectomy) as the fix for a female who cycles constantly, but no page anywhere in the set gives a ballpark cost for that surgery, unlike impaction surgery and MBD treatment, which the cost guide prices directly.

The brumation guide requires a pre-brumation vet visit but does not say whether that is a second annual exam or an extra one, and the cost guide's vet line only covers "annual wellness exam and fecal," leaving the actual number of vet visits per year, and their total cost, unclear.

**Trust**

A near-identical ad card, "[card: the printable care package for this species, with its price and a link to the store]," is dropped into the health issues, tank setup, brumation, eggs, growth, and safe foods guides, six of the fourteen pages. Once or twice reads as a natural upsell; six times inside husbandry and medical content reads like the paid product is riding along on every serious topic in the guide.

The Salmonella claim in the handling guide, "a Salmonella outbreak traced to pet bearded dragons, closed in December 2025, made 20 people sick across 14 states, put 9 in the hospital and killed 1," is stated as fact with no source link, unlike most other cited claims on the site, which name VCA, Merck, or NC State directly.

**Set grade: A-**

The set is deep, consistent on the numbers that matter most (temperature, enclosure size, quarantine, feeding ratios), and unusually honest about where its own sources disagree. Two fixes first: reconcile the UVI target between the tank setup guide and the shared UVB lighting guide so a buyer knows which ceiling to trust, and add the lay box and spay-surgery costs to the cost guide, since egg-laying is presented as a near-certainty for females but is the one predictable cost left unpriced.

## uromastyx

**Page verdicts**

00 Care guide hub: A. Finish it, the emergency card and buy list are both usable on their own.
01 Encyclopedia: B. Finish it, but see the wild-lifespan number flagged below.
02 Cost guide: A. Finish it, prices per line item and honest about what starter kits get wrong.
03 Handling guide: A. Finish it, the tail-anatomy sexing method and the pairing rules are real, specific content.
04 Health issues guide: A. Finish it, ties every issue back to a fixable husbandry cause.
05 Tank setup guide: A. Finish it, though see the water-dish conflict below.
06 Feeding guide: A. Finish it, and the brumation cooldown steps are unusually concrete.
07 Enrichment guide: B. Finish it, honest that no uromastyx-specific research exists and says so upfront rather than dressing up borrowed studies as settled fact.
08 Vs. bearded dragon: B. Finish it for the uromastyx side, but its bearded dragon numbers do not match that species' own pages, see below.

**Conflicts**

The tank setup guide says a uromastyx's water dish is temporary: "After those two months the dish comes out, since older uromastyx take most of their water from food" (05-tank-setup-guide.txt). The health issues guide and the hub both say the opposite, that an adult keeps a bowl in the enclosure indefinitely: "A water bowl stays available too, even if it goes untouched" (04-health-issues-guide.txt), and "A bowl stays available even if it goes untouched" (00-care-guide-hub.txt). A reader cannot tell whether to remove the dish at two months or leave it in for life.

The shared Reptile Emergency Plan table lists the uromastyx's normal night low as 68°F, with "68°F (20°C), and never below 65°F" as the point to act (sidebar/01-reptile-emergency-plan-guide.txt). The tank setup guide treats 65°F itself as the ordinary nightly low: "nighttime dropping to around 65F, usually without needing supplemental night heat" (05-tank-setup-guide.txt). One page treats 65°F as fine every night, the other treats it as the temperature you should never sink below.

The vs. bearded dragon comparison page, part of this set, gives the bearded dragon's lifespan as "8-12 years" and its basking temperature as "~100°F." The bearded dragon's own hub and encyclopedia pages state 10 to 15 years and a 95 to 110°F adult basking range. The comparison undersells the animal it is not even about.

**Gaps**

The sidebar's Safe Weeds and Wild Plants guide is built partly around uromastyx and describes itself as "the forage list this site was missing," but no uromastyx page, including the feeding guide, links to it or mentions wild forage. A reader doing everything the species pages say would never learn plantain, clover, sow thistle, hawkbit, mallow, and chickweed are safe additions.

Nothing in the set gives a sexing method confirmed for species other than Mali, the handling guide is explicit that its tail-anatomy method is specific to that species.

No page prices an exotic-vet emergency visit beyond the routine $50 to $100 exam, so a reader budgeting from the cost guide alone has no ballpark for an actual impaction or egg-binding emergency, unlike the bearded dragon set, which prices its surgeries directly.

**Trust**

The encyclopedia lists "Wild Lifespan: 15-25 years," equal to or longer than the 15 to 20 years the cost guide gives as the typical captive lifespan, with no caveat about predation or the poaching pressure the same page's History section describes. A wild lifespan that matches or beats the captive one, with no explanation, reads as a data error rather than a researched figure.

The cost guide's claim that a uromastyx can live "possibly as long as 60" years is presented as a fun fact with no named source, unlike the vet-visit and MBD claims elsewhere in the same set, which cite VCA or the Center for Bird and Exotic Animal Medicine directly.

**Set grade: A-**

Deep, specific, and willing to admit where the research runs out (the enrichment guide) or where sources disagree (the feeding guide's brumation section). Two fixes first: settle whether the water dish stays or goes for an adult, since two of this set's own pages disagree, and correct the bearded dragon numbers on the comparison page so it does not misinform a reader about the other animal it is selling against.

## green-anole

**Page verdicts**

00 Care guide hub: A. Finish it, honest that this cheap animal is not an easy one.
01 Encyclopedia: A. Finish it, short but the genome-sequencing history is genuinely interesting, not filler.
02 Cost guide: A. Finish it, direct about the mismatch between the animal's price and what keeping it well actually costs.
03 Handling guide: A. Finish it, real sexing method, real catching method, and it corrects the "American chameleon" myth cleanly.
04 Health issues guide: A. Finish it, covers egg binding in a single female with no drama and a clear same-day-vet line.
05 Tank setup guide: A. Finish it, though it never says how often to replace the UVB bulb, see below.
06 Feeding guide: A. Finish it, the "why it stopped eating" list reads a real animal instead of a checklist.
07 Enrichment guide: A. Finish it. This is the strongest page across all three sets reviewed: it reports null research results honestly, explains exactly why a null result on stress hormones does not mean a bare cage is fine, and still ends with a real, usable build order.

**Conflicts**

None found. Temperatures, humidity, UVI target, enclosure size, feeding portions, and quarantine length all match across every page in this set and against the shared sidebar guides that cover them.

**Gaps**

No page says how often to replace the UVB bulb. The tank setup guide specifies the fixture and the UVI target but never a swap schedule, and the cost guide's monthly total has no line for it either, unlike the bearded dragon and uromastyx guides, which both give a 6 to 12 month replacement rule. A reader following this set alone would have no idea the bulb's UV output fades long before it looks dim.

Nothing in the set gives an owner a way to track body condition or weight over time. Every other check here is reactive, watch for these symptoms, while the bearded dragon and uromastyx sets both include a routine for catching a slow decline before it becomes visible. For an animal this guide itself calls fragile enough that "metabolic bone disease... progresses in weeks," an early-warning habit seems like a real omission rather than a size-driven exemption.

**Trust**

The cost guide's claim that a proper setup runs "roughly twenty to thirty times the lizard's own price tag" only holds near the middle of its own price range. Against the $5 low end it quotes elsewhere on the same page, a $390 to $470 setup is closer to 80 to 94 times the animal's cost, not 20 to 30. The point the guide is making, that the sticker price is misleading, still stands, but the specific multiplier undersells its own case at the cheap end.

Nothing else raised a flag. No unsourced outbreak statistic, no repeated product placement, and the one specific external citation (a reptifiles.com care sheet for the 10-year lifespan claim) is linked rather than just asserted.

**Set grade: A**

The strongest of the three sets. It is internally consistent, it prices things honestly, and it is the only one of the three willing to say "the research says nothing" instead of dressing up a guess as settled science. Two fixes first: add a UVB bulb replacement schedule so the lighting section matches the standard the other two species guides already set, and give owners some simple way, even just a monthly photo or a note on shed frequency, to track condition over time in an animal that the guide itself says has almost no margin for error.
