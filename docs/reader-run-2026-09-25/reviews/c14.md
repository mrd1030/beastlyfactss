## leopard-gecko

1. Page by page
- Care guide hub: A. Every number links to the article that backs it, plus a buy list and an emergency card.
- Encyclopedia: B. Short, but real history (Tremper, Bell, Mack lines) instead of filler.
- Cost guide: A. Dated tables, a real lifetime total, honest about sick-visit cost.
- Handling guide: A. The tail-drop warning and the two tail-movement reads are genuinely useful.
- Health issues guide: A. Cryptosporidiosis coverage is clear about mortality and diagnostic limits.
- Enclosure setup guide: A-. The humidity split (whole tank vs humid hide) is the single most useful fact in the set.
- Feeding guide: A. Honest that sources disagree on adult frequency instead of inventing a fake precise number.
- Enrichment guide: A-. Cites Bashaw 2016 and Rickman 2025 by name, gives a real priority order.
- Temperature guide: A. Flags LafeberVet vs Zen Habitats vs Merck disagreeing outright and still commits to a usable number.
- vs. Crested Gecko comparison: B+. Solid decision aid, one or two lines read a bit like copy ("looks friendlier at the pet store").

2. Conflicts
The species pages put basking UVI at 0.5 to 1.5 (0.5 to 0.7 for albino morphs), but the shared UVB Lighting guide's Ferguson Zone 1, which explicitly lists leopard geckos, gives UVI 0.6 to 1.4 (sidebar/13-uvb-lighting-complete-guide.txt). The two ranges overlap but don't match at either end, so a reader trying to hit "the" number gets two slightly different targets depending which page they're on.

3. Gaps
- Enclosure cleaning cadence (spot clean vs. deep clean) isn't mentioned on the enclosure setup page and that page never points to the guide that has one. It's covered only in Salmonella and Reptile Hygiene (sidebar), which the enclosure page doesn't link to at all.
- Breeding and incubation beyond the egg-binding paragraph in the health guide isn't covered anywhere in the set; probably fine for a "getting one" reader, worth a line if the site ever adds a breeding guide.

4. Trust
Nothing reads as an ad beyond the disclosed affiliate notices and a printable-care-package upsell card at the end of the enclosure guide. The 51% cryptosporidiosis figure is sourced to one Thai facility and presented that way rather than as a universal rate, which is the right level of hedging.

5. Set grade: A-. Deep, well-sourced, and unusually honest about where sources disagree, held back by the UVI mismatch against the shared UVB guide and the missing cleaning cadence.
Two changes first: reconcile the basking UVI number with the shared UVB guide's Ferguson Zone 1 range, and add one line on cleaning frequency to the enclosure setup guide with a link to the hygiene guide.

## crested-gecko

1. Page by page
- Care guide hub: A. First-week numbers, buy list, and emergency card all check out against the linked articles.
- Encyclopedia: B. Short but substantive: names the 1994 rediscoverers and the founder-population detail.
- Cost guide: A. Clear equipment/gecko/vet breakdown, calls out the lay-box and second-setup costs people miss.
- Handling guide: A. The permanent tail loss framing and the sexing-by-bulge detail are both handled carefully.
- Health issues guide: A. Direct about MBD progression and the calcium-sac visual check.
- Enclosure setup guide: A. The 85°F hard ceiling is repeated everywhere it needs to be, and cleaning cadence is stated plainly.
- Feeding guide: A. Flags the raspberry disagreement openly instead of picking a side and hiding it.
- Enrichment guide: A-. Clear on what's required (climbing complexity) vs. optional (bioactive).
- Humidity guide: A. The best single explanation of a wet/dry cycle in the whole set, with a real 24-hour table.

2. Conflicts
The enclosure setup guide and hub both set basking UVB at UVI 1.0 to 2.0, but the shared UVB Lighting guide's Ferguson Zone 1, which names crested geckos directly, sets that same species at UVI 0.6 to 1.4 (sidebar/12-uvb-lighting-complete-guide.txt). Here the gap is bigger than for leopard gecko: 2.0 sits well above the zone's stated ceiling of 1.4, so someone following the species page's top end could be running meaningfully more UVB than the shared framework says this species should get.

3. Gaps
None found after the sidebar check. Cleaning, quarantine duration, hygiene, and cold-night numbers are all either on the species pages directly or confirmed matching in the shared guides (quarantine 3 to 6 months, emergency-plan night low 65 to 72°F both check out).

4. Trust
Generally careful. The feeding guide states avocado, citrus, and rhubarb as "genuinely toxic" without hedging, which is a common claim in the hobby but stated with more confidence than the evidence behind some of it (persin toxicity in reptiles specifically isn't well documented); it's not contradicted elsewhere in this set, so it's a watch-item rather than a conflict.

5. Set grade: A. The most internally consistent and best-explained set of the four, let down only by a UVB number that disagrees with the site's own shared lighting guide by a wider margin than it should.
Two changes first: pull the basking UVI target down to match Ferguson Zone 1 (or explain why crested geckos get a higher number here than the shared guide allows), and soften the absolute "genuinely toxic" phrasing on avocado to match the more careful tone used elsewhere on this page.

## gargoyle-gecko

1. Page by page
- Care guide hub: A. Directly confronts the "needs no heat" myth up front, which is the thing most likely to hurt this species.
- Encyclopedia: B. Covers the 2012 taxonomic split cleanly.
- Cost guide: A. Realistic that morphs and color are the price driver, not personality.
- Handling guide: A. Genuinely useful crested-gecko comparison table baked into the prose, plus real breeding and sexing detail.
- Health issues guide: A. Floppy tail syndrome explanation ties the cause (bare glass) straight to the fix (clutter).
- Enclosure setup guide: A. States the 86°F ceiling and explains why "no heat" is a misconception rather than just asserting it.
- Feeding guide: A. Flags a real outlier source (insects twice a month vs. weekly) instead of averaging it away.
- Enrichment guide: B+. Honest that no species-specific study exists and labels the borrowed leopard/crested gecko research as borrowed.

2. Conflicts
The shared Reptile Emergency Plan table gives one combined row, "Crested & gargoyle gecko: 65-72°F overnight; sustained below 72°F, day and night, causes lethargy and appetite loss" (sidebar/01-reptile-emergency-plan-guide.txt). But the gargoyle's own hub and enclosure guide say "Nights to the mid-60s are fine and 65°F is the floor" and "Nights can drop into the low 70s or even the mid-60s without issue, and 65°F is the floor" (00-care-guide-hub.txt, 05-tank-setup-guide.txt). The shared table says anything under 72°F is a problem for this species; the species' own pages say the mid-60s are fine. That's a direct disagreement on a number a reader would use during an actual power outage.

3. Gaps
The hub's "Day one" quarantine row never states a duration ("Quarantined away from any reptile you already keep... with a fecal exam inside that window"), unlike the leopard and crested hubs, which both say "3 to 6 months" outright. The sidebar quarantine guide does give 3 to 6 months, but a reader who only reads the hub row gets no number at all.

4. Trust
No ad-like language found. The enrichment guide's upfront admission that nothing has been studied on this exact species, rather than presenting borrowed data as gargoyle-specific, is a trust point in the set's favor.

5. Set grade: B+. Well-written and unusually candid about the limits of its own evidence, but the cold-tolerance contradiction is the kind of thing that matters in an actual emergency.
Two changes first: fix the overnight cold-tolerance disagreement between the gargoyle pages and the shared emergency-plan table (split the row or correct one side), and add "3 to 6 months" directly to the hub's quarantine line the way the other two species hubs do.

## african-fat-tail

1. Page by page
- Care guide hub: A. Tight first-week list, and correctly leads with the tail as the health gauge instead of a day count.
- Encyclopedia: B. Useful trade-history detail (Ghana/Togo/Benin export pipeline, wild-caught legacy).
- Cost guide: A. Explains why the hygrometer and gram scale are on the buy list rather than just listing them.
- Handling guide: A. The sexing-by-incubation-temperature explanation is a genuinely rare level of detail for a care page.
- Health issues guide: A. Ties every common problem back to one of two husbandry causes, which is the right frame.
- Enclosure setup guide: A. Clear about how this species differs from the leopard gecko it's constantly compared to.
- Feeding guide: A. The best hedging in the whole read: multiple real schedules given honestly instead of forcing one number.
- Enrichment guide: A-. Actually has a fat-tail-specific data point (the 2000 nest-site temperature study) rather than pure borrowing.

2. Conflicts
None found. Enclosure temperatures, humidity numbers, and calcium schedules match across the hub, cost guide, and every species article in this set.

3. Gaps
The shared Reptile Emergency Plan table (sidebar/01-reptile-emergency-plan-guide.txt) has rows for leopard gecko and for "Crested & gargoyle gecko" but none for the African fat-tail gecko, and the hub itself has no "Power outage" line the way the leopard and crested hubs do. The tank-setup guide's night temperature (70 to 75°F) is a reasonable stand-in, but nothing states an actual power-outage floor for this species. Separately, the shared UVB Lighting guide's Ferguson Zone list never mentions the African fat-tail gecko at all, so its own species page's "2 to 5%" UVB recommendation can't be cross-checked against a UVI target the way leopard and crested geckos can.

4. Trust
Very clean. The feeding guide's line that "there is no universal day count" for appetite loss, backed by a specific hobbyist report of a 6-to-7-month refusal at stable weight, is the kind of claim that could read as reassurance-for-clicks elsewhere but is framed carefully and consistently with the rest of the page.

5. Set grade: A-. The most careful and internally consistent set of the four, held back only by a missing emergency-plan anchor and an unclassified UVB zone.
Two changes first: add an African fat-tail row to the shared emergency-plan table (or state a night-low number on the hub itself), and add the species to the shared UVB guide's Ferguson Zone list so the 2 to 5% figure has a UVI equivalent to check against.
