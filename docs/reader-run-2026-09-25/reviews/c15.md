## mourning-gecko

**Pages**
- 00 hub: A. Everything a first-week owner needs in one scroll, numbers link straight to sources.
- 01 encyclopedia: B. Good natural history, no care advice, which is its job.
- 02 cost guide: A. Real price table, honest about type labels not being morphs.
- 03 handling guide: A. Answers the real question (no) and gives a workable alternative.
- 04 health issues guide: A. Ties MBD to egg-binding clearly, gives a real vet-now line.
- 05 tank setup guide: A. Escape-proofing is framed as priority one and explained why.
- 06 feeding guide: A. Schedule, calcium logic, and a genuinely useful "why they stop eating" list.
- 07 enrichment guide: A. Honest that there is no species-specific research, still gives a concrete priority order.

**Conflicts**
1. Enclosure to buy first: the cost guide's setup table lists "18x18x24 in planted vivarium" as the item to price out (02-cost-guide.txt), while the tank setup guide and hub both say start with a 12x18x18 for a pair or small group and only step up to 18x18x24 as the colony grows (05-tank-setup-guide.txt, 00-care-guide-hub.txt). A reader pricing the cost guide's table buys the wrong size for a starter colony.
2. Humidity floor: the hub and tank setup guide both give 60 to 80% as the working range, but the same tank setup guide separately instructs drying the enclosure back to "50 to 60% before the next misting" (05-tank-setup-guide.txt), a number below the stated range.

**Gaps**
- No fallback plan if UVB is skipped. Every page calls it "strongly recommended," never required, but none says what a D3-only regimen would need to look like as backup, the way the tokay set does.
- No egg-binding surgery cost. The cost guide gives general illness-visit ranges but nothing for the specific dystocia surgery the health guide raises as a real possibility for this species.
- No product-agnostic how-to for sealing enclosure gaps, just "aquarium-safe putty," which is fine but leaves a first-timer guessing at technique.

**Trust**
Nothing reads as overstated or like a plug. The tokay pair-housing study is explicitly flagged as a different genus and "a supporting signal, not proof" (07-enrichment-guide.txt), which is the right level of hedging, and the same honesty carries through the cost guide's type-label claim ("no strong evidence of a meaningful price premium tied to type").

**Set grade: A-**. Internally consistent, well linked, and useful for someone actually setting one up. Fix first: reconcile the 12x18x18-vs-18x18x24 starter enclosure between the cost and setup guides, and correct the 50 to 60% dry-back number against the stated 60 to 80% floor.

## leaf-tailed-gecko

**Pages**
- 00 hub: A. Correctly leads with captive-bred and the cool, dry-basking reversal.
- 01 encyclopedia: B. CITES and trade history are genuinely interesting, no care content, as expected.
- 02 cost guide: A. Explains why the smallest species is the most expensive, useful mislabeling warning.
- 03 handling guide: A. The stress-signal list (bug stare, gaping, scream) is specific and usable.
- 04 health issues guide: A. Dehydration framed correctly as the real emergency, not MBD.
- 05 tank setup guide: A. The per-species temperature and humidity table is the standout page in this set.
- 06 feeding guide: A. Night-feeding logic and the tap-water rule are both clearly justified.
- 07 enrichment guide: B. Honest that there is zero Uroplatus research, the "can you find it instantly" test is genuinely useful.

**Conflicts**
Quarantine length for mites versus general quarantine. The health issues guide says "a 4 to 6 week quarantine on paper towel for every new arrival is what keeps them out" of mites (04-health-issues-guide.txt), while the hub's first-week numbers and the shared quarantine guide both call for 3 to 6 months, with the Merck citation putting the floor at 90 days (00-care-guide-hub.txt; sidebar/05-reptile-quarantine-guide.txt: "quarantine periods of 3-6 months for new reptiles are recommended"). A reader following the health guide alone would end quarantine two to four months early.

**Gaps**
- No dechlorination note. The setup and feeding guides say to use tap water for minerals but never mention treating chlorine or chloramine, something the tokay set handles by default ("dechlorinated water").
- No mention of CITES Appendix II paperwork. The encyclopedia flags the genus's 2004 Appendix II listing, but no page tells a US buyer whether that touches them at the point of purchase.

**Trust**
"Specialist breeders describe dehydration as a genuine fast killer of this species" appears verbatim in both the hub and the health guide, always attributed to an unnamed "specialist breeders." It is probably true given how the rest of the page reads, but the sourcing is vaguer than the handling guide's named citations (Reptile Supply, ReptiFiles) a few pages later in the same set.

**Set grade: A-**. The most rigorous set of the three on care mechanics, let down by one real quarantine-length conflict. Fix first: reconcile the 4-to-6-week mite quarantine with the 3-to-6-month standard, and add a line on treating tap water before misting.

## tokay-gecko

**Pages**
- 00 hub: A. Legal status, bite handling, and the cost split all land in the first-week list.
- 01 encyclopedia: A. The wild trade numbers (5 million a year, mostly to traditional medicine) are the most striking material in any of the three sets.
- 02 cost guide: A. Direct about wild-caught being the expensive option once parasites are counted.
- 03 handling guide: A. Named sources (Smithsonian, PetMD, Reptiles Magazine) for every stress signal, the strongest-sourced page across all three species.
- 04 health issues guide: A. The 2025 parasite study and the Merck-based quarantine section are both genuinely useful.
- 05 tank setup guide: A. Clear on why this gecko runs opposite to a desert species.
- 06 feeding guide: A. The five-minute portion rule and the mouse-as-treat limit are concrete and easy to follow.
- 07 enrichment guide: A. States its one caveat plainly: one study, captive-bred subjects, not a license to pair casually.

**Conflicts**
Adult size. The encyclopedia and hub both give 10 to 15 inches (25 to 38 cm) (01-encyclopedia.txt, 00-care-guide-hub.txt), but the sidebar overview says tokays are "averaging 10 to 12 inches and capable of reaching 16" (sidebar/08-uromastyx-tokay-africangrey-jumpingspider-overview.txt). The largest figure in the sidebar piece is outside the species page's stated range.

**Gaps**
- No explanation of the winter cool-down. The feeding guide says "a tokay kept with a seasonal cool-down eats less through it while holding its weight," but no page says whether a deliberate cool-down is recommended for a pet, or what temperature and duration it would involve.
- No human first-aid guidance after a bite. The handling guide covers getting the gecko to let go in detail but stops there, even though it says the bite "can draw blood" and cause "a real laceration."

**Trust**
The 2025 parasite study is quoted with precise percentages, 42.9%, 19.1%, 14.3%, 9.5%, from a sample of 21 farmed geckos (04-health-issues-guide.txt). That is true to the source, but presenting one-decimal precision from n=21 without naming the sample size in the hub's version of the same stat risks reading as more solid than it is.

**Set grade: A**. The best-sourced set of the three, with named citations throughout and honest caveats on the one study it leans on. Fix first: correct the 16-inch sidebar figure against the species page's 15-inch cap, and add a line on whether a home winter cool-down is actually advisable.
