## hissing-cockroach

1. Page grades.
Hub (00): A. Every first-week number links out and the escape barrier gets flagged twice before you'd need it.
Encyclopedia (01): A. Short by design but the history section (Schaum, Brunner von Wattenwyl, the lookalike species dealers mix in) earns its place.
Cost guide (02): A. Clean tiered pricing, dated sourcing, honest that vet care is basically zero.
Handling guide (03): A. The hiss mechanism and the habituation research (Varnon 2025, citing Davis and Heslop 2004) is more rigorous than most pet-site handling copy.
Health issues guide (04): A. Prevention-first, explains the harmless mite species by name instead of just saying "don't worry."
Tank setup guide (05): A. The petroleum jelly barrier is called out three separate times across the set, which is right given it's the one mistake that empties a tank.
Feeding guide (06): A. Explains why "nothing eating" usually isn't a problem before it gets to when to worry.
Enrichment guide (07): A-. Refreshingly honest that no preference study exists on this species, but it's the thinnest of the deep dives content-wise.

2. Conflicts: None found. Numbers (2-10 adults/gallon, 6-7 month molt cycle to adulthood, 60-70% humidity, 75-85°F) repeat identically across every page that touches them.

3. Gaps (after sidebar check):
- How long to isolate a newly bought roach before adding it to an existing colony: not mentioned on any species page, but the sidebar's quarantine guide answers it directly (three months, separate tools). Covered only there.
- No guidance on a safe male-to-female ratio for a first colony, only that all-male groups need "a hide per male and a few spare."
- No mention of noise or odor for someone weighing this as a display pet in a shared living space (the encyclopedia calls them "odorless" but nothing on the hiss volume itself).

4. Trust: The hub's Fun Fact claims "Madagascar hissing cockroaches are one of the few insect species where the father plays an active role in rearing offspring. Males guard and protect the female and young after birth, and juveniles stay with the family group for weeks." This is stated as settled fact but is a much stronger parental-care claim than anything in the encyclopedia's own natural history section or the enrichment guide's colony-behavior discussion, neither of which mentions paternal care at all. It reads as overstated next to the rest of the set's careful sourcing.

5. Set grade: A-. The set is unusually well-researched for an invertebrate niche, consistent on every number, and the enrichment guide is honest about the limits of the evidence. First fix: verify or soften the paternal-care fun fact, since it's an outlier claim the rest of the set doesn't back up. Second fix: add a line on colony sex ratio to the cost or tank setup guide, since "all-male groups need spares" is the only ratio guidance given anywhere.

## millipede

1. Page grades.
Hub (00): A. The emergency card correctly separates the two opposite failure modes (too dry vs. too wet) instead of listing symptoms flat.
Encyclopedia (01): A. The 2014 SFO seizure detail and the mite-driven import restriction give real texture.
Cost guide (02): A. Explains why this species costs more than its care difficulty implies, with sourcing listed.
Handling guide (03): A. Correctly reframes the risk (falls, not the animal biting you), which most sites get backwards for this species.
Health issues guide (04): A. Sourced, and it separates commensal mites from a real infestation instead of treating all mites as a problem.
Tank setup guide (05): A. The "substrate is also the food" framing is followed through consistently rather than stated once and dropped.
Feeding guide (06): A. Good on the pesticide risk in foraged leaf litter and why a quiet millipede is usually just eating the floor.
Enrichment guide (07): A-. Honest that no millipede-specific research exists; borrows the tarantula housing study with an explicit caveat about how far it transfers.

2. Conflicts: None found. Substrate depth (4-6 inches), humidity (70-80%), and enclosure size (10-15 gallon minimum, ~40-gallon or 20-long recommended) match across the hub, cost, setup, and enrichment pages.

3. Gaps (after sidebar check):
- Quarantine duration is actually answered directly on two species pages (cost guide and health guide both give the three-month, separate-tub rule), so this is not a gap here.
- No guidance on how to reintroduce or merge groups after quarantine ends.
- The cleanup-crew section flags "some concern isopods eat millipede eggs, nobody has shown that they do" but doesn't say what a cautious keeper should do in the meantime beyond keeping isopods out of breeding tanks specifically.

4. Trust: Nothing overstated found. The set is unusually careful to flag uncertainty rather than paper over it (the *Spiropoeus fischeri* mislabeling issue, the isopod-egg question, the "nothing published on millipede enrichment" line).

5. Set grade: A. This is the strongest of the four sets: sourced, internally consistent, and willing to say "we don't know" instead of inventing an answer. First fix: add a line on merging a quarantined animal into an established group. Second fix: give a concrete fallback for keepers who want a cleanup crew and are breeding (e.g., pull isopods only once eggs are laid) rather than leaving it as an open question.

## praying-mantis

1. Page grades.
Hub (00): A. The perch-drop number (4x body length) is repeated at the exact point a buyer needs it, not buried.
Encyclopedia (01): A. The Chinese mantis's 1896 introduction story is a genuinely useful detail, not filler.
Cost guide (02): A. The full lifetime-cost math ($175-$660) is more honest than most cost guides that stop at setup.
Handling guide (03): A. Correct and specific on sexing (8 vs. 6 abdominal segments) and on why this species can't be housed like a stick insect.
Health issues guide (04): A. The mismolt section is the best-explained failure mode across all four sets, with real instar-count sourcing (Tenodera aridifolia, grass mantid sex differences).
Tank setup guide (05): A. Correctly separates the height rule from the humidity rule and explains why each exists rather than just stating them.
Feeding guide (06): A. The fruit fly culturing instructions are genuinely usable, with a timeline, not just "buy some."
Enrichment guide (07): A-. Honest borrow from the tarantula study with the caveat spelled out; thin only because there's genuinely little to add for this species.
Ootheca guide (08): A. Directly useful for the exact panic moment (unexpected eggs) it's written for, including cold-storage instructions.

2. Conflicts: None found. Height/drop measurements, molt counts, humidity by species, and feeding intervals all agree everywhere they appear.

3. Gaps (after sidebar check): None found that the sidebar doesn't already cover. Quarantine, pesticide risk to feeders, and gut-loading all have direct sidebar guides linked at the right points, and the cost guide already prices out a full mantis lifetime rather than leaving that as an open question.

4. Trust: The hub's Fun Fact calls the mantis "the only insect that can turn its head 180 degrees." This is a widely repeated claim but a strong absolute ("only") for a fact that's hard to verify and isn't sourced or repeated anywhere else in the set, unlike the ear/bat-detection claim next to it, which is well-established. Worth a source or softening.

5. Set grade: A. This is the most complete of the four sets: every number a buyer needs is repeated at the point of use, the health and enrichment guides both explain mechanism rather than just listing rules, and the short lifespan is stated plainly and often enough that nobody buys one by accident. First fix: source or soften the "only insect" head-rotation claim. Second fix: none needed beyond that; this set has no real hole.

## stick-insect

1. Page grades.
Hub (00): A. Leads with the permit problem before the animal, which is the right order for this species.
Encyclopedia (01): A. The Lord Howe Island stick insect rediscovery and the Carausius lab-stock history are strong, verifiable detail.
Cost guide (02): A. The species-identification section (Indian vs. two-striped vs. native walkingsticks) is doing real safety work, not just padding.
Handling guide (03): A. Good on thanatosis and defensive glands with real citations (Skelhorn 2018, Niekampf 2024), and clear on what to do with eggs.
Health issues guide (04): A. Correctly frames molting failure as the two-sided humidity problem and gives a real vet-referral path (ARAV directory).
Tank setup guide (05): A. The 0.5mm mesh-gap detail for nymphs is the kind of specific number a generic guide skips.
Feeding guide (06): A. Species-by-species plant table is genuinely useful rather than a generic "leaves" answer.
Enrichment guide (07): A-. Honest that the list is short by nature; the thinnest deep dive here but appropriately so.

2. Conflicts: None found. Enclosure ratios (2x length/width, 3x height), temperature (63-77°F for Indian), and the parthenogenesis/egg-disposal guidance agree everywhere they appear.

3. Gaps (after sidebar check):
- No species page mentions isolating a newly acquired stick insect before adding it to an existing group, unlike the millipede and hissing cockroach sets, which state a quarantine window directly. The sidebar's quarantine guide covers the general rule; here it is covered only there, and nothing on the species pages tells a reader that rule applies to this animal too.
- The sidebar's quarantine guide separately flags that an escaped stick insect is a release under the same USDA framework the cost guide describes, which the species pages don't connect for the reader even though they're clearly the same legal issue.

4. Trust: Nothing overstated found. The legal section is unusually careful, distinguishing "clearly legal" (native, self-collected) from the gray area of buying a native species out of state, rather than flattening it into a yes/no.

5. Set grade: A-. Strong on the one thing that actually trips up US keepers (the permit question) and consistent everywhere else. First fix: add a line to the handling or cost guide pointing new-arrival stick insects at the same three-month quarantine rule the millipede and hissing cockroach guides state directly. Second fix: connect the escape-as-release point from the sidebar quarantine guide into the species set itself, since it's a legal consequence specific to this animal that a reader would otherwise only find by chance.
