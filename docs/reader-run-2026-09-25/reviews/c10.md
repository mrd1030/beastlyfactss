## quaker-parakeet

**Page by page**
- 00 Care guide hub: A. Every number in the first week list is sourced and actionable, and the emergency card is genuinely useful.
- 01 Encyclopedia: A. Honest about the JFK escape-crate story being unverifiable rather than repeating it as fact.
- 02 Cost guide: A. A worked $400 to $590 example with dated retail sources, not just a range.
- 03 Legal and handling guide: A. The New Jersey statute walkthrough (permit exists but bars pet use) is the most careful legal reasoning in the set.
- 04 Health issues: A. Clear vet triggers, and it states plainly which problems are diet-fixable versus which need a vet.
- 05 Cage setup: A. Would act on the bar spacing and nest-box warning immediately.
- 06 Feeding: A. The 70 percent pellet floor and portion size are unambiguous.
- 07 Enrichment: A-. Useful, but honest that there is no quaker-specific research, and the nest-weaving fact already appears on four other pages in the set.

**Conflicts**
The Florida power-outage statistic does not match between two pages. The hub says: "Florida Power and Light logged 498 outages from the birds in the first five months of 2001." The legal and handling guide says: "nests were linked to over 1,000 power outages in Florida in a single year, costing hundreds of thousands of dollars." These read as two different claims about the same phenomenon with no note that one extrapolates from the other.

**Gaps**
No page mentions bathing or misting for a quaker, not even the enrichment or cage setup pages. The sidebar only covers bathing in the context of cockatoo dust control and general molt care, neither of which points a quaker owner anywhere. Nothing in the set explains how to choose or vet an avian vet, despite the buy list telling you to line one up. Nothing covers introducing a second quaker or a compatible companion bird, though the enrichment page ranks "a companion bird or a genuinely involved owner" fourth on its priority list. The full-jurisdiction legal deep dive that every page links to was not included in this file set, so its 52-jurisdiction breakdown could not be checked against the summary given on the hub and cost pages.

**Trust**
Nothing here reads as false or contradicted. The one line worth flagging as close to advertising, inside an affiliate-disclosed article, is the cost guide singling out one product by name: "The Prevue flight cage, 31 by 20 by 53 inches with half inch bar spacing, lists at $286 and sells for $190 when it is on sale, and that gap is most of the difference between the two ends of the total." It also flags a real flaw in that same cage (20 inch depth versus the 24 inch minimum) in the same breath, which cuts against reading it as a pure plug, but it is the only specific product get this kind of spotlight.

**Set grade: A-.** Deep sourcing, an honestly argued legal section, and consistent numbers everywhere except the outage stat. First fix: reconcile or footnote the 498-outages-in-5-months figure against the "over 1,000 in a year" claim. Second: add two lines on bathing to the cage setup or enrichment page, since it is a real gap with nothing in the sidebar to cover it.

## canary

**Page by page**
- 00 Care guide hub: A. The first-week list correctly leads with cage shape over size, which is the mistake the set says most new owners make.
- 01 Encyclopedia: A. The coal-mine history is specific and dated (1896 to 1986) rather than a vague aside.
- 02 Cost guide: A. Breaks price down by breed and explains the male-female price gap rather than just stating it.
- 03 Handling guide: A. Quotes VCA and LafeberVet directly rather than paraphrasing into vague advice, and states plainly when the source material does not cover canaries specifically.
- 04 Health issues: A. Ties nearly every listed disease back to diet at the close, which is the single most useful sentence on the page.
- 05 Cage setup: A. Bar spacing, perch diameter, and the air-quality section are all specific enough to shop from.
- 06 Feeding: A. Gives two different vet sources' pellet percentages and reconciles them into one practical target instead of hiding the disagreement.
- 07 Enrichment: A. The corticosterone-and-cage-size study is a genuinely useful, uncommon citation for a page like this.

**Conflicts**
None found. The feeding guide's two pellet percentage figures (60 to 70 percent from one source, 75 to 80 from another) look like a conflict at first, but the same page states both explicitly and lands on "about three quarters" as the practical target, which the cage setup page also uses. That is disclosure, not a hidden disagreement.

**Trust**
None found. Sourcing is attributed and quoted (VCA, LafeberVet), price tables are dated to September 2026 with the store named, and hedges like "with rare exceptions" on the male-only-singing claim are used correctly rather than dropped.

**Gaps**
Outdoor housing is mentioned twice, once for pox vaccination and once for pox risk climbing in wet years, but no page anywhere covers what an outdoor aviary setup actually needs: predator-proofing, winter housing, or when indoor-only is the safer call. Nothing covers introducing a mate to a solo canary, despite the FAQ suggesting a pair as one valid setup. Nothing tells a new owner how to find or vet an avian vet.

**Set grade: A-.** The strongest single-page writing of the three sets, let down only by dangling outdoor-keeping references with no page behind them. First change: either add a short outdoor aviary section or drop the pox-vaccine and wet-year lines so the set stops implying a topic it never covers. Second: add two lines on introducing a mate to the handling or cage setup page.

## zebra-finch

**Page by page**
- 00 Care guide hub: A. Leads with "never one bird" before cage dimensions, correctly treating group size as the real decision.
- 01 Encyclopedia: A. The detail that lab colonies descend from aviculture stock, not wild imports, is a genuinely interesting and specific fact.
- 02 Cost guide: A. Explains why the bird is cheap but the commitment isn't, rather than just listing a price range.
- 03 Handling guide: A. Three separate named sources (Lafeber, PetMD, a named avian vet) agree on the same point instead of one vague claim repeated.
- 04 Health issues: A. Explains the predator-species mechanism for why finches hide illness, not just the fact that they do.
- 05 Cage setup: A. Clear on the one dimension not to sacrifice (length) versus the one that can flex (depth).
- 06 Feeding: A. Specific gram and teaspoon amounts throughout, not vague portions.
- 07 Enrichment: A. Cites an actual field study on wild group size and a captive corticosterone study, the best-supported page across all three sets.

**Conflicts**
The adult weight figure does not quite match across pages. The encyclopedia gives adult size as "0.3-0.5 oz," which converts to roughly 8.5 to 14 grams. The health issues page states: "A healthy adult zebra finch weighs about 10 to 16 grams." The top end is off by close to 2 grams, which is a meaningful gap for a bird this size when the same page tells you to weigh it daily and treat a one-gram change as significant.

**Gaps**
No page says what to do if a hen lays an unwanted clutch despite following the advice to offer nesting material without a nest site. The set tells you how to prevent breeding but not what to do if it happens anyway. Nothing covers finding or vetting an avian vet, the same gap as the other two sets in this batch.

**Trust**
None found. This is the most rigorously sourced set of the three, citing the Merck Veterinary Manual, VCA, RSPCA-style guidance, and a named 2025 journal study by name rather than "studies show."

**Set grade: A.** The best-supported and most internally consistent of the three sets. First change: reconcile the 0.3-0.5 oz encyclopedia figure with the 10-16 gram health page figure so the two don't disagree at the top end. Second: add two or three lines on what to do about an accidental clutch, since the set spends real effort telling owners how to prevent one.
