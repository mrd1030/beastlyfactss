## neon-tetra

1. Page ratings:
- 00 Care guide hub: A. Every first-week number links to its source article, the buy list is real, the emergency card is usable at the tank.
- 01 Encyclopedia: A-. Good natural history (the Hindenburg story is a nice hook), no care content by design, which is correct for this page's job.
- 02 Cost guide: A. Real numbers, dated prices, an honest "where a cheap fish gets expensive" section instead of just a table.
- 03 Handling guide: A. Covers acclimation, sexing, and a genuine breeding section most beginner guides skip.
- 04 Health issues guide: A. Clear on NTD versus columnaris, gives a euthanasia path.
- 05 Tank setup guide: A. The blackwater/tannin detail and the "let the tank mature" section earn their place.
- 06 Feeding guide: A. The 1-2mm mouth detail is used consistently and the dead-fish warning ties feeding to disease.
- 07 Enrichment guide: A. A cited 2010 study, not just received wisdom, and it says so.

2. Conflicts: Every neon page (hub, tank setup, health issues, encyclopedia deep dive) gives quarantine as "2 to 4 weeks." The sidebar's Fish Quarantine and Treatment Guide sets a hard floor: "a 30-day period is the minimum time for quarantine, but longer periods may be necessary," backed by Merck and, separately, VCA's ich guidance. A reader following the neon pages alone would release a new fish 2 to 16 days before the site's own general standard says it is safe.

3. Gaps, after the sidebar check:
- No CO2 or liquid fertilizer guidance for the "dense midground live plants" the tank setup guide calls for; neither the species pages nor the sidebar cover planted-tank fertilization.
- No brand or type guidance for the liquid test kit beyond "liquid water test kit" on the buy list.
- Nothing on how to tell a healthy NTD-negative fish is still a false-negative risk during the 2-4 week window itself, i.e. what to actually watch for daily in quarantine (the sidebar quarantine guide has this, so covered only there, and the neon pages give no reason to open it).

4. Trust: The "over-diagnosed" claim about neon tetra disease appears three times (hub, health guide fun fact, health guide FAQ) with no citation, just "aquatic specialists" and "likely." Compare the cardinal tetra set, which sources the same disease to the Merck Veterinary Manual by name. Not false, but repeated confidently enough that a reader could mistake it for an established, sourced fact rather than a hedge.

5. Set grade: A-. The set is unusually complete and well cross-linked for a beginner fish, and the one real problem is a number, not a gap: quarantine duration disagrees with the site's own general standard by a wide margin. First fix: bring the neon quarantine number in line with the 30-day sidebar floor, or explain in the neon health guide why this species is treated as an exception. Second fix: cite a source for "over-diagnosed" the way the cardinal set does for the same disease.

## cardinal-tetra

1. Page ratings:
- 00 Care guide hub: A. Same strong format as neon, correctly adjusted numbers (ten fish, warmer water, 30-day quarantine).
- 01 Encyclopedia: A. The Schultz/Myers naming-priority story is genuinely interesting and well told.
- 02 Cost guide: A. Cites Project Piaba, Practical Fishkeeping, Seriously Fish and Animal Diversity Web by name for its lifespan and price claims, the best-sourced cost guide of the three sets.
- 03 Handling guide: B+. Good on acclimation and pH/cold tolerance data, but has no sexing section at all, unlike the neon handling guide it otherwise mirrors.
- 04 Health issues guide: B. Well sourced, but see the ich conflict below.
- 05 Tank setup guide: A. Cites a 2023 phylogeography study for origin and Seriously Fish for every parameter; genuinely more rigorous than most care sheets.
- 06 Feeding guide: A. Consistent with the neon feeding guide, adjusted for this species' faster wild feeding pattern.
- 07 Enrichment guide: B+. Honest that there is no cardinal-specific study and the neon result is borrowed, which is the right way to handle it, but it is thinner than the other pages as a result.

2. Conflicts: The health issues guide says ich treatment for cardinal tetra should not use heat: "Raising the temperature is a widely repeated ich remedy that this protocol does not call for, and it is worth knowing that 50% mortality for this species sits near 33.7°C." But the sidebar's ich guide explicitly lists cardinal tetra, by name, alongside discus, as a species that CAN take the standard 86°F heat cure for two weeks: "Discus and cardinal tetras can. Goldfish, koi, zebra danios, neon tetras and bristlenose plecos cannot." 86°F is 30°C, nearly 4°C below the 33.7°C mortality point the cardinal page itself cites, so the reasoning given for skipping heat does not actually support skipping it, and it contradicts the sidebar's own classification.

3. Gaps, after the sidebar check:
- No sexing guide for cardinal tetra (male vs. female), though the closely related neon tetra page covers this and cardinal readers are directed to compare the two species.
- No breeding section, again present for neon but absent here.
- No CO2/fertilizer guidance for the dense planting the tank setup and enrichment guides both call for.

4. Trust: "Huge numbers of cardinal tetras are still sustainably wild-harvested... a rare case where the aquarium trade directly funds rainforest conservation" appears unqualified in the tagline, fun fact, and encyclopedia overview. The enrichment guide is more careful: "It is a genuinely contested claim and it is not a marketing line." The unqualified version reads like a feel-good marketing line exactly where it appears most (the page most people skim), while the honest caveat is buried three clicks deep.

5. Set grade: B+. Strongest sourcing of the three sets, undercut by a real contradiction on ich heat treatment that could lead a keeper to withhold a treatment their fish can safely tolerate. First fix: resolve the ich heat conflict, either by updating the health guide to match the sidebar's "cardinal can take 86°F" classification or by explaining why this species page deliberately overrides it. Second fix: add the "contested claim" caveat to the hub and encyclopedia, not just the enrichment guide.

## bristlenose-pleco

1. Page ratings:
- 00 Care guide hub: A. The species-confirmation warning up front is the single most useful thing in this set and it leads every list.
- 01 Encyclopedia: A. Honest about unresolved taxonomy instead of papering over it.
- 02 Cost guide: A. The common-pleco mix-up gets real identification features (dorsal fin ray count, head shape), not just a warning.
- 03 Handling guide: A. Specific, practical, and unusually good on a genuinely awkward-to-handle fish (net-snagging spines, herding into a cup).
- 04 Health issues guide: A. The copper warning is clear and repeated appropriately; internal parasites section is honest about needing a vet rather than guessing.
- 05 Tank setup guide: A. Ties pH, KH and driftwood tannins together correctly instead of treating them as separate topics.
- 06 Feeding guide: A. The 85/15 plant-to-protein ratio turned into an actual weekly schedule is genuinely more useful than most feeding guides.
- 07 Enrichment guide: A. Rare for a care site to say "the standard explanation is questionable" and then cite the two studies that question it.

2. Conflicts: None found.

3. Gaps, after the sidebar check:
- No quarantine duration is stated anywhere in the bristlenose set itself, only a generic link to "hospital tank." The sidebar quarantine guide gives 30 days minimum, but nothing on the species pages tells a reader that number applies here or gives a reason to click through, so it is covered only in the sidebar.
- No breeding or spawning process (egg count, timeline, fry care), despite caves being explicitly described as spawning sites in the enrichment guide.
- No tankmate list beyond corydoras catfish as a single example; a reader stocking a community tank around a bristlenose gets no other names to check.

4. Trust: Specific branded products are named inline rather than only in the buy list: Hikari Tropical Sinking Wafers (twice), an AquaClear 70 filter, and CaribSea Super Naturals Aquascape Sunset Gold sand. Each article carries an affiliate disclosure, so this is not concealed, but naming specific SKUs inside the setup and feeding advice itself, rather than as a separate "recommended products" callout, reads closer to product placement than the neon and cardinal sets, which keep pricing generic and sourced to retailers rather than specific items.

5. Set grade: A-. This is the best-researched set of the three, with no factual conflicts found and real identification help exactly where a beginner needs it most (confirming the species before buying). First fix: state a quarantine duration on at least the health issues guide rather than leaving it to the sidebar. Second fix: pull the specific product names out of the body copy and into the existing buy list, where a disclosed recommendation belongs.
