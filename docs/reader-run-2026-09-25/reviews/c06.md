## tarantula

Pages, grade and verdict:
- 00 care guide hub: A. Every first-week number links to its source article and the numbers hold up on a second pass.
- 01 encyclopedia: B+. Good CITES history, but it is pure background, nothing actionable.
- 02 cost guide: A. Sexing from a molt, seller vetting questions, and the "no insurance, few vets" reality all land.
- 03 handling guide: A. The eye injury case study earns its place, it is the one page that could actually change behavior.
- 04 health issues guide: A. Clear split between dehydration, molt problems, falls and nematodes, with a real emergency list.
- 05 tank setup guide: A. Covers adult and sling housing separately, which most care sheets skip.
- 06 feeding guide: A. The "when to worry" checklist (abdomen, water dish, mouth, sex, recent changes) is the best single passage in the set.
- 07 enrichment guide: A-. Honest about a study that does not fully support its own headline, rare for a pet site.
- 08 vs. emperor scorpion: B+. Useful comparison table, though it exists mostly to funnel traffic both directions.

Conflicts: None found. Quarantine length (three months), fall risk framing, molt duration (15 minutes to several hours) and dehydration signs all match across the hub, the health guide, and the sidebar molting and quarantine articles.

Gaps, after the sidebar check:
- Bite first aid beyond "roughly a hornet sting" is not spelled out anywhere, unlike the detailed sting first aid given for the emperor scorpion.
- No page in this set states a specific mite-on-substrate treatment product; the fix given is just "improve ventilation and sanitation."
- "Are Tarantulas Legal?" and "10 Surprising Tarantula Facts" are both linked repeatedly from every sidebar but neither file exists in this set, so the state-legality question the hub promises to answer is not actually answered here.

Trust: Nothing reads as an ad. The cost guide is careful to flag its own comparison figure ("An emperor scorpion lives 5 to 8 years whatever its sex") as an assist to the sister article rather than a hard sell. The calcium-dusting FAQ answer is a good model of honesty: it recommends skipping it while admitting "there's no strong scientific evidence it causes harm."

Set grade: A-. This is the strongest of the four sets, thorough and internally consistent, and it earns the grade by being honest about a study's mixed results instead of cherry-picking. First fix: write the two missing linked articles (legal, facts) since the hub's own Deep Dive list promises them. Second: add a line of first aid for an actual bite, not just the hair-in-eye scenario.

## emperor-scorpion

Pages, grade and verdict:
- 00 care guide hub: A. Legal check placed first in the first-week list is the right call for a species banned in three places.
- 01 encyclopedia: B+. The CITES export-quota numbers (Benin 42,781 in 2001) are a genuinely interesting, sourced detail.
- 02 cost guide: A. Debunking the $800 price rumor by name is exactly the kind of thing a buyer needs before shopping.
- 03 handling guide: A. Real first aid steps for a sting, plus a child-specific Poison Control line, not just "it's mild."
- 04 health issues guide: A. Cites a named 2024 study for the molt-vulnerability claim, and clearly separates emergency from manageable-at-home.
- 05 tank setup guide: A. Humidity and substrate depth get equal top billing, matching what the health guide says kills this animal.
- 06 feeding guide: A-. Solid, though the "3 to 4 crickets" portion line and the hub's "2 to 3 insects" line for adults are close enough to be the same guidance stated two ways, not a real conflict, just worth tightening.
- 07 enrichment guide: A-. Refreshingly upfront that there is no species-specific research and the enclosure study is a borrow from tarantulas.

Conflicts: None found. Quarantine length (three months) for a newcomer joining a group is consistent between the enclosure guide, the enrichment guide, and the sidebar quarantine article. The "three jurisdictions ban it outright" line in the cost guide matches the hub's named list (NYC, Hawaii, DC).

Gaps, after the sidebar check:
- No page gives an emperor-scorpion-specific escape-recapture routine (where it hides, how to search); the sidebar quarantine/escape guide gives only a generic tarantula-and-scorpion catch-cup method, not species-specific hiding behavior.
- The legal guide itself ("Are Emperor Scorpions Legal?") is linked from every sidebar but is not one of the files in this set, so the permit process in Maine and Rhode Island and the "unclear" states are named but never explained.
- No page states how to tell a stressed post-purchase scorpion (there is no wild-caught adjustment section the way the hermit crab and tarantula pages have one), even though the species is imported via CITES ranching quotas per the encyclopedia page.

Trust: Nothing sounds like an advertisement. The cost guide's handling of the $800 rumor is a good-faith correction rather than a hedge. The claim that this species is "one of the few arachnids you can keep in a group" is backed by a real caveat (three conditions: space, hides, food) rather than stated as a free pass.

Set grade: A-. Tight and well-sourced, let down only by the missing legal deep-dive that the hub explicitly promises. First fix: write or link the actual legal guide content instead of just naming which states restrict it. Second: add a short post-purchase acclimation note, since these are wild-collected animals per the encyclopedia's own import numbers.

## jumping-spider

Pages, grade and verdict:
- 00 care guide hub: A. Door-placement detail (low, since the retreat goes at the top) is a small thing that actually prevents a real mistake.
- 01 encyclopedia: A-. The Lucas the Spider pop-culture origin story for its popularity is a nice, verifiable hook.
- 02 cost guide: A. Sexing a juvenile by scale color before maturity is genuinely useful and specific to this species.
- 03 handling guide: A. The defensive-posture cue (front legs raised, end the session) is concrete and easy to apply.
- 04 health issues guide: A. Names an actual vet directory (ARAV) and gives a real transport protocol, rare specificity for an invertebrate page.
- 05 tank setup guide: A. The spider ICU instructions are a genuine emergency tool, not filler.
- 06 feeding guide: A. The abdomen-as-calendar framing plus the ant warning (with the mechanism: bite or formic acid) is well argued.
- 07 enrichment guide: A-. Cites specific named studies (a 2024 eLife paper, a 2020 Learning and Behavior review) rather than vague "research shows."

Conflicts: None found. Humidity target (50-60% for Phidippus), fasting tolerance (2-3 weeks for a healthy adult) and premolt signs are stated the same way across the hub, feeding guide, health guide and enclosure guide.

Gaps, after the sidebar check:
- No page or sidebar article gives a species-specific rehousing or catch method for a jumping spider; the sidebar rehousing guide's species table covers tarantulas, scorpions and mantises but has no jumping-spider row, so cleaning the enclosure around an animal you are told never to grab is left unaddressed.
- No page states whether jumping spiders carry any legal restriction anywhere, which is likely a non-issue but is never said outright the way it is for the other two arachnids in this batch.
- The feeding guide gives fruit fly culture cost in the cost guide ($28-34) but neither page says how long a single culture actually lasts a spiderling, only that it is the ongoing monthly cost.

Trust: Nothing sounds oversold. The pages are careful to flag their own limits, twice noting explicitly that the fasting-duration numbers are "hobbyist-reported, not veterinary-established" rather than dressing up anecdote as fact.

Set grade: A. The strongest, most specific writing of the four sets, and the one most willing to admit where its own numbers come from. First fix: add a jumping-spider row or note to the shared rehousing guide, since handling advice alone doesn't cover moving the animal during a deep clean. Second: a one-line legal note, if only to say there isn't one, matching the pattern set by the other two arachnid guides.

## hermit-crab

Pages, grade and verdict:
- 00 care guide hub: A. Framing the animal correctly up front, "nobody's starter pet," sets expectations the boardwalk-shop version never does.
- 01 encyclopedia: A-. The 2018 captive-rearing breakthrough (Mary Akers, 204 crabs against a prior record of 24) is a strong, specific fact.
- 02 cost guide: A. Directly attacks the "cheap boardwalk crab" misconception with the actual cost driver (the setup, not the animal).
- 03 handling guide: A. Autotomy mechanism explained, plus concrete male/female sexing by gonopore location.
- 04 health issues guide: A. Post-purchase stress as the named leading cause of death, with a real protocol for a crab found out of its shell (firm vs. soft).
- 05 tank setup guide: A. The "never heat from below" warning is tied directly to the molting risk explained elsewhere, not stated in isolation.
- 06 feeding guide: A. Names two specific toxic additives (copper sulfate, ethoxyquin) with a mechanism for how the second one hides on a label, and lists real sources.
- 07 enrichment guide: A. The Elwood and Appel shock-versus-shell research is the best sourced enrichment case in any of the four sets, and it presents the Briffa disagreement instead of hiding it.

Conflicts: None found. The buried-molt duration (weeks to about three months), quarantine window (three months), and humidity target (75-85%) all match across the hub, handling guide, health guide, enclosure guide and enrichment guide.

Gaps, after the sidebar check:
- No page gives a number or method for checking a buried crab's condition without digging it up (the bottle-dome trick is given only for protecting a molting crab from tankmates, not for a keeper worried about a crab buried unusually long).
- The feeding guide names a hydrometer reading (1.021 to 1.026) for the saltwater dish but no page says what to do if a keeper doesn't own a hydrometer, given the marine salt mix instructions assume no aquarium background.
- Legal status is never raised at all in this set, which is likely correct for a hermit crab but is worth confirming given every other invertebrate set in this batch addresses it directly.

Trust: Nothing sounds like a pitch. The cost guide's "why cheap boardwalk crab is misleading" section reads as a genuine correction, not a means to upsell a bigger tank. The enrichment guide's choice to show a live academic disagreement (Elwood vs. Briffa) rather than resolve it is a strong trust signal.

Set grade: A. The most consistently well-sourced set of the four, and the only one that directly confronts the "impulse buy" reputation its own species has. First fix: add a plain-language fallback for readers without a hydrometer for the salt dish. Second: a short note on how long is too long for a buried crab to go unchecked, since "don't dig it up" is repeated often without a corresponding "but if X, do check."
