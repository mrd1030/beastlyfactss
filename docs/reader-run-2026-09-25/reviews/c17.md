## blue-tongue-skink

1. Page grades:
- 00 hub: A. First-week numbers, buy list, emergency card and FAQ all trace back to a real deep dive. I'd finish it and shop off it directly.
- 01 encyclopedia: A. The John White 1790 description and the pygmy blue-tongue "found inside a snake" story make this worth reading, not a spec sheet.
- 02 cost guide: A. Dated price check, real tables, and it argues against the cheaper Indonesian option instead of just listing it.
- 03 handling guide: A. Named sources for the huff scale and the tail-drop mechanics, and it tells you what to do at each stage, not just what the behavior means.
- 04 health issues: A. MBD, parasites, brumation by species, and weight math are all actionable, with a cited parasite-occurrence study.
- 05 tank setup: A. Built around the Northern vs Indonesian split rather than a generic care sheet with a footnote.
- 06 feeding guide: A. Flags the vet-vs-pet-sheet feeding disagreement itself instead of quietly picking a side.
- 07 enrichment: B+. One real study applied well, but it is a single small study (nine animals) generalized to the whole species without saying so again here.

2. Conflicts: Adult size is given two ways. The hub and encyclopedia say "17-24 inches (43-60 cm)." The health-issues guide says "An adult Northern blue-tongue runs about 17 inches long on average, up to 22." These aren't necessarily contradictory (species range vs. one subspecies' average) but no page says so, so a reader lands on 24 in one place and 22 in another with nothing bridging them. Otherwise none found. The feeding-frequency split (vet guidance every 1 to 3 days vs. pet-industry sheets at once or twice a week) is disclosed on the same page as a real, sourced disagreement, not a hidden conflict.

3. Gaps (checked against sidebar first): Whether two blue-tongue skinks can be housed together is never addressed anywhere in the set or the sidebar, and new owners of a "chunky, personable" lizard are a plausible group to wonder about this. How to sex a skink, or what proof of captive-bred origin to ask a seller for, isn't covered; the cost guide gets close (Northern vs. Indonesian) but stops at price and country of origin. Bioactive or live-plant enclosure options aren't mentioned, only inert soil/sand or coco fiber substrate. Quarantine, heating, shedding, hygiene, stool checks, gut-loading and UVB are all genuinely covered in the sidebar guides and linked from the relevant species page, so those are not gaps.

4. Trust: The cost guide's setup table lists "Moderate UVB (T5 HO Arcadia 6%)" inside an otherwise generic spec list, the one place a specific bulb brand appears rather than a spec range. It's disclosed as affiliate content and the retailers named for the price check (Zen Habitats, Josh's Frogs, PetSmart) read as a genuine "here's where we checked," but naming one bulb brand there reads slightly like product placement next to otherwise brand-neutral numbers. Nothing else oversells: the site actively steers a first-time buyer away from the cheaper Indonesian animal rather than toward a sale, which cuts the other way from typical affiliate-site incentives.

5. Set grade: A-. The Northern-vs-Indonesian split isn't a gimmick bolted onto one page, it runs consistently through cost, setup, handling, and health, and every number traces to a named source. First two changes: reconcile the 17-24 vs. 17-22 inch figures on one page instead of leaving two numbers stand unexplained, and add a line on solitary housing since nothing in the set says skinks shouldn't be paired up.

## fire-skink

1. Page grades:
- 00 hub: A. First-week numbers, buy list, emergency card and FAQ are consistent with the deep dives and give a real shopping and care plan on one page.
- 01 encyclopedia: A. The taxonomic shuffle (Lygosoma to Lepidothyris to Riopa to Mochlus) and the 2009 species split are genuine history, not padding.
- 02 cost guide: A. Real per-item and per-quart substrate math, and it steers a buyer toward captive-bred over the cheaper wild-caught animal.
- 03 handling guide: A-. Good on temperament, tail autotomy and first-timer mistakes, but the second half turns into a breeding article (sexing, mating temperature, incubation, hatchling care) that doesn't belong under "handling."
- 04 health issues: A. Ties every listed problem back to humidity or substrate, with a genuinely useful step-by-step for a dropped tail.
- 05 tank setup: A. Substrate math, humidity plan, and an honest note that published UVB targets for this species vary.
- 06 feeding guide: A. Explains why an unseen skink is probably still eating rather than starving, which is the actual anxiety this species creates.
- 07 enrichment: A. States plainly that there's no published research on this species and reasons from a blue-tongue study instead, then gives a real answer on cohousing.

2. Conflicts: None found on numbers, schedules or costs; substrate replacement (3 to 4 months), temperature and humidity ranges, and UVB percentage all match across the hub, cost guide, and tank setup guide.

3. Gaps (checked against sidebar first): The cost guide's setup table specifies a "glass terrarium," but the sidebar's UVB guide states directly that "a glass lid costs you most of" the UVB output, and the blue-tongue skink's sibling guide on this site recommends PVC over glass specifically because glass holds humidity worse, a real concern here given the 60 to 70% target. Nothing in the fire skink set says whether the recommended enclosure has a mesh or glass top, or addresses the glass-vs-PVC tradeoff for a high-humidity burrower at all. That's a real gap, not answered by the sidebar since the UVB guide raises the exact problem without applying it here. Quarantine, heating, salmonella, shedding, stool checks, and gut-loading are all genuinely covered in the sidebar and correctly linked, so those are not gaps.

4. Trust: Nothing reads as an ad; no specific retailer or bulb brand is named in the setup table, and the site recommends the pricier captive-bred animal over its own cheaper listed option. The one line worth flagging isn't dishonest so much as incomplete: "A lower-output UVB tube in the 5 to 6% range... is a reasonable, well-supported choice" sits right after the same article admits "published UVB targets for this species split" by a factor of four (Ferguson Zone 2 at UVI 0.7-1.0 up to 3.0-4.0). Calling the low end "well-supported" glosses over that the site's own fun-fact box just described a real disagreement.

5. Set grade: A-. The set correctly treats substrate and humidity as the one thing this species lives or dies by and never drops that thread, and it's honest about the total absence of species-specific research rather than dressing up a borrowed study as fire skink data. First two changes: move the sexing and breeding material out of the handling guide into its own section or article, and add a line addressing enclosure top material (mesh vs. glass) given the UVB and humidity stakes already established elsewhere on the site.

## tegu

1. Page grades:
- 00 hub: A. Leads with the law, which is the right call for a species with an active state-by-state ban list, then gives real numbers for everything else.
- 01 encyclopedia: A. The 2006 Florida population, the nearly 3,300 pulled from Miami-Dade, and the 2021 prohibited-list date make this feel researched rather than templated.
- 02 cost guide: A. Honest that the "ready made 8ft enclosure" is really 8x2x2, not the 8x4x4 an adult needs, and prices the real one at $2,200 to $3,140.
- 03 handling guide: A. The dog comparison is backed with named sources and immediately qualified ("it doesn't happen automatically"), and the bite protocol cites an actual case report.
- 04 health issues: A. Covers prolapse and scale rot with real first aid, not just "see a vet," and ties MBD and respiratory infection back to this species' scale specifically.
- 05 tank setup: A. States plainly where it departs from LafeberVet's smaller minimum and why (this page's 8x4x4 versus their 6x3x3).
- 06 feeding guide: A. The obesity framing (an adult on two meals a week "will look hungry every day, it is not") is the single most useful line in the set.
- 07 enrichment: A. Opens by saying no tegu study exists and never lets the reader forget the evidence is borrowed from monitors, a rare level of honesty for this kind of page.

2. Conflicts: The tank setup guide's diet section states the feeding schedule as fact: "Feed a young tegu every day. From one to three years old, every other day. Past three years, every third day," and the hub repeats this same schedule as the site's official numbers. But the feeding guide's own primary table gives a different, more granular schedule with a different adult cutoff: subadults (1 to 2 years) at 3 times a week, adults (past 2 years, not 3) at 2 times a week. The feeding guide itself notices the mismatch and calls the daily/every-other-day/every-third-day version "older care sheets," saying the two "sit within a day a week of each other," but that reconciliation lives only on the feeding page. A reader who only sees the hub or the tank setup guide gets the older schedule presented as the current one, with no note that the site's own feeding guide uses a different cutoff age and a different table.

3. Gaps (checked against sidebar first): Outdoor housing is never mentioned in any tegu-specific page despite the adult enclosure footprint (8x4x4 feet, often a room) being exactly the size where keepers commonly move to an outdoor pen in warm months; the sidebar's outdoor housing guide does answer "can lizards go outside" generically, but nothing in the tegu set points a reader there or flags it as an option, so it counts as covered only in that sidebar article. Nothing on where to actually source a legal, captive-bred tegu or vet a seller, beyond price and a state-legality check. Quarantine, heating, salmonella, shedding, stool checks and UVB are all genuinely covered in the sidebar and correctly linked.

4. Trust: Nothing here reads as an advertisement. No specific bulb or product brand is named, the site tells buyers a common cheaper enclosure option doesn't actually meet the size requirement, and it repeatedly discounts its own "dog-like" marketing hook by tying it to the daily work required, not to a purchase.

5. Set grade: A. This is the strongest of the four sets: it fronts the legal risk before the cute factor, does real math on enclosure and substrate cost instead of a vague range, and is the only set that explicitly labels its enrichment evidence as borrowed rather than dressing it up as tegu-specific. First two changes: reconcile the two feeding schedules on the tank setup guide and hub instead of stating the older one as settled fact, and add a line on outdoor housing as an option given the size of the enclosure being discussed.

## ackie-monitor

1. Page grades:
- 00 hub: A. Leads with legal status and the emergency card, and the first-week numbers (130-170°F basking, 12-24in substrate) are unusual enough that a skimming reader actually needs them upfront.
- 01 encyclopedia: A. The 1982 Australian export ban and the smuggling note give real context for why "red" and "yellow" ackies are priced the way the cost guide prices them.
- 02 cost guide: A. The "choosing a seller" section (specializes, writes real listings, has a reputation, stands behind shipping, has a fecal result) is the most concrete buying advice in any of the four sets reviewed today.
- 03 handling guide: A. The session-length table by age and the tongue-flick and tail-whip reads are specific and sourced, not generic "watch its body language" filler.
- 04 health issues: A. Egg cycling and egg binding get real numbers (18in nest depth, 86-87°F) rather than a passing mention, which matters since females lay eggs with no male present.
- 05 tank setup: A. Splits ambient humidity (20-50%) from burrow humidity (80%+) explicitly, which is the detail most generic care sheets flatten into one number.
- 06 feeding guide: A. States outright "we couldn't find a single, consistently sourced number" for how long an ackie can go without eating rather than inventing one.
- 07 enrichment: A. Names the study species (Varanus albigularis) and says plainly the ackie is a different species in the same genus, genus-level evidence and not a species-specific finding.

2. Conflicts: None found. Basking temperature, UVI target, substrate depth, enclosure size, and the no-rodent-staple rule all match across the hub, cost guide, tank setup, and feeding guide.

3. Gaps (checked against sidebar first): Nothing in the set or the sidebar says whether two ackies can be housed together, or whether males fight the way the tegu and fire skink pages in this same site say their males do; given the health guide already discusses females laying eggs, the reader is left to guess whether a same-enclosure pair is workable at all. How to actually sex an ackie (probing, hemipenal bulge, or any visible marker) is never covered anywhere, even though the health guide assumes the reader might have a female and the cost guide discusses buying from a specialist breeder. Quarantine, heating, salmonella, shedding, stool checks, gut-loading and UVB are all genuinely covered in the sidebar and correctly linked, so those are not gaps.

4. Trust: The cost guide's setup table lists "Strong desert UVB (T5 HO Arcadia 12%/Desert)," the same pattern of naming one bulb brand inside an otherwise generic price table seen on the blue-tongue skink's cost guide. It's disclosed as affiliate content and the rest of the buying advice (steering readers away from online distributors and toward a specializing breeder with a fecal result) argues against the cheap, fast option rather than for a sale, so this reads as a minor consistency issue rather than a real ad. Nothing else oversells: the "backflips for crickets" framing is followed immediately by "this is also a more demanding lizard," not left to stand alone as marketing.

5. Set grade: A. This is a close second to the tegu set: it is upfront that the ackie needs a bigger, hotter, more specialized setup than its size suggests, it declines to state a number it doesn't have (days without food) rather than padding the answer, and it correctly separates ambient from burrow humidity where most sources would give one figure. First two changes: add a line on whether ackies can be paired or housed in groups, since the health guide already assumes a keeper might have a female, and add basic sexing information so a buyer isn't left guessing what they actually purchased.
