THE TRAP. Secondary sources circulate a Delaware prohibited list reading roughly
"elephants, wolverines, hyaenas, kangaroos, wallabies, armadillos, skunks, raccoons,
hippopotamus, peccaries, bats, rhinoceroses, wild rodents, crocodiles, cobras, coral
snakes, mambas, vipers, rattlesnakes, copperheads, cottonmouths, pythons, anacondas,
gila monsters and komodo dragons". No such possession prohibition exists in chapter 903.
Those names come from other parts of the scheme, chiefly 7.5.2.10, which is a restriction
on what SALES PERMIT HOLDERS may BREED. Reading it as a ban puts the ball python, the
prairie dog and the capybara in the wrong bucket, which is exactly what happened here.

STATUTE, Del. Code tit. 3 § 7201, verbatim:

  "No person shall bring into this State, possess, sell or exhibit any live wild mammal
   or hybrid of a wild mammal or live reptile not native to or generally found in
   Delaware without first securing a permit under this chapter."
  Plus an outright ban, with no permit available, on non-native venomous snakes whose
  venom poses a risk of serious injury or death. Permit fee is $25 under § 7202.
  Note what is absent: birds, fish, amphibians, invertebrates. All outside the chapter.

REGULATION 903:

  6.1.1 Exempt mammals: Chinchillas, Degus, Ferrets, Gerbils, Guinea pigs, Hamsters,
        Hedgehogs, Mice, Norway rats, Possums, Rabbits and Sugar gliders.
  6.1.2 Exempt reptiles: Anoles, Agamas, Asian Water Dragons, Basilisks, Bearded dragons,
        Chameleons, Geckos, Iguanas, Skinks (except the five-lined skink), Swift lizards,
        and Tegus.
        Note: lizards are exempted generously and NO snake, turtle or tortoise is exempt.
  6.2   Unless exempt under 6.1, anyone owning or having custody of an exotic must first
        obtain a permit from the Department.
  4.7   The State Veterinarian will not issue Individual, Exhibitor or Sales permits for
        gila monsters, beaded lizards or komodo dragons. This is the ONLY possession
        prohibition in the chapter.
  7.5.2.10 Sales Permit holders may not breed: crocodilians, tuataras, giant snakes over
        5 feet (anacondas, pythons, boas), giant monitors, giant tortoises, and rare or
        endangered species. A BREEDING restriction on one permit class.

NATIVE SPECIES go to DNREC under Del. Code tit. 7 c. 7 instead, because a native animal
is not exotic. Native reptiles are not available for private ownership, with one carve-out
that matters: a captive-bred native snake in an aberrant colour morph may be held under
permit. That is why the garter snake is banned here and the corn and milk snakes, whose
pet trade is almost entirely colour morphs, are conditional.
