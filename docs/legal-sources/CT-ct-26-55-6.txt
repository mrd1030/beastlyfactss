HOW THE REGULATION WORKS. Four categories, and an animal in none of them is not restricted by
this section at all:
  Category One    four mammal families: Felidae, Canidae, Ursidae, Hominidae. Possession only by
                  municipal parks, zoos, aquaria, nature centres, museums and USDA-registered
                  exhibitors, labs and research facilities. No private route.
  Category Two    a long specific list. Possession only by those bodies plus a treating vet.
  Category Three  Connecticut's own natives. Bars import and export, NOT possession.
  Category Four   the state endangered, threatened and special concern list under § 26-306.
                  A real permit route, per animal, for that animal's life, non-transferable.

THE DEFINITIONS DO MORE WORK THAN THE LISTS. Subsection (a):
  (21) "Wild animal" reaches birds, mammals, reptiles, amphibians AND INVERTEBRATES. Connecticut
       has authority over a tarantula and has simply not used it, which is a different answer
       from the jurisdictional gap that clears the same animal in Arizona, Nevada and
       Pennsylvania.
  (7)  "Domestic animal" is defined by what selective breeding has done to the animal rather
       than by species name, and expressly excludes a wild animal raised in captivity over many
       generations that is still wild by nature. That single definition is why the pet rabbit is
       clean here: Category Two picks up "a species referenced as injurious wildlife in
       50 CFR 16.11", which lists the European rabbit, and the domestic animal definition takes
       a house rabbit out of the scheme before the categories are ever reached. Minnesota and
       Illinois name the same species with no equivalent definition and are much harder.

TWO THINGS TO READ EXACTLY:
  The rodent entry, Category Two (a)(3)(B)(xxv), puts the WHOLE ORDER Rodentia in Category Two
  and excepts nine: guinea pig, gerbil, chinchilla, rat, mouse, hamster (Mesocricetus auratus),
  dwarf hamsters, Patagonian mara, American beaver. So the prairie dog, capybara, degu and
  flying squirrel are all barred to private keepers, and the Patagonian mara being excepted while
  the capybara is not shows the line is deliberate.
  The monitor entry, (a)(3)(B)(xxxv), names the Nile, water, black-throated, white-throated and
  crocodile monitors. The black-throated and white-throated are subspecies of Varanus
  albigularis, NOT the savannah monitor, which is Varanus exanthematicus and appears nowhere.

THE BENGAL is carved out of Category One by name, "except Bengal cat pursuant to section 26-40a",
with no generation cutoff, and the clause opens "notwithstanding changes in taxonomic
nomenclature". It is the cleanest hybrid answer on the map.

(h)  Disease Reporting and Prevention .
(1) Any person who possesses a wild animal showing any symptoms of illness within 
six weeks of the importation of such wild animal shall, not later than twenty-four hours 
after recognition of such symptoms, file a report with the commissioner on a form prescribed 
by the commissioner. The commissioner may require that any wild animal imported into 
the state that shows symptoms of illness or disease be quarantined for a period of time to 
be determined by the commissioner.
(2) Any person who possesses a wild animal that continues to show symptoms of illness 
or disease during the quarantine period described in subdivision (1) of this subsection shall
immediately have such animal taken to a licensed veterinarian for examination, or if the 
animal is dead, shall have such animal taken to an animal disease laboratory, or such other
laboratory designated by the commissioner, for examination. Such person shall request that
a copy of the animal disease laboratory’s, or other laboratory’s, findings be sent to the 
Department of Energy and Environmental Protection and the Department of Agriculture.
