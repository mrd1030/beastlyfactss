READ THIS FIRST. 717F is the statute every summary of Iowa quotes and it answers none of the nine
animals on the tail of this map. Its dangerous wild animal definition at 717F.1(5) is canids,
hyaenids, felids, bears, rhinos, elephants, primates, crocodilians, swine, and a squamate list
limited to water and crocodile monitors, helodermatids, atractaspids, venomous snakes and three
large constrictors. It also carries an unusually precise Bengal and Savannah carve-out at fourth
filial generation.

The rule that can reach a pet is ch. 481A, and it has two hooks, both anchored to Iowa:

  481A.1(36) "Wild animal" means a wild mammal, bird, fish, amphibian, reptile, or other wildlife
  FOUND IN THIS STATE ... the ownership and title to which is claimed by this state.

  481A.42 Protected nongame species include wild fish, wild birds, wild bats, wild reptiles, and
  wild amphibians ... However, nongame does not include game, fish ..., fur-bearing animals,
  turtles, or frogs.

So an exotic pet is outside the chapter and no invertebrate is protected nongame at all. What is
inside is Iowa's own wildlife, and the definition of game is drawn by FAMILY with examples:
481A.1(21)(f) is "The Sciuridae: such as gray squirrels and fox squirrels", which takes in the
flying squirrels, the chipmunks and the ground squirrels. 481A.38 then bars possessing game except
as the chapter provides, and 481A.61(1) is the only provision under which a person may hold a live
game animal: a licensed game breeder may possess any game animal.

One rule worth knowing for how narrow Iowa's native protection is: 571 IAC 76.1(2)(a) declares the
common garter snake unprotected nongame outright, alongside the starling and the house sparrow.

==== Iowa Code 481A.42, protected nongame ====
481A .42 Nongame protected - e x clusion. P rotected nongame species include wild fish, wild
birds, wild bats, wild reptiles, and wild amphibians, an egg, a nest, a dead body or part of a

==== Iowa Code 481A.60, game breeder licence required ====
481A .60 Raising game - r ulemaking authority . A person shall not raise or sell game or fur
-bearing animals of the kinds protected by this chapter , except rock doves and pigeons, without
first procuring a game breeder 's license as provided by law . The commission may adopt rules
which ensure that all game birds, game animals, and fur -bearing animals handled and confined by
licensed game breeders are provided with humane care and treatment. A violation of a rule adopted
by the commission is a cause for license revocation. This section does not apply to governmental
zoos and exhibits.

==== Iowa Code 717F.1(5), the dangerous wild animal definition ====
