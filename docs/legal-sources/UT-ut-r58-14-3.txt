READ THIS FIRST. Table 3b-1 never mentions the raccoon, and R657-3b-1(2) explains why: possession of
coyotes and raccoons belongs to the Agricultural and Wildlife Damage Prevention Board, not to DWR. This
is that board's rule. It bars importing, distributing, relocating or possessing a live raccoon, and the
only way out is a per-animal variance for "An organization or corporation" possessing one for research
or exhibition. The rule was amended effective 23 May 2023. The enabling statute is cited in R657-3b as
Utah Code 4-23-11; the Act was renumbered to 4-23-101 to 4-23-111 in 2017, so the current section is
probably 4-23-111, which le.utah.gov did not serve to this container.

==== Utah Admin. Code R58-14-3, General ====
 (1) The Division of Wildlife Resources with
 the cooperation of the Department of Agriculture and Food and the Department of
 Health and Human Services, shall enforce this rule. 
 (2) The Agricultural and Wildlife Damage
 Prevention Board, by authority granted under Title 4, Chapter 23, the
 Agricultural and Wildlife Damage Prevention Act, declares it unlawful to
 import, distribute, relocate, or possess a live raccoon (Procyon
 lotor ) or coyote (Canis latrans). 
 (3) An organization or corporation wishing to
 possess raccoons or coyotes for research, or exhibition purposes may request a
 variance from the Department.
 (a) A separate
 variance shall be obtained from the Department for each raccoon and coyote
 possessed. 
 (b) A variance for a
 raccoon or coyote may not be transferred without the express permission of the
 Department. 
 (4) Nuisance
 raccoons and coyotes may not be relocated following capture, but may be
 captured and euthanized or otherwise destroyed at the location where capture is
 unfeasible. 
 (5) Any raccoon or
 coyote that bites or scratches a person or domestic animal shall be handled in
 accordance with Section
 R386-702-12 .
