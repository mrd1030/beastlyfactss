The four sections that decide pet questions:
  503.075  Amphibians. Protected list is three frogs and four toads; everything else unprotected.
  503.080  Reptiles. Protected: Gila monster, northern alligator lizard, Mojave fringe-toed
           lizard, Rosy Boa (Lichanura orcutti), Sonoran mountain kingsnake, western pond
           turtle; desert tortoise threatened. Everything else unprotected.
  503.110  Prohibited species. Importation, transportation and possession all barred.
           Permits only for accredited zoos, universities, scientific institutions,
           commercial aquaculture and nonprofit educational exhibitors.
  503.140  Species needing NO permit. Forty-five categories. The three that matter most:
           (q) all felines except mountain lions and bobcats
           (aa) nonvenomous, nonindigenous reptile species and subspecies
           (qq) nonindigenous amphibians except bullfrogs and 503.110 species
           Subsection 4 expressly preserves county and city ordinances.

The default for a species on neither list is NRS 503.597: unlawful without the written
consent and approval of the Department to bring any wildlife into the state. NAC 503.140
is the list of exemptions from that, so absence from it leaves the requirement standing.

NRS 501.097 defines wildlife as any wild mammal, wild bird, fish, reptile, amphibian,
mollusk or crustacean found naturally in a wild state. Insects and arachnids are outside
the Department's authority entirely.

Two traps in this chapter. NAC 503.140(1)(q) reads like a ban and is an exemption, which
is what put the serval in the wrong bucket on this map and in the serval guide. And
503.110(1)(d)(5) names Oryctolagus cuniculus under the common name Wild European Rabbit,
with no domesticated races carve-out, which is why the pet rabbit is recorded unclear.
