READ THIS WITH KS-ks-exotic-and-native.txt. That file explains the closed system: K.S.A.
32-1002(a)(2) bars possessing any wildlife unless a regulation permits it, 115-20-3 permits
exotics, 115-20-2 permits natives. This file carries the three pieces that decide which Kansas
native lands where.

FIRST. The snapping turtle carve-out in 115-20-2(b)(13) is not a prohibition, it is a handoff.
Kansas files the common snapping turtle and the two soft-shelled turtles under FISHING, which is
why they are struck from the reptile clause. K.A.R. 115-7-5(d): "A valid Kansas fishing license
shall be required to take common snapping turtles and soft-shelled turtles." The bag and
possession limits for them live in the fishing regulations rather than in Article 20, so the
two-per-species reptile cap in 115-20-2(e) never applies to a snapping turtle. If a Kansas entry
says a native reptile is outside 115-20-2, check Article 7 before concluding nothing permits it.

SECOND. Possession and taking are licensed separately. K.A.R. 115-9-7 lists fifteen activities
that need no hunting or fishing license, and subsection (i) is "possession of wildlife that was
legally acquired by the individual", with (h) covering donated wildlife. So the hunting license
that 115-20-2 implies for taking a native reptile, by exempting only invertebrates at (i), does
not attach to keeping a bought one. The caps do.

THIRD. The exclusion in 115-20-2(b) is the trap, and the two lists it points at are short enough
to check. 115-15-1 names three reptiles (broad-headed skink, checkered gartersnake, New Mexico
threadsnake) and one turtle (northern map turtle) as threatened, and two salamanders as
endangered. 115-15-2 names nine reptiles as nongame species in need of conservation, BOTH hognose
snakes among them, plus one turtle (alligator snapping turtle) and three amphibians. A Kansas
native on either list is outside 115-20-2 and outside 115-20-3, and 115-15-3 is written for
threatened and endangered wildlife rather than for the nongame tier, so there is no permit route.
That chain bans the pet hognose in Kansas exactly as it bans the flying squirrel.

Nativity is settled from the Kansas Herpetofaunal Atlas, maintained by Travis W. Taggart with the
Sternberg Museum of Natural History at Fort Hays State University and the Kansas Herpetological
Society: https://webapps.fhsu.edu/ksherp/. Its group index pages (account.aspx?o=<order>&t=0)
list established taxa, not-yet-established taxa and extinct taxa separately, and its SINC marks
match K.A.R. 115-15-2 name for name, which is what makes it usable for a legal question.
Established and relevant: Chelydra serpentina, Terrapene ornata, Terrapene triunguis, Trachemys
scripta, Thamnophis sirtalis, Lampropeltis triangulum (the Eastern Milksnake, alongside
Lampropeltis gentilis), Heterodon nasicus, Heterodon platirhinos, Ambystoma tigrinum. Absent from
the checklist entirely, in both the established and the not-yet-established sections: Pantherophis
guttatus (Kansas has P. emoryi and P. obsoletus), Lampropeltis californiae (Kansas has
L. calligaster and L. holbrooki), Anolis carolinensis.


==== K.A.R. 115-7-5, bullfrogs and turtles, in full ====

(a) Legal equipment and methods for taking bullfrogs shall be the following: (1) Hand; (2) hand
dip net; (3) hook and fishing line; (4) gig; (5) bow and arrow with barbed head and a line
attached from arrow to bow; and (6) crossbow and arrow with barbed head and a line attached from
arrow to crossbow. (b) Legal equipment and methods for taking common snapping turtles and
soft-shelled turtles shall be the following: (1) Hand; (2) hook and fishing line; (3) set line;
(4) hand dip net; (5) seine; (6) turtle trap; and (7) gig. (c) Artificial light and boats may be
used while taking bullfrogs and turtles. (d) A valid Kansas fishing license shall be required to
take common snapping turtles and soft-shelled turtles.


==== K.A.R. 115-9-7, the license exemptions, in full ====

(a) carrying or assist with carrying wildlife for another while in the company of that individual;
(b) possession of wildlife for the purpose of dressing, cleaning, processing for human consumption
or preparing for human consumption; (c) assisting with the dressing, cleaning, processing for human
consumption or preparing for human consumption; (d) performance of taxidermy work; (e) possession
of finished taxidermy work; (f) possession of finished wildlife products; (g) accompanying one or
more hunters in the field who are engaged in hunting, except the accompanying individual shall not
be in possession of hunting equipment for the shooting, capturing or harvesting of wildlife; (h)
possession of donated wildlife that was acquired, possessed and given by another; (i) possession of
wildlife that was legally acquired by the individual; (j) wildlife observations; (k) nature
observations and studies; (l) feeding of wildlife; (m) watering of wildlife; (n) accidental killing
or injuring of wildlife such as vehicle collision with wildlife; or (o) assisting with the tracking
of wounded wildlife.
