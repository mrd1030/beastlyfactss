READ THIS WITH VA-va-two-schemes.txt. That file explains the split: 4VAC15-20-50 puts native and
naturalized animals outside the exotic permit table and inside chapter 360. What it stops short of
is the rest of 4VAC15-360-10(A), and the subdivisions are where three cells are actually decided.

FIRST. The one-per-physical-address cap in the opening sentence applies "unless specifically
listed in this subsection". Two subdivisions list herps, and they go opposite ways.

  (A)(5) gives the SNAPPING TURTLE its own numbers instead of the cap: a daily limit of five, a
  season of 1 June to 30 September, a minimum curved-line carapace length of 13 inches, and no
  taking from the banks or waters of designated stocked trout waters. The size floor is the part
  that reaches a keeper, because it rules out a hatchling.

  (A)(6) withholds the private-use allowance entirely: "The following species may not be taken or
  possessed in any number for private use: red-eared slider and all reptile and amphibian Species
  of Greatest Conservation Need designated in Virginia's 2015 Wildlife Action Plan." IN ANY NUMBER.
  This is not the cap set lower, it is the permission removed, and with it removed nothing permits
  possession of what 4VAC15-20-50 defines as a wild animal.

SECOND. The red-eared slider is named by common name, and it is naturalized rather than native:
the incorporated list prints "Red-eared Slider * Trachemys scripta elegans *". Naturalized is
expressly excluded from "nonnative (exotic) animal", so the permit table never reached it and
(A)(6) does. Virginia's own two sliders, the Cumberland and the yellow-bellied, are on the SGCN
list and are barred by the same subdivision.

THIRD. The SGCN list is a moving document cited by a fixed date. The regulation names the 2015
Wildlife Action Plan; DWR now publishes the list as revised for the 2025 Action Plan and says on
its own page that the 2025 rankings were built from the 2015 ones. The three species this settles
are on both.

Several SGCN entries name the conservation action as "Continue maintaining 'no possession'
regulations (5.1.4)", which is the department describing 4VAC15-360-10(A)(6) from the other side.
That is the best confirmation available that the subdivision is read as a possession bar rather
than as a collecting rule.

FOURTH, and this is the one that is easy to miss. The permit table at 4VAC15-30-40 operates only
on nonnative (exotic) animals, so a family-wide row on it stops at the Virginia border of the
family. "Ambystomatidae All species, except Ambystoma mexicanum" reaches the mole salamanders
Virginia does not have and does NOT reach A. tigrinum, A. maculatum or A. opacum, which are on the
native list. Reading a Virginia native off that table is how the tiger salamander sat here as a
permit animal when it is barred outright.


==== 4VAC15-360-10(A), the operative subsection ====

A. Possession limits. Except as otherwise provided for in § 29.1-418 of the Code of Virginia,
4VAC15-20-130, 4VAC15-320-40, and this chapter, it shall be lawful to capture and possess live for
private use and not for sale or export no more than one individual of any native or naturalized, as
defined in 4VAC15-20-50, species of amphibian or reptile per physical address, and 20 individuals
of any single native or naturalized, as defined in 4VAC15-20-50, species of aquatic invertebrate
and nongame fish unless specifically listed in this subsection:

  ... 5. The daily limit for bullfrogs shall be 15 and for snapping turtles shall be five. Snapping
  turtles shall only be taken from June 1 to September 30 and must have a minimum curved-line
  carapace length of 13 inches. Bullfrogs and snapping turtles may not be taken from the banks or
  waters of designated stocked trout waters.

  6. The following species may not be taken or possessed in any number for private use: red-eared
  slider and all reptile and amphibian Species of Greatest Conservation Need designated in
  Virginia's 2015 Wildlife Action Plan.

  7. Native amphibians and reptiles, as defined in 4VAC15-20-50, that are captured within the
  Commonwealth and possessed live for private use and not for sale may be liberated under the
  following conditions: a. Period of captivity does not exceed 30 days; b. Animals must be
  liberated at the site of capture; c. Animals must have been housed separately from other
  wild-caught and domestic animals; and d. Animals that demonstrate symptoms of disease or illness
  or that have sustained injury during their captivity may not be released.

  8. Native or naturalized amphibians and reptiles, as defined in 4VAC15-20-50, may not be taken or
  possessed in any number from state or federal land without an appropriate permit or license.
