## guppy

**1. Page by page**
- Hub (00): Would finish. Acts as a numbers index with links to everything else. Grade A.
- Encyclopedia (01): Would finish, it's short. The Endler/Aripo River selection study and the naming history (Peters vs. Guppy) are genuinely interesting and not filler. Grade B+.
- Cost guide (02): Would finish and act on it. Clear price tables, dated prices, and the fry-glut warning is the most useful line on the whole site. Grade A.
- Handling/population guide (03): Would finish. Answers "why doesn't this fish get handled" honestly instead of padding out fake handling tips. Grade A.
- Health issues guide (04): Would finish and act on it. Five conditions, clear signs, clear treatment, and the guppy-specific salt warning is a real caveat, not boilerplate. Grade A.
- Tank setup guide (05): Would finish and act on it. Every number from the hub is explained here with the reasoning behind it. Grade A.
- Feeding guide (06): Would finish. Honest about disagreement in adult feeding frequency instead of forcing a false precision, and the "why guppies stop eating" list is genuinely diagnostic. Grade A.
- Enrichment guide (07): Would finish, and it's the standout page. It's not decor tips, it's a welfare argument built on a specific research finding (harassed females produce smaller daughters and less successful sons even though total fry count looks fine). Grade A.

None of the eight felt thin or padded. This is a tight set.

**2. Hub and encyclopedia**
Both earn their place. The hub is pure numbers-and-links, no prose duplication of the deep dives; the encyclopedia carries natural history (origin, the naming mix-up, the Endler experiment) that appears nowhere else. Neither page disagrees with a deep dive on any number I could find. Tank size, temperature, watts per gallon, sex ratio, feeding frequency, and lifespan all match word for word or close to it between the hub and the linked article, e.g., hub: "76 to 78°F as the target inside 72 to 82°F" vs. tank setup: "comfortable in the 72 to 82°F range, with 76 to 78°F as a solid target."

**3. Could I set this animal up from this set alone?**
Yes, mostly. Tank, water, filter, feeding, sexing ratio, and disease recognition are all here with numbers I could act on immediately. Two real gaps: nothing explains how to actually sex a guppy visually beyond "males are smaller and colorful, females bigger and plainer" (no mention of the gonopodium, the anatomical marker every livebearer guide usually leads with), and nothing walks through what to actually do once fry appear, no birthing tank setup, no "how to net a fry," no timeline for when fry can join the main tank. The population-plan article raises the fry question and the enrichment article raises fry survival via cover, but neither one closes the loop into a fry-rearing how-to. The feeding guide answers what to feed fry once they exist, so that part connects, it's the physical handling of a birth and the newborns that's missing.

**4. Overlap and conflict across deep dives**
Overlap: the "up to 8 broods from stored sperm over 5 to 10 months" fact appears in the cost guide and the handling guide, worded almost identically both times, that's repetition but not padding since each article needs it to make its own point. The 1-male-to-2-to-3-female ratio is repeated across the hub, handling guide, feeding guide, and enrichment guide; consistent every time, never contradicted. Betta and goldfish being unsuitable tankmates is repeated in the handling guide and tank setup guide with the same reasoning both times. I did not find a single numeric or instructional conflict between any two deep dives. Tank size, temperature, watts, pH, feeding frequency, and quarantine length all match everywhere they're stated.

**5. Trust**
Nothing here reads like it was written by someone who has never owned a fish. The line that most convinced me a real keeper wrote this: "Underneath all that fancy coloring, a $200 Dragon guppy and a $3 wild-type guppy are the same species with the same basic needs. The twist is that the fancier, more expensive strains tend to be somewhat less resilient than plain wild-type guppies, so you're often paying a premium for looks, not hardiness." That's a specific, slightly against-the-sale observation nobody copies from a pet store website. The enrichment article's honesty that "the harassed females still produced offspring, so a keeper counting fry would see nothing wrong" is the same kind of tell, someone who has actually watched a tank and knows fry count is a bad welfare proxy.

**6. Grade: A-.** Reason: internally consistent, no filler, and the enrichment page turns a decor decision into an actual welfare argument backed by named research, which is rare for a species this commonly kept. Two changes I'd make first: add a short, explicit visual-sexing explainer (the gonopodium, not just size and color) since a beginner buying "assorted guppies" needs to know what they're looking at before the ratio advice matters, and add a fry-rearing section (birthing box or dense-cover tank, when to separate, when fry rejoin adults) since the set raises the fry question three separate times without ever showing what to physically do when it happens.

**Not covered anywhere**
- How to visually sex a guppy (the actual anatomical marker, not just "smaller and colorful"): no answer, not even in the Health and More list.
- What to physically do once fry appear (birthing box or breeder trap setup, when to move fry out, when they can rejoin adults): no answer anywhere in this set or the Health and More list.
