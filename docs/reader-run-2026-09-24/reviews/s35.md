## milk-snake

1. Page by page
- 00 Hub: A. Numbers link to sources, and the emergency card correctly separates "always see a vet" (respiratory, scale rot) from routine concerns.
- 01 Encyclopedia: A-. The AMNH-sourced disruptive-coloration counter-theory to simple mimicry is a real addition, not filler.
- 02 Cost guide: A. Subspecies-tiered pricing (Pueblan vs Sinaloan/Honduran vs morphs) is specific rather than a single range.
- 03 Handling guide: A. Debunks the "milk" name plainly, and the coral snake rhyme answer is refreshingly honest about the rhyme's real limits instead of just repeating it.
- 04 Health issues: A. Names the one variable (moisture) behind three separate conditions, which is the kind of synthesis a generic care sheet skips.
- 05 Tank setup: A. Specific numbers throughout, and openly flags a real disagreement in the hobby (halogen vs. ceramic heat) rather than hiding it.
- 06 Feeding guide: A. The three-tier age table (hatchling/juvenile/adult) is more granular than the hub, with a clear "why hatchlings eat snakes" explanation for the no-cohabitation rule.
- 07 Enrichment guide: A. States plainly that there is no research on this species and the guide is borrowing from corn snake studies, then names the borrow every time it uses it. Rare honesty for this format.
- 09 vs Corn Snake: A-. Correctly frames the choice as aesthetic/identification rather than difficulty, doesn't manufacture a fake difference.

2. Hub and encyclopedia
Both earn their place. The hub is action-first with sourced numbers; the encyclopedia's disruptive-coloration research note doesn't appear anywhere else in the set. No numeric disagreement: enclosure size, humidity, temperature, and lifespan all match between hub and deep dives.

3. Could I actually keep this animal
Yes. Enclosure, heat, humidity, substrate, feeding sizing, and the no-cohabitation rule are all specific and consistent. One real gap the hub creates and a deep dive answers without cross-referencing: the hub's feeding row only mentions "juveniles" and "adults," but the feeding guide has a third tier, hatchlings, fed every 5 to 7 days on pinkies specifically, which the hub skips entirely. Anyone starting with a hatchling and reading only the hub would use the juvenile 7-to-10-day interval, which is close but not what the feeding guide itself recommends for that age.

4. Overlap and conflict across deep dives
Read twice: the no-cohabitation/cannibalism rule appears in the hub, the handling guide, the tank setup guide, and the feeding guide, each with slightly different supporting detail (temperament framing, setup framing, prey-species framing), which is repetition in service of a genuinely important rule rather than padding.
No numeric conflicts. The one flagged disagreement is the tank setup guide's own admission that "not every care sheet agrees on how to heat a milk snake," some recommend ceramic heat emitters, this article recommends halogen, presented as an acknowledged industry split rather than something the guide pretends is settled.

5. Trust
This set does more to earn trust than most: the enrichment guide states outright that no study exists on this species and cites the corn snake research it's borrowing from by name every time, rather than presenting borrowed evidence as established fact. The sentence that most reads like an actual keeper wrote it: "A snake that seems to be getting fat gets a longer interval or a smaller feeder, not a skipped meal here and there." That's a specific, slightly contrarian piece of practical judgment, not a generic feeding tip.

6. Grade: A. Reason: consistent numbers, an honestly-flagged hobby disagreement instead of false certainty, and an enrichment guide that says plainly when it has no direct evidence rather than inventing some.
Two changes: add the hatchling feeding interval (5 to 7 days) to the hub's first-week feeding row so it isn't the only deep dive detail missing from the router page. Cross-link the tank setup guide's heating fun fact to whatever decides between halogen and ceramic for a given setup, since right now it names the disagreement but doesn't tell a reader how to choose.
