**2. Hub and encyclopedia: earn their place?**
Yes. The encyclopedia stays in natural history and legal history, no care overlap with the hub at all. The hub is a router with the same numbers as its linked articles: "4x2x2 ft, about 120 gallons" (hub) matches "4x2x2 ft (roughly 120 gallons) is the adult minimum" (tank setup guide). Basking temps (95-110°F adult, 105-115°F juvenile), humidity (30-40%), and the diet flip (juveniles ~80% insect, adults ~80% greens) all match word for word between hub and source article. No disagreement found between hub and any deep dive.

**3. Could I actually keep this animal after reading the whole set?**
Yes, more thoroughly than most care sets manage. Setup, diet by age, handling, illness red flags, brumation, egg-laying, and weight tracking are all covered with real numbers and real decision points (not just "consult a vet"). The set is unusually good at cross-linking: the feeding guide points to brumation for appetite loss, brumation points to the growth guide for weigh-ins, the eggs guide points to the tank setup guide for temperature and the health guide for calcium. One place a question surfaces without its answer following it: the cost guide's own upfront-setup table lists "Initial vet exam and fecal test, $120-$245" as a row inside the "$400 to $800" total, then two paragraphs later says "the first vet exam and fecal test sit on top of that" - a reader adding up the table gets a different number than the text tells them to expect.

**4. Overlap and conflict across the deep dives**
Overlap is intentional and mostly non-repetitive: cage size and UVB numbers appear in the hub, cost guide, tank setup guide, and shopping list, but each one adds a different layer (dollars, brand picks, or the biology). The feeding guide and safe foods guide split the "what to feed" question cleanly, schedule and physiology in one, item-by-item tiers in the other, with no contradiction.
One real, if minor, factual tension the articles disclose themselves: the feeding guide states "one veterinary teaching hospital treats refusing food for 1 to 2 or more days as a reason to seek care in adults, while a separate DVM-reviewed source says healthy adults can physiologically fast 1 to 2 weeks." That is not a hidden conflict, the article names both sources and tells the reader how to hold both at once, which is good practice rather than sloppiness.
The one unflagged conflict is the cost guide's internal contradiction above (table includes the vet exam in the $400-800 total; body text excludes it).

**5. Trust**
The set names its sources by name (VCA, Merck Veterinary Manual, NC State's veterinary hospital) and dates its price checks ("Prices last checked September 2026 at Josh's Frogs and PetSmart"). It also does something rare: it flags disagreement between real sources on the fasting-tolerance question rather than smoothing it into one false number, which is the strongest trust signal in the set. The most convincing line, and the one that reads like an actual keeper rather than a content mill, is in the eggs guide: "A gravid female's belly can feel firm and evenly lumpy, almost like a bag of marbles rolling under the skin... though you should still never press hard to check," paired with the two named worked examples (Dragon A, Dragon B) that follow.

**6. Grade: A.** Deep, cross-linked, numerically consistent across fourteen pages, and honest about the one place its sources disagree. It loses a notch only for the cost guide's table-versus-text contradiction on the initial vet exam. First two changes: fix that one line in the cost guide so the vet exam is either in the $400-800 total or explicitly on top of it, not both, and add one sentence somewhere tying the "wait 7-14 days before handling" rule to the "3-6 month quarantine" window so a new owner knows which clock governs the first months.

**7. Not covered anywhere**
