## tarantula

1. Page by page
- 00 Hub: A. Correctly leads with the fall risk, and the emergency card separates dehydration/molt/injury from what's actually just premolt fasting.
- 01 Encyclopedia: A-. The CITES timeline (Mexican redknee in 1985, all fifteen Poecilotheria in 2019, with the import numbers behind that second listing) is a genuinely researched addition.
- 02 Cost guide: A. Honest about the real cost being time, not money, and flags plainly that there is no tarantula vet insurance.
- 03 Handling guide: A. States the fall risk and the urticating-hair eye risk without softening either, and correctly separates New World from Old World defenses.
- 04 Health issues: A. Clear on what's preventable versus what isn't (Dyskinetic Syndrome, oral nematodes), and doesn't oversell treatment options that don't really exist for this species.
- 05 Tank setup: A. States the humidity disagreement between sources outright rather than picking one number and presenting it as settled.
- 06 Feeding guide: A. The life-stage table plus the mature-male die-off explanation is specific and consistent with the rest of the set.
- 07 Enrichment guide: A. The strongest page in this entire batch of four species. Reports a real study's unflattering result (no enclosure preference shown) alongside the flattering ones, and says outright that a guide quoting only the good parts "is not telling you the truth."
- 09 vs Emperor Scorpion: A-. Clean, specific comparison, and doesn't flatten the sexed lifespan gap into a single number.

2. Hub and encyclopedia
Both earn their place. The encyclopedia's CITES history doesn't appear anywhere else. No real numeric disagreement between hub and encyclopedia, both cite the 15-30 year range for females and much shorter male lifespans.

3. Could I actually keep this animal
Yes, and unusually well. Enclosure shape, substrate, lid type, feeding by life stage, and the dehydration-versus-premolt distinction are all specific and consistent. The set is honest about what's genuinely unresolved (humidity range, juvenile feeding interval) instead of pretending certainty it doesn't have. One thing the hub raises and only the enrichment page really answers: the hub tells you not to worry about a spider "sitting still," but doesn't say why that's true until the enrichment guide explains that inactivity was nearly the entire observed behavior in the one controlled study that exists, in both a plain and an enriched enclosure. A reader who stops at the hub gets the reassurance without the actual evidence for it.

4. Overlap and conflict across deep dives
Read twice: the dehydration "death curl" description and the "check water first when a tarantula stops eating" advice appear near-verbatim in both the health guide and the feeding guide, which is fine since it's the single most important emergency sign in the set.
One real numeric conflict: the cost guide and hub both give male lifespan as "4 to 7 years," while the vs. Emperor Scorpion comparison table gives it as "3 to 6 years, often less." These are close but not the same range, and nothing in the set explains the gap (species difference, source difference, or just inconsistent rounding). A second soft inconsistency: the hub's headline fun fact uses a female Mexican red knee living 25 to 30 years, while the cost guide's actual budgeting section uses a female Chilean rose hair at 15 to 20 years, two different species used for two different "the number to remember" claims without ever saying they're different animals.

5. Trust
This is the most convincing set of the four reviewed. The enrichment guide's handling of its own source, a 2024 PLoS ONE study, is the clearest example: it reports that the spiders showed no preference between enclosures when given the choice, calls that "the result that ought to make everyone cautious," and states plainly that "a guide that quotes only the flattering ones is not telling you the truth." That is not something a content mill writes. Runner-up: the feeding guide's note that fireflies are avoided industry-wide even though "the strongest documented toxicity data is actually for reptiles rather than tarantulas directly," which is the kind of caveat someone only adds if they actually checked the primary claim rather than repeating it.

6. Grade: A. Reason: a real study reported with its inconvenient result intact, open acknowledgment of unresolved disagreements (humidity, juvenile feeding interval) instead of false precision, and consistently specific, actionable numbers everywhere else.
Two changes: reconcile the male lifespan figure (4-7 vs 3-6 years) between the cost guide and the comparison guide, or state that they're citing different species. Add one line to the hub's "don't worry about stillness" claim pointing to the enrichment guide's actual evidence, instead of asserting it without the source that makes it credible.

7. Not covered anywhere
- What actually happens during a rehousing, beyond being told it exists as a linked topic (the "catch-cup-and-card method" and urticating-hair first aid are named only as a title, not explained in any file here). None within this set.
- Whether a subadult or adult that has never fasted before suddenly refusing food, with no darkening abdomen, should be treated differently than classic premolt behavior. None.
- How to tell a genuinely stuck molt apart from a slow-but-normal one in the first few hours, before it's clearly gone wrong. None.
