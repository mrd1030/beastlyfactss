## mourning-gecko

**1. Page by page**
- Hub: A. Everything a first week needs, links point to the right places, nothing padded.
- Encyclopedia: A. The parthenogenesis history (Cuellar and Kluge 1972, the multiple hybrid lineages) is genuinely interesting, not filler natural history.
- Cost guide: A. Real price ranges, a dated price check, a debunk-shaped caveat on the Type A to D labels.
- Handling guide: A. Answers the actual question (no) and explains why with scale, not vibes.
- Health issues guide: A. Short, triaged, tells you what is an emergency and what is not.
- Tank setup guide: A. Specific numbers throughout, and correctly puts escape-proofing above everything else.
- Feeding guide: A. The "why they stop eating" list is the kind of thing you actually search for at 11pm.
- Enrichment guide: A-. Honest that there is no species-specific research, and says so instead of dressing up the tokay study as proof.

**2. Hub and encyclopedia**
Both earn their place. The hub is a router, not a rewrite: it distills numbers and links out rather than re-arguing them, and I did not catch it disagreeing with a deep dive. Setup cost ($200 to $450), monthly upkeep ($10 to $25), vet exam ($35 to $105), lifespan ("up to 10 years, some reports of 15") all match word for word between the hub and the cost guide. The encyclopedia adds history the hub does not touch and does not repeat any deep dive.

**3. The set as a whole**
Yes, I could buy, set up, feed, and keep this animal healthy from this set alone: enclosure size and escape-proofing, humidity and misting rhythm, powdered diet and insect rotation, calcium logic, and the emergency signs are all here and consistent with each other. Two real gaps. First, nobody says whether a mourning gecko needs a standing water dish. Hatchling hydration is covered ("misting 1 to 2 times daily"), but adults' drinking source is never stated outright, and it is missing from the hub's buy list too. Second, the hub and tank setup guide both say "adults may eat hatchlings, so separate young as they appear," but nothing in the set says where a separated hatchling goes, what it needs differently, or when it can rejoin the colony. That is a real logistics question the set raises and never answers. A smaller unstated link: the feeding guide's "swollen abdomen paired with straining" as the tell for egg-binding lives in the feeding article's "When to Worry" section, while the health issues guide covers egg-binding without that specific distinguishing detail, so the two pages that most need to be read together on this exact symptom do not point at each other.

**4. Overlap and conflict across the deep dives**
Heavy overlap, no numeric conflicts I could find. The calcium-for-a-lone-female point appears nearly verbatim in the cost guide FAQ, the feeding guide FAQ, and the hub FAQ: "a lone pet with no companion at all still lays a pair of eggs roughly every 4 to 6 weeks, year-round." The escape-proofing instruction ("seal every gap... with aquarium-safe putty") is repeated close to word for word in the hub, the handling guide, and the tank setup guide. None of it contradicts itself, which given how much repetition there is, is itself worth noting.

**5. Trust**
The line that most convinced me a real keeper wrote this is the feeding guide's admission that "at least one independent care sheet is more cautious, saying it's hard to say for certain [powdered diet is] adequate long-term on its own, especially for growing juveniles." A site selling confidence would not include a hedge like that. The behavioral detail in the enrichment guide, "arching, nipping, and charging when females sort out the pecking order," also reads like observation rather than paraphrase.

**6. Grade: A-.** Reason: consistent numbers, honest hedging, and real triage, let down only by the missing water-source answer and no guidance on managing hatchlings once separated. First change: state plainly whether adults need a water dish or misting alone covers it. Second change: add a short section on where separated hatchlings live and when they rejoin the colony.

**7. Not covered anywhere**
- Whether adult mourning geckos need a standing water dish, or misting is genuinely sufficient.
- Where a separated hatchling should be housed and when it can safely rejoin the colony.

## emperor-scorpion

**1. Page by page**
- Hub: A. Legal status is the first thing listed before husbandry, which is the right order for an animal that is banned in three jurisdictions.
- Encyclopedia: A. The CITES quota numbers (Benin's 42,781 ranched specimens for 2001) are specific enough to be real, not generic.
- Cost guide: A. Actively debunks the $800 price claim instead of repeating it, which most cost guides would not bother doing.
- Handling guide: A. States plainly that "docile" means lower risk, not risk-free, and does not oversell handleability.
- Health issues guide: A. Cites an actual 2024 paper (Guo et al., in *Insects*) and is honest that it is a different species used for supporting biology, not a direct study.
- Tank setup guide: A. Substrate depth and humidity are correctly framed as the two things that matter, ahead of temperature.
- Feeding guide: A. The fasting-is-normal framing and the abdomen-width rule of thumb are genuinely useful, not padding.
- Enrichment guide: A-. States outright that there is no *Pandinus imperator* enrichment research, then borrows a tarantula study carefully, flagging it as "not a close" one.

**2. Hub and encyclopedia**
Both earn their place rather than repeating the deep dives. The hub's numbers, humidity (70 to 90%, sweet spot 75 to 80%), temperature (75 to 85°F ambient), substrate depth (5 to 6 inches minimum), vet cost ($80 to $200-plus), and lifespan (5 to 8 years, full range 4 to 9), match the cost, health, and tank setup guides exactly. I found no disagreement between the hub and any deep dive. The encyclopedia's ranching and CITES history is not covered anywhere else in the set.

**3. The set as a whole**
Yes, this set gets you to a working setup, a feeding routine, and a working list of health emergencies. Two gaps worth naming. First, molt frequency by life stage is never addressed: how many times a juvenile molts before adulthood, or how often an adult molts, so a keeper has no way to anticipate the vulnerable window the health and enrichment guides both warn about. Second, a stuck molt is repeatedly flagged as "often fatal without experienced help," but nothing tells a keeper what that help looks like or what, if anything, can be tried at home before it is too late. One unstated cross-reference: the tank setup and enrichment guides both discuss group housing and say "start with animals raised together" and "be prepared to separate," but the actual answer to how long to quarantine a newly bought scorpion before introducing it to an existing group sits in the separate Invertebrate Housekeeping guide (in the Health and More list), which none of the scorpion-specific articles point to directly.

**4. Overlap and conflict across the deep dives**
No numeric conflicts. Heavy, near-verbatim repetition. The Guo et al. 2024 quote about a freshly molted exoskeleton being "too soft to provide protection against predators and conspecifics" appears in the health issues guide, the tank setup guide, and the enrichment guide. The UV fluorescence fun fact appears in the hub, the encyclopedia, the tank setup guide, and the enrichment guide, four times over. "The standard failure is one good hide and three scorpions" and its close variants show up in the hub, tank setup guide, and enrichment guide. All consistent with each other, just repeated a lot.

**5. Trust**
The line that most convinced me a real keeper wrote this: "This species' tolerance for cage mates is genuinely unusual among predatory arthropods, most of which will eventually kill each other when housed together." That is a specific, falsifiable claim about the broader hobby, not a generic reassurance. The cost guide naming and rejecting the $800 price rumor is the second strongest trust signal in the set.

**6. Grade: A-.** Reason: internally consistent, well triaged, and honest about the limits of the research, held back by the missing molt-frequency information and no concrete guidance for a stuck molt beyond "get experienced help." First change: add expected molt frequency by age so a keeper can anticipate the risk window. Second change: link the group-housing sections to the quarantine timeline for new arrivals instead of leaving that answer in a different guide with no pointer.

**7. Not covered anywhere**
- How many times a scorpion molts as a juvenile, or how often an adult molts.
- What a keeper can actually try, if anything, when a molt looks stuck, beyond "seek experienced help."
