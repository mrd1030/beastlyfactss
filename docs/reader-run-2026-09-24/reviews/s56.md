## leopard-gecko

**1. Page by page**

- 00 Care guide hub: Finish it. Dense router with real numbers and an honest FAQ on the humidity mistake. A
- 01 Encyclopedia: Finish it, short. The named-breeder morph history (Tremper, Bell, Mack) and "essentially every gecko sold is captive-bred" line are genuinely informative. A-
- 02 Cost guide: Finish it. Breaks price by source (pet store vs. breeder vs. designer morph) rather than one flat range. A
- 03 Handling guide: Finish it. The tail-autotomy section is the best-written page in the set, specific and consequential. A
- 04 Health issues guide: Finish it. Straightforward on cryptosporidiosis without either downplaying or sensationalizing it. A-
- 05 Tank setup guide: Finish it. Clear on the humidity trap (whole-tank vs. humid hide) that most guides get wrong. A
- 06 Feeding guide: Finish it. Explicitly says when sources disagree rather than picking one number and hiding the rest. A
- 07 Enrichment guide: Finish it. Cites two named studies with real findings (thermal and feeding enrichment beat mirrors). A
- 09 Temperature guide: Finish it. The most transparent page in the set about competing source numbers, and says which one it picked and why. A
- 10 vs. crested gecko guide: Finish it. Genuinely useful comparison, not just an excuse to plug two product pages. A-

**2. Hub and encyclopedia**

Both earn their place. The hub's numbers (enclosure size, temperatures, feeding schedule, budget) track the deep dives closely, including carrying forward hedges like "sources don't agree on one exact day count" for feeding. The encyclopedia's only overlap is size and difficulty; the captive-bred-supply history and named early breeders are unique to it.

I found no disagreement between the hub and any deep dive. Where a deep dive itself flags disputed numbers (temperature, feeding frequency), the hub's summary reflects the same uncertainty rather than flattening it into a single false-precision figure.

**3. The set as a whole**

Yes, thoroughly. Ten pages between them cover buying, housing, heating, feeding, handling, illness, and even a competitor species comparison, with specific numbers at every step and repeated, unusually candid admissions of where the underlying sources disagree. This is the strongest-sourced set of the four I reviewed today.

What's thinner: nothing here explains what a fecal test at the first vet visit actually screens for beyond cryptosporidiosis, and no page details how to safely introduce a dropped-tail gecko back to a shared space if one is ever housed with another (a minor gap, since the tank setup guide says to house them alone anyway, so it may not matter in practice). One thing raised on one page and only resolved by careful cross-reading: the tank setup guide says "well-ventilated... with an escape-proof screen top" without stating bar spacing or gap tolerance, a detail the corn snake set gives explicitly for its own species but this set never states for the leopard gecko.

**4. Overlap and conflict across the deep dives**

The three-hide system, the 30-40%/70-80% humidity split, and the belly-heat-on-a-thermostat rule are repeated near-verbatim across the hub, tank setup guide, enrichment guide, and temperature guide. That's warranted repetition for the single most important setup fact, not padding, since each version adds something (the enrichment guide frames it as behavioral choice, the temperature guide gives the actual gradient numbers).

No numeric conflict across the deep dives. If anything this set is unusual for actively surfacing its own disagreements rather than hiding them: the temperature guide states outright, "The sources do not all agree with each other, let alone with this table," and names three different sources with three different night-temperature floors (LafeberVet's 70s, Zen Habitats' 60°F, Merck's 5°C-below-daytime rule) before picking 65°F and saying why.

**5. Trust**

The willingness to say "sources don't agree" and then explain which figure was chosen and on what basis, repeated across the temperature guide and the feeding guide, is the single strongest trust signal across all four species I reviewed. The sentence that most convinced me a real, careful keeper wrote this: "A gecko kept a few degrees too cool goes off food long before it looks sick. Getting the numbers right is the whole job." That's a specific, correct, non-generic insight about a real failure mode, not a filler sentence.

Nothing in this set gave me reason to doubt it.

**6. Grade: A.** The best-sourced and most self-aware set of the four reviewed today, transparent about disagreement rather than papering over it, with specific and consequential advice (tail autotomy, the humidity trap) throughout. First two changes: state bar spacing or gap tolerance for the enclosure's screen top the way the corn snake set does for its cage, and add a line explaining what the initial fecal test actually screens for beyond cryptosporidiosis.

- What the initial fecal test screens for beyond cryptosporidiosis: None found.
