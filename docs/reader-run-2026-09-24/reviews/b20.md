2. Articles graded C or lower
None in this bundle.

3. Title-versus-content gaps
- Reptile Heating and Thermostats: promises to prevent burns, and does, but never states a concrete maximum safe basking-surface temperature number a keeper could check against; it's all method (thermometer, probe placement) and no target figure.
- Reptile Quarantine: the FAQ says lizards are not established to get inclusion body disease, but the body text's severity table lists IBD-linked neurological signs under a general "reptile" watch-list without repeating that lizard caveat there, so a lizard owner skimming the table alone could misread it as applying to their animal too.

4. Overlap and conflict
This is a tightly cross-referenced set and it holds together well: the emergency plan's cold-floor table, the heating guide's POTZ discussion, and the shedding guide's humidity notes all point at each other without contradicting each other. The quarantine article and the salmonella article both cover new-animal risk but from different angles (parasites/IBD vs. bacterial shedding) and don't overlap in claims. One near-duplication worth flagging: the shedding guide and the stool/urates guide both mention retained shed and dehydration signs, but consistently, not in conflict.

5. Trust
The heating and thermostats article is the most convincing piece because it corrects its own earlier position mid-article: "For an overhead source it is not settled, and this guide previously stated it more firmly than the evidence supports... Thermostat manufacturers genuinely disagree." That kind of visible self-correction is rare and reads as more trustworthy than a confident wrong answer would. Most convincing single sentence, from the same article: "A thermostat with a correctly set target temperature can still produce a burn if its probe has slipped off the basking surface onto bare glass or substrate, since the display keeps showing an accurate reading for the wrong spot." Nothing in the bundle triggered doubt; claims are consistently attributed to Merck, VCA, or named studies rather than asserted flatly.

6. Bundle grade: A. Reason: six technically dense articles that cross-reference cleanly, cite named veterinary sources throughout, and are honest about the limits of the advice (e.g., admitting probe placement for overhead bulbs isn't settled science).
Two changes first: add a concrete target basking-surface temperature ceiling to the heating guide so "verify it" has something to verify against, and repeat the lizard/IBD caveat directly in the quarantine article's symptom table, not just its FAQ.
