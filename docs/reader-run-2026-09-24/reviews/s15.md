## stick-insect

**1. Page by page**
- 00 Hub: Finish it, act on it immediately (permit warning, enclosure math, buy list). A.
- 01 Encyclopedia: Finish it, mostly natural history you cannot act on but genuinely interesting (Lord Howe Island rediscovery). B (thin, but earns it with real content).
- 02 Cost guide: Finish it, act on the legal section before buying anything. A.
- 03 Handling guide: Finish it, act on body-not-leg grip and the egg disposal choice. A.
- 04 Health guide: Finish it, act on height and humidity as prevention. B (thin, but there genuinely isn't much more to say).
- 05 Tank setup: Finish it, act on the whole page. A.
- 06 Feeding guide: Finish it, act on the plant table and the covered jar. A.
- 07 Enrichment guide: Finish it, honest rather than padded about having almost nothing to add. A-.

**2. Hub and encyclopedia**
The hub earns its place: every first-week number links out and nothing on it duplicates a deep dive's reasoning, it just states the number. The encyclopedia earns its place too, since its material (parthenogenesis history, Carausius morosus, the Ball's Pyramid rediscovery) appears nowhere else in the set. No disagreement found: hub and cost guide both give "$77 to $98" and the same lifespan breakdown (4 to 10 months nymph, 5 to 12 adult, females near 18 months) word for word. The encyclopedia's "Wild Lifespan: 6 months-2 years" sits beside the hub's captivity numbers without being flagged as a different measurement, which could confuse a reader skimming both pages in one sitting.

**3. The set as a whole**
Yes, you could set up, buy, feed, and keep this animal healthy from these seven pages alone. What's missing: nothing here tells you how to identify which species you actually have, and nearly every care detail (food plant, humidity, whether it needs a water bowl) is stated as "species-specific" or "the species sheet says which." The feeding guide's line about Haaniella needing "an open bowl of water low in the enclosure, and the species sheet says which" assumes a document that isn't part of this set. A buyer following only these pages has no way to confirm species identity before applying any of the species-specific advice.

**4. Overlap and conflict across deep dives**
Read twice, or more: the three-times-body-length height rule appears in the cost guide, health guide, tank setup, and enrichment guide, always identical. The covered water jar for cut foliage appears in tank setup, feeding, health, and enrichment guides, always with the same warning. Leg autotomy (partial regrowth in nymphs, none in adults) appears in the handling and health guides and matches exactly. No numeric or instructional conflicts turned up anywhere in this set.

**5. Trust**
The citations read as real and specific rather than decorative: Skelhorn in Current Biology (2018) on thanatosis, a named 2024 BMC Zoology anatomical survey, the Amateur Entomologists' Society caresheet, Bugs In Cyberspace's own hatch-rate numbers reported as a range rather than a single tidy figure. Nothing here reads as invented. The sentence that most convinced me a real keeper wrote this: "a nymph drowned in the food jar is the routine avoidable loss in this hobby." It is specific, unglamorous, and repeated with the same phrasing across multiple pages, which is what an experienced keeper's pet peeve sounds like.

**6. Grade**
A-. Consistent numbers, honest about the limits of the evidence (the enrichment guide flatly says there is no phasmid research), and every claim ties back to something actionable. Two changes: add a short species-identification page or table, since so much advice branches on species and nothing here helps a buyer tell them apart; and give the legal section a state-by-state table instead of the current one paragraph, since "check current USDA rules for your own situation" is the one place the set punts.

**7. Not covered anywhere**
- How to identify which stick insect species you actually have. None.
- Where to legally source a species native to your own state. None.
- What a vet visit would even look like if one were ever attempted. None.

## california-kingsnake

**1. Page by page**
- 00 Hub: Finish it, act on the whole thing, especially the solo-housing rule. A.
- 01 Encyclopedia: Finish it, short but genuinely informative (breeding history, wild lifespan data). B.
- 02 Cost guide: Finish it, act on the budget and the solo-housing warning. A.
- 03 Handling guide: Finish it, act on the fear-signal table and the timing rules. A.
- 04 Health guide: Finish it, act on the husbandry-first framing. A.
- 05 Tank setup: Finish it, act on the two security rules. A.
- 06 Feeding guide: Finish it, act on prey sizing and the weigh-monthly habit. A.
- 07 Enrichment guide: Finish it, honestly labeled as borrowed evidence rather than padded as original research. A-.
Note: the hub and every deep dive point to "Is It Legal to Own a California Kingsnake? State-by-State Guide," but that file was not part of this set, so it cannot be reviewed here.

**2. Hub and encyclopedia**
Both earn their place. The hub's numbers match the deep dives closely: lifespan "20 years or more" matches the cost guide exactly, and the 48x24x24 adult enclosure matches tank setup exactly. One soft disagreement: the hub says "Hatchlings and juveniles every 5 to 7 days," while the feeding guide's own table splits them, "Hatchling | Every 5 to 6 days" against "Juvenile | Every 5 to 7 days." Not a real contradiction, but the hub rounds two different numbers into one. The encyclopedia adds a second lifespan figure worth flagging beside the hub's: "5.5 years on average in the wild... up to 33.3 years in captivity" against the hub and cost guide's flatter "20 years or more." The two aren't contradictory, but a reader who sees "33.3" on one page and "20 or more" on another might reasonably wonder which is the real ceiling.

**3. The set as a whole**
Yes, you could set up, feed, handle, and keep this snake healthy from these pages alone. What's missing: there is no enclosure guidance for the in-between juvenile stage, the set jumps from "a 20-gallon tank works for a hatchling" straight to the 48x24x24 adult setup with nothing about sizing up gradually. Sourcing (breeder versus pet store, and how to check a snake is a true captive-bred kingsnake rather than an escape-risk hybrid) is also absent. The feeding guide points to "the reptile stool and urates guide" for reading droppings, and that guide is in the Health and More list, so that question is genuinely answered elsewhere in the site, just not in this set.

**4. Overlap and conflict across deep dives**
Read many times over: the ophiophagous, solo-housing rule appears near-verbatim in the hub, encyclopedia, cost guide, handling guide, tank setup, feeding guide, and enrichment guide, seven times in seven pages. Given the stakes (a cage-mate gets eaten), the repetition is defensible, but it is still the same paragraph rewritten seven ways. The corn snake comparison shows up in nearly every deep dive too, which starts to feel like a device more than a fact worth repeating each time. The 48-hour post-feeding handling wait is consistent between the handling and feeding guides. No numeric conflicts turned up beyond the lifespan framing above.

**5. Trust**
The handling guide's source table, crediting PetMD, the RSPCA, and Tree of Life Exotics by name for specific numbers, reads as carefully checked rather than invented. The enrichment guide's own line, "do not present corn snake findings as kingsnake findings, which is the labeling this guide exists to demonstrate," is an unusually self-aware admission that built real trust. The sentence that most convinced me a keeper wrote this: "The species makes feeding easy. The keeper's only job is not to make it generous." It's opinionated in exactly the way a person with years of feeding schedules behind them would be.

**6. Grade**
A-. Thorough, well-sourced, and consistent on every number that matters. Two changes: cut the corn-snake comparison down to one or two mentions instead of nearly every article, and add a mid-growth enclosure step between the 20-gallon hatchling tank and the 48x24x24 adult one.

**7. Not covered anywhere**
- How to size an enclosure for the juvenile stage between hatchling and full adult. None.
- How to vet a breeder or verify a snake is genuinely captive-bred. None.
