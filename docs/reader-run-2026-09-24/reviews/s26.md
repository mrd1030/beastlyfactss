## axolotl

**1. Page by page**

- Hub: Finish it, it's the fastest way to the numbers you need and every row links out correctly. Grade A.
- Encyclopedia: Finish it, gives origin, wild lifespan gap, and the genuinely interesting Dumeril breeding history. Grade A.
- Cost guide: Finish it, tables plus a dated price check make it usable for actual budgeting. Grade A-.
- Handling guide: Finish it, short and answers the one question (can I touch it) clearly. Grade A-.
- Health issues guide: Finish it, ties every ailment back to temperature with real thresholds and a vet trigger list. Grade A-.
- Tank setup guide: Finish it, the two non-negotiables (cold, no gravel) are stated up front and never buried. Grade A-.
- Feeding guide: Finish it, the by-life-stage schedule and the foods-to-avoid list are both genuinely actionable. Grade A.
- Enrichment guide: Finish it, specific enough (sand saucer, rotate one item for 5 to 7 days) to actually do. Grade A-.

No page here felt thin or padded. Each fun fact box adds a new detail rather than restating the section above it.

**2. Hub and encyclopedia: earn their place?**

Yes, cleanly split. The encyclopedia stays in natural history (Lake Xochimilco, wild diet, the 1864 Dumeril shipment, wild lifespan of 5 to 6 years) and never repeats care instructions. The hub is pure care data: first-week numbers, an emergency card, a buy list. Neither reads like a rewritten deep dive.

No disagreement found between hub and deep dives. Every number I checked matches word for word: hub says "Between 60 and 68°F. Above 72°F starts to cause chronic stress, and it can become fatal in the mid-70s," and the tank setup guide says the identical thing. The hub's budget line ("$30 to $100 for the axolotl itself... $200 to $500 for a basic setup or $450 to $900 with a chiller, and $15 to $45 a month") matches the cost guide's numbers exactly, table and all. That level of consistency across seven pages is unusual and reads as intentional, not accidental.

**3. The set as a whole**

Yes, you could set this animal up, feed it, and catch the health emergencies, with a few gaps. Missing: how to pick a healthy axolotl at the point of sale (the cost guide prices morphs like "mosaic" and "GFP" but never explains what those look like or why they cost more). Also missing: any chiller sizing guidance, wattage or capacity against tank size, versus just naming one premium model.

A question raised without its answer flagged: the cost guide says "our introduction to the axolotl and its color varieties explains what those morphs actually are," so that gap is at least pointed at, just not inside this set. The handling guide's drip-matching acclimation step correctly points to the tank setup guide for target numbers. The health guide correctly points to the feeding guide for overfeeding. Cross-linking discipline here is genuinely good, better than most sets, which is why I only found one real unflagged gap: nothing in this set says what a healthy axolotl looks like when you're standing at the store tank.

**4. Overlap and conflict across the deep dives**

Read twice: the no-gravel/impaction warning appears in full in the tank setup guide, the feeding guide, the health guide, and the enrichment guide. Each states it slightly differently and each is relevant to that page's topic, so it's not filler exactly, but by the fourth repetition ("no gravel, no coarse sand, no small loose stones anywhere in the tank") it stops adding anything new.

One soft inconsistency: the feeding guide defines an adult as "12-plus months, over about 7.5 inches," while the hub and encyclopedia both give adult size as "9 to 12 inches." Those aren't contradictory (7.5 inches could be the low threshold for adult status, 9 to 12 the typical range), but the guide never says so, and a reader comparing the two numbers could reasonably wonder if their axolotl is under- or overgrown.

Otherwise the numbers hold up everywhere I could check them: temperature thresholds, feeding schedule by life stage, and tank size are identical across every page that states them.

**5. Trust**

Nothing here made me doubt the writer's hands-on knowledge. If anything the opposite: "Prices last checked September 2026 at Josh's Frogs and PetSmart" is a dated, falsifiable claim, not a hedge. The sentence that most convinced me someone actually keeps these: "Keep juveniles under 5 inches on a bare-bottom tank until they're larger and less prone to accidental ingestion." That's a specific, non-obvious threshold no one invents without having raised one through that stage.

**6. Grade and two changes**

Grade: A-. Consistent numbers across seven pages, real specificity, no filler, and disciplined cross-linking. It loses the plus for the repeated gravel warning and the undefined morph terms.

Two changes: cut the no-gravel explanation down to one full statement (tank setup) with a short pointer from the other three pages instead of restating it each time. And add a line in the cost guide defining what "mosaic" and "GFP" morphs actually look like, since the term is priced but never explained anywhere in this set.

**Not covered anywhere**

- How to tell a healthy axolotl from a sick one at the point of purchase. Not in the Health and More list either.
- What mosaic and GFP morphs actually look like. Not in the Health and More list.
- Chiller sizing or wattage guidance matched to tank size. The Health and More list has cooling-without-a-chiller and general chiller advice, but nothing on sizing one correctly.
