No page felt thin or padded enough to tag. This set is unusually tight for eight pages on one small fish.

**2. Hub and encyclopedia vs. deep dives**

The hub earns its place: it's a router with a first-week checklist, an emergency card, and a buy list that no single deep dive assembles in one place. The encyclopedia earns its place too, holding taxonomy, the 1854 genus history, and the L-number system that no other page touches.

No disagreement in numbers. Tank size (20g floor, 29-30g better), temperature (74-80°F), pH (6.5-7.8), lifespan (5-10 years, up to 12-14), and cost ($7-25 fish, $155-265 setup) all match exactly between the hub and the deep dives that cover them. That consistency itself is a point in the set's favor.

**3. Could I actually keep this animal after reading the set?**

Yes, mostly. I'd know the tank size, the water numbers, that driftwood is mandatory rather than décor, the feeding schedule and ratio, what to avoid medically, and how to move the fish without hurting it or myself. The buy list on the hub covers the shopping trip.

Gaps: GH/KH (general and carbonate hardness) is never given a number for this species, even though the class-wide page on the topic is linked from every sidebar; I'd have to go read that page and guess how it applies here. Nobody tells me how to actually confirm a juvenile is a true bristlenose beyond "ask for the species name" and "bristles don't show until 3 inches" — there's no visual or genetic tell offered for a small juvenile, which is exactly the case the whole set worries about. The 85/15 plant-to-protein ratio in feeding is given as a target with no guidance on portion size or how it interacts with driftwood grazing.

Questions raised on one page that another page in the set quietly answers: the tank setup page raises "the evidence on wood-eating plecos" as a link without saying what it shows; the enrichment page is where that's actually resolved (bristlenose relatives are not true wood digesters, the benefit is likely microbial). The feeding page's "how much wood counts as enough" is also answered fully only in enrichment. Neither page connecting to enrichment says this in the body text; you have to follow the inline link and trust it lands somewhere useful, which it does.

**4. Overlap and conflict across deep dives**

Heavy, deliberate repetition, and no numeric conflicts. The common-pleco size trap (12-24 inches, 75+ gallons, San Marcos River removal) appears in the hub, encyclopedia, cost guide, and tank setup, told with the same 400-plus-fish, January 2022 figure each time. The driftwood-is-required message appears in tank setup, feeding, and enrichment, each adding a different layer (setup: what to buy; feeding: why it matters for digestion; enrichment: what the actual research says), so it reads as escalation rather than padding. The corydoras catfish comparison shows up in cost, handling, health, and feeding guides, always making a slightly different point (lifespan parity, tankmate fit, breathing behavior contrast, diet overlap), which is efficient reuse rather than a lazy copy-paste.

No numbers disagree anywhere I could find.

**5. Trust**

Nothing shook my trust. The one sentence that most convinced me a real keeper wrote this: "This is a nice case of a care recommendation being right for possibly the wrong reason. The hobby arrived at 'plecos need wood' from the wood-digestion story, and the recommendation survives the story being questioned, because the outcome without wood is bad regardless of why." That's not SEO copy. It's someone who read the actual 2009 and 2020 studies, noticed they don't fully back the standard explanation, and said so instead of quietly keeping the easier myth.

**6. Grade and two changes**

Grade: A-. Internally consistent, admits uncertainty where the science is genuinely uncertain, and every deep dive adds something instead of restating the hub.

First two changes: give a real GH/KH number or range specific to this species instead of leaving it to the cross-species page, and add a concrete way to tell a true bristlenose from a common pleco at the size stores actually sell them, since the set flags this as the single most expensive mistake but never gives a juvenile-stage tell beyond waiting for bristles at 3 inches.

**Not covered anywhere**

- A GH/KH range specific to bristlenose plecos: None found even after checking the Freshwater pH, GH, and KH page's one-line summary in Health and More, which covers the topic generally but not this species' numbers.
- A juvenile-stage way to distinguish a true bristlenose from a common pleco before the 3-inch bristle mark: None.
- Concrete portion sizing for the 85% plant/15% protein feeding ratio (how much food, not just how often): None.
