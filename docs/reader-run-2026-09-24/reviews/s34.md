2. Hub and encyclopedia
Both earn their place. The hub is a router with sourced numbers; the encyclopedia supplies the captive-origin history that nothing else repeats. No numeric disagreement between them, tank size, digging depth, humidity, and lifespan all match what the deep dives say.

3. Could I actually keep this animal
Yes, and this is a genuinely complete set. Tank type, depth, humidity, diet ratios, handling technique, and the emergency signs are all specific and actionable. One real gap: the enrichment guide's central finding, that a tunnel-and-chamber structure stops stereotypic digging while depth alone does not, is never mentioned on the enclosure setup page, even though that page's own headline is "Depth Is Everything." A reader who only reads the enclosure guide would set up deep bedding and reasonably believe they'd solved the digging need, then hit the enrichment guide later and learn depth was only half the answer. The enclosure page does link to the enrichment guide once, at the very end, but frames it as extra reading rather than "the thing that actually stops corner-digging."

4. Overlap and conflict across deep dives
Read twice: the "never by the tail" warning appears verbatim in the hub, the handling guide, and the health guide FAQ. Read twice: humidity thresholds (40%/50%) appear in both the enclosure guide and the health guide, consistently.
The one real tension: the enclosure guide states "Gerbil care comes down to one detail more than any other: providing deep substrate," while the enrichment guide states plainly "Almost every gerbil care sheet tells you to provide deep bedding. The research says that is roughly half the answer," and later "Do not assume deep bedding alone prevents stereotypic digging, since the study tested exactly that and it underperformed a burrow." These aren't a numbers conflict, but they are two deep dives making opposite claims about what matters most, and only one of them cites a study for its claim.

5. Trust
Nothing raised doubt, if anything the opposite. Citing a specific 1997 journal study by name and author for a single behavioral claim, and separately naming a university veterinary source for Tyzzer's disease and malocclusion, is a level of specificity generic content mills don't bother with. The sentence that most reads like an owner: "A gerbil digging in 2 inches of shavings on a glass floor is going through the motions of a behavior it can't actually complete, real tunnel-building needs real depth to work with." That's a description of watching it happen, not a paraphrase of a care sheet.

6. Grade: A. Reason: consistent numbers throughout, two real studies cited by name, and the set correctly reverses the hamster answer on both socialness and schedule instead of copy-pasting a template.
Two changes: reconcile the enclosure page's "depth is everything" framing with the enrichment page's "depth is half the answer" finding, ideally by naming the tunnel-and-chamber requirement on the enclosure page itself. Add the still-missing legal guide's content (California's whole-rodent-order ban) directly into the hub's legal row instead of only as a linked headline, since it's a genuine trap most owners wouldn't expect.

7. Not covered anywhere
- Whether the "prompt amputation" tail-slip injury needs to be followed by any special care (bandaging, infection watch) beyond "see a vet." None.
- Whether a gerbil pair can be safely introduced to a third or fourth gerbil later, or only works at initial setup. None.
- Nail trimming or grooming specific to gerbils (the cross-species grooming guide's summary is framed around rabbits and molting, not confirmed to address gerbil nails). None.
