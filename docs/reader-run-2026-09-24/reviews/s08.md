- Encyclopedia: Would finish, good history (Lathrop, Castle, Little). Grade A.

2. Hub and encyclopedia vs deep dives
The hub earns its place: it's a router with numbers and links, not prose duplicating the articles. The encyclopedia earns its place too, it's the only page carrying the Lathrop/Castle/Little history and taxonomy, nothing else touches that. No numeric conflicts found between hub and any deep dive. The hub's cost line ($100-$210 setup, $10-$20/month) matches the cost guide exactly, and the bar spacing, bedding depth, and protein numbers all match word for word across hub, tank setup, and feeding pages.

3. Could I keep this mouse?
Yes, fully. Cage size, bar spacing, bedding, wheel size, temperature range, diet, portions, toxic foods, handling technique, and red-flag symptoms are all there with real numbers. One unanswered handoff: the enrichment guide says a hideout is needed "to build the nest inside," and the tank setup guide separately recommends a hideout for cover, but neither page tells you if one hideout serves both jobs or if you need a nest box plus a separate cover hide. A new owner buying from the list would guess one item covers it, correctly, but the text never actually says so.

4. Overlap and conflicts among deep dives
Genuine overlap: the "hamster/gerbil pellet is wrong" point appears in the tank setup, feeding, and hub pages, each time attached to the 20-25% vs 14-16% protein split. It's repeated but each mention adds a slightly different angle (why the tub is wrong, what to buy instead), so it doesn't feel like padding. The "male mice smell more, don't strip the cage fully" point appears in both the health guide and the enrichment guide, worded almost identically both times, this is the one true duplicate passage in the set. No numeric conflicts: bar spacing (quarter inch/6mm, 5mm tighter), temperature (64-79°F), humidity (30-70%), lifespan (1.5-2 years) all agree everywhere they appear.

5. Trust
Nothing raised doubt. Citations are specific (RSPCA pages by URL, Merck Veterinary Manual, a named Current Biology finding on trimethylamine and TAAR5, named researcher Brianna Gaskill with actual gram and temperature figures). The sentence that most convinced me someone who keeps mice wrote this: "Where the dry food goes is one of the cheapest welfare changes available for this species," in the feeding guide, it's a small, specific, unglamorous claim exactly the kind a hands-on keeper would bother to say.

6. Grade and two changes
Grade: A. Consistent numbers, real citations, no filler, and the enrichment page in particular is unusually well sourced for a pet-care article.
Two changes: (1) State explicitly whether the enrichment "hideout for the nest" and the tank setup "hideout for cover" are the same purchase or two items. (2) Trim the repeated "don't fully strip a male's cage" passage to one canonical version and have the other page link to it instead of restating it near-verbatim.

7. Not covered anywhere
- Whether a single hideout serves both nesting and general cover, or two are needed: None (not answered even after checking the Health and More list, which only offers the unrelated Three Small Mammals roundup).

## hissing-cockroach

1. Page by page
- Hub: Would finish. Clear numbers, legal warning flagged early. Grade A.
- Encyclopedia: Would finish, taxonomic history and pop-culture notes are a nice touch. Grade A.
- Cost guide: Would finish. Honest about near-zero vet costs. Grade A.
- Handling guide: Would finish, the hiss mechanism section is genuinely well explained. Grade A.
- Health issues guide: Would finish, short but complete for a low-risk species (thin, but earns it given how little goes wrong with this animal).
- Tank setup guide: Would finish. The petroleum jelly barrier is the one detail that actually matters and it's given real weight. Grade A.
- Feeding guide: Would finish. Honestly flags a real disagreement between sources rather than hiding it. Grade A.
- Enrichment guide: Would finish. Refreshingly honest that it's a welfare framework, not an enrichment-item study. Grade A.

2. Hub and encyclopedia vs deep dives
The hub earns its place as a router, not a rehash. The encyclopedia adds real value (Schaum's 1853 description, the genus split, Letterman and Fear Factor appearances, the note that dealers mix this species with two lookalikes) that no deep dive covers. No numeric conflicts between hub and deep dives: cost figures ($3/$24/$65), temperature (75-85°F, breeding at 85-95°F), humidity (60-70%), and enclosure sizes (5 gallon minimum, 10-20+ for a colony) all match exactly between the hub and their source articles.

3. Could I set this up?
Yes, and more completely than most exotic-pet guides manage. Enclosure size, substrate depth, temperature, humidity, escape barrier, sexing, diet, water source, and red-flag symptoms are all specified with numbers. Two real gaps surface only when you compare pages: the hub's "not one on its own" line and the enrichment guide's colony-housing argument both assume you're keeping a group, but the handling guide separately recommends "a males-only group" for pet keepers wanting to avoid breeding, and nothing tells you how many males, or whether an all-male group has the same fighting risk profile mice and gerbils get flagged for elsewhere on the site. Second, the feeding guide states plainly that pet-industry sheets and an academic extension sheet disagree on feeding frequency (near-constant dry food vs. a sparse weekly ration) and tells the reader to use the lighter end if produce keeps molding, but never says which schedule the site's own hub numbers actually reflect, so a reader following the hub literally doesn't know which authority it's following.

4. Overlap and conflicts among deep dives
The petroleum-jelly escape barrier is described in near-identical detail in the hub, cost guide (implicitly), tank setup, and handling guide's first-timer mistakes list, this is warranted repetition since it is the single most important fact in the set, not padding. The molting vulnerability window (soft, pale, defenseless for hours) is described almost word for word in both the health issues guide and the enrichment guide, that one is a duplicate passage rather than reinforcement. No numeric conflicts: humidity, temperature, and substrate depth all agree everywhere. The feeding guide is the one page that surfaces an actual disagreement (schedule frequency between pet-industry and academic sources) and states it honestly rather than picking a number and hiding the conflict.

5. Trust
The site's honesty about its own evidence limits is what earned trust here: the enrichment guide states directly "we nearly wrote this guide saying no research exists" and then draws a clear line between a welfare-assessment framework and an actual enrichment-item study, refusing to oversell it. The feeding guide's admission that sources disagree on feeding frequency, rather than papering over it, is the single sentence that most convinced me a real keeper wrote this: "Sources disagree... This is a real difference in approach, not just phrasing."

6. Grade and two changes
Grade: A. Specific, honestly limited claims, a real safety detail (the jelly barrier) given the prominence it deserves, and no numeric conflicts.
Two changes: (1) State which feeding schedule (constant dry food vs. sparse weekly) the hub's own numbers are based on, since the feeding guide admits both exist. (2) Trim the molting-vulnerability passage to one canonical spot instead of repeating it near-verbatim in both the health and enrichment guides.

7. Not covered anywhere
- How many males is safe in an all-male group, and whether adult males fight the way mice or gerbils do: None (the Health and More list's pesticide, rehousing, housekeeping, emergency-plan, and four-animals pages don't touch group sizing or aggression for this species).
- Which of the two disputed feeding schedules the hub's own numbers reflect: None (not addressed in the Health and More list either).
