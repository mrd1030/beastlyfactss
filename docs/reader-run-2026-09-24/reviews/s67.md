## lovebird

**1. Page by page**
- 00 Hub: A. The "one bird or two" decision is front and center where it belongs.
- 01 Encyclopedia: A. The 1818/1887 naming history and the 1992 Tanzanian export suspension are real, non-repeated content.
- 02 Cost: A. Specific ranges, a September 2026 price date, and an honest steer toward two birds if you can't give one two hours a day.
- 03 Handling: A. Names the temperament mismatch with the species' own cute name and backs it with concrete triggers and fixes.
- 04 Health issues: A. Cites VCA, LafeberVet, PetMD, and the University of Michigan's Animal Diversity Web by name, with specific numbers (23-day incubation, leuprolide's 3-4 week window).
- 05 Cage setup: A. Bar spacing framed correctly as a safety issue, not a preference.
- 06 Feeding: A. Explains exactly why dusting seed with supplement powder fails, mechanically, not just "don't do it."
- 07 Enrichment: A. Names contra-freeloading and gives a real toy-rotation number (3 to 5, weekly).
- 08 vs. budgie: A. Honest that lovebirds can injure or kill a mismatched cage mate, not just "keep them together for company."

**2. Hub and encyclopedia vs. deep dives**
Both earn their place. The encyclopedia's breeding and export history is unique, and the hub's numbers all trace back to a deep dive with nothing invented. I checked every hub number against its linked article, cage size, bar spacing, budget, lifespan, and found no disagreements. This is the most internally consistent of the sets I reviewed.

**3. Could I do this?**
Yes, and confidently. Cage, diet ratio, the calcium-and-egg-laying mechanism, and the one-bird-or-two decision are all covered in real depth with sourced reasoning, not just assertions. One loose end: the handling guide states lovebirds should "never share space with another species," unlike budgies, but never says why, and nothing else in the set or the Health and More list explains cross-species housing risk for this species specifically.

**4. Overlap and conflicts across the deep dives**
Heavy, deliberate repetition on the one-bird-or-two decision: the cost guide and handling guide both use the near-identical "closer to keeping a pair of finches" line and the same 4-to-8-week mourning detail for a separated pair, this is clearly one fact reused by design, not sloppiness. The 75-80% pellet ratio and 18x18x24 cage minimum repeat consistently everywhere. I found no numeric conflicts between any two articles in this set, an unusual result compared to other species sets.

**5. Trust**
The line that most convinced me a real bird keeper wrote this: "According to VCA Animal Hospitals, removing eggs as soon as they're laid can push a hen to lay even more, because her body reads an incomplete nest as a signal to keep producing." That's a specific, counterintuitive, sourced mechanism, not generic advice. Naming the exact drugs (leuprolide acetate, a deslorelin implant) and their real duration differences reads the same way. Nothing in the set raised doubt.

**6. Grade: A.** Specific, sourced, cross-checked, and the one deep dive I found with zero internal numeric conflicts.
Two changes: explain why lovebirds can't share space with other bird species (the claim is made twice but never justified), and add a line in the cage or enrichment guide on why round cages disorient this species specifically, since it's asserted but not explained.

**Not covered anywhere**
- Why lovebirds can't be housed with other bird species, unlike budgies. None.
- Why a round cage disorients a lovebird given its eye position. None.
