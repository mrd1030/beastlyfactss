2. Hub vs encyclopedia
Both earn their place. The hub is action-first (buy list, emergency card, first-week numbers) and the encyclopedia is history and taxonomy (the Mollienesia name, the 1905 hobby entry, the contested black molly origin). No disagreement: adult size, difficulty, temperature range, and the salt caveat are stated the same way in both, down to nearly identical wording on the salt dispute.

3. The set as a whole
Yes, you could set this up, buy it, feed it, and keep it healthy from this set alone. Tank size, water chemistry, cycling, feeding by life stage, sexing, and the shimmy protocol are all covered with real numbers.
One real gap: the hub's buy list includes a water test kit and mentions testing nitrate for water changes, but the actual water-change routine (25-30% weekly, thresholds at 20 ppm and 40 ppm nitrate) is only a hub bullet point that links off this species' set entirely, to a general aquarium page not included here. Within this set, nobody actually walks through doing a water change.
The tank setup guide raises but doesn't fully resolve which filter to buy: hang-on-back/canister for turnover, or sponge for fry safety. It says "which one you pick is really a question of what you're optimizing for" and leaves the buyer to decide before they know whether they're breeding yet.

4. Overlap and conflict across the deep dives
Extensive overlap, no numeric conflicts. The salt question (65 ppt regulation ability, disputed standing use, 1 tablespoon per 5 gallons for shimmy treatment, never marine or table salt) is repeated near-identically across the hub, encyclopedia, health guide, and tank setup guide. The breeding numbers (20-60 fry typical, 10-100 documented range, 4-6 week gestation, female sperm storage) repeat across the hub, cost guide, and handling guide, always with the same figures. Sex ratio (2-3 females per male) repeats across the hub, handling guide, and enrichment guide, consistently.
The shimmy causes (temperature, pH, hardness, chlorine) are consistent between the tank setup guide and health guide, worded almost the same way both times, which suggests one was written with the other open rather than independently.

5. Trust
The specificity is what sells it: a named source (Dr. Joanne Norton) taking a specific position against routine salt use, a precise salinity figure (65 ppt) attributed to peer-reviewed research on sailfin mollies, and dated, retailer-attributed prices. The sentence that most convinced me a real keeper wrote this: "The vigilance finding is the one that connects to the grazing section below. A female molly spending her time watching for males is a female molly not spending it grazing." That's a specific causal link between two different pieces of research that a generic content mill wouldn't bother making.
Nothing here reads as fabricated or copy-pasted from a competitor.

6. Grade: A-. Consistent numbers throughout, honest handling of a genuinely disputed topic (salt), and enrichment content that's actually species-specific instead of generic. First two changes: walk through an actual water change with real numbers inside this set rather than pointing off to a page not included here, and settle the filter choice with a clearer default (start with sponge, upgrade to HOB if not breeding) instead of leaving it as an open question.
