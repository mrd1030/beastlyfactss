## red-eared-slider

**1. Page by page**
- 00 hub: A. A genuinely useful router: every number sourced from a deep dive, an emergency card, a buy list. I'd finish it and act on it immediately.
- 01 encyclopedia: B+. The dime-store history and the invasive-species angle are real content, not filler, but it adds nothing actionable beyond the hub.
- 02 cost guide: A. Tables plus a clear "budget for the adult, not the hatchling" thesis, backed by named retailers and a date.
- 03 handling guide: A-. Honest that this isn't a cuddly pet, with a clear scoop technique and real mistakes to avoid.
- 04 health issues guide: A. Cites VCA, Merck, and a named 2020 feeding trial (Stancel et al.) to correct a common myth about pyramiding and calcium. This is the strongest page in the set.
- 05 tank setup guide: A. Specific numbers for everything, and it flags the exact mistakes ("filtration alone isn't a substitute for water changes") that trip up new owners.
- 06 feeding guide: A. Schedule, ratios, safe/unsafe foods, and seven diagnosable reasons for appetite loss, with a clear vet threshold.
- 07 enrichment guide: B (filler). The one real study (colored objects, escape behavior) is good, but roughly half the page re-runs tank setup's water volume, filtration, and basking-platform advice almost verbatim rather than building on it.

**2. Hub and encyclopedia**
The hub earns its place: it's a numbers digest, not a rewrite, and every figure I checked against the deep dives matches. Water temp (72-82°F), basking (85-95°F), UVB target (2.0-3.0 UV index), adult shell size (25.4 cm females, 17.78 cm males), and the cost ranges ($550-1,250 upfront, $380-720/year) are identical between the hub and their source articles. No disagreement found there.
The encyclopedia is softer duplication territory: it gives adult size as "5-12 inches (13-30 cm); females significantly larger," which is a much wider, vaguer range than the tank setup guide's specific averages. It also states wild lifespan as "up to 30 years... captive up to 41.3 years," while the cost guide separately floats "40 to 70 years" as a "far less documented" claim. Neither is flatly wrong, but a reader comparing the two pages gets two different ceiling numbers (41.3 vs. 70) for the same claim of "sliders can live longer than 30 years."

**3. The set as a whole**
Yes, someone could set up the tank, feed correctly by age, and recognize a health emergency from this set alone. What's missing: how to actually cycle a new tank before the turtle moves in (the tank setup guide name-drops "the nitrogen cycle" and links out, but that page isn't in this set); water-testing target numbers for ammonia/nitrite (a test kit is on the buy list with no target values); and quarantine or introduction steps for adding a second turtle, despite the handling guide warning at length about housing multiples badly. The tank setup guide raises "gravid females need a nesting option" and the feeding guide separately covers egg-binding symptoms in depth, but neither page points to the other, so a reader has to notice the connection themselves.

**4. Overlap and conflict across the deep dives**
Heavily overlapping but not contradictory: tank size (75-100 gal minimum, 100-125 better), filtration ("rated well above tank volume"), and basking platform requirements appear near-identically in the cost, tank setup, and enrichment articles. The diet protein/plant ratio wording matches between the hub and feeding guide. I didn't find a hard numeric contradiction, but the lifespan spread noted above (41.3 vs. 40-70 years) is a real inconsistency between two articles making the same kind of claim.

**5. Trust**
The named sources (VCA, LafeberVet, Merck, Animal Diversity Web, a specific 2016 Baines UV-Tool paper, a specific 150-day feeding trial, a 2025 PLOS ONE paper) are unusual specificity for a pet-care site and raise my confidence. The sentence that most convinced me a real keeper wrote this: "Sliders genuinely learn to associate their keeper with food... that association is about the meal, not about you specifically." That's a distinction only someone who has actually watched the behavior and second-guessed it would bother making.

**6. Grade: A-.** Deep, well-sourced, internally consistent on the numbers that matter, dragged down only by the encyclopedia's vaguer figures and the enrichment page's redundancy. First two fixes: trim enrichment's repeated husbandry paragraphs down to genuinely new material, and reconcile the two different lifespan ceilings into one stated range.

**7. Not covered anywhere**
- Target ammonia/nitrite numbers for water testing (a test kit is recommended, but no values to test against).
- Quarantine or introduction steps when adding a second slider to an existing setup.
