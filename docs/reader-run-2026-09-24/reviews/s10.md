## fire-bellied-toad

**1. Page by page**
- Hub: Finish it. Everything actionable: sizes, water depth, temps, feeding, buy list, emergency card. Grade A.
- Encyclopedia: Finish it. Short, but the chytrid-fungus history (traced to Korean fire-bellied toads in trade) is a genuine hook, not filler. Grade A.
- Cost guide: Finish it. Real numbers, and it admits its own table doesn't add up to its own total. Grade B.
- Toxic skin/handling guide: Finish it. The staged unken-reflex description is specific and actionable (stop at stage one). Grade A.
- Health issues guide: Finish it. Clear emergency triage, ties every issue back to water quality. Grade A.
- Tank setup guide: Finish it. The most useful page in the set, corrects a competing source's number (PetMD's 1-2 inch water depth) with a reason. Grade A.
- Feeding guide: Finish it. Covers why the animal won't eat, not just what to feed it. Grade A.
- Enrichment guide: Finish it. Honest that nothing exists on this species and says so before borrowing. Grade B+.

**2. Hub and encyclopedia**
Both earn their place. The encyclopedia carries natural history and taxonomy no deep dive repeats (the 2018 Science paper tracing chytrid to Korea). The hub is a router with its own numbers, not a rehash. No numeric disagreement between hub and any deep dive: water depth (3 inches), temperature range (75-78°F, 65 floor, 82 ceiling), feeding frequency (2-6 items every 2-3 days), and lifespan (10-15 years, 20 recorded, 30 max) all match word for word across hub, cost guide, and tank setup guide.

**3. Could I actually keep one?**
Yes, mostly. I could buy the animal, build the tank, feed it, and recognize red leg syndrome. What's thinner: no exact night-time temperature number is ever given, only "cooler at night" with 65°F as a floor. No filter size or GPH is suggested for a 10 gallon water section. A few questions get answered on a different page than the one that raises them, without a pointer: the tank setup guide tells you to dechlorinate and filter but the actual quarantine timeline (6-8 weeks) and the warning against RO/distilled water only show up on the hub, sourced from an amphibian quarantine guide that isn't in this set. The feeding guide's line "It is new, or it has company it does not like" assumes you already know new toads need a settling week, which is stated on the hub but not re-explained there.

**4. Overlap and conflict across deep dives**
Read twice or more: the toxin/unken reflex description (toxic skin article, health article, enrichment article), the "never house with other species" rule (four separate articles), the diet rotation list of crickets/waxworms/earthworms/silkworms/dubia/soldier fly larvae (tank setup and feeding guide, nearly word for word), and the wild-caught-stock-means-fecal-exam point (cost guide and health guide, near identical). No numbers actually disagree once you read closely. The closest thing to friction is water depth: the tank setup guide notes "PetMD's sheet puts the water at 1 to 2 inches... this is a toad that swims, so give it the 3," which is a disclosed disagreement with a named source, not an internal one.

**5. Trust**
Nothing made me doubt the set. What convinced me a real keeper wrote it: "A cricket that lives on in the enclosure chews on a resting toad and fouls the water when it dies." That's a specific, slightly unpleasant detail nobody invents without having seen it. The enrichment guide's plain admission that "Nothing on Bombina" exists, before it borrows evidence from African clawed frogs and dendrobatids, also builds trust rather than undermining it.

**6. Grade: A-.** Consistent numbers, honest sourcing, and nothing padded. Two changes: give an actual night-time temperature figure instead of "cooler," and state a filter size or turnover rate for the water section instead of leaving it to "a small filter."

**7. Not covered anywhere**
- Exact night-time temperature (only a floor of 65°F is given). None found even in the Health and More summaries.
- Filter size/turnover rate for the water section. None.
- How to introduce a new toad to an existing group day by day (quarantine length is given, the acclimation process itself is not, since the amphibian quarantine guide is only summarized here). None.
- Sexing or breeding information. None.
- Which water conditioner or dosage to use. None (understandably left out to avoid product pushing, but genuinely missing).

## fire-skink

**1. Page by page**
- Hub: Finish it. Dense, numeric, and it flags its own budget's flaw (see below). Grade A.
- Encyclopedia: Finish it. The taxonomic shuffle (four genus names in a century) is a real, non-filler hook. Grade A.
- Cost guide: Finish it, but with a caveat: it prices a thermostat its own text says shouldn't be used with the bulb it's paired with. Grade B-.
- Handling guide: Finish it. Direct, comparative, and honest that this is not a cuddly lizard. Grade A.
- Health issues guide: Finish it. Ties every issue to one root cause and says so plainly. Grade A.
- Tank setup guide: Finish it. Gives the actual substrate ratio and a disclosed UVB debate (Ferguson Zone 2 vs a much higher published range). Grade A.
- Feeding guide: Finish it. Explains why you rarely see it eat, which most owners will actually wonder. Grade A.
- Enrichment guide: Finish it. Borrows from a blue-tongue study and says so up front. Grade B+.

**2. Hub and encyclopedia**
Both earn their place. But the hub disagrees with its own linked cost guide in a way worth flagging as a real conflict, not just overlap: the hub's first-week numbers list "Setup roughly $660 to $720 with the on/off thermostat," and in the very next line: "The thermostat: The $130 unit priced in the cost guide is an on/off model, and its maker says not to run a basking bulb on one. A bulb wants a dimming thermostat, which costs more." The cost guide's own line-item table lists "Thermostat, on/off model | $130" inside the $660-720 total, then separately warns the reader off using it with the bulb the same table prices. The headline budget number is for a combination the page tells you not to build.

**3. Could I actually keep one?**
Mostly yes: enclosure size, substrate mix, temperature and humidity targets, and the feeding schedule are all concrete and consistent. What's missing until you reach the cost guide: the actual dimming thermostat is never priced, so the real all-in cost is unstated, only implied as "runs higher." The tank setup guide references "the respiratory infection and shedding problems dry air causes" without repeating the numbers, which are on the health issues page, so that connection is made but the reader has to trust the link rather than see the number twice. The encyclopedia's claim that "most become confident enough to feed from tongs" sits a little oddly next to the handling guide's "even settled fire skinks tend to stay squirmy and quick," though the two aren't strictly contradictory: comfort at a feeding dish is not the same as comfort in a hand.

**4. Overlap and conflict across deep dives**
Read twice or more: the substrate depth and ratio (tank setup and enrichment guide, near-identical wording), the "why you never see it" behavioral explanation (hub, tank setup, enrichment guide, handling guide's fun fact box, four times over), and the blue-tongued skink comparison (cost, handling, health, and tank setup guides all make the same contrast). No hard numeric conflicts: 36x18x18 minimum, 80-86°F ambient, 92-96°F basking, 60-70% humidity, and the twice-to-three-times weekly adult feeding schedule all match everywhere they appear.

**5. Trust**
The thermostat mismatch above is the one thing that made me doubt the cost guide specifically, though the fact that the page discloses its own problem instead of hiding it is itself reassuring about the writer's honesty. The line that most convinced me a keeper wrote this: "Fire skinks get called expensive substrate by keepers who bought one and then never saw it again." That's a specific, slightly self-deprecating joke that reads like it was heard at a reptile show, not generated to fill space.

**6. Grade: B+.** Strong, consistent husbandry numbers undercut by a cost guide that prices equipment its own text says is the wrong combination. Two changes: price the dimming thermostat instead of the on/off one in the main total, and add a second number to the encyclopedia's tongs-feeding claim so it doesn't read against the handling guide's caution.

**7. Not covered anywhere**
- The actual dimming thermostat price, so a true "buy everything you need" total. None.
- Sexing or breeding information. None.
- What group housing of females (as opposed to males) looks like, if it's viable at all. None, the enrichment guide only says "cautiously, and not by default" without detail.
- Specific basking bulb wattage. None.
