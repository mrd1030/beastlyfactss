## cherry-shrimp

1. Page by page
- Hub (00): Finish it. Dense, well-organized, and the emergency card and buy list are genuinely usable. A
- Encyclopedia (01): Finish it. The taxonomic-name history (davidi, then buried under three other names for a century, then restored) is a real story, not filler. A
- Cost guide (02): Finish it. Grading, pricing, and the invasive-species warning are all handled with sourced numbers. A
- Handling guide (03): Finish it. The osmotic shock explanation with a specific TDS threshold (30-40 ppm) is more precise than most sites bother with. A
- Health issues guide (04): Finish it. The copper LC50 number and its source species are both named honestly instead of implied to be cherry-shrimp-specific. A
- Tank setup guide (05): Finish it. Direct and practical, though the origin claim conflicts with the encyclopedia (see below). B+
- Feeding guide (06): Finish it. The "feed a pea, skip the next if leftovers remain" rule is concrete and easy to follow. A
- Enrichment guide (07): Finish it. Honest about the sentience review's limits rather than overselling it. A

2. Hub vs encyclopedia
Both earn their place. The hub is action-first, the encyclopedia is history and taxonomy. But they disagree on origin. The encyclopedia states: "Origin: Taiwan and eastern China." The tank setup guide states the species is "native to streams, rivers, ponds, and lakes across Taiwan, eastern China, the Korean peninsula, and Vietnam." That is a real geographic range conflict between two pages in the same set, not just a wording difference. No other numbers disagree: adult size (1.5 inches), difficulty, and temperature ranges all match.

3. The set as a whole
Yes, largely. Tank size, hardness, temperature, feeding, molting, copper danger, and colony sizing are all here with real numbers. You could set this up and keep the colony healthy.
One question raised but not fully closed: the tank setup guide says amano shrimp are "a common, compatible tankmate" while ghost shrimp "are more likely to prey on smaller or freshly molted cherry shrimp." The cost guide also mentions ghost shrimp as "not always a safe tankmate... as our health issues guide touches on," but the health issues guide in this set never actually discusses tankmate predation, only water chemistry. That cross-reference points to content this set doesn't deliver.
The water-change guidance is internally consistent (10% weekly or 20% biweekly) but the hub's own FAQ never explains what to do if source water is naturally too hard or too soft beyond "test and adjust gradually," without saying what product amount to start with.

4. Overlap and conflict across the deep dives
Heavy overlap, one real conflict (the origin/range difference above). Otherwise consistent: GH/KH numbers (floor of 6 dGH/2 dKH, target 6-8 dGH/2-4 dKH) appear identically in the hub and tank setup guide. The copper LC50 (0.0313 mg/L, safe target 0.003 mg/L, tested on a related species not cherry shrimp itself) is repeated accurately and consistently across the hub, cost guide, and health guide, always correctly attributed to Macrobrachium nipponense rather than overstated as cherry-shrimp-specific. Molt frequency (3-4 weeks adult, more often when young) matches across the health guide and feeding guide. The "leave the shed shell in the tank" advice is identical everywhere it appears.

5. Trust
The set consistently cites real, specific sources: a named 2014 toxicity study with a DOI-linked PMC article, a 2023 study on epibionts in imported shrimp, a 2025 USFWS invasion risk screening, and named retailers with "prices last checked" dates. The sentence that most convinced me a real keeper wrote this: "It's a good reminder that 'quarantine because you should' and 'quarantine because something specific is actually riding along' are often the same advice," from the handling guide's discussion of the epibiont study. That's an observation, not a fact restated from a source.
The one thing that undercuts trust: the origin conflict in point 2. It's a small thing, but a site this careful about attaching citations to every number should not disagree with itself on where the animal is from.

6. Grade: A-. Extremely well-sourced and cross-consistent on every number that matters for care, with one real factual conflict (origin range) and one dangling cross-reference (tankmate predation promised but not delivered). First two changes: fix the origin line so the encyclopedia and tank setup guide agree, and either move the ghost-shrimp predation discussion into the health guide as promised, or stop pointing to it from the cost guide.

7. Not covered anywhere
- Ghost shrimp as a cherry shrimp predator/tankmate risk: promised by a cost-guide link into "our health issues guide," which does not cover it, and not covered in the Health and More list excerpts shown either.
- A starting dose or amount for a remineralizing product when source water tests too soft: the set says to adjust "gradually" but gives no starting quantity.
