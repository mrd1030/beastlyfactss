## tokay-gecko

1. Page by page
- Hub: finish it, yes. Act on it: the whole first-week list is usable on its own. Grade A.
- Encyclopedia: finish it, yes. Act on it: little, it is background. Grade B+.
- Cost guide: finish it, yes. Act on it: budget and the captive-bred vs wild-caught call. Grade A.
- Handling guide: finish it, yes. Act on it: how to read a bark or gape and what to do mid-bite. Grade A.
- Health issues guide: finish it, yes. Act on it: the vet-call list and the quarantine plan. Grade A.
- Tank setup guide: finish it, yes. Act on it: the whole build, temps, humidity, décor. Grade A.
- Feeding guide: finish it, yes. Act on it: schedule, portion, why it stopped eating. Grade A.
- Enrichment guide: finish it, yes. Act on it: less than the others, since pairing is a maybe. Grade B+.

2. Hub and encyclopedia
Both earn their place. The hub is a router with numbers pulled from the deep dives, not a rewrite of them, and the encyclopedia adds wild trade history none of the guides touch, the scale of the Indonesian export trade for medicine rather than pets. No numbers disagree between hub and any deep dive that I could find. Hub: "Temperature: Basking 90 to 105F, cooler zone 80 to 85F, and nights at 75 to 80F." Tank setup guide: "Basking area 90 to 105F, cooler zone 80 to 85F, and nighttime should stay warmer than many homes naturally run, 75 to 80F." Same numbers, same wording almost exactly, which reads as generated from one source rather than two authors drifting apart.

3. Could I actually keep this animal
Yes, close to fully. I could buy the enclosure, hit the right temperature and humidity, feed on the five-minute rule, and know what a real emergency looks like. What is still missing after reading everything given: the actual state-by-state legal breakdown (the hub summarizes it but the dedicated legal article was not part of this set), and a concrete pairing protocol beyond "quarantine, careful introduction, space and cover." The enrichment guide raises pairing and points at the quarantine guide for the quarantine part, but never says how long to observe a newly paired pair before trusting them unsupervised. That question sits between the enrichment guide and the health guide's quarantine section and neither closes it.

4. Overlap and conflict across deep dives
Read twice: the husbandry pattern (temperature, humidity, calcium/UVB, wild-caught risk) appears in the health guide's closing section and again, condensed, on the hub. The two-males-never rule appears in the handling guide, the tank setup guide, and the hub. None of that reads as padding, it is the same load-bearing fact showing up where a reader would actually need it.
One soft mismatch: the hub's gut-loading line gives "24 to 72 hours, 48 the most cited," while the feeding guide only ever says "gut-load for two to three days," which is 48 to 72 hours. Not a contradiction, but the feeding guide quietly drops the 24-hour end the hub states as valid. Nothing else disagreed on a number I could check.

5. Trust
The named-source handling guide is the strongest trust signal here: it separates what the Smithsonian says from what PetMD says from what two different Reptiles Magazine writers say, and it does not smooth over the fact that they are not all making the identical point. The sentence that most convinced me a keeper wrote this: "A tokay may ignore the bowl entirely and drink the droplets off the walls instead," which is the kind of detail nobody invents without having watched a gecko do it. The 2025 parasite study (21 farmed geckos, 42.9% carrying parasites) and the 2024 pair-housing study are both cited with journal and year, not vaguely gestured at.

6. Grade and fixes
Grade: A-. Reason: consistent numbers, real citations, and a clear top-line warning (this bites, plan accordingly) that the rest of the set backs up rather than softens.
Two changes: fold the legal deep dive into this reading set since the hub leans on it hard, and reconcile the 24-hour gut-load window between the hub and the feeding guide.

7. Not covered anywhere
- A concrete timeline or checklist for introducing a captive-bred pair and knowing when to separate them. None.

## blue-tongue-skink

1. Page by page
- Hub: finish it, yes. Act on it: the whole first-week list, and it flags the Northern/Indonesian split immediately. Grade A.
- Encyclopedia: finish it, yes. Act on it: little directly, but the export-law history explains the price gap the cost guide only states. Grade A-.
- Cost guide: finish it, yes. Act on it: budget by origin, the real total range. Grade A.
- Handling guide: finish it, yes. Act on it: the huff scale and how to lift a heavy lizard safely. Grade A.
- Health issues guide: finish it, yes. Act on it: MBD warning signs, parasite treatment drugs by name. Grade A.
- Tank setup guide: finish it, yes. Act on it: the whole Northern vs Indonesian build. Grade A.
- Feeding guide: finish it, yes. Act on it: schedule, ratio by age, the eight reasons for a food strike. Grade A.
- Enrichment guide: finish it, yes. Act on it: scatter feeding and the dig box, both cheap and specific. Grade A-.

2. Hub and encyclopedia
Both earn their place, and this is the cleanest pairing of the two sets. The encyclopedia's history section explains why Northerns are captive-bred and Indonesians are wild-caught (Australia's 1982 export ban) which the cost and handling guides both rely on without re-explaining. I found no numeric disagreement between the hub and any deep dive. Hub: "Basking surface: Northern 105 to 115°F, Indonesian 100 to 105°F." Tank setup guide: "Northern basking surface: 105 to 115°F. Indonesian basking surface: slightly cooler, 100 to 105°F." Identical. The one place the encyclopedia adds nuance the hub skips: encyclopedia calls 15 to 20 years "a captive figure" since wild lifespan is not reported, while the hub's Lifespan row just states "15 to 20 years, some past 30" with no caveat. Not a conflict, but the hub is less careful about it.

3. Could I actually keep this animal
Yes, close to fully, and the Northern/Indonesian fork is handled better here than in most care sets I would expect: every guide restates which numbers change by origin instead of averaging them into one soft number. What is still missing: the actual state legal detail again lives only in the hub's summary since the dedicated legal article was not part of this set. One thing raised without being tied together: the feeding guide flags the real disagreement between vet sources (every 1 to 3 days) and pet-industry sheets (once or twice a week) for adult feeding, and says to let body condition be the guide, but no page in this set explains how to read body condition on a skink the way the daily-stool-check article does for hydration.

4. Overlap and conflict across deep dives
Read twice: the wild-caught Indonesian parasite risk appears in the cost guide, the health guide, and the tank setup guide's quarantine section, each time with a slightly different angle (cost, drug names, setup timing), which is the good kind of repetition. The bearded dragon comparison shows up in the cost, handling, tank setup, and enrichment guides, always with a different specific claim (same footprint, longer lifespan, comparable setup cost, same dig-box trick), not the same sentence copy-pasted. No numeric conflicts found between any two deep dives.

5. Trust
The feeding guide naming the real vet-versus-industry disagreement on adult feeding frequency, instead of picking one number and hiding the dispute, is the strongest trust signal in either set. The sentence that most convinced me a keeper wrote this: "A bowl of chopped salad is a two minute event for an animal that forages across ground for hours in the wild," which is an observation, not a fact pulled from a sheet. The Wolf et al. 2014 parasite study and the mealworm enrichment study are both cited by name and year rather than gestured at generically.

6. Grade and fixes
Grade: A. Reason: consistent numbers throughout, an honestly flagged disagreement on adult feeding frequency instead of a false-precision number, and the Northern/Indonesian split carried through every single guide rather than just the tank setup page.
Two changes: add a body-condition reference (weight and shape by life stage) somewhere in the set, since the feeding guide leans on it without defining it. And bring the legal guide into the reading set given how much the hub depends on it.

7. Not covered anywhere
- How to judge body condition on a blue-tongue skink, the standard the feeding guide tells you to use instead of a fixed feeding number. None.
