## guinea-pig

**1. Page by page**
- 00 hub: A. Comprehensive and well-organized, with a genuinely useful emergency card and numbers that trace back to their source articles.
- 01 encyclopedia: A-. The 16th-century European remains (Mons, Belgium; Hill Hall Manor, Essex) are specific, real history, not filler, and it doesn't overlap with the care content.
- 02 cost guide: A. Clear tables, dated retail sources, and correctly frames the pair requirement as the real budget driver rather than the animal's own price.
- 03 handling guide: A. The predator-approach framing (why coming from above is scary) is a real, non-generic explanation, and the stress/content signal lists are concrete.
- 04 health issues guide: A. The mites-versus-ovarian-cysts differential table is the standout: specific, comparative, and something a keeper could actually use to triage at home.
- 05 tank setup guide: A. Cites Merck and PetMD by name for the vitamin C dosing and gives a real reason (light/air/time degradation) for the 90-day pellet rule rather than just stating it.
- 06 feeding guide: A-. Thorough and specific, though its own FAQ admits sources disagree on alfalfa for adults ("one source says avoid it outright... another allows it as an occasional treat") without resolving the tension.
- 07 enrichment guide: A-. The 80%-of-the-day foraging statistic is used well to justify concrete changes (scatter hay, hide vegetables) rather than just cited and dropped.
- 09 scurvy guide: A. The best page in the set: stage-by-stage symptoms, weight-based dosing explained rather than just given, and a clear explanation of why fortified pellets alone fail.

**2. Hub and encyclopedia**
The hub is a genuine digest: enclosure size (7.5/10.5 sq ft), temperature (65-79°F, danger above 80°F), and vitamin C dosing (10-25 mg/kg, 20-25 mg/day typical) all match their source articles exactly. The encyclopedia adds real, non-duplicated history rather than restating the hub. One small inconsistency: the hub and tank setup guide both say hay is "roughly 80%" of the diet, while the feeding guide gives a range of "70 to 80%." Not a real contradiction, just an unreconciled rounding between two pages on the same fact.

**3. The set as a whole**
Yes, this set is enough to set up, feed correctly, and recognize both routine and emergency health issues, and the vitamin C material in particular is unusually thorough and specific. What's missing: nothing here explains how to actually introduce or bond two guinea pigs (the set insists a pair is close to mandatory but never says how to do that introduction safely), and nothing covers spaying decisions in practice given how common and serious ovarian cysts are described as being (the health guide recommends spaying as "definitive treatment" but nothing discusses whether to spay preventively). The health guide raises antibiotic-associated enterotoxemia as a real risk from bladder-stone treatment but only in a linked, off-set article, so a reader following only this directory learns a bladder stone might need antibiotics without learning which ones are dangerous.

**4. Overlap and conflict across the deep dives**
The vitamin C dosing figures (Merck and PetMD numbers) are repeated identically across the hub, tank setup guide, and scurvy guide, which is good, consistent repetition rather than drift. The one real, if minor, inconsistency is the alfalfa hay guidance: the feeding guide's own FAQ flags that its sources disagree on whether adult guinea pigs should get alfalfa "outright" avoided or as an occasional treat, and the article never picks a side. The hay percentage (70-80% vs. 80%) is a similar soft mismatch.

**5. Trust**
The named, sourced figures (Merck Veterinary Manual, PetMD, specific percentages worked out from per-kilogram numbers) and the willingness to say "sources disagree" rather than paper over it both build confidence. The sentence that most convinced me a real keeper wrote this: the mites-versus-cysts differential, "An older intact female losing hair evenly on both sides without scratching points toward cysts before mites." That's the kind of pattern-matching detail that comes from having actually needed to tell the two apart, not from a generic symptom list.

**6. Grade: A.** Deep, well-sourced, mostly self-consistent, and honest where sources genuinely disagree. First two fixes: resolve or reconcile the alfalfa-for-adults disagreement in the feeding guide instead of leaving both positions stated, and add a short, concrete bonding/introduction section for pairs given how central "always keep two" is to this entire set.

**7. Not covered anywhere**
- How to actually introduce or bond two guinea pigs safely, despite pairing being treated as close to mandatory throughout.
- Which specific antibiotics are dangerous for guinea pigs (named only as a link to an off-set article, not explained here) despite the health guide raising the risk directly.
