## budgie

- 00 Care guide hub: finish it. Numbers you need in week one, an emergency card, a buy list, and clean FAQ. Grade A.
- 01 Encyclopedia: finish it, short. Origin, wild diet, and the 1840 Gould history are the payoff, not care advice. Grade A-.
- 02 Cost guide: finish it. Real dollar ranges for setup, food, and the vet costs people skip. Grade A.
- 03 Handling guide: finish it. A concrete settle-then-taming ladder with a stop signal. Grade A.
- 04 Health issues guide: finish it. The leg-tumor-that-looks-like-a-sprain section alone is worth the page. Grade A.
- 05 Tank setup guide: finish it. Width over height, bar spacing, and the nonstick-pan warning in one place. Grade A.
- 06 Feeding guide: finish it. The goiter-squeak section is specific and useful, told in first person by someone who's watched budgies eat. Grade A.

- 09 Budgie vs cockatiel: finish it. A genuine comparison with a table, not a coin flip. Grade A-.

**2. Hub and encyclopedia: earn their place?**
Yes, mostly. The hub is a router: every hard number (cage size, bar spacing, diet split, budget) is followed by "(link: ...)" to the article that owns it, so it doesn't compete with the deep dives, it summarizes and sends you onward. The encyclopedia is pure natural history and doesn't touch care advice at all, so there's no overlap risk there.
No disagreement found. Hub: "18x18x18 inches... a wider flight-style cage... closer to 30x18x18 inches for a pair." Cage setup guide: "An absolute minimum for a single budgie is often cited around 18x18x18 inches, but a wider flight-style cage serves them far better, something closer to 30x18x18 inches for a pair." Same numbers, same hedging. Diet split (60-80% pellets), temperature range (65-85°F, ideal 70-75°F), and cost figures ($175-475 setup, $350-900 first year) all match word for word between hub and their source articles.

**3. Could I actually keep this animal after reading the whole set?**
Yes, for day-to-day care: cage, diet, taming, enrichment, and red-flag symptoms are all covered with specific numbers. What's thinner is the "first 48 hours" bridge: the hub says to quarantine 30-45 days and settle the bird a week or two before taming, but nothing in this set ties those two clocks together, does taming prep start during quarantine or after, and does day one of "settle" overlap with day one of "quarantine"? Also unanswered: how do you actually find or vet an avian vet, several pages tell you to "find one before you need one" but none say what to look for.
A question raised without its answer flagged: the cost guide names an initial well-bird exam ($45-150) but doesn't say when a new owner should schedule it relative to the quarantine period; the health issues guide is the natural place for that and doesn't cover it either.

**4. Overlap and conflict across the deep dives**
Heavy, intentional overlap: cage size, bar spacing, and the 60-80% pellet split are repeated in the hub, cost guide, and cage setup guide, and the pellet split shows up a third time in the feeding guide. No numbers disagree between them.
The cage setup guide and the feeding guide both cover diet, but from different angles (the cage guide gives the split and the "why," the feeding guide gives the mechanics and the iodine/goiter risk), so it reads as intentional depth rather than filler.
One soft overlap: the health issues guide mentions goiter briefly ("less common with a modern pelleted diet but still worth knowing") and the feeding guide gives it a full section. No conflict, just different depth, and the feeding guide is clearly the deeper source.
The enrichment guide restates the cage setup guide's "social needs" section almost exactly (a lone budgie needs a companion or heavy daily interaction), which is a real repeat rather than a cross-reference.

**5. Trust**
Nothing raised a real doubt. Sources are named and dated where it matters (VCA, PetMD, Merck Veterinary Manual, a named avian vet's case report, "prices last checked September 2026 at PetSmart"), and claims are hedged appropriately ("often cited," "typically," "one avian vet's case report"). The most convincing line, and the one that reads like it came from someone who has actually kept the bird, is in the feeding guide: "Put a seed mix in front of a budgie and watch what it does. It works the bowl over for the millet, leaves most of the rest, and by the end of the week the dish looks full while the bird has eaten one thing. I have looked after neighbors' budgies on and off for years."

**6. Grade: A-.** Consistent numbers across nine pages, a genuinely useful emergency card, and one first-person passage that sells the whole set's credibility; it loses half a grade because the enrichment guide is thin and the hub-to-quarantine timing gap is never closed. First two changes: give the enrichment guide one budgie-specific detail it doesn't share with every other parrot page, and add a line somewhere tying the quarantine clock to the taming clock so a new owner knows which one starts first.

**7. Not covered anywhere**
- How to actually find or evaluate an avian vet before you need one, beyond "find one before you need one."
- Whether the initial well-bird vet exam should happen during or after the 30-45 day quarantine period.
