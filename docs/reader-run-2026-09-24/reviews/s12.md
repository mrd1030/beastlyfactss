## ferret

**1. Page by page**
- Hub (00): A. First week numbers, emergency card, and buy list all in one place; you'd act on this immediately.
- Encyclopedia (01): B. Worth finishing for the 1933 flu history, but it gives you nothing to act on.
- Cost guide (02): A. Real, dated dollar ranges and an honest split between setup cost and the vet bills that actually add up.
- Handling guide (03): A. Explains why kits nip, the fix, and the deafness note that reframes "ignoring me" behavior.
- Health issues guide (04): A. Names the real emergencies and gives concrete watch-for lists per condition.
- Cage setup guide (05): A. Cage size, the overheating danger, and the ferret-proofing step most owners skip.
- Feeding guide (06): A. Schedule, banned foods, and a same-day-vet threshold stated in hours, not days.
- Enrichment guide (07): A. One specific, checkable behavior (lying awake, eyes open) instead of vague "provide enrichment" advice.
- Adrenal disease guide (09): B (thin against 04). Adds a useful symptom table and treatment options, but its causes section repeats article 04 almost word for word, including the same Dutch neutering study and fun fact.

**2. The hub and the encyclopedia**
The hub condenses deep-dive numbers into a first-week checklist without just re-running the articles; it earns its place as an index with its own emergency card and buy list. The encyclopedia stays in natural-history lane and never touches care advice, so there's no overlap to worry about. Every figure I cross-checked (cage size, temperature range, budget, lifespan) matched exactly between the hub and the deep dives it links to. No disagreements found.

**3. The set as a whole**
Yes, you could set up, feed, handle, and watch for real emergencies after reading this set. What's missing: no guidance on where to actually get a ferret responsibly, just prices for each source. More notably, the cost guide tells you to "check your local laws" and both it and the hub link to a legal guide for state-by-state detail, but that file wasn't included in this set, so the live question "can I even own one where I live" has no answer in what I read. That's not the set's fault exactly, it's a page this set points to and doesn't hand you.

**4. Overlap and conflict across the deep dives**
Articles 04 and 09 both explain the adrenal disease cause debate and the Dutch neutering study, in near-identical language in places. No numbers disagree, it's straight duplication rather than conflict. The handling guide and enrichment guide both cite "several hours of daily out-of-cage time" and agree. The cage setup guide and health issues guide both cover GI blockage from swallowed rubber and foam, consistently, and cross-link to each other.

**5. Trust**
The adrenal disease prevalence range (under 1% to 70%, depending on the study) is presented as genuinely debated instead of flattened into one authoritative-sounding number, and the disputed fat percentage in the diet section gets the same honest treatment. The sentence that most convinced me a real keeper or vet-adjacent person wrote this: "A healthy adult ferret can develop dangerous blood sugar drops within a single day of not eating... hours matter here, not days." That's specific and practical in a way a copied pet-blog page usually isn't.

**6. Grade**
A-minus. Thorough, internally consistent, and honest about disputed numbers. It loses a notch for the 04/09 duplication and for dangling the legal-status question on a page not in this set. Two changes I'd make first: differentiate or merge articles 04 and 09 so the neutering debate isn't told twice almost verbatim, and make sure the legal guide travels with this set so "is this even legal here" doesn't dead-end.

**7. Not covered anywhere**
- Where to actually buy or adopt a ferret responsibly, beyond price by source: not covered.
- Cost or duration of the deslorelin implant named as a treatment option in article 09: not covered, and it's also absent from the cost guide.
- Legal status by state: not "not covered," it's covered by a linked article that simply isn't in this file set, so it doesn't count as a true gap in the site, just a gap in what I could check.

## gargoyle-gecko

**1. Page by page**
- Hub (00): A. First week numbers, emergency card, buy list, and a genuinely useful FAQ.
- Encyclopedia (01): A. The taxonomic history (the 2012 genus split, the export-permit freeze) is interesting on its own and quietly explains why captive lines exist at all.
- Cost guide (02): A. Clean price bands by morph, and an honest framing of the real cost being a fifteen-to-twenty-year commitment.
- Handling guide (03): A. Age-based temperament, both defense mechanisms explained clearly, and a direct crested gecko comparison that actually helps you choose between the two.
- Health issues guide (04): A. Ties each condition to a specific husbandry cause rather than listing symptoms in a vacuum.
- Tank setup guide (05): A. Names and corrects a specific misconception ("no heat needed") instead of just stating numbers.
- Feeding guide (06): A. States real source disagreements outright, including debunking an unsourced "avocado kills within 24 hours" claim while keeping the avoid-avocado advice as precaution.
- Enrichment guide (07): B+ (honestly thin). No gargoyle-specific research exists, and the article says so upfront rather than faking it, but that also makes it the least actionable page in the set.

**2. The hub and the encyclopedia**
The hub pulls numbers from the deep dives into a first-week list without restating them at length, and the encyclopedia never strays into care advice, so neither duplicates the other. Every number I checked (enclosure size, temperature range and ceiling, humidity target, budget, lifespan) matched across the hub and the linked deep dives. No disagreements found.

**3. The set as a whole**
Yes, this is fully actionable: setup, feeding, handling, and the four real emergencies are all covered with specifics. The set is unusually good at flagging when a question is answered elsewhere, the health guide names the tank guide's substrate section directly, the feeding guide points to the encyclopedia and a separate gut-loading guide. One thing left to infer rather than stated plainly: the encyclopedia mentions New Caledonia stopped issuing export permits, which implies every pet-trade gargoyle is a captive-bred descendant of older stock, but no page says that outright.

**4. Overlap and conflict across the deep dives**
Floppy tail syndrome and respiratory infection each appear in both the tank setup guide and the health issues guide, but from different angles (prevention versus symptoms) and each links to the other, so it reads as deliberate rather than padded. The closest thing to a conflict is inside the feeding guide itself: one detailed care sheet splits the feeding schedule differently than the majority of sources, and the article says so directly rather than hiding it, so it's a disclosed disagreement, not a hidden one.

**5. Trust**
The feeding guide tracing the "avocado is lethal within 24 hours" claim back to nothing and saying so, while still keeping the avoid-avocado guidance as precautionary, is careful writing you don't get from a rewritten pet-blog page. The sentence that most convinced me a real keeper wrote this: the enrichment guide's flat admission that "nothing on Rhacodactylus auriculatus" exists, instead of dressing up borrowed crested and leopard gecko research as species-specific.

**6. Grade**
A. Consistent, well cross-linked, and unusually honest about the limits of the evidence behind it. Two changes I'd make first: give the enrichment guide more to work with even if it stays borrowed, since it's the thinnest page in an otherwise strong set, and add one plain sentence stating that pet-trade gargoyles are captive-bred only, since the encyclopedia implies it but never says it.

**7. Not covered anywhere**
- Breeding, sexing, or egg incubation: the health guide mentions females "lay eggs" in passing but nothing in this set covers reproduction.
- A concrete UVB bulb brand or wattage example: the set specifies "12-inch low-output T5" but never names a product the way the feeding guide names specific diet brands.
- None beyond that.
