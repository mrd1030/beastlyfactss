## red-footed-tortoise

**1. Page by page**

Encyclopedia: Finish it. The pastel de morrocoy trade history and smuggling estimate are real context no other page carries. A

**2. Hub and encyclopedia**

Both earn their place. The hub compresses seven articles into first-week numbers; the encyclopedia carries taxonomic and trade history (Spix's 1824 description, the 1982 and 2017 name changes, CITES export figures) found nowhere else. No disagreement found: the hub's enclosure size (18-24 sq ft), temperature (90-95°F basking, 70°F night floor), humidity (70-80%), and diet split (90/10, with the 70/20/10 leafy-green/vegetable/fruit breakdown) all match the tank setup and feeding articles exactly, including the same fruit percentage nuance (10% in one table, "closer to 15%" in the prose) appearing consistently in both the hub and the feeding article rather than drifting between them.

**3. The set as a whole**

Yes. This is the most tightly built of the four sets: enclosure type (enclosed, not open-top, with the mechanism explained), humidity target and how to hit it, temperature day and night, diet ratio with a specific protein source list, and the full disease list with causes are all here. The constant, explicit contrast against Russian and sulcata tortoises (repeated in the cost, handling, health, and tank setup articles) means a reader who has looked at either of those other guides never gets confused about which rules apply to which species.

One question raised and only partly closed: the tank setup article says a red-foot needs a lower UVB "Ferguson zone" than open-habitat tortoises and offers either a strong T5 HO or a weaker Arcadia Forest 6% as valid options, but doesn't say how a keeper should decide between the two for their specific setup beyond "give real shade options either way." That's a genuine unresolved choice left to the reader, though it's honestly flagged as such rather than glossed over.

**4. Overlap and conflict across the deep dives**

Read twice: the Russian/sulcata three-way comparison (size, cost, handling, brumation) appears in the cost, handling, and tank setup articles, each time making a consistent point. The 50-70+ year lifespan and the "commitment can outlast the owner" framing repeat in the hub and cost article verbatim.

No conflicts found. The shell rot section is a good example of the set's internal discipline: the health article, tank setup article, and hub all state the same nuance the same way, that humidity itself doesn't cause shell rot, stagnant or soggy substrate does, without any page slipping into treating "humid" and "wet" as the same risk.

**5. Trust**

The specific study citations carry real weight: a controlled study on red-footed tortoise hatchlings comparing high-starch to high-fiber diets for pyramiding and bone density, and a named 2025 cognitive bias study with an exact sample size (15 tortoises), are both too specific and too easy to check to be invented filler. The line that most convinced me a real keeper wrote this: "A tortoise fed one chopped salad in one dish gets no foraging behavior at all, which is most of what these animals do with their day in the wild." That's a keeper's frustration with bad advice, not a rewritten fact sheet.

Nothing raised doubt. Even the fun facts (clucking males, spoon-sized hatchlings) are used to illustrate real husbandry points rather than as padding.

**6. Grade: A.** Reason: internally consistent, well sourced, and unusually good at flagging exactly where its advice diverges from other tortoise species on this same site, which is the most common place a care set silently confuses a reader.

Two changes: give a concrete decision rule for choosing between the T5 HO and Arcadia Forest 6% UVB options rather than leaving both on the table. Add the specific soak depth/temperature/minutes for this species directly into the feeding or enclosure article instead of only referencing the water dish in passing, since soaking is mentioned three times across this set but never with a number attached here.
