## boa-constrictor

**1. Page by page**
- 00 Hub: A. Finish it. First-week numbers, an emergency card, a buy list, all actionable on day one.
- 01 Encyclopedia: B+. Finish it. Short, but the Cayos Cochinos collection story is real, specific, and not repeated anywhere else in the set.
- 02 Cost guide: A. Finish it. Real prices, a dated snapshot, and a named source list for the retail numbers.
- 03 Handling guide: A. Finish it. The most heavily sourced page in the set (ReptiFiles, LafeberVet, Florida Museum, a 2011 bite-infection review), and it changes what you'd actually do.
- 04 Health issues guide: B. Finish it. Actionable symptom list, but every section ends "always see a vet" with no source behind any of it, thin compared to the handling guide's rigor.
- 05 Tank setup guide: A. Finish it. Concrete dimensions, a sizing formula, and the two-hide rule explained rather than just stated.
- 06 Feeding guide: A-. Finish it. Clear schedule and the prey-width rule, though it never says what to do if a boa refuses a meal.
- 07 Enrichment guide: A-. Finish it. Unusually honest that no boa-specific research exists, and still gives a workable priority order.

**2. Hub and encyclopedia**
Both earn their place. The hub is a router with real numbers pulled from the deep dives (I checked every one: temperature, humidity, enclosure size, feeding schedule, handling threshold, lifespan, and budget all match their source article word for word). It doesn't just repeat the deep dives, it compresses them into a usable first-week list you wouldn't want to hunt for across seven pages. The encyclopedia isn't just a stats box either, the CITES trade history and the Cayos Cochinos near-wipeout is unique content.

No disagreement found between hub and any deep dive. Example check: hub says "Basking area 88 to 92°F, occasionally up to 95°F, ambient warm side 80 to 85°F, cool side 75 to 80°F, nighttime 70 to 80°F," and the tank setup guide says the identical thing verbatim. Same for the feeding schedule and the $400 to $1,200 setup cost. This set is internally consistent, which is itself worth noting given how often that isn't true.

**3. Could I actually keep this animal**
Mostly yes. I could set the enclosure up (05), budget for it (02), feed on schedule (06), and recognize an emergency (04, hub card). What's missing: nobody tells me how to actually secure the enclosure door against an animal this strong. The hub warns the heat source must be "genuinely inaccessible," which implies the snake can push at things, but no page covers locking clips or latches for the enclosure itself. Nobody says what to do if an adult boa skips a meal, which will happen on a 2-to-4-week cycle and will worry a first-timer. Nobody gives a checklist for what makes a breeder "reputable," a phrase used twice (health guide, implicitly cost guide) as the entire IBD-prevention plan.

Where one page raises a question another answers without saying so: the health guide says IBD prevention is "buying captive-bred animals from reputable, established breeders," but the cost guide's lineage-fraud warning (pet stores selling imperators as red-tails) is directly relevant to vetting a seller and isn't cross-linked. The tank setup guide recommends a front-opening enclosure "for your own ease of access with an animal this size," but never connects that to the handling guide's second-person rule, both are really the same size-driven decision made twice, independently.

**4. Overlap and conflict**
The boa-vs-ball-python comparison appears in the cost guide, handling guide (twice, nearly identical wording), and tank setup guide. No numbers conflict, but "boas are bigger, more food-motivated, and confident" is close to a repeated stock line rather than three separate observations.
The 48-hour post-feeding wait is stated in the hub, handling guide, and feeding guide. Consistent every time, and each instance credits it to the same reasoning (regurgitation risk), so this reads as intentional cross-referencing rather than padding.
Two hides (warm and cool) appears in the hub, tank setup guide, and enrichment guide, again consistent, no conflict.
I found no numeric disagreement anywhere in the set.

**5. Trust**
The handling guide's citations are specific enough to check (LafeberVet's posture notes, the Florida Museum on musking, a named 2011 bite-infection review), which is unusual rigor for a pet-care page. The sentence that most convinced me an actual keeper wrote this: "never allow a boa or python to form a complete loop around your neck... there have been documented deaths from exactly this scenario with large captive constrictors." That's a specific, high-stakes warning stated plainly, not hedged. The enrichment guide admitting "nothing has been published on boa enrichment" instead of inventing a citation is the other strong trust signal. What cost some trust: the health guide has zero citations while sitting next to a handling guide with five, and its four sections each end on the same "always see a vet" template.

**6. Grade and fixes**
Grade: A-. Internally consistent, mostly sourced, and the hub actually compresses the deep dives instead of padding around them.
First fix: source the health issues guide the way the handling guide is sourced, right now it's the weakest page next to the strongest.
Second fix: add enclosure security (locks, latches) as its own item, given how much the set already emphasizes this snake's strength.

**Not covered anywhere**
- How to secure the enclosure against an animal this strong (locks, latches).
- What to do if an adult boa refuses a scheduled meal.
- A concrete checklist for what makes a breeder "reputable."
- UVB bulb type or wattage, beyond "a low level of UVB" being recommended.
