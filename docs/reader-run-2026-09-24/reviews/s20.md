## angelfish

**1. Page by page**

Hub: finish it, it front-loads every number you need and links out cleanly. A.
Encyclopedia: finish it, the taxonomy history (Schultze filing it as a marine dory in 1823, the 2022 genome by a high schooler) earns its spot. A-.
Cost guide: finish it, clear tables and a real freshwater-versus-marine warning. A.
Handling/territorial guide: finish it, the "raised together, still eaten later" point is the kind of thing a first-timer needs before buying, not after. A.
Health issues guide: finish it, concise and tied back to husbandry rather than a symptom list. A-.
Tank setup guide: finish it, the height-over-length argument and the lid warning are both genuinely non-obvious. A.
Feeding guide: finish it, the sunken-belly-despite-eating point is the standout fact. A.
Enrichment guide: finish it, a real cited study with a named null result instead of the usual generic "provide enrichment" filler. A.

**2. Hub and encyclopedia**

Both earn their place. The hub is a router with numbers and links, not a rewrite of the deep dives, and the encyclopedia is the only page that carries the natural history and the import/genome timeline, nothing else touches that. No disagreement found between hub and any deep dive: tank sizes (29 gal single, 55 gal group), temperature (78-82°F), pH (6.5-7.0), and the 8 to 12 year lifespan all match word for word between the hub and the linked articles.

**3. The set as a whole**

Yes, you could set this fish up, stock it, feed it, and watch for trouble. The gap is medication and the hospital tank: the health guide and hub both point to metronidazole and copper/anti-ich treatments and to a separate quarantine article, but that quarantine article is not in this set, so "how long do I actually isolate a new fish" (the hub says 30 to 60 days) is answered only by a link, not by anything read here. The feeding guide raises "why did my angelfish stop eating" and answers it well on its own, but the parasite behind the sunken-belly case is never named past "a stomach parasite specific to cichlids," which is a question a reader would carry into a vet visit unanswered. The tank setup guide tells you to add angelfish last; the handling guide is where the predation reasoning for that rule actually lives, and the tank setup piece links to it, so that connection is made explicitly, which is more than most of these sites bother to do.

**4. Overlap and conflict among the deep dives**

Heavy, deliberate overlap: the sunken-belly parasite point appears in the hub, feeding guide, and feeding guide's own FAQ; the "add last" rule appears in the hub, handling guide, tank setup guide, and enrichment guide; the 8 to 12 year lifespan appears in four different articles. None of it disagrees. Numbers for tank size, temperature, pH, filtration, wattage, and the 2-to-5-minute feeding rule are identical everywhere they appear. The closest thing to friction is the "how long can it go without food" section, which openly juggles two weeks (survival), one week (planning ceiling), and 7 to 10 days (generally fine) in the same paragraph and calls it hobbyist consensus rather than pretending it's one hard number, which reads as honest rather than sloppy.

**5. Trust**

Nothing here reads as padded or made up. The specificity is what sells it: brand names for medication (Fish Zole, MetroPlex), a named 2010 peer-reviewed study with authors and journal, and the detail that a "20-gallon long" tank is often only 12 inches tall despite the bigger volume. The line that most convinced me a fishkeeper wrote this: "an adult angelfish will eat small fish... even to small fish the angelfish was raised alongside as a juvenile." That is not a generic warning, it is a specific, counterintuitive failure mode someone has clearly watched happen.

**6. Grade and two changes**

Grade: A-. Reason: accurate, consistent, well-linked, and it tells you the things that actually surprise a new owner instead of restating "keep the tank clean." Two changes: bring the linked quarantine and ich-temperature-ceiling articles into the same set so the 30-to-60-day number and the treatment plan aren't left as dangling links, and name the actual parasites behind hexamita and the sunken-belly condition instead of "a stomach parasite specific to cichlids," since a reader will want that word before calling a vet.

**7. Not covered anywhere**

- The exact quarantine duration logic and what a hospital tank setup looks like: only linked, not included in this set.
- The name of the specific stomach parasite that causes sunken belly despite normal eating: referenced twice, never named.
- What "aquatic vet" actually means in practice, i.e. how to find one: mentioned as rare and specialized, no pointer given.

## degu

**1. Page by page**

Hub: finish it, dense and well organized, doubles as an emergency card. A.
Encyclopedia: finish it, the 1964 MIT-to-Vermont colony history is a genuinely good hook. A.
Cost guide: finish it, but the cage price table lists a "24x24x48 in" cage while the rest of the set says 28x18x28. B (conflict).
Handling guide: finish it, the hamster contrast (solitary vs. never-solo) is used well and consistently. A.
Health issues guide: finish it, ties every disease back to a specific husbandry cause. A.
Enclosure setup guide: finish it, but it contradicts its own FAQ on cage size. B (conflict, see below).
Feeding guide: finish it, the sugar section is the strongest writing in the set. A.
Enrichment guide: finish it, a cited study on scent-marked sand baths, unusually specific for a "provide enrichment" page. A.
Degu vs. chinchilla guide: finish it, useful comparison, but its lifespan figure for degu (5-9 years) doesn't match the rest of the set (5-8 years).

**2. Hub and encyclopedia**

Both earn their place, the hub for the numbers and the emergency card, the encyclopedia for the wild-colony ecology and research history that no other page carries. One disagreement did surface between the hub and a deep dive: the hub states cage size as "About 28 by 18 by 28 inches minimum for a pair," which matches the enclosure guide's own body text ("About 28 by 18 by 28 inches is the minimum for a pair"), but the enclosure guide's own FAQ answers the same question with "24 by 18 by 24 inches for a pair at minimum." That is a contradiction inside a single article, not just between pages.

**3. The set as a whole**

Mostly yes: you could house, feed, and watch this animal for trouble by the end of the set. Two real gaps. First, the legal status article is referenced constantly (hub, FAQ, "where to go next") but was not among the files provided in this set, so "is this even legal where I live," which the hub calls the most regulated small rodent sold as a pet, is never actually answered here. Second, cage size is genuinely unresolved across the set: the hub and the enclosure article's body both say 28x18x28 inches, the same article's FAQ says 24x18x24, and the cost guide prices a "24x24x48 in multi-level cage." A reader trying to actually buy a cage has three different numbers to reconcile and no page tells them which one is real. The sand-versus-dust question is at least handled honestly: the hub flags outright that the enrichment guide recommends sand while the cost guide prices chinchilla dust, and says the card is following the enrichment guide, which is more transparency than most sites give a self-contradiction.

**4. Overlap and conflict among the deep dives**

The "never one, never by the tail, diabetes from any sugar" trio repeats across the hub, handling guide, health guide, feeding guide, and enrichment guide, consistently and without contradiction on the substance. The wheel size does have a small conflict: the hub and enclosure guide both say "11 to 12 inches," but the cost guide table prices a "12-14 in solid exercise wheel." Lifespan has the same kind of small slippage: 5 to 8 years everywhere except the degu-vs-chinchilla comparison table, which says 5 to 9. Neither of these is dramatic, but between them and the cage-size problem, the set has three separate places where a number quietly drifts between pages that are otherwise careful.

**5. Trust**

The research citations are what sell this set: a specific 2010-and-later body of work on dust bathing as scent communication, the detail that isolated degus self-mutilate, and the very specific 1964-to-1978 lab colony history down to named people and animal counts. The line that most convinced me a real keeper wrote this: "Let them eat their own droppings. It is a nutritional requirement rather than a habit to discourage." That is a detail nobody adds unless they have actually kept the animal and had to explain it to someone recoiling from it. Against that, the three-way cage-size conflict is the one thing that actively undercuts trust, since it is not a minor rounding difference, it is a contradiction inside the same article's own body and FAQ.

**6. Grade and two changes**

Grade: B+. Reason: excellent research and voice, undercut by a genuinely confusing and unresolved cage-size number that appears three different ways, one of them self-contradicting. Two changes: fix the enclosure guide's FAQ to match its own body text and correct the cost guide's cage dimensions to match, and either add the legal-status article to future sets like this one or stop linking to it as if it were already covered.

**7. Not covered anywhere**

- Which states or countries actually restrict or ban degu ownership: the legal guide is linked repeatedly but not included in this set.
- The correct cage dimension, given three conflicting numbers (28x18x28, 24x18x24, 24x24x48) across three pages.
- None beyond those two; feeding, health, handling, and enrichment questions all resolve inside the set.
