## zebra-danio

**1. Page by page**

Hub: Finish it. Concise, sourced, and the FAQ explicitly flags where sources disagree (5-6 vs 8-10 fish) instead of hiding it. A
Encyclopedia: Finish it. The Streisinger/zebrafish lab history is genuinely interesting and appears nowhere else. A
Cost guide: Finish it. The GloFish price comparison (same species, 4x the price) is a nice piece of consumer information most sites skip. A
Handling guide: Finish it. Specific technique (trap-and-guide) rather than vague advice. A
Health issues guide: Finish it. The mycobacteriosis section is unusually deep and includes the human-infection angle most fish sites leave out entirely. A
Tank setup guide: Finish it. Shows two outside sources agreeing (Seriously Fish, Aquarium Co-Op) rather than just asserting a number. A
Feeding guide: Finish it. The live-food-triggers-spawning warning is exactly the kind of thing a beginner needs before it happens to them. A
Enrichment guide: Finish it. Leads with a real citation (Lee et al. 2019) measuring survivorship, not just behavior, which is a stronger form of evidence than most enrichment pages offer. A

**2. Hub and encyclopedia**

Both earn their place, and this pair is the best cross-checked of the two species sets read here. The hub's numbers (10 gal minimum / 20 gal long better, 64-77°F, pH 6.0-8.0, 4-5x turnover, 20-30cm jump height) match every deep dive exactly, down to which named source (Seriously Fish, Aquarium Co-Op) backs which number. No disagreement found between hub and any deep dive. The encyclopedia and hub don't repeat each other either: the encyclopedia carries natural history and lab-science background, the hub carries only actionable numbers.

**3. The set as a whole**

Yes, fully. Tank size, temperature (heater genuinely optional, and the reasoning is given), water chemistry, filtration turnover rate, feeding frequency and portion, the jump-lid warning, and disease identification are all here with specific numbers and sources. This is the most complete and best-connected of the four sets reviewed.

The one place a question is raised and only partly answered within this set: the feeding guide says a planted tank produces "better larval survival and less anxious adults than bare ones" per the enrichment research, then recommends keeping the tank planted rather than stripping it to prevent an accidental spawn. That's a real cross-reference to the enrichment page, and it's handled correctly, actually named ("weigh that last part against what the enrichment research measured") rather than left implicit.

**4. Overlap and conflict across the deep dives**

Read twice: the school-size disagreement (Aquarium Co-Op's 5-6 vs Seriously Fish's 8-10) is repeated identically, with the same two named sources, in the hub, handling guide, and tank setup guide. The 64-77°F range and the "heater is optional" point also repeat across the cost, tank setup, and hub pages, consistently.

No numeric conflicts found anywhere in this set. Even the two-source school-size disagreement is presented the same way every time it appears (name both sources, give both numbers, recommend the higher one), rather than drifting between pages.

**5. Trust**

The specific research citations do real work here: a named 2005 study in Transactions of the American Fisheries Society with exact critical thermal maxima (41.7°C) and minima (6.2°C), and a 2019 Journal of Fish Biology paper measuring larval survivorship rather than just behavior, are both the kind of detail that takes real reading to find and would be pointless to fabricate for a fish this cheap. The line that most convinced me a real keeper wrote this: "The commonest mistake with this species is a small cube. Danios in a short tank do laps, and the behavior that gets described as energetic is a fish running out of tank." That's an observation, not a fact pulled from a care sheet.

Nothing here raised doubt. Even the one place the set could have oversold itself, the CTMax/CTMin fun fact, is immediately walked back: "the real comfort range to aim for is the much narrower 64 to 77°F band... not the edges of what the species can endure."

**6. Grade: A.** Reason: complete, internally consistent, transparent about source disagreement instead of hiding it, and backed by real primary research rather than recycled hobbyist claims.

Two changes, both minor since there is little to actually fix: give an explicit number or range for how many eggs/fry survive an "accidental" spawn in a planted community tank, since the feeding guide raises the tradeoff but never quantifies it. Add one line to the health guide on what water parameter to check first when swim bladder disorder shows up, since "poor water quality" is named as the top cause but the guide doesn't say which reading (ammonia, nitrite, temperature swing) to check first.

**7. Not covered anywhere**

- Rough odds or fry count for an unplanned spawn in a planted community tank. None.
- Which specific water parameter to test first for suspected swim bladder disorder. None.
