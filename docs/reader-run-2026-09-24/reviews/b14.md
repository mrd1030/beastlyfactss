2. IVDD: Why Short Legs and Bad Backs Come From the Same Gene: would finish it, openly overturns the standard "restrict exercise and ban stairs" advice with cited odds ratios instead of just repeating it. Grade A.
3. Labrador Enrichment: A Gene Deletion Explains the Appetite: would finish it, one clean study (Raffan et al., Cell Metabolism, 2016) turns "Labradors are always hungry" into an actual, actionable feeding plan. Grade A-.

Nothing here drops to a C or lower, so there is no fix list to run. The only real note is the succession planning piece, and the fix there is not about the writing, it is about where it lives: it would sit more naturally in a birds or exotics folder.

Title versus content gaps: only one worth flagging. The succession planning article is filed in a bundle called "dogs-cats-general," but its entire content, lifespan tables, guardian planning, pet trusts, is built around cockatoos and tortoises specifically because dogs and cats rarely outlive their owners the way those species do. A reader who opened this bundle expecting a dog or cat succession angle would find none. Every other article in this set delivers exactly what its title promises, the eye disease guide even flags its own gaps (no feline certification scheme exists, and it says so plainly rather than pretending otherwise).

Overlap and conflict: no numeric disagreements turned up anywhere in this set. Where two articles share ground, they reinforce rather than contradict each other. The Persian enrichment guide's mention that polycystic kidney disease is "strongly associated with the breed" lines up with how the eye disease guide treats Persian-linked conditions, and both correctly attribute the breed's problems to flat-face conformation rather than to the coat or temperament. The dental disease and eye disease guides both handle the same kind of apparent contradiction the same careful way: numbers that look inconsistent between studies turn out to be explained by different diagnostic methods (anesthetized exam versus conscious consult for dental disease, DNA test versus eye exam for PRA and CEA), and both articles walk through why rather than just picking the bigger number. That kind of internal consistency in method, even across different topics, is a good sign for the site as a whole.

Trust: this is the strongest batch reviewed so far for a specific reason, several articles go out of their way to correct their own field's popular wisdom instead of repeating it. The IVDD guide states outright that "there is no evidence supporting the widely repeated 48-hour cutoff beyond which recovery is supposedly impossible," and that banning stairs and restricting exercise in chondrodystrophic breeds may be "counter-productive," backed by actual odds ratios rather than folk wisdom. The eye disease guide flatly states that the popular explanation for tear overflow in flat-faced breeds, that facial folds physically press on the tear ducts, "is wrong," and gives the real mechanism instead. Nothing in the batch reads as an unsupported or overstated claim; if anything, several articles are more cautious than typical pet content, explicitly calling out where a widely repeated statistic (the 39.9% versus 9.90% eyeball-orbit figure, for instance) comes from a small sample and should not be over-read. The most convincing sentence in the set is from the dental disease guide, precisely because it resolves a contradiction instead of hiding it: "Those numbers look contradictory and are not. The high figures come from anesthetized examinations with probing. The low figures are how often a diagnosis gets recorded during a conscious consult."

Bundle grade: A. Reason: five of six articles are exceptionally well sourced and unusually willing to correct their own field's popular myths, and the sixth is a strong article that simply belongs in a different folder. Two changes first: move or duplicate the succession planning piece into a folder that actually matches its content (birds and tortoises, not dogs and cats), and, if the site ever collects the myth-corrections these articles make (the 48-hour spinal cutoff, the tear-duct fold explanation, the raised-bowl bloat advice elsewhere in the health hub), it would make a strong standalone piece in its own right since the pattern is clearly there across multiple guides already.
