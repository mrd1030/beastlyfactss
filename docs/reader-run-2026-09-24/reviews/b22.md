3. Title-versus-content gaps
- Welcome to BeastlyFacts: no real gap, though a first-time reader gets no sense of the site's tone or a sample topic beyond one linked article (the giraffe heart myth), so the "welcome" doesn't actually orient a new visitor toward what kind of animal content dominates.
- Crows Are Smarter Than You Think: the title promises smarts generally, but the piece leans heavily on tool use, memory, and social behavior while barely touching problem-solving speed or comparative IQ-style measures readers sometimes expect from "how smart" framing; not a real flaw, just worth knowing going in.

4. Overlap and conflict
This bundle is unusually well cross-linked and internally consistent. The constriction article and the gaboon viper article both mention each other accurately (constrictors skip venom, vipers skip constriction) with no contradiction. The chameleon color article and the reptile-awareness-day article from a different bundle both state chameleons don't change color to match backgrounds; consistent. No numeric or factual disagreements were found between any two articles here. One soft overlap: both the crows article and the dolphins article discuss mirror self-recognition and cite it as contested science in almost identical terms (crows: "mixed and debated"; dolphins: extensive discussion of the same critique), which is repetition of a caveat rather than a conflict.

5. Trust
The dolphin names article is the most convincing in the bundle because it actively resists overstating its own subject, walking back the "dolphins have names/language" pop claim with a side-by-side table of "popular claim" versus "what the research actually shows," and it flags its own cited researchers' caveats rather than hiding them: "an animal may learn that a particular call brings a companion closer without grasping the connection between the call and that individual." The most convincing single sentence in the whole bundle is from the constriction article, since it's concrete and numeric: "Within six seconds of the coils closing, arterial blood pressure at the femoral artery had fallen to half its baseline." Nothing here reads as unsourced or exaggerated; even the crow article walks back its own famous "Betty the hook-bending crow" story once newer research complicated it, which is a good sign for the whole set.

6. Bundle grade: A. Reason: consistently well-sourced, willing to correct popular myths with named studies and specific numbers, and unusually honest about contested or overturned findings rather than picking the more exciting version.
Two changes first: give the welcome/about post one more concrete example article or topic area so a first-time visitor gets a real sense of the site beyond one link, and tighten the repeated "mirror test is contested" caveat across the crow and dolphin articles into a single cross-link rather than two separate full explanations.
