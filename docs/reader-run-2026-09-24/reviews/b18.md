## overviews-2

1. Article ratings. This directory uses the same short comparison template as its companion set and holds the quality just as consistently:

Four Pets People Get Wrong Before You Bring One Home: finish it, learned a red-eared slider needs 75 to 100-plus gallons as an adult and a koi can live 25 to 50 years. Grade A.
Four Pets You Only Really Understand by Comparison: finish it, learned California kingsnakes carry documented resistance to rattlesnake venom but not coral snake venom. Grade A.
Three Livebearers That Breed Whether You Plan to or Not: finish it, learned platies and swordtails share a genus and interbreed, and mollies alone tolerate real salt. Grade A.
Rabbit and Budgie, Two Classic Pets Kept Too Small: finish it, learned budgies need cage width for side-to-side flight, not height. Grade A, though at two species it is the shortest and thinnest entry here.
Three Small Mammals Dismissed as Just Rodents: finish it, learned flying squirrels commonly live around 10 years, sometimes past 19, against a rat's 2 to 3. Grade A.
Three Reptiles Worth Starting With: finish it, learned red-footed tortoises are genuine omnivores that eat carrion and snails in the wild. Grade A.
Four Pets, Four Things Nobody Mentions: finish it, learned cockatoos are reportedly a rescue's most commonly surrendered bird and stick insects sold commercially can carry a federal permit problem. Grade A.
Four Pets Decided Before You Set Up the Tank: finish it, learned a female guppy can produce broods for 5 to 10 months after one mating. Grade A.
Four Animals That Break Their Own Category's Rules: finish it, learned uromastyx are strict herbivores among lizards that mostly eat insects. Grade A.
Three Pets Whose Drama Isn't the Real Story: finish it, learned female veiled chameleons live 2 to 6 years against a male's 6 to 8 because of the toll of egg-laying. Grade A.
Four Fish, Four Completely Different Difficulty Levels: finish it, learned the visual trick for telling cardinal and neon tetras apart and that zebra danios are a major genetics research organism. Grade A.
Two Small Birds, Two Completely Different Pets: finish it, learned zebra finches are flock animals that never bond one-to-one the way parrotlets do. Grade A, another thin two-species entry but still substantive.

2. No article graded C or lower.

3. Title versus content gaps: essentially none across the set. The two-species pieces (Rabbit and Budgie, Two Small Birds) are shorter than the four-species format, which is a real structural gap between what the directory otherwise promises and what these two deliver, but neither one omits anything its narrower title claims to cover.

4. Overlap and conflicts: the savannah monitor's obesity and fatty liver disease framing in "Four Pets, Four Things Nobody Mentions" is consistent with, not contradicted by, its Deep Dive listing elsewhere in the same directory ("Common Savannah Monitor Health Issues, Obesity Is the Defining One"). Tortoise lifespans differ by species without conflicting: red-footed tortoises 50 to 70 years, Russian tortoises 40-plus, sulcatas 70 to 100, each internally consistent within its own article. No numeric claim in this directory contradicts another one in the same set.

5. Trust: the strongest, most checkable claims (California kingsnake venom resistance, zebra danio's roughly 70 percent human gene overlap, the guppy's 5-to-10-month sperm storage) are specific enough and consistently framed with real caveats (resistance is to rattlesnake venom specifically, not coral snake) that they read as sourced. The cockatoo surrender statistic is attributed to "one behavior consultant" rather than a study, which is appropriately hedged rather than stated as settled fact. The most convincing sentence in the bundle is the kingsnake venom-resistance line in "Four Pets You Only Really Understand by Comparison," because it names the specific limit of the resistance rather than oversimplifying it into blanket immunity.

6. Directory grade: A. Reason: twelve comparison pieces, each anchored in specific, checkable facts rather than generic pet-care filler, with real internal linking to the deeper cost, handling, health, and setup guides behind every claim. The only soft spot is structural: two entries cover only two species instead of the format's usual four, making them noticeably shorter than the rest of the set. First two changes: bring the rabbit-and-budgie and zebra-finch-and-parrotlet pieces up to the four-animal format the rest of the directory uses, or explicitly justify the shorter format in the piece itself, and add one clarifying line to the savannah monitor's obesity framing noting it is a captivity-driven problem rather than an inherited one, since "cheap because it's overfed" could otherwise read as blaming the species.
