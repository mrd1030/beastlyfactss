## cockatiel

**1. Page by page**
- 00 Hub: A. Numbers, emergency signs, buy list, all in one stop.
- 01 Encyclopedia: A. The 1788-1832 naming history and the 1949-1967 color-mutation timeline are genuinely distinct content.
- 02 Cost: A. Clear ranges, a September 2026 price date, and an honest note that the cage usually beats the bird as the biggest expense.
- 03 Handling: A. The crest cues, the flat-palm instruction, and "never chase a loose bird" are all actionable.
- 04 Health issues: A. Names the seed-diet-to-vitamin-A-deficiency chain plainly.
- 05 Cage setup: A. Width-over-height reasoning and the 30-minute nonstick-fume detail are concrete.
- 06 Feeding: A. Refreshing in its honesty: "we couldn't find a single, consistently sourced number" for how long a cockatiel can go without eating, rather than inventing one.
- 07 Enrichment: A. Names its own evidence base as thin, two studies, and doesn't oversell it.
- 08 vs. cockatoo: A. A real decision aid, not filler.

**2. Hub and encyclopedia vs. deep dives**
Both earn their place; neither just restates a deep dive. But there's a real number conflict between the hub and one of the deep dives.

The hub says: "Cockatiels live 10 to 15 years, so this is a long-term financial commitment." The cost guide repeats the same 10-15 figure and builds its whole budgeting argument on it. But the vs. cockatoo comparison says, in its table and again in body text: "A cockatiel's 15 to 25 year lifespan is already a real commitment." The encyclopedia itself backs the higher number: "10 to 14 years in the wild; up to 25 years in captivity, the oldest on record 36." So the hub and cost guide are quoting a lifespan that is roughly half what the encyclopedia and the comparison article claim for the upper bound. That's not a rounding difference, it changes the entire "long-term commitment" argument the cost guide is making.

**3. Could I do this?**
Yes, this is the most usable set of the two I reviewed. Cage size, diet ratio, taming steps, toxic foods, and the emergency sign list are all consistent and specific. Two loose ends:
- The enrichment guide lists "bathing opportunity" as priority 5 but never says how often or how (misting, a shallow dish, a shower perch), and nothing else in the set covers it.
- That article also says company can be "a compatible bird or a real daily routine with you," but the only companion-keeping page in the Health and More list, Colony and Aviary Keeping, names only budgies, canaries, and finches, not cockatiels, so how to actually pair a cockatiel with another bird is raised and never answered here.

**4. Overlap and conflicts across the deep dives**
Heavy repetition, expected: the 75-80% pellet ratio, the 20-25% fresh food cap, the 0.5-inch bar spacing, and the nonstick-cookware warning all appear near-verbatim in three or more articles. The lifespan conflict above is the one real numeric disagreement: cost guide "10 to 15 year lifespan" versus vs. cockatoo guide "15-25 year lifespan" and the encyclopedia's "up to 25 years... oldest on record 36."

**5. Trust**
The line that most convinced me a real keeper wrote this: "We couldn't find a single, consistently sourced number for exactly how many hours or days a cockatiel can safely go without eating, and would rather say that honestly than invent a round figure." That's not something a content mill writes. The specific detail that a teaspoon of treats for a cockatiel equals "a dinner-plate-sized portion for a person" also reads like real husbandry knowledge, not filler.

What made me doubt them: the 10-15 vs. 15-25 year lifespan split. It's the kind of number a careless second writer would grab from a different source without checking the site's own encyclopedia page.

**6. Grade: A-.** Deep, specific, admirably honest about its own evidence gaps, undercut by one real lifespan conflict that touches the cost guide's core argument.
Two fixes: reconcile the 10-15 vs. 15-25 year lifespan number across the hub, cost guide, and vs. cockatoo article, and add bathing frequency/method somewhere in the set.

**Not covered anywhere**
- Bathing frequency and method for a cockatiel. None.
- How to actually pair a cockatiel with a companion bird (species compatibility, introduction). None; the only companion-keeping page named covers budgies, canaries, and finches, not cockatiels.
