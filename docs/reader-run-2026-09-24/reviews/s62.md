## betta-fish

1. Page by page:
- Hub: A. Tight first-week numbers and an emergency card that's actually specific (dropsy, columnaris signs, velvet sheen).
- Encyclopedia: A-. The 1840-to-1909 naming history (Cantor, then Regan) is a genuine differentiator from the hub, not filler.
- Cost guide: A. Clear tables, dated prices, honest that food is not the real cost (electricity is).
- Handling guide: A. Correctly frames "handling" as mostly not handling, with real transfer technique.
- Health issues guide: A, and the standout page of the set. The fin-rot-vs-regeneration section with zebrafish and turquoise killifish regrowth percentages by age is unusually rigorous for a pet care site.
- Tank setup guide: A. Every number a beginner needs, matches hub and water parameters guide.
- Feeding guide: A. Honestly flags that sources disagree on frequency rather than inventing false precision.
- Enrichment guide: A. Names three real studies and is blunt that a mirror is a stress test, not a toy.
- Vs. goldfish guide: A. Clean, no filler, corrects the "3-second memory" myth in passing.
- Water parameters guide: A. The ammonia/nitrite zero-tolerance point is made forcefully and correctly.

2. The hub and encyclopedia earn their place. The hub is the actionable checklist; the encyclopedia carries the Siamese-fighting-fish history and the 1840s-1900s spread through France, Germany, and California that no other page in the set touches. No numeric disagreement between them: temperature (76-82°F), size (2.5-3 in), and lifespan (2-4 years, up to 5) all match.

3. After this set you could cycle a tank, buy the right gear, feed correctly, and recognize the illnesses that matter, but two questions raised here have answers only in the Health and More list, not in this set: what specific tankmates and group sizes actually work (the hub says "corydoras, mystery snails, ghost shrimp or small tetras" and "never tiger barbs," but the mechanics live in the separate Aquarium Stocking and Tankmates guide), and how to actually cycle a tank before adding the fish (the hub and tank setup guide both say "cycle first" but point to a separate cycling guide for the how).

4. Overlap and conflict: pyramiding-style repetition here is the "test water before medicating" message, repeated near-verbatim across the hub FAQ, cost guide, and health guide FAQ, consistent every time. One soft tension: the feeding guide's own text says sources disagree on frequency (once a day vs. up to three smaller meals), while the tank setup guide's FAQ flatly states "2 to 4 small, high-protein betta pellets, once or twice a day" as if it were settled. Not a hard numeric conflict, just an inconsistent level of hedging on the same question.

5. Trust: this is the most scientifically careful set of the four. Citing zebrafish fin regeneration timelines and turquoise killifish regrowth rates by age (98% young, 46% old) to explain why an older betta's fin damage never fully closes is the kind of detail a hobbyist copying a template would not include. The sentence that reads most like a real keeper: "A ragged fin is a symptom with several possible causes... One picture cannot tell those apart, which is why fin rot gets diagnosed so often in fish that do not have it."

6. Grade: A. Rigorous, internally consistent, and unusually honest about what the sourced research doesn't answer. Two changes: fold the tankmate specifics into the betta set itself rather than leaving them entirely in the shared list, and make the feeding guide's "sources disagree" hedge consistent across the tank setup FAQ too.

7. Not covered anywhere:
- None. Every gap raised in section 3 (tankmates, cycling mechanics) is answered by a named guide in the Health and More list, even though its full text isn't in this set.
