## conure

1. Page by page

Hub: Finish it, dense and well sequenced (quarantine first, then cage, diet, handling). Act on: cage size, temperature, pellet conversion schedule, emergency signs. Grade A.
Encyclopedia: Finish it, short but the mutation history (yellow-sided as a misidentified separate species, the pineapple cross) is a real fact, not filler. Act on: little directly. Grade B+.
Cost guide: Finish it. Act on: itemized setup cost, vet cost table, morph pricing. Grade A.
Handling guide: Finish it. Act on: reading beak contact versus biting, step-up training, the monomorphic-sexing fact. Grade A.
Health issues guide: Finish it. Act on: nutritional disorder signs, feather-destructive behavior triage, viral disease list. Grade A.
Cage setup guide: Finish it. Act on: cage size, bar spacing, lighting schedule, kitchen-hazard warning. Grade A.
Feeding guide: Finish it, and unusually direct about naming its sources. Act on: the four specific failure modes of an all-seed diet, portion sizes. Grade A.
Enrichment guide: Finish it, candid that no conure-specific research exists. Act on: foraging setup, sleeping pouch caution, priority order. Grade A.

2. Hub and encyclopedia

Both earn their place. The hub is not a rehash: it leads with quarantine timing before cage setup, which none of the deep dives do individually, and gives a genuinely useful FAQ on cost-versus-morph and how demanding the species is. The encyclopedia's breeding history (Massena and Souance, 1854; the 1980s import wave; Steve Garvin's pineapple cross) does not reappear anywhere else in the set. No numeric disagreement surfaced between hub and linked articles: cage size (24x24x30 minimum), lifespan (20-25+ years), and the 75-80% pellet floor all match exactly.

3. The set as a whole

Yes, this is a genuinely complete set for a first-timer: cage, diet (with an unusually detailed four-mechanism explanation of why seed is bad, not just a blanket warning), handling and trust-building, and enrichment all connect. One real gap the set itself surfaces without answering it: the wild lifespan is given twice, in the encyclopedia as "25 to 30 years in the wild, and the same is reachable in captivity, though the captive average is nearer 10 years, put down to poor diet and neglect," but the hub and cost guide both then state the flat figure "20 to 25 years or more, some reaching 30" as if it were the expected outcome, without ever reconciling why the encyclopedia's own captive average is so much lower. A careful reader is left wondering which number to actually plan around, and no page in the set addresses that gap directly. Beyond that, the set never gives a wing-clipping recommendation for this specific species even though the cage setup guide discusses free-flight time at length; that topic is deferred entirely to a class-wide guide not included here.

4. Overlap and conflict across deep dives

The "beak contact is usually balance, not biting" point and the "wild parrots spend up to six hours a day foraging" statistic each repeat verbatim across two or more articles (handling/health and feeding/enrichment respectively), consistent messaging rather than padding. The lifespan figures are the one real inconsistency: encyclopedia's "captive average nearer 10 years" versus the hub/cost guide's "20 to 25 years or more" are not reconciled anywhere, and a reader has to notice it themselves rather than being told which is realistic.

5. Trust

Nothing raised doubt about the pages' honesty; if anything the feeding guide's explicit source list (VCA, Merck, Lafeber) and its four separately-named failure mechanisms of an all-seed diet read as unusually careful. The sentence that most convinced me a real keeper wrote it: "A conure that refuses a plain pellet will often take a foraging format, and swapping the shape of the food is a legitimate route to the same nutrition, and no compromise on it," from the feeding guide, because it solves a specific real problem rather than restating generic advice.

6. Grade and two changes

Grade: A-. Thorough, well sourced, and consistent, held back by the unreconciled lifespan gap and by deferring wing clipping to a guide outside this set despite raising the topic. Two changes: (1) reconcile the encyclopedia's "captive average nearer 10 years" against the hub's "20-25 years or more" instead of stating both without comment; (2) add a short conure-specific note on wing clipping to the cage setup or handling guide rather than leaving it entirely to an outside article.

7. Not covered anywhere

- Why the realistic captive lifespan might be far below the 20-25 year headline figure, and which number a new owner should actually plan around: raised by the encyclopedia's own aside but never resolved anywhere in the set, including the Health and More list, which has no lifespan-specific entry.
- A conure-specific wing-clipping recommendation: the topic is opened in the cage setup guide but the actual guidance sits only in a class-wide guide not included in this set.
