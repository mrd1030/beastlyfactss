## crested-gecko

**1. Page by page**
- 00 Hub: A. Finish it, act on it immediately. Numbers, buy list, emergency card, all usable on day one.
- 01 Encyclopedia: B+. Short but earns it: rediscovery history and the honest "wild lifespan not documented" caveat are real content, not filler.
- 02 Cost guide: A. Two clean tables, a source and date for prices, honest about what pushes you to either end of the range.
- 03 Handling guide: A. The tail warning up front, treadmilling explained concretely, a real stop-signal list.
- 04 Health issues guide: A. Causes tied to fixes, clear escalation line to a vet, no padding.
- 05 Tank setup guide: B. Solid on size and the heat ceiling, but the basking-temperature row is confusing (see below), and it correctly defers humidity detail rather than repeating it.
- 06 Feeding guide: A. Nine reasons a gecko stops eating, each with a distinct fix, plus one flagged disagreement (raspberries) instead of pretending certainty.
- 07 Enrichment guide: B. Good specifics (magnetic ledges, deliberate gaps between perches) but leans generic in the plant and hide sections, the kind of advice that fits any arboreal reptile.
- 09 Humidity guide: A. The best page in the set. Named sources, a 24-hour table, and it treats too-much-humidity as seriously as too-little.

**2. Hub and encyclopedia**
Both earn their place. The hub is a router with real numbers and an emergency card, not a rehash of the deep dives, and the encyclopedia's rediscovery history and taxonomy don't appear anywhere else in the set.

They do disagree on one number: captive lifespan. The encyclopedia hedges: "Wild Lifespan: Not documented (the widely quoted 10-20 years is a captive range; wild longevity for this species has not been studied)." The hub and cost guide both state a flatter, higher figure: "Lifespan: 15 to 20 years in captivity, with the earliest known captive individuals living into their 30s." One page treats 10-20 as the commonly cited (and implicitly softer) captive number, the other two assert 15-20 as fact. A reader comparing the two would reasonably wonder which number to trust.

**3. Could I keep this animal after reading the set?**
Yes, mostly. I could buy the right enclosure, set the heat ceiling, run the humidity cycle, feed CGD on schedule, and know when to call a vet. Two gaps stood out. First, the enrichment guide tells me to add "a shallow water dish, though most hydration actually comes from misted droplets" (tank setup guide), but neither page tells me how often to clean or refill that dish, given the wet/dry cycle sets a mist schedule but not a water-dish schedule. Second, the tank setup guide raises a soft-jaw MBD sign only in passing via a link, but the fuller explanation lives entirely on the health issues guide; that's a case where the connection is at least made explicit through a link, so it's a minor complaint rather than a real gap.

**4. Overlap and conflict across the deep dives**
Read twice: the tail-does-not-regrow fact (handling guide, hub FAQ, cost guide), the 2-3 week no-eating window (feeding guide, hub FAQ), and the full Deep Dive/Health-and-More sidebar list, verbatim, on every single page. That last one is expected site structure, not padding, but it does mean real content is a small fraction of each page's total text.

One real numeric conflict: how often to weigh the gecko. The health issues guide says "Weigh your gecko weekly, weight change is often the earliest sign that something's off." The feeding guide says "Weighing the gecko monthly, or every few days during a suspected seasonal slowdown, is a good way to catch a real declining trend early." Weekly and monthly are not the same habit to build, and nothing in either article acknowledges the other's number.

A softer issue: the tank setup guide's own temperature table has an internal wrinkle. The basking row reads "82-85°F at the surface, right up against the hard maximum below (some sources: 72-75°F to be safe)". That parenthetical undercuts its own row instead of resolving it, a reader is left unsure whether 82-85 or 72-75 is the actual target.

**5. Trust**
The humidity guide names its sources by name, PetMD, Zen Habitats, and the Merck Veterinary Manual, and quotes Merck directly on why sealing an enclosure "frequently causes skin and respiratory disease." That's a level of specificity generic content doesn't bother with. The feeding guide's flag on raspberries ("a real point of disagreement... worth knowing rather than presenting either way as settled") reads the same way: someone protecting accuracy over confidence.

The single sentence that most convinced me a keeper wrote this: "one specific flavor, watermelon, is called out as having unusually high acceptance among fussy eaters." That's too specific and too odd to be filler.

**6. Grade and two fixes**
Grade: A-. Deep, specific, mostly internally consistent, and honest about disagreement where it exists.
1. Fix the weekly-vs-monthly weigh-in conflict between the health and feeding guides.
2. Rewrite the tank setup guide's basking-temperature table row so it states one number instead of undercutting itself with a bracketed alternative.
