## .reader/r0924-x/birds-shared-2

1. Grades

Reading Bird Droppings: finish it, and I'd actually use it. Learned the three-part dropping anatomy and the polyuria-versus-diarrhea distinction, both genuinely actionable. A.

Bird Emergency Plan (Power Outages, Travel): finish it. The reptile-reversal framing is a smart hook and the CO-poisoning section is the most useful thing in the whole bundle. A.

Feather Dust, Air Quality, and Bird Fancier's Lung: finish it. Learned a real, named human illness I hadn't heard of, with a case report cited honestly (pigeon exposure, not parrot). A.

Bird Feather Loss and Molt guide: finish it. The "can it reach that spot" diagnostic is the single best practical tool in the bundle. A.

Bird First Aid Kit and Grooming: finish it. The overgrown-beak-as-liver-disease-flag is the standout fact, and the blood feather protocol correctly flags that pulling it is now discouraged. A-.

2. Grade C or lower

None. All five clear a B+ or better on my read.

3. Title versus content gaps

Bird Emergency Plan: the title promises "travel" as a co-equal topic, but the emergency section is almost entirely power outages and CO; travel gets one solid section but far less depth than the title implies, and there's no mention of temperature limits for a carrier left in direct sun, which is the flip side of "never in a parked car."

First Aid Kit and Grooming: the title says "grooming" broadly, but the piece is really about nails, beak, and blood feathers, as its own subtitle says; a reader expecting wing trims gets redirected to another article, which is fine but worth knowing before clicking.

4. Overlap and conflict

Heavy, deliberate overlap: all five articles cross-link the same droppings guide, household hazards guide, and feather loss guide, and all carry near-identical Deep Dive and Health-and-More sidebars. That's clearly by design for a hub-and-spoke site, not sloppy duplication, but reading five in a row means seeing the same 25-item sidebar five times.

No factual conflicts found. The droppings article's 24-hour rule and the emergency plan's temperature range (75-80°F for a sick bird) are consistent with each other and with the first aid article.

5. Trust

Most convincing sentence: the droppings guide's claim that lime-green stool and urates together point to chlamydiosis, sourced to VCA and stated with a specific mechanism (liver disease) rather than a vague scare line.

Nothing here reads as overstated or invented. The bird fancier's lung article is the one place I'd have expected padding, since it's an unusual claim to build a whole article around, but it hedges carefully throughout (case report used pigeons, not parrots; "documented but uncommon"), which increased rather than decreased my trust in it.

6. Bundle grade: A-, one line: five tightly sourced, non-redundant-in-substance guides that each teach something a bird owner can act on the same day, with cautious sourcing throughout.

Two changes first: trim the repeated 25-line sidebar block down or vary it more by article so five reads in a row don't feel like the same footer five times, and give the emergency-plan article an actual heat-stroke temperature threshold for the travel section to match how specific the cold-weather guidance already is.
