## tiger-salamander

1. Page by page:
- 00 Hub: Finish it. Act on the first-week numbers and buy list immediately. Grade A.
- 01 Encyclopedia: Finish it. Act on nothing directly, but the PNAS hybridization story and neoteny note are genuinely new. Grade A.
- 02 Cost guide: Finish it. Act on budget and lifespan numbers. Grade A.
- 03 Handling guide: Finish it, short but not thin, it earns its length by explaining why you won't see the animal. Grade B+.
- 04 Health issues: Finish it. Act on the obesity/impaction/MBD prevention list. Grade A.
- 05 Tank setup: Finish it. Act on every substrate, temperature, and water number. Grade A.
- 06 Feeding guide: Finish it. Act on the schedule table and the "why it stopped eating" checklist, the single most useful page in the set. Grade A.
- 07 Enrichment: Finish it. Act on the priority order, and respect that it admits the research base is nearly empty rather than padding it out. Grade A-.
- 09 vs. Axolotl guide: Finish it, but do not act on its numbers without cross-checking (see below). Grade C, for the conflicts it introduces.

2. Hub and encyclopedia: both earn their place. The hub is a router with real numbers per line, not prose, so it doesn't compete with the deep dives, it previews them. The encyclopedia adds wild-history content (bait trade, the 2007 PNAS hybridization study, neoteny) that appears nowhere else. No hub-vs-deep-dive conflict: the hub's 60-75°F/never above 78°F and 12-15 year/25-year-record lines match the setup and cost guides exactly.

3. Set as a whole: yes, you could set up, buy, feed, and keep this animal healthy from this set alone. Size, substrate, temperature, humidity, water, lighting, feeding schedule, supplement schedule, and red flags are all here and mutually consistent (setup guide and feeding guide match line for line). What's missing: the legal guide is referenced constantly (hub, cost guide) but is not one of the files in this set, so "eleven jurisdictions ban it" is asserted with no way to check it here. The setup guide's line that "some very large enclosures can occasionally support a small group, worth researching carefully" raises a question, how large, how many, what signs of aggression to watch for, that no page in this set answers. A new owner is told cohabitation is possible in principle and then given nothing to act on.

4. Overlap and conflict across deep dives: heavy, deliberate repetition of three facts (fossorial/rarely-visible-is-normal, appetite-loss-is-a-red-flag, larval-stage-looks-like-an-axolotl) across four or five articles each. That repetition is consistent every time, so it reads as reinforcement, not padding.

The one real conflict is handling tolerance. The handling guide (03) says: "Direct handling should genuinely be avoided with this species," listing skin oils, toxin secretions, and stress/overheating as reasons. The comparison guide (09) says the opposite: "A tiger salamander is one of the more handling-tolerant salamander species in the hobby: with clean, damp, chemical-free hands and short sessions, many individuals stay calm and some genuinely recognize their keeper at feeding time." These are not reconcilable as written, one is "avoid it," the other is "one of the more tolerant species, some know their keeper." A reader who reads only the comparison page would handle this animal far more than the dedicated handling page recommends.

Temperature has a smaller mismatch. The hub and setup guide give 60-75°F, never above ~78°F. The comparison guide's table says "60-72°F, dies above ~80°F," and its body text repeats "80°F can be fatal." The lower band (72 vs 75) and upper ceiling (78 vs 80) both drift between the two sources.

Lifespan drifts too: the hub and cost guide converge on "12 to 15 years typical, 25 the captive record." The comparison guide's table instead says "10-20 years," a wider, differently framed number that isn't wrong exactly but doesn't match the other two.

5. Trust: the specificity mostly builds confidence, dated source checks ("Prices last checked September 2026 at Josh's Frogs and PetSmart"), a named PNAS paper with authors and year, a named vet reviewer on the PetMD source. The sentence that most convinced me a real keeper wrote this: "a salamander that is going round and heavy behind the front legs is getting more than that," from the feeding guide, that's a specific, physical, non-generic tell you only get from having watched one. What made me doubt the set is the handling contradiction above: two pages give opposite core advice on physical contact with the animal, and neither one flags the disagreement.

6. Grade: B+. The husbandry content (setup, feeding, health) is thorough, consistent, and actionable, but a direct contradiction on handling between two pages in the same set, plus temperature and lifespan drift in the comparison page, is a real trust problem for a "read one page, get one answer" set. First two changes: reconcile the handling guide and the comparison guide on whether this species tolerates handling, and align the comparison guide's temperature ceiling and lifespan range with the setup/cost guide numbers.

7. Not covered anywhere:
- How large an enclosure needs to be, and how many animals, before cohabitation is safe to attempt. None.
- What the eleven banned jurisdictions actually are (the legal guide is referenced but not included in this set, so this can't be verified from what's here). None.
