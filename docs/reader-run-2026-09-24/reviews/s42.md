## swordtail

**1. Page by page**
- Hub: would finish, the "not two males" rule and the escape-gap warning are both immediately actionable. Grade A.
- Encyclopedia: would finish, the Xiphophorus stock center history is genuinely distinct from the care pages. Grade A-.
- Cost guide: would finish, real per-fish and per-month numbers with dated, linked sources. Grade A.
- Handling guide: would finish, though roughly a third of it (ratio, tank size, lid, platy hybridization) repeats the enrichment guide's own list nearly point for point. Grade B+ (filler in the "First-Time Keeper Mistakes" section).
- Health issues guide: would finish, treating the sex ratio as an actual health topic instead of a behavior footnote is the standout idea. Grade A.
- Tank setup guide: would finish, the jumping section alone justifies the page. Grade A.
- Feeding guide: would finish, the five reasons a fish stops eating is a genuinely useful troubleshooting list. Grade A.
- Enrichment guide: would finish, honest that there is no swordtail-specific research and clear about what is borrowed from guppies. Grade A-.

**2. Hub and encyclopedia**
Both earn their place. The hub compresses the deep dives into numbers with links, and the encyclopedia is wild origin, taxonomy, and the Xiphophorus research history, none of which the hub or the deep dives repeat. I found no numeric disagreement between the hub and the deep dives: tank size (20 gallon floor, 29 to 30 gallon target), temperature (64 to 82°F, 72 to 79°F steady), pH (7.0 to 8.4), and the ratio all match across pages. The one place the hub's tidy summary flattens real nuance is the ratio: the hub states "one male to three or four females" as the rule, but its own FAQ admits "some say two or three is enough," and the enrichment guide separately gives "two to three females per male minimum." All three numbers point the same direction but are not literally the same number.

**3. Could I keep this animal**
Yes, largely. I could size a tank, cycle it, get the ratio right, feed a real diet, spot the health emergencies, and plan for fry. One thing this set raises but never answers within itself: the tank setup guide recommends a "basic LED light on an 8 to 10 hour daily cycle" to support live plants, but the plants the ratio and enrichment sections depend on for female refuge are never given their own light or care requirement beyond that one line, so a first-time planted-tank owner is left guessing which plants will actually survive that lighting. The feeding guide's fry section also assumes the reader already knows fry need separating from adults to survive, a fact stated in the tank setup and handling guides but not restated where the fry are actually being fed.

**4. Overlap and conflict across the deep dives**
The clearest duplication is between the handling guide's "First-Time Keeper Mistakes" list and the enrichment guide's "What Not to Do" and "Priority Order" lists: both cover exactly two males, undersized tanks, a missing lid, and platy hybridization, in nearly the same order. Reading both back to back feels like the same page twice.

The cost guide and the handling guide also both explain, at similar length, that swordtails and platies interbreed and that a single female can produce 20 to 100 fry roughly every four weeks without remating, each treating it as a discovery the reader hasn't hit yet.

No hard numeric conflicts turned up. The closest is the ratio point above, and a soft one in feeding: the hub's first-week list says "once or twice a day" flatly, while the feeding guide adds "though some care sheets recommend two to three smaller meals instead," a detail the hub drops without noting the disagreement exists.

**5. Trust**
The sourcing here is a real strength: LiveAquaria and SeriouslyFish listings are linked directly rather than just named, prices are dated to a specific month, and the Merck Veterinary Manual backs the disease descriptions. The sentence that most convinced me a real keeper wrote this: on the female-to-male sword reversal, "It's been studied since the 1930s, but it remains a genuinely disputed question, not settled fact, researchers disagree on whether this is true sex reversal or simply male traits emerging late in a fish that was missexed as a juvenile." A content mill states things flatly; this hedges exactly where the actual science is unsettled. The enrichment guide's admission that "Nothing on swordtails specifically" exists in the research and that it is borrowing from the guppy literature is the same kind of honesty.

**6. Grade**
A-. Numbers are consistent, sourcing is real and dated, and the set is honest about what it doesn't know. It loses a notch for the handling and enrichment guides duplicating the same mistake list almost verbatim, and for a hub ratio number that its own FAQ immediately complicates. Two changes: cut the repeated ratio and platy-hybridization material out of one of the handling or enrichment guides and cross-link instead, and give the hub's ratio line the same range the deep dives actually use so a skimming reader doesn't walk away with an oversimplified number.

**Not covered anywhere**
- A concrete plant list or lighting spec for the female-refuge planting the ratio strategy depends on: none of these pages name specific plants or say what that basic LED light needs to keep alive, and nothing in the Health and More list fills that gap either.
- None beyond that.
