## aquatic-inverts-smallmammal-shared-2

**1. Article by article**
- Aquarium Power Outages, Transport, and Moving Your Fish: finish it. Correctly explains why oxygen, not darkness or cold, is the real threat, and flips the whole logic for axolotls (heat risk, not cold) instead of pretending one rule fits every species. Grade A.
- Aquarium Stocking and Tankmates: finish it. Goes past the inch-per-gallon rule with a real welfare study, a four-check tankmate test, and a full species table with known bad pairings. Grade A.
- Aquarium Water Changes: How Much, How Often, and What the Number Is For: finish it. Explains the actual mechanism (a filter never removes nitrate) with cited toxicity research, then gives a genuinely useful old-tank-syndrome rescue plan that most hobby sites get backwards. Grade A.
- Argentine Tegu Feeding: Schedule by Age, the Protein-to-Plant Split, and the Obesity Trap: finish it, on its own merits, though it has nothing to do with the other four articles in this set. Specific schedule, a clear protein/vegetable split by age, and a real distinction from the similarly-sold Colombian tegu. Grade A-.
- Cooling an Aquarium Without a Chiller: finish it. A ranked table of methods instead of a listicle, backed by a named 2024 metabolic study and Extension sources, and it talks a reader out of the method (frozen bottles) most lists recommend first. Grade A.

**2. For articles graded C or lower**
None. Nothing here drops below A-.

**3. What the title promises that the article doesn't fully deliver**
All five titles are answered about as fully as the format allows. The one soft gap: the tegu feeding article promises "the obesity trap" specifically but spends equal or more space on gout from excess protein and the brumation-versus-illness distinction, so a reader who came only for the obesity angle gets more than that, not less, which is a fine trade but not quite what the title narrows to. The cooling article's title says "without a chiller" and then spends its last section recommending a chiller for anyone dealing with recurring heat, which isn't a broken promise (it says up front that a chiller is the real fix) but is worth knowing before clicking if a chiller is exactly what a reader is trying to avoid buying.

**4. Overlap and conflict**
The four aquarium articles overlap in a coordinated way rather than a repetitive one: the water changes article explains why nitrate accumulates and defers filtration mechanics to the filtration guide; the stocking article uses that same nitrate ceiling (20 ppm good, 40 ppm full) to define tank capacity instead of restating the chemistry; the power outage article and the cooling article both treat axolotls as running the opposite emergency from a typical fish (heat risk instead of cold risk) and state the same danger threshold (sustained water above about 75°F / 24°C) from the same named source (R. Loh's WSAVA paper), with no discrepancy between the two citations. Numbers agree everywhere they overlap: goldfish's 65-75°F comfort band appears the same way in both the cooling article and the outage article's references to it.
The one real oddity in this set isn't a factual conflict, it's a structural one: the Argentine tegu feeding article shares no Deep Dive or Health-and-More sidebar overlap with the other four at all (its own sidebar list reads "none" where the fish and amphibian articles all share the same eleven-item list), which confirms it comes from a completely different part of the site with no attempt to connect it to the aquarium content it's bundled with here. It also borrows its brumation link from a tortoise guide rather than a tegu- or lizard-specific one, and says so plainly ("The brumation guide is written for tortoises, but... the same instruction"), which is honest but underscores that the tegu piece is patched together from adjacent species content rather than fully native to its own species page.

**5. Trust**
Every article names its sources specifically rather than gesturing at "experts" or "studies show": NC State's Gregory Lewbart on power outages, a named 2010 Applied Animal Behaviour Science paper on shoaling group sizes, a 2020 nitrate toxicology study and a 2005 Chemosphere review with their actual authors, Merck's aquatic systems reference by name, UF/IFAS and Texas A&M AgriLife Extension publications, and a 2024 peer-reviewed goldfish metabolism paper with its calculated Q10 value. The water changes article's explanation of why a big "catch-up" water change kills fish in a neglected tank, because the fish adapted gradually to the drift and a fast correction reverses that adaptation faster than their bodies can follow, is the kind of counterintuitive, well-explained mechanism that separates real expertise from a listicle. The tegu article is thinner on named sourcing by comparison (it cites "Florida's wildlife agency" and "the reptile manual" without the specificity the aquarium pieces show) but nothing in it reads as wrong or invented.
The single most convincing sentence in the set: "The old fish in a neglected tank are not tougher than the new one that died. They were exposed to the same water, one small step at a time over months, and adapted to each step. The new fish met all of the steps at once." That's an explanation, not just an instruction, and it's the kind of insight that comes from actually understanding the chemistry rather than repeating a rule.

**6. Grade: A.** Four excellent, tightly cross-referenced aquarium articles plus one good but disconnected reptile feeding guide, all individually well-sourced and specific rather than generic. It stays at A rather than dropping because the odd inclusion doesn't create any factual conflict, it's just oddly placed. Two changes: either drop the tegu article from this particular bundle or add a line explaining why it's grouped with fishkeeping content, since right now it reads like a filing error; and give the tegu article's brumation cross-link a species-appropriate destination (or a short bridging sentence on what differs for a tegu) instead of sending readers to a tortoise-specific guide with only a caveat attached.
