2. Hub and encyclopedia

Both earn their place. The hub is a genuine index with its own numbers, not a copy of the deep dives, and the encyclopedia carries the Nagaoka/Niigata and 1914 exposition history that appears nowhere else. No hub-versus-deep-dive numeric disagreement turned up: pond depth (3 feet minimum, 3-5 preferred), feeding temperature bands, and quarantine length (4-6 weeks) all match word for word between the hub and their source articles.

3. The set as a whole

Yes, mostly. A first-time keeper leaves this set able to size a pond, choose filtration, feed by temperature, quarantine correctly, and recognize an emergency. What is missing is anything on actually building the thing: the pond setup guide names an EPDM liner and says "sized to the pond you are actually digging" but never gives sizing math, and it never explains how a beginner judges "oversized" filtration in gallons-per-hour terms. The cost guide gives a filtration price range ($300-800) but the setup guide never ties a GPH number to it. Water testing (ammonia/nitrite/pH ranges for koi specifically) is assumed knowledge, not stated anywhere in this set, and the health guide references "correcting water quality immediately" without ever saying what a safe number looks like. That answer likely lives on the class-wide cycling or pH/GH/KH guides named in the sidebar, but the koi pages never say so, so a reader following only this set never learns to test water at all before being told to correct it.

4. Overlap and conflict across deep dives

Heavy, deliberate overlap: quarantine length (4-6 weeks), the "koi have no true stomach" fact, and the KHV 80-100% mortality figure repeat across the cost, health, and feeding articles almost verbatim, which reads like consistent messaging rather than padding. One soft inconsistency: adult size is given as "12-36 inches" on the hub/encyclopedia but as "commonly 24 to 25 inches, up to 3 feet" in the handling guide and the vs-goldfish guide, and as "around two feet is normal" in the enrichment guide. These are not contradictory ranges so much as three different ways of rounding the same fact, but a careful reader will notice the hub's floor of 12 inches never reappears anywhere else, making it feel like a placeholder rather than a checked number.

5. Trust

Nothing raised real doubt; the set is unusually candid about its own limits. The line that most convinced me a real keeper wrote it: "the pond setup guide sizes filtration off waste volume before it sizes anything off gallons," from the health issues article, because that is exactly the kind of internal cross-reference a hobbyist makes, not marketing copy. The enrichment guide flatly stating "there is no controlled koi enrichment study to point at" instead of inventing one is the other strong signal.

6. Grade and two changes

Grade: A-. Deep, consistent, honest about its evidence gaps, and the numbers hold up across pages. Two changes: (1) add real water-testing target numbers (ammonia/nitrite/pH) directly into the pond setup or health guide instead of assuming the reader already knows them; (2) give the pond setup guide an actual sizing method, gallons per hour of filtration per pound of fish or similar, instead of just "oversized."

7. Not covered anywhere

- Specific safe ammonia/nitrite/pH ranges and how to test for them: not stated in this set (the health guide tells you to correct water quality but never defines the target numbers).
- How to actually size filtration in GPH or turnover-per-hour terms: not given even though "oversized" is repeated as the key requirement.
- What a beginner does during the first pond cycle before adding fish (fishless cycling numbers, timeline): not covered in this set.
