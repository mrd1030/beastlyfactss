## amano-shrimp

1. Page by page
- Hub (00): Finish it. Acts as a real router with numbers and links. A
- Encyclopedia (01): Finish it. Short, but the taxonomy history (Stimpson, the Chicago fire, the 2006 rename) is genuinely interesting and nowhere else. A
- Cost guide (02): Finish it. Real prices with sources and dates, and it explains the biology behind the price instead of just listing numbers. A
- Handling guide (03): Finish it, though half of it retells the breeding-biology story already on the hub and encyclopedia. B+
- Health issues guide (04): Finish it. Cites an actual 2025 journal study with a DOI and a specific mg/L threshold, which is rare. A
- Tank setup guide (05): Finish it. The filtration section corrects a real misconception (sponge for intake safety, not for calm water) instead of just repeating the checklist. A
- Feeding guide (06): Finish it. Clear on what algae they can and can't eat, with a citation for each. A
- Enrichment guide (07): Finish it, but "What the Decapod Sentience Review Found" opens by admitting there's no shrimp-specific data, then quotes 300 papers anyway. (filler) B

2. Hub vs encyclopedia
They earn their place. The hub is action-first (numbers, buy list, emergency card) and the encyclopedia is natural history (scientific name, the 1860 description, the 1980s renaming). Neither just restates the other's numbers as a wall of text. No disagreement found: adult size, difficulty, and origin all match word for word or in substance.

3. The set as a whole
Yes, you could set this animal up, buy it, feed it, and keep it alive. Tank size, water chemistry, feeding schedule, acclimation, and the main failure mode (starvation after algae runs out) are all here.
What's missing: an actual quarantine tank size is never given, just "a separate cycled tank." The hub buy list includes "a separate cycled tank for quarantine" but no gallon number appears anywhere in the set. Water changes get a generic cross-link to the site's general aquarium page rather than a shrimp-specific percentage; the hub says "small and steady" and to test nitrate instead of following a calendar, but never says how much is "small."
The tank setup guide raises a real question it doesn't answer: it says a hang-on-back filter is recommended for extra current, but never says whether that changes the sponge-filter buy-list recommendation on the hub, or whether you need both.

4. Overlap and conflict across the deep dives
Heavy overlap, no numeric conflicts. Copper danger (the 0.15-0.20 mg/L medication range vs the 1.15 mg/L lethal threshold) is repeated near-verbatim in the health guide, feeding guide, and enrichment guide. The "eggs never hatch, larvae need brackish water" explanation appears in the encyclopedia, cost guide, handling guide, health guide, and enrichment guide, five times over. The GH/molting connection (4-15 dGH, 6-8 target) is stated once in the tank setup guide and referenced everywhere else without repeating the actual numbers, which is the right way to do it and the one thing that isn't duplicated wholesale.
No two pages give different numbers for the same thing. Everything from tank size (10 gallon minimum, one per 2 gallons) to temperature (64-82°F, cooler is better) to lifespan (2-3 years) stays consistent site-wide.

5. Trust
Nothing here reads as fabricated. Prices are dated ("last checked September 2026") and sourced to named retailers. The sentence that most convinced me a real keeper wrote this: "Watch them eat. An amano that grabs food and runs off with it is a hungry amano, and that behavior is so characteristic that it gets treated as charming rather than as information." That's a specific, slightly cynical observation a marketing writer wouldn't invent, and it's the kind of detail that only comes from actually watching the animal.
The one thing that made me pause: the enrichment guide leans on a decapod sentience review that explicitly has nothing on this species, then still gives it a full section heading. It's honest about the gap but the section still exists to pad the article.

6. Grade: A-. Accurate, well-sourced, internally consistent, and the deep dives don't just restate the hub. First two changes: give quarantine an actual tank size instead of "a separate tank," and cut the five-times-repeated egg/larvae explanation down to one full telling with links, the way the GH story is already handled.

7. Not covered anywhere
- Quarantine tank size in gallons: none given, only "a separate cycled tank."
- A specific weekly water-change percentage for this species: the set defers to the site's general aquarium water-change page, which was not in this set.
