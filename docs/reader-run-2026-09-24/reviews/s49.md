## ghost-shrimp

**1. Page by page**

Hub: Finish it, yes. Everything you need before buying is here in one scan. A
Encyclopedia: Finish it, yes, short and interesting (the 2012 genus reclassification). A
Cost guide: Finish it. Real numbers, honest about the feeder vs pet split. A
Handling guide: Finish it, but it is really an acclimation guide, not a handling guide, since the animal is never handled. A (thin premise, but not thin content)
Health issues guide: Finish it. Clear, prioritized, and it tells you what has no cure (white ring of death) instead of pretending otherwise. A
Tank setup guide: Finish it. The compatibility section is the most useful thing in the whole set. A
Feeding guide: Finish it. Short but complete, and it does not dodge the cannibalism angle. A
Enrichment guide: Would finish it, but it is the weakest page. Its content is mostly a restatement of handling and health advice reframed as "recovery," plus a research claim ("the decapod sentience review") it never actually cites or sources, unlike every other page in the set. B- (filler in the research section)

**2. Hub and encyclopedia**

Both earn their place. The hub is a genuine router: numbers with sources, not a rewritten deep dive. The encyclopedia is the only page with the taxonomic history (Palaemonetes to Palaemon) and doesn't repeat husbandry numbers at all. No disagreement found between hub and any deep dive; the hub's numbers (5 gal minimum, 72-82°F, pH 7-8, copper 0.03 mg/L, one year lifespan) match the sourced deep dives exactly.

**3. The set as a whole**

Yes, you could set this animal up, buy it, feed it, and give it a real shot at survival. Tank size, water chemistry, filtration, feeding, and the four failure modes (copper, feeder stress, bad molts, cannibalism) are all covered with specific numbers.

What's missing: how do you actually test for copper, and what does a positive reading mean you should do beyond "don't use that product"? No page walks through remediation. Also missing: since "ghost shrimp" covers multiple mixed species (the enrichment page states this plainly, and the tank setup page's brackish-breeding fun fact backs it up), no page tells a buyer how to tell which one they actually have, or whether that changes any of the numbers given.

One question raised without an answer pointed elsewhere: the health guide says the white ring of death traces to "GH numbers that make or break a molt" and points to the shrimp molting guide for those numbers, but that guide isn't in this set, so a reader here never gets the actual GH figure, only "3 to 15 dGH" from the tank setup page, which is a range, not a target for molting specifically.

**4. Overlap and conflict across the deep dives**

Heavy overlap, expected for a small connected set: feeder-tank stress, the 2-4 week quarantine window, and the 0.03 mg/L copper ceiling all appear near-verbatim in the cost, handling, and health guides. Lifespan (roughly one year, up to two) is repeated in the cost and health guides with the same three sources (Shrimp Farm, FishLab, Animal Diversity Web) both times, consistently.

No numeric conflicts found. The one soft tension: the enrichment guide claims "ghost shrimp is not one species" and treats identification as unresolved, while the encyclopedia commits to a single binomial (Palaemon paludosus) as "the most common aquarium species." These don't actually contradict, since the encyclopedia itself flags shipment mixing, but the enrichment page states it more starkly than any sourced article does, with no citation behind it.

**5. Trust**

The specific, checkable detail is the tell: a named 1990 study in the Journal of Crustacean Biology with a DOI, testing salinity from 0 to 35 ppt and reporting a 96-hour LC50, is not something you fabricate for a shrimp people spend two dollars on. The line that most convinced me a real keeper wrote this: "Leave the shells in: they eat them for the minerals." That's a specific, slightly counterintuitive habit nobody would guess without having kept the animal.

What gave me pause: the enrichment page's unsourced "decapod sentience review" claim, dropped in without the citation style every other page uses. It's probably real (the 2021 UK review is a known thing), but this is the one page that doesn't show its work.

**6. Grade: A-.** Reason: accurate, consistent, honestly hedged where sources disagree, let down only by one unsourced page.

Two changes: cite the sentience review by name in the enrichment guide, matching every other page's sourcing standard. Add the actual GH/hardness target for safe molting directly to this set instead of only in an external guide, since it's the single most consequential number for keeping the shrimp alive past week two.

**7. Not covered anywhere**

- How to identify which species is actually in your bag, and whether care differs by species. None.
- What to do practically after a positive copper test (beyond "don't add it"). None.
- The specific GH number that prevents a failed molt, as opposed to the general 3-15 dGH housing range. None.
