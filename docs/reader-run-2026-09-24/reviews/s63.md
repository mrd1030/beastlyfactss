## ball-python

1. Page by page:
- Hub: A. Comprehensive first-week numbers with an emergency card that correctly flags IBD-adjacent neurological signs elsewhere in the health guide.
- Encyclopedia: A. The Benin/Ghana/Togo export history (four million snakes since 1978, ranching since the early 2000s) is real added value, not filler.
- Cost guide: A. Clear tables, morph pricing spread, and an honest "$500-$1,500 first year" total.
- Handling guide: A. The two hard timing rules (48-72 hours post-feeding, never during shed) are stated as rules, not suggestions.
- Health issues guide: A, and the best page in the set. The internal parasite section distinguishes protozoa, nematodes, and Cryptosporidium serpentis by actual risk level rather than lumping "parasites" together, and the IBD section explains transmission mechanics (mites carrying infected blood) rather than just naming symptoms.
- Tank setup guide: A. Matches the hub number for number.
- Feeding guide: A. The thawing and prey-rotation sections cite the Merck Veterinary Manual directly and explain the heat-pit mechanism for why cold prey doesn't register as food.
- Enrichment guide: A, notably careful about its own evidence. Correctly distinguishes what the 35-snake PLOS ONE study actually tested (whole housing systems) from what it didn't (individual items), which most enrichment content blurs.
- Vs. boa constrictor: A. Clean comparison, correct on egg-laying vs. live birth.
- Vs. corn snake: A. Clean comparison, correctly separates pythonid from colubrid.

2. The hub and encyclopedia don't repeat the deep dives; the hub is the checklist and the encyclopedia carries taxonomy and the CITES trade history (roughly 80% of Appendix II snake exports) found nowhere else in the set. No numeric conflict between them.

3. This set would let you legally acquire, house, feed, and handle a ball python start to finish, and the health guide in particular tells you what a real emergency looks like versus a normal hunger strike. Two questions raised but not answered inside this set: the legal state-by-state detail (linked from the hub as "Is It Legal to Own a Ball Python" but not included here), and "10 Surprising Ball Python Facts," linked from three different articles' Deep Dive lists but also absent.

4. Overlap and conflict: the "ball pythons refuse food, and it's usually not an emergency" message repeats consistently across the hub, health guide, and feeding guide, with matching numbers (3-6 months healthy fast, worry past a week combined with symptoms). One small inconsistency: the feeding guide's numbered refusal-cause list puts "breeding season (Oct-March)" first and gives the seasonal window by name, while the health guide's version doesn't mention the season at all, just adding it as a general fact, and the hub's own numbers guide splits the difference. Not a contradiction, just uneven detail on the same point across pages.

5. Trust: naming Kaufmann and colleagues' PLOS ONE rack-vs-terrarium study, and then explicitly warning readers not to over-read it ("it did not test individual enrichment items against each other"), is a level of intellectual honesty rare in pet content. The sentence that most reads like a real keeper: "Do not let feeding response drive enclosure design, since a snake that strikes at movement is not asking for a more exciting room."

6. Grade: A. Deep, well-sourced, consistent across pages, and honest about the limits of its own citations. Two changes: bring the legal guide and the "10 Surprising Facts" page into this species' own set instead of leaving them as external-only links, and standardize the appetite-refusal cause list (season, shed, temperature, stress) so all three pages present it in the same order and detail.

7. Not covered anywhere:
- State-by-state legal status, permit states, and the two outright bans.
- The species facts in "10 Surprising Ball Python Facts," which is linked from the handling guide specifically ("the species away from its care sheet") but isn't in the Health and More list either.
