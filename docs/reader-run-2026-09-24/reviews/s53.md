**2. Hub and encyclopedia**

The hub earns its place: it is a genuine index with numbers pulled from each deep dive, not a rewrite of them, and the FAQ answers are short enough not to duplicate the articles' own FAQs word for word. The encyclopedia earns its place too, since its only overlap with the hub is size and difficulty, and its real content (wild range, wild diet, the mining history) appears nowhere else.

One real disagreement: the hub and cost guide both say lifespan is "6 to 12 years, up to 15 reported." The encyclopedia says "up to 24 years recorded in captivity." Both cannot be the site's real position. Quoting both: hub, "Lifespan: 6 to 12 years, up to 15 reported," versus encyclopedia, "up to 24 years recorded in captivity." A buyer reading both pages gets two different upper bounds.

**3. The set as a whole**

Yes, mostly. Cage, diet, budget, and the health emergency list are all specific enough to act on. What's still missing after reading everything: no page states egg food quantity (just "two or three times a week," no amount), and no page explains what a "cage cover" (listed in the hub's buy list) is actually for or when to use it.

Two questions get raised on one page and answered only on another without saying so. The feeding guide says a canary that "sits fluffed and low ... has stopped eating because it is ill" and points to "the canary health guide," which does list fluffed/lethargic as the general warning sign, so that one is actually flagged correctly. But the cage setup guide says light should follow "the season instead of holding it fixed year round," and only the enrichment guide (linked from that same sentence) explains why: photoperiod drives song, molt, and reproduction. That link is present, so it is not silent, but a reader skimming just the cage guide would not know the light schedule affects breeding hormones, only that it should vary.

**4. Overlap and conflict across the deep dives**

Heavily repeated: the cage-shape argument (width over height, flight over climbing) appears near-verbatim in the hub, cage setup guide, and enrichment guide. The "confirm the sex before buying for song" advice appears in the hub, cost guide, and handling guide, each time with the same fun-fact wording almost word for word. The Jackson's Chameleon comparison link is pasted into five different articles (cost, handling, health, cage, feeding... actually cage and enrichment too) with nearly identical phrasing each time, which reads like a template insert rather than a real body link.

One numeric near-conflict: the feeding guide gives pellet share as "at least 60 to 70 percent, another 75 to 80," landing on "about three quarters" as the practical target, while the cage setup guide only says "a quality canary seed mix or a pelleted food," treating them as interchangeable options rather than pellets being the clear majority. Not a hard contradiction, but the cage guide undersells how pellet-heavy the diet should actually be next to the feeding guide's specific numbers.

**5. Trust**

The specific, named citations (VCA, LafeberVet, a corticosterone-in-droppings study) and the practical detail that "lights out, perches out" is how a vet actually catches a bird in the dark both read as someone who has handled these birds, not someone paraphrasing a pet store flyer. The sentence that most convinced me a real keeper wrote this: "A male that falls silent in July is molting. One that falls silent the week his cage moved next to the television is telling you about the television." That is too specific and too casually correct to be generic filler.

What made me doubt them: the lifespan conflict above, and the fact that five separate articles carry the identical cross-species comparison plug, which reads like an SEO habit more than a genuine "see also."

**6. Grade: B+.** Strong, specific, well-cited content dragged down by one real factual conflict (lifespan) and a repetitive, template-feeling cross-link. First two changes: fix the lifespan number so the hub, cost guide, and encyclopedia agree, and vary or trim the repeated Jackson's Chameleon comparison link so it doesn't appear in nearly every article with the same sentence.

**7. Not covered anywhere**

- What a cage cover is for and when to use it: not explained on any page in this set.
- Exact egg food quantity (only frequency, "two or three times a week," is given): None found.
