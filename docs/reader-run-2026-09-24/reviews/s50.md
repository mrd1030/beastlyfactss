## russian-tortoise

**1. Page by page**

Hub: Finish it. Dense but organized, and the emergency card alone justifies the page. A
Encyclopedia: Finish it. Short, and the CITES/wild-caught-vs-captive-bred history actually changes how you should shop. A
Cost guide: Finish it. The $15,000-$30,000 lifetime number is the kind of math a care site usually skips. A
Handling guide: Would finish it, but it is thin relative to the others, mostly restating "observation pet" and the sulcata comparison. B (thin)
Health issues guide: Finish it. Covers five real conditions with causes, and is honest that pyramiding's cause is "not completely understood," which most hobby sites overstate. A
Tank setup guide: Finish it. The best page in the set: it shows its sources disagreeing on square footage instead of averaging them away. A
Feeding guide: Finish it. The calcium ceiling (not just floor) and the overweight test are both things a beginner would not think to ask about. A
Enrichment guide: Finish it, solid and specific about substrate depth and rotation timing. A-

**2. Hub and encyclopedia**

Both earn their place. The hub compresses seven articles into first-week numbers without duplicating full explanations; the encyclopedia's wild-caught trade history (Uzbekistan as main exporter, CITES Appendix II) appears nowhere else in the set. No disagreement found: the hub's enclosure figures (8 sq ft floor, 12 or 24 sq ft targets), temperature (95-100°F basking, 60°F night floor), and humidity (30-50% ambient) all match the tank setup article word for word, including the fact that two different outside sources disagree on the square footage target, which both pages report the same way rather than picking a winner.

**3. The set as a whole**

Yes, close to fully. Enclosure, heat, UVB, humidity, diet, and the brumate-or-don't decision are all covered with real numbers and named sources (LafeberVet, Merck Veterinary Manual, VCA). Budget for both the ~$700 setup and the ~$15,000-30,000 lifetime cost is spelled out plainly.

One question raised on one page and only answered by inference elsewhere: the feeding guide says pyramiding is "long attributed to" fast growth on high-protein or overfed diets, stated fairly plainly, while the health guide says the same condition is "not completely understood" and hedges harder, citing VCA. A reader bouncing between the two gets a different confidence level on the same fact depending which page they land on first, and neither page points this out.

The soaking schedule (mentioned in the hub, sourced to an external soaking guide not in this set) is referenced but the actual depth-and-minutes detail lives entirely outside the reviewed pages; within this set alone you only get "juveniles 10-15 min twice a week, adults about once."

**4. Overlap and conflict across the deep dives**

Read twice: the sulcata comparison appears in the cost, handling, and tank setup articles, each time making the same "stays small enough to live indoors, unlike a sulcata" point. Quarantine and fecal-testing advice for new tortoises appears in both the cost article (implicitly, via captive-bred recommendation) and the health article directly.

Real tension, not a hard numeric conflict: feeding guide, "protein-rich food pushes fast growth, and shell deformity has long been attributed to exactly that." Health guide, on the same condition: "VCA Animal Hospitals says plainly that it is not completely understood... the research does not converge on one cause." Same site, two different confidence levels on the same causal claim.

**5. Trust**

The line that most convinced me a real keeper (or someone working closely from vet literature) wrote this: the bladder-stone section citing a specific 2021 survey of 101 tortoises across three Japanese hospitals, giving the exact percentage (8.9%) and noting the study "could not confirm a link between diet and stone formation in its own cases." A site optimizing for filler doesn't include a study result that undercuts its own tidy narrative about diet.

What gave me pause: nothing structural, but the protein/pyramiding tension above is worth fixing since it makes the site look less careful than the health page alone suggests it is.

**6. Grade: A-.** Reason: thorough, well sourced, and unusually honest about scientific uncertainty, docked only for the handling guide's thinness and the protein/pyramiding confidence mismatch.

Two changes: tighten the pyramiding-cause language in the feeding guide to match the health guide's hedge. Expand the handling guide with something concrete, like actual bite/ram-injury risk between housed females or a real escape-proofing checklist, since right now it is the one page that could be cut without losing information.
