## uromastyx

**1. Page by page**
- 00 hub: A. Dense but genuinely useful, every number traceable to a deep dive, plus a real emergency card.
- 01 encyclopedia: B+. The CITES/Morocco wild-trade history is real, non-repeated content, worth the separate page.
- 02 cost guide: A. Clear tables, a named-retailer price check, and an honest warning about undersized "complete" starter kits.
- 03 handling guide: A. The tail-doesn't-drop detail is specific and directly useful, not generic reptile advice.
- 04 health issues guide: A. MBD, impaction, and respiratory infection all get a cause and a threshold, plus the nice non-issue callout on salt crusting so owners don't panic over something normal.
- 05 tank setup guide: A. The heat and humidity numbers are unusually precise, and it explains why (hindgut fermentation) rather than just listing them.
- 06 feeding guide: A. The best page in the set: schedule, a long safe/avoid list with reasons attached, and nine specific causes of appetite loss with a real threshold for concern.
- 07 enrichment guide: B+. Honest that there's no uromastyx-specific research and says so upfront, borrowing from gecko and hognose studies instead of pretending otherwise. Loses a notch for restating the tank setup guide's heat and burrow requirements at length.
- 08 vs-bearded-dragon guide: A-. A genuinely comparative page, not a rehash of either species' hub, with a clear table and a real bottom-line recommendation.

**2. Hub and encyclopedia**
The hub earns its place as a digest: the enclosure size (4x2x2, up to 8x4x4 for Egyptians), basking range (110-120°F, 130°F+ for Egyptians), humidity (20-30%), and UVB target (UVI 4.5-6.0) all match the tank setup guide word for word. The encyclopedia adds real, non-duplicated content (the genus's CITES Appendix II status, the gap between that paperwork and enforcement in Morocco).
One real tension: the health issues guide, citing outside veterinary guidance, warns that "sand, dirt, and even wood chips can cause impaction when swallowed," recommending tile, reptile carpet, or paper instead. But the hub and the tank setup guide both call sand (packed 4 inches deep) the required substrate for this species to burrow. The health guide never reconciles this with its own setup guide's substrate recommendation, so a careful reader hits a direct disagreement about whether sand is safe or something to avoid.

**3. The set as a whole**
Yes, this set gets you to a working setup, a feeding plan, and a clear sense of what's an emergency. What's missing: nothing on introducing a uromastyx to an existing collection safely (quarantine is only in the "Health and More" list, not covered species-specifically), and no guidance on brumation logistics (how to actually cool the enclosure down, whether to feed during it) beyond "appetite drops for 1-2 months, that's normal." The feeding guide raises breeding-cycle appetite changes and territorial males but nothing in this set explains how to sex a uromastyx or manage a pair, despite the enrichment guide flatly stating "not casually" on cohousing. The tank setup guide's substrate mix (sand/topsoil/excavator clay) is answered in more detail than the hub's simpler "fine sand," a case where a reader who only reads the hub would build a less burrow-friendly substrate than the deep dive recommends.

**4. Overlap and conflict across the deep dives**
The enclosure size, basking temperature, and UVB numbers repeat across the hub, cost guide, tank setup, feeding, and vs-bearded-dragon pages, always consistently. The substrate conflict noted above (sand vs. tile/carpet/paper) is the one real disagreement I found. A smaller inconsistency: the vs-bearded-dragon table calls uromastyx "Ferguson Zone 4 (most intense)" but the species' own tank setup guide never uses the Ferguson zone label at all, just a UVI number, leaving a reader to notice on a different page that the zone system even applies here.

**5. Trust**
The specificity works in this set's favor: named sources (Center for Bird and Exotic Animal Medicine on salt glands), a real taxonomic history (Merrem, 1820), and an upfront admission on the enrichment page that no uromastyx-specific research exists rather than manufacturing a citation. The sentence that most reads like real keeper experience: "A uromastyx drinks its salad... which is how a lizard lives at 20 to 30 percent humidity," paired with the health guide's detail that new owners mistake the lack of bowl-drinking for a health problem. That's a specific, correctable misconception a real keeper would have actually seen.

**6. Grade: A-.** Precise, well-sourced, and mostly self-consistent, held back by the sand-versus-no-sand contradiction between the health and setup guides and by the enrichment page's redundancy. First two fixes: resolve the substrate disagreement explicitly (state why sand is acceptable here despite the general vet warning, or drop the vet-guidance aside), and trim the enrichment guide's repeated heat/burrow paragraphs to what it adds beyond the tank setup guide.

**7. Not covered anywhere**
- How to sex a uromastyx or manage a breeding pair or cohabiting animals, despite feeding and enrichment both raising breeding-season and territorial behavior.
- Practical brumation logistics for this species specifically (temperature drop procedure, whether/how to feed), beyond "appetite drops for 1 to 2 months."
