## cockatoo

**1. Page by page**
- 00 Hub: A. Leads with the over-bonding trap and the surrender statistic instead of burying them.
- 01 Encyclopedia: A. The 1988-1999 Indonesian export-quota history, with the 1991 figure of trappers exceeding local quotas "by as much as eighteen times," is real, sourced-feeling content.
- 02 Cost: B (thin math). Good ranges and an honest push toward adoption, but see the conflict below.
- 03 Handling: A. Names Pamela Clark and Chicago Exotics directly and gives concrete over-bonding warning signs.
- 04 Health issues: A. Ties lipomas, fatty liver, and plucking back to one root cause instead of listing them as unrelated.
- 05 Cage setup: A. Names specific pellet brands and specific cage models rather than staying generic.
- 06 Feeding: A. The "small handful equals a dinner plate" portion image is concrete and memorable.
- 07 Enrichment: A. The most intellectually honest article in the set, it says outright that a widely repeated claim (training beats medication for plucking) can't be verified and refuses to repeat it.
- 09 Screaming and plucking: A. Explains the mechanism (contact-calling, reinforcement) instead of just labeling the behavior a problem.

**2. Hub and encyclopedia vs. deep dives**
Both earn their place; the encyclopedia's trade history isn't repeated anywhere else. But the hub inherits a real numbers problem from the cost guide.

The hub's FAQ and first-week card both say setup runs "$250 to $1,300 or more." The cost guide's own table lists a stainless steel cage alone at $1,450 to $1,550, already above the hub's entire setup ceiling, and the paragraph right under that same table then says "a large, appropriately sized cage runs $200 to $1,000," which contradicts the table two lines above it. Three numbers for one cage, in one article, that don't agree with each other or with the hub that quotes it.

There's a smaller math oddity too: the hub and feeding guide both give "75 to 80% pellets, with fresh vegetables and fruit covering no more than another 20 to 40%," which can sum past 100%. The cage setup article's version, "most of the rest is vegetables, seeds under about 10%," is the one that actually adds up, and it's not the version the hub repeats.

**3. Could I do this?**
Mostly, and the parts that matter most (bonding, screaming, plucking, the surrender pattern) are the most rigorously covered of anything in this set. The cage budget confusion above would leave a buyer unsure whether to plan for $1,300 or $3,500. And nothing in this set, or the Health and More list, covers bathing, despite the cage and enrichment guides both dwelling on feather dust and air quality; how a cockatoo bathes is never addressed.

**4. Overlap and conflicts across the deep dives**
Heavy expected repetition: 4-6 hours of daily interaction, 75-80% pellets, and the surrender statistic appear near-identically in three or more places. The real conflict is the cage-cost self-contradiction inside the cost article described above, plus the milder diet-percentage math in the hub and feeding guide versus the cage setup guide's cleaner version.

**5. Trust**
The line that most convinced me a knowledgeable person wrote this: "Its full text is not openly available, and the claim that training outperformed the other treatments circulates widely without a readable source behind it, so this guide does not make it." A content mill repeats a viral claim; this site checked it and declined to use it. Naming Pamela Clark, Chicago Exotics, and Van Zeeland's actual review by name throughout adds to that.

What made me doubt them: the cage-price table contradicting its own next paragraph, in the one article a buyer needs a straight number from most.

**6. Grade: B+.** The most intellectually honest writing of anything reviewed, undercut by a real budgeting contradiction inside its own cost article.
Two fixes: reconcile the cage-cost numbers in the cost guide (the table, the paragraph below it, and the hub all currently disagree), and add a line on bathing frequency somewhere in the cage or enrichment guide.

**Not covered anywhere**
- Bathing frequency and method for a cockatoo. None.
- A single, consistent upfront cage cost (the set gives three different ranges). None resolve it.
