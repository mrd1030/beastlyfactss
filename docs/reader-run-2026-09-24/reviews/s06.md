# hermit-crab

**1. Page by page**
- Hub: A, would finish. Acts as a real map: numbers, buy list, emergency card, FAQ. Nothing here reads as filler.
- Encyclopedia: A, would finish. The Crazy Crab marketing history and Mary Akers captive breeding record are new, not a repeat of the deep dives.
- Cost guide: A, would finish. Priced table with a checked date, plus the actual argument for why the crab price is misleading.
- Handling guide: A, would finish. Autotomy explanation and the buried timeline (days versus weeks to three months) are concrete and actionable.
- Health guide: A, would finish. Names post purchase stress as the real killer and gives a milestone (first molt) to know you're past it.
- Tank setup guide: A, would finish. Every first week number traces back here with the actual reasoning attached (why never a heat mat under the tank, why not calcium sand).
- Feeding guide: A, would finish. Names two specific toxins by name and cites three sources.
- Enrichment guide: A, would finish. Built on actual shell choice research (Elwood and Appel) rather than generic "add toys" advice, and says openly that the sentience debate isn't settled.

**2. Hub and encyclopedia**
Both earn their place. The hub is a router, not an article: every claim on it links out or is marked as coming from the encyclopedia, so it isn't duplicating the deep dives so much as indexing them. The encyclopedia adds wild history the care pages never touch. No disagreement found: the hub's "10 to 20 years or more when kept well" and the cost guide's identical phrase match, and the hub's "up to 4 inches" adult size matches the encyclopedia word for word.

**3. Could I actually keep one after reading all of this?**
Yes, close to fully. I know the tank, the temperature, the 75 to 85% humidity number and why it matters, the substrate depth and mix, both water dishes, the shell count, the diet, and the two ingredients to avoid on a label. What's thinner: nobody tells me how to check the substrate is moistened correctly (a squeeze test, a target feel) beyond "moisten with dechlorinated water." Nobody gives the actual saltwater mix ratio, just "an aquarium salt mix." The health guide raises "there's almost no vet care" and tells me to isolate a sick crab, but the how long to isolate a new arrival question is answered on a page outside this set (Invertebrate Housekeeping, in the Health and More list) without the set itself saying so; a first time reader on the tank setup or health page would want that pointed out, not just filed under a shared class guide.

**4. Overlap and conflict across the deep dives**
Heavy overlap, no conflict. Humidity (75 to 85%), temperature (75 to 85°F, warm side 80°F), and substrate depth (6 inches or 3x the largest crab) each appear near verbatim in the tank setup guide, the health guide, and the enrichment guide. The molting buried-for-weeks-to-three-months figure appears in the handling guide, the health guide, and the enrichment guide, worded almost the same each time. The "never disturb a buried crab" rule and the PPS description repeat between the hub, the health guide, and the FAQ blocks on three separate pages. None of it contradicts; it's the same handful of facts distributed everywhere rather than cross-referenced.

**5. Trust**
Nothing here reads as fabricated or hand-wavy, which is itself a little suspicious for a beach-shop pet with almost no vet literature behind it; a skeptical reader would want to know where "before a molt a crab reabsorbs calcium from its old exoskeleton" comes from, since it's stated as fact with no citation on that page. The line that most convinced me a real keeper wrote this: "Skip calcium sand, it cements when wet, and avoid gravel or mulch." That's the kind of mistake only someone who has actually cleaned out a ruined substrate mix would bother to warn against.

**6. Grade: A minus**
Consistent numbers, real sourcing on the feeding page, and welfare research that admits it's contested. The two changes: add the saltwater mix ratio and a substrate moisture check to the tank setup page instead of leaving both to "moisten with dechlorinated water," and cross-link the quarantine-duration answer from the health or handling page instead of leaving it to a shared class guide the reader has to find on their own.

**7. Not covered anywhere**
- The specific marine salt mix ratio or specific gravity target for the saltwater dish: none.
- How to tell the substrate is at correct moisture by feel: none.
- What to do with a shed exoskeleton after a successful molt (eat it, remove it, leave it): none.

# flying-squirrel

**1. Page by page**
- Hub: A, would finish. Leads with the legal question ahead of cost, which is the right order for this species, and every number links to its source page.
- Encyclopedia: A, would finish. The 2019 to 2020 Florida trafficking case, with dates and a dollar figure, is real reporting, not filler natural history.
- Cost guide: A, would finish. Two priced tables, a dated price check, and it's honest that the legal question comes before the financial one.
- Handling guide: A, would finish. The bonding window (6 to 8 weeks) and what an unbonded squirrel actually does (glides away, may nip) are specific and useful.
- Health guide: A, would finish. Names the exact mechanism (calcium pulled from bone) and the progression from lethargy to paralysis, with a vet threshold stated plainly.
- Tank setup guide: A, would finish. Explains why height beats floor space and why a hamster wheel is dangerous, not just "wrong size."
- Feeding guide: A, would finish. Names the 2:1 calcium to phosphorus target and cites a 2014 dietary study by name.
- Enrichment guide: A, would finish. States upfront that no research exists on flying squirrel enrichment and explains exactly why borrowing from the sugar glider is defensible; also the one page that flags where the borrow breaks (gnawing).

**2. Hub and encyclopedia**
Both earn their place. The hub is structured as a router with sourced numbers, not prose duplicating the articles. The encyclopedia's Linnaeus and trafficking-case material appears nowhere else. No disagreement found between them or against the deep dives: cage size (24x24x36 or 30x18x36), temperature (65 to 75°F, avoid past 80°F), bonding window (6 to 8 weeks), price ($200 to $450), and lifespan (3 to 6 years wild, ~10 captive, documented past 19) all match wherever they repeat.

**3. Could I actually keep one after reading all of this?**
Mostly, with one real gap: the legal question the hub puts first is never actually answered in this set. The hub gives a summary (9 states clear, 4 with conditions, 19 needing a permit) but the actual state list lives on a linked article outside these eight files. That's flagged and clickable, not hidden, but a reader who stops at the hub still doesn't know their own state's answer. Elsewhere, the set is coherent: cage, temperature, lighting, diet, the 2:1 calcium ratio, and bonding are all covered with the reasoning attached. The health guide raises "the feeding guide has the daily bowl and the supplement schedule," which is an explicit cross-reference, so that one is handled correctly rather than left implicit.

**4. Overlap and conflict across the deep dives**
Heavy overlap, no conflict. The calcium-to-phosphorus imbalance and metabolic bone disease progression (lethargy, reduced appetite, hind limb weakness, tremors, fractures, paralysis, seizures) appear near identically in the hub, the health guide, and the feeding guide. The sugar glider comparison, unrelated animal, same solution through convergent evolution, is repeated in the cost guide, the handling guide, the tank setup guide, and the enrichment guide, each time making the same point about gliding membranes evolving independently. The axle-free wheel warning appears in the hub and the tank setup guide word for word. Nothing disagreed across any of it.

**5. Trust**
The dated trafficking case (Marion County, January 15, 2019, a Bushnell dealer paid $213,800, seven people charged) is specific enough to check and reads as real research rather than padding. The line that most convinced me a real keeper wrote this: "A standard hamster or rat wheel is genuinely dangerous for a flying squirrel, not just the wrong size... it can seriously injure a small, bouncy animal's spine or tail mid-run." That's a specific injury mechanism, not a generic safety warning, and it's the kind of detail that comes from having actually seen or heard about the injury.

**6. Grade: A minus**
Consistent numbers across every page, a health guide that states a clear vet threshold, and an enrichment guide honest about having zero direct research to draw on. The two changes: put an actual short state legal list, or at least the states named in the summary, directly on the hub instead of only on a linked page, and add the calcium and vitamin D3 dosing amount (how much powder, how often) to the feeding guide instead of just "dusted onto food regularly."

**7. Not covered anywhere**
- The actual state-by-state legal list (only a count summary appears in this set; the detail lives on a page outside these files).
- Supplement dosing amount or frequency beyond "regularly": none.
- How to introduce a new squirrel to an existing pair or group, or a quarantine period before that: none, including in the Health and More list excerpts.
