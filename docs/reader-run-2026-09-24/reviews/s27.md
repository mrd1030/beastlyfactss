## rosy-boa

**1. Page by page**
- Hub: finish it, grade A. Numbers are sourced, dated, and honestly hedged ("prices move, treat as a snapshot").
- Encyclopedia: finish it, grade A. Short, distinct from the hub, good history section.
- Cost guide: finish it, grade A. Real vendor names, a real vet price list, and it shows its math.
- Handling guide: finish it, grade B+. Solid and actionable, but leans hard on the boa constrictor comparison to pad length.
- Health issues guide: finish it, grade A. The humidity-reversal framing and the "hard to spot illness" section are the most useful writing in the set.
- Tank setup guide: finish it, grade A. Clear numbers, and it explains the two failure modes (humidity, rough lid) instead of just listing gear.
- Feeding guide: finish it, grade A. Portion sizing plus the "overfeeding is the real risk" framing is genuinely useful.
- Enrichment guide: finish it, grade B. Honest that there's no species-specific research, but leans on two borrowed studies to argue against the standard tank size, which is a stretch worth reading critically.

**2. Hub and encyclopedia**
Both earn their place. The hub is a router with numbers and links, the encyclopedia is background and natural history, and neither restates a deep dive's reasoning. One internal mismatch, though, sits inside the hub itself: it says "Lifespan: 20 to 30 years typical, the captive average nearer 18 to 22," which reads as two different ranges in the same sentence. The encyclopedia and the cost guide agree with each other cleanly: "averaging 18-22 years in captivity with documented individuals past 30" (encyclopedia) and "18 to 22 years is the captive average and past 30 is documented" (cost guide). The hub's "20 to 30 years typical" isn't in either of those, so it disagrees with its own set, not the deep dives.

**3. Could I actually keep one after reading all of this**
Yes, mostly. Enclosure size, temperature, humidity, substrate, prey size, feeding schedule, and handling timing are all specific and consistent enough to shop and set up from. Two real gaps. First, no page states whether a rosy boa needs UVB lighting at all, a basic yes/no that every other section covers with a number. Second, nobody gives a substrate depth in inches; the enrichment guide asks for "deep enough to tunnel" and the buy list says "deep enough to hold a tunnel," but neither commits to a figure. A new owner has to guess how many bags of aspen to buy, even though the cost guide separately calculates the volume in cubic feet for a substrate change, without ever stating the starting depth.

One unstated connection: the health guide's rostral abrasion section blames "a coarse screen lid," and the fix (a smooth, well-fitted lid) is explained in full in the tank setup guide, which the health article does link to. That one is handled. The one that isn't stated is between the enrichment guide's substrate depth request and the tank setup guide's substrate section, which never gives a number either, so the question bounces between two pages and lands nowhere.

**4. Overlap and conflict across the deep dives**
The boa constrictor comparison is repeated almost verbatim in five of six deep dives (cost, handling, health, tank setup, feeding), each time restating that the boa constrictor is 6 to 10 times longer and needs proportionally more of everything. It's accurate every time, but reading the set straight through, it's the same sentence with different nouns swapped in.

No numeric disagreements. Temperature (90°F basking, 65-75°F cool side), humidity (30-40% baseline, 40-50% during shed), feeding intervals (5-7 days juvenile, 10-14 days adult, 3-4 weeks mature), and prey sizing (1 to 1.5x body width) all match word for word across every article that cites them, including the FAQ blocks. That level of exact repetition across seven separate files reads more like shared source data than seven people independently agreeing, which is worth noting under trust below.

**5. Trust**
The specific, dated sourcing is what earns trust here: "Prices last checked September 2026 at Backwater Reptiles, CountyMax, ReptMart and RodentPro," a named mobile exotic vet price list with an exam fee broken into new-client versus established-client, and a wattage-to-kWh calculation for the heat mat. That's not hand-waved. Working against it: every FAQ answer across all seven pages restates the same numbers in the same order, which is efficient but also exactly what a templated content system produces, not what seven different write-ups from lived experience would look like.

The sentence that most convinced me an actual keeper wrote something here: "Anchor stacked rock properly. A snake that burrows underneath a stack is under it when it shifts." That's a specific, slightly grim detail nobody includes unless they've thought about what happens when a snake digs under a rock pile.

**6. Grade and two changes**
Grade: A-. The set is accurate, internally consistent almost everywhere, and gives a genuinely different care profile (dry, small, docile) instead of copy-pasting generic snake advice. First change: fix the hub's lifespan line so it states one range instead of two. Second change: add a substrate depth number to the tank setup guide, since it's the one concrete gap left after everything else in the set is specific.

**7. Not covered anywhere**
- Whether UVB lighting is needed at all. None.
- A substrate depth in inches or a cubic-foot starting fill. None.
