## hedgehog

**1. Page by page**
- Hub: A. Every first-week number in one place, with a source page for each. I would finish it and act on it immediately.
- Encyclopedia: B. Thin on care advice by design, but the Salmonella outbreak stat and the hybrid-origin history earn it a slot. Not filler, just a different job.
- Cost guide: A. Real price tables, dated, with the legal check placed first where it belongs.
- Handling guide: A. Explains huffing, balling, and self-anointing clearly enough that a nervous new owner would not panic.
- Health issues guide: A. The WHS correction (3 percent, not 10) is the standout, and it is backed with a study description, not just asserted.
- Tank setup guide: A. The 72°F hibernation-risk threshold is repeated with the same number everywhere it appears, which is exactly what you want from a safety-critical figure.
- Feeding guide: A. It admits its own portion-size numbers disagree with each other rather than hiding it. That is rare and it is a point in its favor.
- Enrichment guide: A. Cites an actual 2024 owner survey with real percentages instead of generic "provide enrichment" advice.

**2. Hub and encyclopedia**
The hub earns its place: it is a router with the emergency card, the buy list, and one line per deep dive, not a rewrite of any single article. The encyclopedia earns its place too, since the outbreak and hybridization history do not appear anywhere else in the set. Neither disagrees with a deep dive on any number I could check. The hub's WHS FAQ ("closer to 3%, with average onset around 3.3 years") matches the health guide's own FAQ word for word in substance.

**3. Could I keep this animal healthy?**
Yes, mostly. Between the hub, tank setup, and feeding guide I could legally check, set up an enclosure, hold the temperature, and feed on a schedule. The feeding guide raises a question the tank setup guide answers without saying so: "cold stress" as a reason a hedgehog stops eating only makes sense once you already know the 72°F hibernation threshold from the tank setup article. Read feeding first and it reads as vague. Similarly, the health guide's "always see a vet for wobbling" only becomes urgent once you know from the tank guide that cold itself can cause wobbling-adjacent sluggishness. Nothing tells you how to actually bathe a hedgehog or how to sex one, and no page in this set or the Health and More list covers either.

**4. Overlap and conflicts across the deep dives**
Heaviest repeats: the 72 to 90°F range and the "no light-emitting heat bulbs" rule appear in tank setup, health issues, and feeding. The lactose-intolerance note appears in cost, health issues, and feeding, always with a link back to the same dedicated article, so it reads as consistent cross-referencing rather than padding. The sulcata tortoise comparison ("closer to a reptile's than a pocket pet's") is repeated in cost, handling, and tank setup almost verbatim, which is closer to filler than real cross-linking.
The one real disagreement, which the feeding guide names itself: "figures range from 3 to 4 teaspoons a day up to 1 to 3 tablespoons, nearly a 2 to 3 times spread" against its own recommendation two paragraphs earlier of "roughly 2 to 3 teaspoons of a protein base plus 1 to 2 teaspoons of chopped produce." No page picks a side beyond "adjusted by body condition," so a new owner is left averaging conflicting numbers.

**5. Trust**
The sentence that most convinced me a keeper wrote this: "Check fecal output, not bowl level" as the way to confirm a shy new hedgehog is actually eating. That is not something you write from a spec sheet. The thing that made me doubt them slightly is the flat-out admitted internal disagreement on daily food quantity; it is honest, but it means the number itself was never actually verified before publishing.

**6. Grade: A-.** Reason: dense, accurate, self-correcting on the one big myth (WHS rate), and honest about its own inconsistency rather than hiding it.
Two changes: pick one daily feeding amount instead of publishing two disagreeing ranges, and add a short bathing and sexing note somewhere in this set.

**7. Not covered anywhere**
- How to bathe a hedgehog, or whether you should.
- How to sex a hedgehog.

## african-fat-tail

**1. Page by page**
- Hub: A. First-week numbers, an emergency card with real trigger symptoms, and a buy list with no upsell.
- Encyclopedia: B. The export-pipeline history (ball pythons, savannah monitors) is a genuine addition, but it quietly contradicts a deep dive on lifespan, see below.
- Cost guide: A. Clear price bands by morph tier, dated, and honest that a normal costs about the same as a leopard gecko.
- Handling guide: A. The chirp-as-communication detail and the two-week wait are both concrete and actionable.
- Health issues guide: A. Names MBD as the top serious risk and ties every listed condition back to a specific husbandry cause.
- Tank setup guide: A. The substrate section is the strongest page in the set, it argues with the "sand causes impaction" claim instead of repeating it uncritically.
- Feeding guide: A. "Tail thickness over day count" is a genuinely useful reframe, and the never-feed list is specific rather than generic.
- Enrichment guide: A (thin). Honest that no study exists for this species and borrows from leopard gecko research instead of inventing content, but it is thin because the whole page is one borrowed study plus a humidity caveat.

**2. Hub and encyclopedia**
Both earn their place: the hub is a pure router and the encyclopedia adds history (Duméril's 1851 description, the trade route through Ghana, Togo, and Benin) found nowhere else in the set. But they disagree on lifespan. Encyclopedia: "the widely quoted 10-20 years is a captive range; wild longevity for this species has not been studied." Cost guide and hub both say: "15 to 20 years is the commonly cited range." That is a real numeric mismatch on the low end, 10 versus 15, inside the same site.

**3. Could I keep this animal healthy?**
Yes. Setup, heat, humidity, substrate, and feeding portions are all there and internally consistent (88 to 92°F warm side, 50 to 70% ambient humidity, 4 inches of substrate, all repeated identically across hub, tank setup, and feeding). One page raises a question another answers without saying so: the feeding guide's "seasonal appetite drop" and "false brumation" only make sense once you have read the tank setup guide's temperature ranges, since neither feeding guide reference explains what temperature actually triggers it. The enrichment guide's claim that "mirrors ranked last" assumes you already trust the leopard gecko study it borrows from, which is fine, but it never says how different the two species' stress responses might actually be.

**4. Overlap and conflicts across the deep dives**
The leopard gecko comparison is repeated almost word for word in cost, handling, health issues, tank setup, and enrichment, five separate call-outs to the same cousin. That is padding by volume even where each individual mention is accurate.
The real tension is on sand and impaction. Tank setup: "the impaction part is narrower than it sounds, though: it tends to happen in geckos that are already unhealthy, dehydrated, or kept at incorrect temperatures, not simply from naturalistic substrate." Feeding guide: "Never keep one on pure sand: it dries the gecko out, interferes with shedding, and can cause impaction if swallowed with prey." The feeding guide states sand-caused impaction as a plain fact, and the tank setup guide walks the same claim back into something narrower. They land on the same practical advice, avoid pure sand, but they disagree on why it matters.

**5. Trust**
The sentence that most convinced me a real keeper wrote this: "A humid hide that has dried out is a hide, not a humid hide." That is the kind of correction only someone who has checked a dried-out hide themselves would bother making. The lifespan mismatch above is what made me doubt them, since it is the kind of number a fact-check pass should have caught before publishing three different pages.

**6. Grade: A-.** Reason: accurate, husbandry-first, and willing to complicate a popular claim (sand and impaction) instead of repeating it, but let a lifespan number drift between pages.
Two changes: fix the 10-20 versus 15-20 year lifespan mismatch between the encyclopedia and the cost guide, and cut the leopard gecko comparison down from five repeats to one or two.

**7. Not covered anywhere**
- Routine enclosure cleaning and spot-cleaning schedule.
- How to sex an African fat-tailed gecko.
