## wild-animals-and-other-2

1. Article ratings

Giraffe heart myth: would finish it. Learned the heart is proportionally normal, the pressure is the real adaptation, and the siphon-drinking story is wrong. Grade A.

Hagfish slime: would finish it. Learned the slime is mostly seawater trapped by protein threads and deploys in 400ms, plus the self-tying escape trick. Grade A.

Hedgehog lactose intolerance: would not finish it happily; the opener is padded and generic. Learned dairy is dangerous for hedgehogs and what to feed them instead. Grade C (filler).

Honey badgers: would finish it. Learned the fearlessness reputation is real but several specific claims (bite force, honeyguide partnership, lion-fighting stories) are unsupported folklore. Grade A.

Glass frog blood: would finish it. Learned the frog hides blood in its liver to sleep transparent, and that the clotting mechanism is still unexplained. Grade A.

Shark danger: would finish it. Learned the actual 2025 bite and death counts versus the scale of human-caused shark deaths. Grade A.

Honeybee voting: would finish it. Learned the quorum and stop-signal mechanism behind swarm site selection. Grade A.

Immortal jellyfish: would finish it. Learned the difference between not aging and being immortal, plus the taxonomy mixup and the disputed 2022 genome paper. Grade A.

International Sloth Day: would finish it. Learned the date is a floating rule, not a fixed day, and why the fur-algae-moth system exists. Grade A.

2. Improving the C-or-lower article

Hedgehog article: cut the first paragraph and the closing two paragraphs, which are pure filler with no facts, adjectives standing in for content ("delightfully surprising," "endlessly fascinating"). Also fix the internal duplication: the bulleted "Why Hedgehogs Are So Fascinating" list repeats facts (self-anointing, dairy, hibernation, foraging distance, heart rate) that get restated nearly verbatim later in the body and again in the "fun fact" bullets at the end. Trim to one mention each.

3. Title versus content gaps

Hedgehog article: the title promises a dairy myth explainer but delivers a full species overview; a reader who wanted just the milk myth gets it in paragraphs 16 to 24 buried under six other sections.

No clear gap in the other eight; each delivers roughly what its headline promises.

4. Overlap and conflicts

The giraffe article and the honeybee article both reference the "alpha wolf myth" and the wombat cube-dung piece as examples of a pattern (a compressed fact becoming a false claim), which is consistent cross-referencing rather than conflict.

No factual disagreements found between articles in this bundle. The honey badger piece and the hedgehog piece both touch on quill/skin material (keratin) without conflict.

5. Trust

Most convincing sentence: "The 11 kg figure is not invented, incidentally... The error is not the number. The error is the word 'exceptionally.'" from the giraffe piece, because it explains precisely how a true number became a false claim rather than just asserting the myth is wrong.

Doubtful moments: the hedgehog article states quills come in a range up to 7,000 in the opener but later says "between 5,000 and 7,000," which is a minor inconsistency rather than a real conflict. The honey badger piece is unusually good about flagging its own weak sources (bite force, machete story, Stoffel-as-sole-evidence), which raises trust in the rest of the piece rather than lowering it.

6. Bundle grade

Grade A-minus. Eight of nine articles are tightly sourced, self-correcting on popular myths, and cross-link sensibly. The hedgehog piece is the outlier: generic, padded, and repetitive against the sharp, citation-heavy style of everything else in the bundle.

Two changes: cut the hedgehog article's filler intro/outro and de-duplicate its repeated facts, and add a Deep Dive / Health and More sidebar to at least the giraffe, honey badger and hedgehog pages, since all nine articles currently show "none" for both sidebar lists despite the species clearly having other content to link to.
