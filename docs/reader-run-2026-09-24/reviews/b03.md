## aquatic-inverts-smallmammal-shared-3

**1. Would I finish it, what I learned, grade**

Fish Quarantine and Treatment: The Hospital Tank Guide. Yes. Learned the actual quarantine floor (30 days minimum, 30-60 for a valuable fish), the bare-tank seeding trick to avoid a fish-in cycle, and the concrete "pull the carbon, add aeration" rules for dosing. Grade A.

Freshwater pH, GH, and KH: Why Chasing a Number Backfires. Yes. The KH-as-buffer mechanism is explained clearly enough that I could stop chasing pH numbers myself, and the method comparison table is genuinely actionable. Grade A.

Giant Millipede Feeding. Yes, quickly. Learned the substrate is the actual diet, produce is a rotating extra, and calcium stays in permanently. Tight and useful. Grade A-.

Invertebrate Emergency Plan: Power Outages, Travel, and Shipping. Yes. Learned that most inverts don't care about a power outage but a hermit crab does, that transport (not the days alone) is the real tarantula risk, and the USPS mailability rule for spiders and scorpions. Grade A.

Invertebrate Molting 101: What's Normal, Species by Species. Yes. The species-by-species duration table is the single most useful thing in the bundle for stopping a panicked keeper from digging up a buried animal. Grade A-.

Household Pesticides and Your Invertebrate Pet. Yes. Learned the mechanism (selectivity is invert-vs-vertebrate, not pest-bug-vs-pet-bug) and got a real prevention checklist, including telling a pest control company in advance. Grade A.

No article here felt thin or padded. This is the strongest bundle of the ones I've read.

**2. Improvements for C-or-lower articles**

None graded C or below.

**3. Title-vs-content gaps**

Fish Quarantine and Treatment: none. Title promises quarantine and treatment, delivers both.

Freshwater pH, GH, and KH: none real, though a reader with a non-goldfish tank has to do a small translation step themselves, which the article flags honestly rather than hides.

Giant Millipede Feeding: none.

Invertebrate Emergency Plan: the title promises "emergency plan" broadly but the piece explicitly declines to give a fixed "safe alone" number for tarantulas. That's a defensible, disclosed choice, not a gap, but a reader searching for a specific day count will leave empty-handed.

Invertebrate Molting 101: none, it delivers exactly what a "101, species by species" title promises.

Household Pesticides: none.

**4. Overlap and conflict**

The millipede feeding guide and the molting guide both describe the same event (a millipede vanishing underground to molt) and agree: several weeks underground, then more days buried while the new shell hardens, don't dig. No conflict.

The emergency plan guide references the molting guide twice (for live-prey risk during a molt, and for pre-molt fasting in tarantulas) and the two are consistent about a tarantula's normal pre-molt fast being a non-emergency.

The pesticide guide and the emergency plan guide both belong to the same invertebrate content family and don't overlap in subject, so no repetition there.

One near-overlap: the fish quarantine guide's aquarium salt section presents two different salt dosing frameworks (VCA's tablespoon-per-5-gallons for ich versus Merck's gram-per-liter ranges for transport stress or general parasite suppression) side by side. The article itself flags these as "different tools for different jobs, in different units," which is the right call, but a skimming reader could easily read only the first number and misapply it to the second use case. Quoting both: VCA says "add about a tablespoon of salt per 5 gallons of water," Merck describes "a 3-to-5 g/L solution for a few hours or days" for transport versus "maintaining a freshwater system at 2 to 3 g/L" for parasite suppression. These aren't contradictory, but they are close enough in phrasing to invite confusion if read fast.

**5. Trust**

Nothing here reads as overstated or invented. Every specific number is attributed to a named source (Merck Veterinary Manual, VCA Animal Hospitals, USGS Water Science School, University of Illinois College of Veterinary Medicine, NPIC, USPS mailing standards, a named 2021 Scientific Reports study). The pesticide article is unusually careful about the limits of its own evidence: it states plainly that direct toxicology on pet tarantulas and scorpions barely exists and that extending insect-specific data to arachnids and myriapods is "a reasonable inference... not a documented finding," which is the kind of honesty that builds trust rather than undermines the article's usefulness.

The most convincing sentence in the bundle is from that same pesticide article: "The selectivity these products advertise is invertebrate-versus-vertebrate, not pest-insect-versus-pet-insect. A tarantula is on the wrong side of that line by definition." It reframes a genuinely counterintuitive risk in one clean logical move, backed immediately by a direct NPIC quote.

A minor soft spot: the emergency plan guide's claim that a hermit crab "breathes through modified gills" is stated as settled fact with no citation attached to that specific sentence, unlike nearly everything else in the piece. It's very likely correct but stands out as the one uncited biological claim in an otherwise heavily sourced article.

**6. Bundle grade and first two changes**

Grade: A. Every article is well sourced, specific, honestly scoped about what it doesn't know, and free of filler. This is the benchmark bundle so far.

First two changes: tighten the fish quarantine guide's salt section so the two dosing frameworks (VCA's tablespoon measure and Merck's gram-per-liter ranges) are visually separated, maybe in a small table, so a skimming reader can't merge them. Second, add a source citation to the emergency plan guide's hermit crab gill-breathing claim, since it's the one unsupported biological assertion in a piece that otherwise cites everything.
