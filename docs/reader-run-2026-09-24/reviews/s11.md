## jacksons-chameleon

**1. Page by page**
- Hub (00): Finish it, it's the router. Numbers check out against the deep dives. Grade A.
- Encyclopedia (01): Finish it. Good history (Boulenger 1896, the 1972 Hawaii introduction, the export ban). Grade A.
- Cost guide (02): Finish it. Real table, dated prices, honest about the wild-caught Hawaii angle. Grade A.
- Handling guide (03): Finish it, though it's short and much of it repeats the live-birth fact already in the encyclopedia and cost guide. Grade B (thin in places).
- Health issues (04): Finish it. Clear vet-now signals, ties each disease back to a husbandry cause. Grade A.
- Tank setup (05): Finish it, act on it directly. The single article you could build a cage from. Grade A.
- Feeding guide (06): Finish it. The best page in the set, it resolves its own numbers (three vs seven insects) instead of hiding the tension. Grade A.

**2. Hub vs. encyclopedia**
Both earn their place. The encyclopedia stays in natural history and never repeats a care number; the hub is all care numbers and never repeats the natural history. No disagreement between them. The one soft spot: the hub's "adult size" row (9-13 inches) has no linked source, it's an orphan fact that happens to match the encyclopedia's own figure exactly, so it isn't wrong, just uncited on the hub.

**3. Could I keep one after reading only this set?**
Yes, on husbandry: enclosure size, temperature gradient (with the night drop explained, not just stated), humidity, UVB bulb and swap schedule, feeding amounts and supplement rhythm are all specific and consistent. Two real gaps. First, quarantine is a "day one" line item on the hub and nothing else, no deep dive walks through what a 3-to-6-month quarantine setup actually looks like day to day; it's only a title in the sidebar. Second, nothing in this set says what to do with the 5-30 live young once a female gives birth. The feeding guide flags a gravid female's changed appetite and the handling guide states the birth numbers, but neonate housing, feeding, or age-to-rehome never gets raised, let alone answered.

**4. Overlap and conflict across deep dives**
Overlap: the live-birth fact (5-30 young, 5-6 month gestation) appears in the encyclopedia, cost guide, and handling guide, always with the same numbers, no drift. The edema/gout vitamin-A-and-D warning is repeated in the health guide, tank setup, and feeding guide, again consistently. One real conflict: tank setup gives ambient air as 68-75°F, while the feeding guide, explaining why a chameleon stops eating, says the species "lives at 70-80°F." Both are plausible bands for the same animal, but they don't match, and nothing cross-references the discrepancy.

**5. Trust**
What raises trust: the feeding guide openly reconciles two sourced numbers (three insects from "a chameleon-specialist protocol," seven from "the hobby's standard care sheet") instead of picking one and pretending there's no tension. The sentence that most convinced me a keeper wrote it: "An adult Jackson's that gets seven every day is on the road to gout." That's a specific, hard-won number, not a hedge. What dents trust slightly: the 68-75 vs 70-80°F ambient mismatch above, and the enclosure/humidity "day-low, night-high" line in tank setup, which admits the misting schedule doesn't actually hold 60-100% at all times, only within it, phrased vaguely enough to be unfalsifiable.

**6. Grade: A-.** Deep and internally checked husbandry content, let down only by the one uncaught temperature mismatch and the missing neonate-care thread. First two changes: reconcile the 68-75/70-80°F ambient figures into one number, and add a short section (or a link) on what happens to the live young after birth.

**Not covered anywhere:**
- Quarantine setup specifics for a new Jackson's chameleon (only a title link, no walkthrough).
- Care for the 5-30 live-born young after a female gives birth.
- Where to find or vet a reputable captive-bred source, beyond "captive-bred is worth the price."

## tegu

**1. Page by page**
- Hub (00): Finish it, dense and well organized. Grade A.
- Encyclopedia (01): Finish it. The Florida invasion numbers (2006 discovery, ~3,300 pulled from Miami-Dade) are the kind of detail a filler page skips. Grade A.
- Cost guide (02): Finish it, honest that the "8ft" enclosure sold at retail is only 8x2x2, not the 8x4x4 an adult needs. Grade A.
- Handling guide (03): Finish it. Cites LafeberVet, ReptiFiles, and a real bite case report by name. Grade A.
- Health issues (04): Finish it, ties every disease to a specific husbandry gap. Grade A.
- Tank setup (05): Finish it and build from it; it also carries the diet section, since no separate feeding article was in this set. Grade A.
- Enrichment (06): Finish it. Upfront that there's no tegu-specific research and says so before making any claim. Grade A.

**2. Hub vs. encyclopedia**
Both earn their place, no repeats. The hub is buy-and-build numbers; the encyclopedia is origin, taxonomy, and the Florida invasion history. No disagreement between the two on the facts they share (Florida ban, April 29, 2021, both name it exactly the same way).

**3. Could I keep one after reading only this set?**
Mostly, yes: enclosure dimensions, temperature, humidity, substrate depth, UVB, and a feeding frequency schedule by age are all here, folded into the tank setup article since a standalone feeding article wasn't part of this set. Two questions this set raises and never answers. First, several pages describe 12-18 inches of substrate across an 8x4 foot floor as something a tegu digs into and lives in, but nothing says how or how often you maintain or spot-clean substrate that deep, which matters given the same set flags respiratory infection from a "too damp" enclosure. Second, nothing anywhere tells you how to sex a tegu, unlike the chameleon set, which explains sexing by horn shape in its own handling guide; here it's simply absent.

**4. Overlap and conflict across deep dives**
Overlap: the Florida ban date and the "8x2x2 sold vs. 8x4x4 needed" mismatch both appear in the cost guide and the tank setup guide, worded almost identically, no drift. The obesity-from-juvenile-diet warning repeats in the health guide and tank setup, consistently. One genuine, openly stated conflict rather than a hidden one: tank setup notes LafeberVet's enclosure floor as 6x3x3 feet against the 8x4x4 the article itself recommends, and separately notes LafeberVet's adult size (5.2 ft/11 lb) against ReptiFiles' (5 ft/15 lb). Both are flagged as source disagreements in the text itself, not left for the reader to discover.

**5. Trust**
The strongest trust signal here is a pattern, not a single line: this set repeatedly names its sources, reconciles them out loud when they differ, and states plainly when there's no research at all ("There is no enrichment study on this species. That belongs in the first line"). The sentence that most convinced me a real keeper wrote it: "Manually misting an enclosure this large, 8 feet by 4 feet by 4 feet, isn't realistic for most keepers on a consistent basis." That's a specific, practical judgment call, not a generic warning. Nothing in the set undercuts that trust; I found no internal contradiction that wasn't already flagged by the text itself.

**6. Grade: A-.** Excellent, source-honest husbandry content across the board, docked only for the two silent gaps (substrate hygiene, sexing) rather than anything wrong on the page. First two changes: add a substrate maintenance/spot-cleaning note to tank setup, and add a sexing section to the handling guide to match the chameleon set's standard.

**Not covered anywhere:**
- How to sex an Argentine tegu.
- Substrate maintenance or spot-cleaning routine for 12-18 inches of deep burrow substrate.
