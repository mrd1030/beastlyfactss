2. Articles graded C or lower
None graded C or below in this bundle.

3. Title-versus-content gaps
- Bioactive Setups for Bearded Dragons: doesn't give a rough cost or a list of what to buy, which "setup" articles on this site often do elsewhere; a reader pricing this out gets nothing here.
- Outdoor Reptile Housing: the title promises "what actually kills them," which is delivered, but never quantifies drowning or overheating rates the way it quantifies raven predation, so those two causes feel asserted rather than shown.

4. Overlap and conflict
The bioactive article and the outdoor housing article both touch tortoise substrate and plant safety but don't conflict with each other or with the weeds article; they're complementary rather than repetitive. The chelonian herpesvirus article and the safe weeds article both reference Russian and sulcata tortoises' health guides without contradicting one another. No numeric disagreements found between articles in this set. One structural oddity: the gut-loading article is about feeder insects generically (crickets, mealworms, dubia roaches, for any insectivorous reptile) but its "Deep Dive" sidebar list is entirely White's Tree Frog articles, which reads like a templating mismatch rather than a genuine content pairing.

5. Trust
The chelonian herpesvirus article is the most convincing piece in the bundle because it is specific and willing to complicate its own argument: it distinguishes true tortoises from box turtles, credits ranavirus rather than herpesvirus as the box turtle killer, and gives a real, sourced number (75 percent of twenty tortoises died in one outbreak) rather than a vague scare line. The most convincing single sentence: "The tortoise later identified as the outbreak's source had already completed a full year of quarantine... and showed no clinical signs for three years afterward beyond an occasional 'wet nose'... When the virus finally triggered an outbreak, 75 percent of the twenty tortoises it exposed died, fifteen animals." Nothing in the bundle reads as overstated or fabricated. The bioactive article is the weakest on rigor since it's personal-anecdote-driven (a named pet dragon, "for me it has been") with affiliate product mentions (Bio Dude kit, specific isopod brands) rather than sourced claims, but it doesn't claim to be anything else.

6. Bundle grade: A-. Reason: consistently well-sourced, specific, and willing to name genuine uncertainty (e.g., gut-loading article admitting most gut-load products underperform their labels), with only one lightweight, affiliate-flavored outlier.
Two changes first: give the bioactive dragon article a rough cost/shopping list like its sibling guides have, and fix the mismatched sidebar on the gut-loading article so it doesn't point to an unrelated frog species.
