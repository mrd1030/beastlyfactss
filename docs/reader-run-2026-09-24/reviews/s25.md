## praying-mantis

1. Page by page
- Hub (00): Finish it, it's the whole plan in one scroll. Grade A.
- Encyclopedia (01): Finish it, short natural history plus the Chinese mantis origin story. Grade B (thin, but that is its job here).
- Cost guide (02): Finish it, you can budget and shop with it. Grade A.
- Handling guide (03): Finish it, tells you when to touch and when not to. Grade B.
- Health issues guide (04): Finish it, prevention checklist you can act on before something goes wrong. Grade A.
- Tank setup guide (05): Finish it, the numbers that actually build the enclosure. Grade A.
- Feeding guide (06): Finish it, table plus the two rules that matter. Grade A.
- Enrichment guide (07): Finish it, honest about having no mantis-specific research and gives a priority list anyway. Grade A-.
- Ootheca guide (08): Finish it, tells you what to do before the hatch, not after. Grade A-.

2. Hub and encyclopedia
Both earn their place. The hub is a router with numbers pulled straight from the deep dives and linked back to them, not a rewrite. The encyclopedia adds something none of the deep dives cover: the 1896 accidental introduction of the Chinese mantis and why its egg cases are the cheap, legal starter species. No numbers disagree between hub and deep dives that I could find. The hub's cost line ("Budget: $15 to $35 for a Carolina or Chinese mantis, up to $150 for an Orchid. A full setup runs $100 to $145") matches the cost guide's own totals ("roughly $100 to $145, before the mantis itself"), and the enclosure numbers (8x8x12, drop of 12 inches for a mantis up to three inches) match word for word between the hub and the tank setup guide.

3. The set as a whole
Yes, you could set up, buy, feed, and keep a common species healthy off this set. What's still missing: nobody tells you how to tell male from female, which matters for both lifespan (females outlive males, repeated three times) and for deciding whether to breed. Nobody gives a number of molts (instars) to adulthood, so a new owner has no way to gauge "is this normal" versus "this is taking too long." The health guide raises "hand-feeding with fine tongs" for a crooked-limbed survivor without saying how that differs from normal feeding, though the feeding guide's forceps method answers it well enough if you connect the two yourself, the text never says so. The ootheca guide raises cold diapause and then punts to "research your specific species," which is honest but leaves a real gap for anyone who actually gets an ootheca from anything other than a Chinese mantis.

4. Overlap and conflict
Heavy repetition, no numeric conflicts. Solo housing and cannibalism appear in the hub, the handling guide, the enrichment guide, and the ootheca guide, each time with nearly the same wording. The pre-molt "off food, still, don't touch it" signal appears in the hub, the handling guide, the health guide, and the feeding guide. Crickets left in the cage chewing on a molting mantis is stated in the tank setup guide, the feeding guide, the enrichment guide, and the handling guide's mistake list. The enclosure height and drop numbers (3x body length, 4x drop) are repeated in the hub, tank setup, and enrichment guides but always match exactly, so it reads as consistent messaging rather than sloppiness, just a lot of it.

5. Trust
This reads like someone who has actually raised these. The line that convinced me most: "One specialist sheet skips them entirely in favor of flies and roaches," in the feeding guide's cricket section, a caveat that only shows up if you've noticed other keepers disagree about crickets and decided to flag it rather than pretend there's one right answer. The honey-drop tip for a reluctant eater and the specific detail that a mantis loses its grip and the fall alone can deform it, not just the failed shed itself, both feel lived-in rather than copied. Nothing made me doubt the set; the closest thing to a soft spot is the ootheca guide admitting there's no reliable way to tell a fertile egg case from an infertile one by looking at it, but that's presented as an honest limit, not a dodge.

6. Grade: A-. Consistent numbers everywhere they matter, and the set is honest about what it doesn't know. The two changes I'd make first: add a short "how to sex a mantis" note, since lifespan and breeding decisions both hang on it and nothing in the set answers it; and give African mantis (or at least one tropical species besides Ghost and Orchid) actual humidity and temperature numbers instead of only a lifespan figure, since the enclosure guide's numbers only cover Chinese/Carolina and Ghost/Orchid.

7. Not covered anywhere
- How to sex a mantis (needed for the lifespan-by-sex claims made three separate times, and for breeding). None answers it.
- Cold diapause specifics (how cold, how long) for temperate species' oothecae. The ootheca guide names the issue and stops at "research your specific species."
- Enclosure temperature and humidity numbers for African mantises specifically, only their lifespan (6 to 9 months) is given anywhere in the set.
- Number of nymph molts (instars) before adulthood, so a keeper has a baseline for what's normal.
