## zebra-finch

**1. Page by page**

- 00 Care guide hub: Finish it. Dense, numeric, and states the one non-negotiable (never one bird) up front. A
- 01 Encyclopedia: Finish it, short. The genome-sequencing and aviculture-founded-research-colonies history is genuinely new information. A-
- 02 Cost guide: Finish it. Names actual sellers and prices, and frames the real cost decision (minimum two birds) honestly. A
- 03 Handling guide: Finish it. Quotes named sources (Lafeber, PetMD, a named vet) rather than paraphrasing vaguely. A
- 04 Health issues guide: Finish it. Cites Merck and VCA by name for each condition, unusually well sourced for this kind of page. A
- 05 Cage setup guide: Finish it, though one paragraph reads confusingly on first pass (see below). B+
- 06 Feeding guide: Finish it. Clear percentages, explains why the seed-selection problem makes an all-seed diet worse than it looks. A
- 07 Enrichment guide: Finish it. Cites a specific field study with numbers (94.2% mixed-sex pairs) rather than a vague claim. A

**2. Hub and encyclopedia**

Both earn their place. The hub is a genuine index, its numbers (cage size, diet split, budget, lifespan) match the deep dives they link to almost exactly, down to carrying over caveats like "some only 3 to 5" for lifespan. The encyclopedia's only overlap with the hub is size and difficulty; the founding-of-research-colonies history and the male/female appearance description are unique to it.

I found no numeric disagreement between the hub and any deep dive. Lifespan, cage size, bar spacing, and diet percentages all match across the hub, cost guide, cage setup guide, and feeding guide.

**3. The set as a whole**

Yes. Buying, housing, feeding, and watching for illness are all covered with real numbers, and the set is unusually disciplined about repeating the one fact that most affects welfare (never keep one) at every opportunity rather than burying it. What's thinner: no page states what to actually do the first day the birds come home beyond quarantine duration, and no page gives introduction steps for adding a third bird to an existing pair beyond a one-line warning in the enrichment guide.

One question raised on one page and only answered, obliquely, elsewhere: the cage setup guide's paragraph on the RSPCA's space guidance is oddly worded, it says to compromise on "width" while keeping "height and length generous," right after saying width matters more than height because finches fly rather than climb. Read closely the two are compatible (length carries the flight path; the sacrificed "width" is depth, not length), but the article never resolves the apparent tension itself, and a reader would need to reread it twice to see it isn't a contradiction.

**4. Overlap and conflict across the deep dives**

The cage-shape argument (wide/long over tall, since finches fly rather than climb) and the "never house solo" rule both appear near-verbatim across the hub, cost guide, cage setup guide, and enrichment guide. That is expected repetition for a fact this central, not filler, since each restatement adds a slightly different angle (welfare framing, cost framing, research framing).

I found no real numeric conflict across the deep dives. The one soft inconsistency is the cage guide's RSPCA paragraph described above, which sits oddly next to its own opening claim about width, though it is not a contradiction once read carefully.

**5. Trust**

The named, quoted sources throughout (Merck Veterinary Manual, RSPCA, VCA, Lafeber, PetMD, a specific 2025 PLoS One study) and the specific statistic on wild pair structure (94.2 percent mixed-sex) read as someone who did real reading rather than paraphrasing a species blurb. The sentence that most convinced me a real keeper or careful researcher wrote this: "It was not that crowded birds were more stressed, which is what you would expect. It was that the number of birds mattered on its own, with density held constant, and only in females." That is too specific and too willing to contradict intuition to be generic filler.

Nothing here raised real doubt; the one soft mark against it is the confusingly worded RSPCA paragraph noted above.

**6. Grade: A-.** Extremely well sourced and consistent, marked down only for one confusingly worded paragraph in the cage setup guide and for not spelling out day-one and multi-bird-introduction steps anywhere in the set. First two changes: rewrite the RSPCA space paragraph in the cage setup guide so "compromise on width" doesn't read as contradicting "width matters more than height" two sentences earlier, and add a short first-day and introducing-a-third-bird section, since the emergency card and quarantine numbers are handled but the actual mechanics of adding a bird are not.

**7. Not covered anywhere**

- Step-by-step instructions for introducing a third bird to an existing pair (only a one-line warning about dominance and a lighter bird losing condition is given): None found.
- What to actually do on day one at home beyond quarantine duration and location: None found.
