## hognose-snake

1. Page by page:
- 00 hub: A. Finish it. Actionable first-week numbers for every topic, each sourced to its own article.
- 01 encyclopedia: B. Finish it. Mostly background, not action, but the only page with origin, wild diet, and trade history, so it earns its keep.
- 02 cost guide: A. Actionable. Dated price check, honest that morph price has nothing to do with care difficulty.
- 03 handling guide: A. Actionable. Explains the bluff display and gives the real, low bite risk with a cited case.
- 04 health issues: A. Actionable. Every problem traces to a fixable husbandry cause.
- 05 tank setup: A. Actionable. States the heat-source debate plainly instead of hiding it.
- 06 feeding: A. Actionable. Gram-weight prey chart and a clear threshold for when to start scenting.
- 07 enrichment: A. Actionable. Names the study, its authors, journal, and year, and turns the finding into a priority list.

2. Hub versus encyclopedia: both earn their place. The hub is a router with real numbers tied to a source article, not a summary of the deep dives, and the encyclopedia is the only page with wild history and the "marginal animal in the trade for decades" backstory. No content overlap between them worth flagging.

One numbers conflict between hub and cost guide, not the encyclopedia. The hub and tank setup guide agree: "A 20-gallon equivalent (30x13x13 inches) minimum for a male, a 40-gallon (36x18x16) for a larger female." The cost guide's setup table instead prices "36x18x18 in enclosure" as the single line item, an inch taller than the female minimum given everywhere else, and it doesn't split by sex at all.

3. The set as a whole: yes, after all eight pages I could legally check (partially; see below), set up, feed, and keep this animal healthy. The pieces fit: dry humidity plus off-substrate feeding plus a monthly weigh-in cover almost every failure mode in the health guide.

One gap the set raises but never answers: both the feeding guide and the hub mention that "seasonal change heading toward brumation can put a snake off food," but no page in this set, and nothing in the Health and More list, explains what brumation actually involves for this species: whether to induce a cooling period, how long, or how to tell normal winter appetite loss from a problem. It's raised twice as a reason not to worry, never once as its own topic.

4. Overlap and conflict across deep dives: heavy, consistent repetition of "feed in a separate container off the substrate to prevent impaction," stated near-identically in the hub, the health issues guide, the tank setup guide, and the feeding guide. Not a conflict, just the same fact four times. The venom/bite FAQ is reused almost word for word between the hub and the handling guide, again consistent rather than contradictory.

The one real conflict: the tank setup guide and hub give the female enclosure minimum as "36x18x16 inches," while the cost guide's price table lists the same line item as "36x18x18 in enclosure." Same species, same context, different height.

5. Trust: the enrichment article's citation, "Nagabaskaran, Skinner and Miller set up a preference test, published in the journal Animals in 2022," with a real methodology (both sides had shelters and a gradient, so the comparison was enrichment against enrichment, not enrichment against neglect) is the strongest trust signal in the set. That's not a sentence a generic content farm writes. The cost guide's "Prices last checked September 2026 at Josh's Frogs and PetSmart" note, with the caveat that retail prices move, is the next best sign of a real person maintaining the page rather than letting it go stale.

The single sentence that most convinced me a keeper wrote this: "A hognose that decides hissing and hooding isn't working will sometimes commit fully to playing dead, rolling belly up, mouth gaping open, tongue lolling out, and occasionally releasing musk for good measure. Flip it right side up mid-act and it will often flop itself back over onto its back." That's a specific, slightly amused detail nobody writes without having watched it happen.

What made me doubt them, briefly: the enclosure height mismatch above, and the hub's oddly precise, forward-dated legal claim that "Colorado allows captive-bred Western and Plains hognose with documentation from May 1, 2026," which I have no way to check from this set and reads like a law that hasn't taken effect yet being stated as settled fact.

6. Grade: A-minus. A genuinely usable, internally consistent set built around one clear husbandry throughline (dry, deep, off-substrate feeding), docked half a grade for the enclosure size conflict and the unexplained brumation gap.

Two changes I'd make first: fix the cost guide's enclosure line to match the 36x18x16 figure used everywhere else, and add even a short paragraph or a linked page on brumation, since two separate articles use it to explain lost appetite without ever defining it.

7. Not covered anywhere:
- Brumation: what it is for this species, whether to induce or manage a cooling period, and how to tell a normal seasonal fast from a health problem. Mentioned twice as a cause of appetite loss, never its own topic, and no title in the Health and More list covers it.
