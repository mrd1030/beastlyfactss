## parrotlet

1. Page by page:
- Hub: A. Genuinely specific first-week numbers, including the 1/4-inch bar spacing detail most bird hubs would round off.
- Encyclopedia: A-. The 1847 description, the slow 1930-to-1980s obscurity, and the 1992 Wild Bird Conservation Act's effect on the species are real history, not filler.
- Cost guide: A. Clear tables, and the one-bird-vs-two framing as a cost and welfare decision (not just a price line) is handled well.
- Handling guide: A. The warning-nip detail and the explicit contrast with how an under-socialized budgie behaves versus an under-socialized parrotlet is the most useful single distinction in the set.
- Health issues guide: A. Correctly separates PBFD's geographic risk pattern (African/Australian/Indonesian species higher risk) from a blanket "test for everything" approach, and is specific about egg-binding signs.
- Cage setup guide: A. The 1/4-inch bar spacing rationale (smaller head than a lovebird) is a genuine, non-generic detail.
- Feeding guide: A. The beak-ridge explanation for why grit isn't needed is a real mechanism, not just a rule.
- Enrichment guide: A-, honest about borrowed evidence. States plainly "Nothing on parrotlets" before citing Amazon parrot and cockatoo studies, which is the right way to handle an evidence gap.

2. The hub and encyclopedia are not redundant: the hub is the actionable checklist and the encyclopedia adds the description history and the 40-to-50-bird wild flock size that shows up nowhere else. No numeric conflict between them; adult size, weight, and lifespan match across both.

3. This set gets you to a legal (no legal guide flagged as needed here), correctly sized, fed, and enrichment-appropriate setup, and it repeatedly and correctly flags the one-bird-vs-pair decision as the single biggest fork in the road. What's raised but not answered in this set: the actual quarantine protocol and disease screening list (chlamydia, salmonella, polyomavirus, PBFD) is named on the hub with a specific 30-to-45-day timeline, but the "Quarantining a New Bird" guide that would walk through it isn't included here, only listed. Same for the photoperiod/hormone mechanism behind egg-laying and plucking, and the pellet-conversion method for a seed-raised bird, both named but not present.

4. Overlap and conflict: the one-bird-vs-pair message repeats, consistently, across the cost guide, handling guide, cage setup guide, and enrichment guide, and every version agrees a single bird bonds harder to its person while a pair bonds to itself and needs a slow supervised introduction. No numeric conflicts found; cage size (18x18x24 in minimum), bar spacing (1/4 in), and temperature (65-80°F) match everywhere they appear.

5. Trust: the beak-ridge mechanism for why grit isn't needed, and the specific claim that a hen can be triggered into laying by "day length, an overabundance of food, or even a mirror or favorite toy," both read like real, checkable ornithology rather than templated copy. The sentence that most convinced me a bird keeper wrote this: "It genuinely does not seem to know how small it is," used consistently across five separate pages to explain everything from bite force to mixed-aviary risk, which is the kind of observation that comes from actually living with the animal rather than summarizing a care sheet.

6. Grade: A-. Specific, consistent, and honest about thin research, but it leans on the shared bird library for quarantine, photoperiod, and pellet-conversion mechanics that a new owner needs in week one and won't find in this set. Two changes: pull the quarantine timeline and screening list into the parrotlet health guide itself rather than only naming it on the hub, and add a short pellet-conversion paragraph to the feeding guide instead of only linking the budgie version.

7. Not covered anywhere:
- None outright; every gap raised in section 3 (quarantine protocol, photoperiod mechanism, pellet conversion method) is named in the Health and More list, just not included as full text in this set.
