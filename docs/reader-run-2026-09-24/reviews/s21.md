## green-iguana

**1. Page by page**
- Hub (00): Finish it. Act on it: the first-week list alone tells you what to buy and what to check. Grade A.
- Encyclopedia (01): Finish it. Act on the CITES/trade history and the wild-vs-captive lifespan split, neither is on the hub. Grade A.
- Cost guide (02): Finish it. Act on the two-purchase framing (kit now, walk-in later) and the monthly budget. Grade B, for an internal math slip (see below).
- Handling guide (03): Finish it. Act on claws/tail-whip/autotomy and the side-approach rule. Grade A-.
- Health issues guide (04): Finish it. Act on the MBD symptom list and the kidney-disease causes. Grade A-.
- Tank setup guide (05): Finish it. Act on every number in it: size, temp, humidity, substrate, UVB. Grade A.
- Feeding guide (06): Finish it. Act on the 80-90% greens rule and the oxalate/goitrogen distinction. Grade A.
- Enrichment guide (07): Finish it. Act on the priority order and the foraging setup. Grade A.

No page reads as thin or filler. Every one carries specific numbers, named sources, or a named study rather than generic reptile-care boilerplate.

**2. Hub and encyclopedia**
Both earn their place. The hub is a router built from the deep dives, the encyclopedia adds material none of the deep dives cover: the 1977 CITES listing, import numbers peaking near a million animals in 1996, and the fact that wild lifespan is about 8 years against the widely quoted 15-20 year captive figure. Neither repeats the deep dives, they cite the same headline numbers (size, lifespan, the 70% first-year mortality rate) but don't re-explain them.

No hard disagreement between hub and any deep dive. One near-miss: the hub's first-week entry and the tank setup FAQ both say basking needs "roughly six halogens," while the hub's own buy list and the cost guide's kit table both list "Halogen basking bulbs, 75 watt, two or three." Read carefully, the kit table is pricing the juvenile setup and six bulbs is for an adult's full basking branch, but the page never says that out loud, so a first-time reader hits what looks like a contradiction before working it out themselves.

**3. Could I actually keep this animal**
Yes, functionally: size, temperature, humidity, substrate, UVB, diet ratios, supplement schedule, and the emergency symptom list are all here with real numbers. What's still missing after reading everything: a fecal/quarantine protocol specific to iguanas (the hub links out to a generic reptile quarantine article, not read here), and legal ownership status, referenced by title in the Deep Dive list ("Is It Legal to Own a Green Iguana?") but the article itself isn't in this set. The enrichment guide raises "the reptile quarantine guide covers the fecal screen" without saying what that screen actually costs or takes, which the cost guide should have priced but doesn't.

A real gap the text doesn't flag: the cost guide's first-year kit total doesn't match its own line items. It sums to $1,041-$1,105 by the table (549+99+130+180+12+18+15+11=$1,041 low end), but the article's prose and FAQ both say "about $985 to $1,100," a number that appears nowhere in the table math. The hub then repeats that same $985-$1,100 figure as fact.

**4. Overlap and conflict across deep dives**
Read twice, near-verbatim: the 70% first-year mortality figure (cost guide, health issues guide, and the hub), the adult size (6-7 ft, up to 20 lbs, handling guide and cost guide), and the humidity-to-kidney-disease link (tank setup and health issues guide). None of these disagree, they're consistent restatements, which is fine for pages meant to stand alone.

One real internal conflict, inside the cost guide itself: "Add those rows and the kit lands at about $1,041 to $1,105" versus the FAQ two paragraphs later, "The first-year kit runs about $985 to $1,100." Those are two different numbers for the same thing on the same page.

One soft tension worth a reader's attention rather than a flat contradiction: the hub states diet is "no insects... ever," while the feeding guide notes "a published tolerance, at under 5% of an adult iguana's diet" before recommending against feeding it anyway. Not a disagreement, but the hub oversimplifies a nuance the feeding guide is careful about.

**5. Trust**
The sourcing raised my confidence, not my doubt: named VCA and LafeberVet links with URLs, a named 1996 conference paper for diet ratios, and a real citation to Burghardt et al.'s 1977 Science paper on hatchling sociality, with the follow-up field studies described honestly as correlational. The one thing that gave me pause was the cost guide's unreconciled math. The sentence that most convinced me a keeper wrote this: "Building toward that end state from the start is genuinely the better approach than upgrading repeatedly," a plain, unglamorous piece of advice nobody writes unless they've done the upgrading themselves.

**6. Grade: A-.** Deep, specific, well-sourced pages let down only by one unfixed arithmetic error and a legal-status article that's referenced but absent from the set. First changes: reconcile the cost guide's kit total with its own table, and either include or stop cross-referencing the legal guide from a set that doesn't have it.

- Legal ownership status by state: named in the Deep Dive list on every page, but the article itself is absent here.
