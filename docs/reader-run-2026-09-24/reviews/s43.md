## discus

**1. Page by page**
- Hub: would finish, front-loads the water-change routine as the defining fact rather than burying it. Grade A.
- Encyclopedia: would finish, the Wattley/Schmidt-Focke breeding history and strain timeline are real additions. Grade A.
- Cost guide: would finish, honest that the fish itself is rarely the biggest expense. Grade A.
- Handling guide: would finish, the strongest page in the set for naming genuine disagreements (acclimation method, neon tetra compatibility) instead of flattening them. Grade A.
- Health issues guide: would finish, the 2019 hole-in-the-head study pushing back on the beef-heart folklore is the standout. Grade A.
- Tank setup guide: would finish, treats filtration and substrate as open debates rather than pretending there is one right answer. Grade A.
- Feeding guide: would finish, the discus milk science is properly cited rather than treated as a cute aside. Grade A.
- Enrichment guide: would finish, honest that there is no discus-specific study and explains why the neon tetra shoaling research doesn't transfer to a hierarchy-driven cichlid. Grade A-.

Nothing here read as thin or padded.

**2. Hub and encyclopedia**
Both earn their place clearly. The hub is numbers and links, the encyclopedia is wild origin, taxonomy, and the breeding history that made the hobby possible, and neither duplicates the deep dives at length. No numeric disagreement between hub and deep dives turned up: tank size (55 gallon floor, 75 the real target), temperature (82 to 86°F), pH (6.0 to 7.0), water-change routine (25 to 50% weekly, some every 2 to 3 days), and group size (5 to 6 or more) all match word for word across the hub, tank setup, and enrichment pages. The one soft mismatch is lifespan: the encyclopedia gives a wild lifespan of "10 to 13 years," while the cost guide gives a captive range of "10 to 15 years... though some sources put the typical captive lifespan closer to 8 to 10." Different populations, so not a real contradiction, but a reader skimming both could come away with two different numbers for the same word.

**3. Could I keep this animal**
Mostly yes, and this set does an unusually good job of telling me where the real disagreements are rather than hiding them. I could size and cycle a tank, hit temperature and pH, feed correctly, and recognize the emergency signs. One real question the set raises without fully closing: the tank setup guide says a canister filter "competes with the fish for dissolved oxygen and can fail dangerously during a power outage," and the Health and More list includes a dedicated power-outage guide, but the discus pages never point a reader there directly even though this species runs both warmer water and a debated filter choice that makes an outage more dangerous for it than for most fish. The link exists in the sidebar, but nothing in the tank setup or health pages says "read this one especially."

**4. Overlap and conflict across deep dives**
The clearest overlap is the discus milk fact, described at similar length and with the same 2010 study in both the hub's Fun Fact and the feeding guide, plus a shorter version in the enrichment guide. That's reasonable repetition for a genuinely interesting fact, not filler.

Two disagreements are named honestly rather than hidden, which is worth crediting: acclimation method (the handling guide quotes general fishkeeping advice for a 60-to-90-minute drip against "at least one discus-specialty breeder" arguing for a fast temperature and pH match instead, because "oxygen depletes in the shipping water"), and neon tetra compatibility (most discus sources exclude neons at 82 to 86°F, while "at least one general aquarium retailer's guide does include neons as compatible"). The tank setup guide does the same with filtration, canister versus hang-on-back. This is the rare case where flagged disagreement reads as trustworthy rather than as a hedge.

**5. Trust**
The sourcing carries real weight: the Merck Veterinary Manual on Spironucleus, a named 2019 Journal of Fish Diseases study on hole-in-the-head disease, a 2010 Journal of Experimental Biology study on discus milk, and a 2018 Aquaculture and Fisheries feeding study on beef heart, all cited by name and used to correct hobbyist folklore rather than just repeat it. The sentence that most convinced me a real keeper wrote this: "That doesn't mean diet is irrelevant, but it does mean the cause is more complicated than the single-factor explanation that circulates in most hobbyist writing, and water quality and stress remain the better-supported levers." That is someone weighing evidence against what everyone already believes, not repeating a care-sheet template.

**6. Grade**
A. This is the most internally honest set of the four I reviewed today: it names its own disagreements instead of picking one answer and hiding the debate, and its science claims are actually sourced. The two changes I'd make: reconcile the wild-versus-captive lifespan numbers so a skimming reader isn't left with two answers to "how long do discus live," and have the tank setup or health page point directly at the power-outage guide given how much more exposed a warm, canister-filtered discus tank is to an outage than a typical community tank.

**Not covered anywhere**
- None. Every question this set raised about itself, the filtration debate, the acclimation debate, the diet-versus-disease question, was either answered within the set or pointed at a specific Health and More page that covers it.
