## african-grey

**1. Page by page**
- 00 Hub: A. Quick numbers, an emergency card, a buy list. I'd act on this in five minutes.
- 01 Encyclopedia: A. The CITES Appendix I vote (95-35-5, Johannesburg, Oct 2016) and the 1.5 million wild-caught export figure are real content, not filler.
- 02 Cost: A. Ranges for bird, cage, monthly, vet, plus a September 2026 price-check date.
- 03 Handling: A. Honest about the N'kisi word-count claim being anecdotal, not proven.
- 04 Health issues: A. Five conditions, each with cause, signs, and action.
- 05 Cage setup: A. Size, bar spacing, placement, and the zinc-hardware warning most sites skip.
- 06 Feeding: A. Explains why no grit, gives six reasons a grey stops eating.
- 07 Enrichment: A. Cites actual studies (Lumeij and Hommers, 18 birds; Meehan/Millam/Mench) instead of generic "buy toys" advice.
- 09 Grey vs. cockatoo: A. A genuine decision tool, not a fluff comparison.

**2. Hub and encyclopedia vs. deep dives**
Both earn their place. The encyclopedia carries natural history and the CITES vote no deep dive repeats, and the hub is a router, not a rewrite.

But the hub's Fun Fact claims African greys "can learn over 1,000 words." Nothing else in the set supports that. The encyclopedia's own bio caps Alex, the most-studied grey ever, at "over 150 words," and the handling article treats even the informal N'kisi claim of "hundreds of words" as unproven owner testimony, not something to repeat as fact. The hub's 1,000-word line is the single most exaggerated number in the whole set and it contradicts the site's own sourced content.

**3. Could I do this?**
Yes, mostly. Cage, temperature, placement, diet ratios, toxic foods, and the five headline illnesses are all here and consistent. Two real gaps:
- The cost guide says this species' strict CITES status "shapes what documentation a legitimately captive-bred bird should come with," then never says what that documentation is, and doesn't link to wherever that answer lives. A hub card mentions a page called "Is It Legal to Own an African Grey Parrot?" but that page isn't in the reading set, so I can't confirm it answers this.
- The enrichment guide lists "bathing opportunity" as priority 6 with zero detail on frequency or method, and the buy list's "misting bottle or shower perch" doesn't say how often to use either.

**4. Overlap and conflicts across deep dives**
Heavy, expected repetition: the 75/80% pellet split, the 4-5 hour interaction number, and the hypocalcemia/plucking/aspergillosis trio all show up in three or more articles, word for word in places. No numeric conflicts between the deep dives themselves. The vs-cockatoo article's lifespan table says "40-60 years," which is a looser rounding of the cost guide's "mean 45, some to 60, exceptional 70-80," but it's a simplification for a comparison table, not a contradiction.

**5. Trust**
The line that convinced me most a bird keeper wrote this: "A bird that has always been bowl-fed does not automatically know that food is inside a device, and the first stage is often food sitting visibly on top of a foraging toy before any of it goes inside." That's a specific, unglamorous detail nobody invents from a template. The zinc-hardware warning and "the dish comes out" instruction land the same way.

What made me doubt them: that "over 1,000 words" fun fact on the hub. It reads like a recycled internet stat nobody checked against the site's own encyclopedia entry, sitting one click away from a page that gives Alex's real, lower number.

**6. Grade: A-.** Deep, specific, mostly internally consistent, let down by one sloppy fun fact and one dangling claim about paperwork nobody explains.
Two fixes: correct or cut the "over 1,000 words" line, and either link the cost guide's CITES-documentation sentence to the legal article or spell out the actual paperwork inline.

**Not covered anywhere**
- What CITES documentation a legitimately captive-bred African grey should actually come with. None.
- Bathing frequency and method (misting vs. shower perch, how often). None.
