## hamster

1. Page by page
- 00 Hub: A. Every number links to its source article, the emergency card is genuinely useful, would finish and act on it.
- 01 Encyclopedia: B+. Short but earns its keep with the origin story (single 1930 female, the 1839-to-1930 research gap). Not actionable, but not filler either.
- 02 Cost guide: A. Real tables, a "prices checked September 2026" date, honest framing that setup costs more than the animal.
- 03 Handling guide: A. Species-by-species temperament, sourced technique (RSPCA, Merck, Bristol), a clear first-timer mistake list.
- 04 Health issues: A. Wet tail is front and center with real numbers (O'Neill et al., 4,504 hamsters) and a same-day-emergency framing that isn't hedged.
- 05 Enclosure setup: A. Specific square inches, bar spacing by species, the sand bath called out as non-optional.
- 06 Feeding guide: A. Flags the free-feed-vs-scheduled disagreement honestly instead of pretending it's settled. Actionable toxic-food list.
- 07 Enrichment guide: A. One study, three numbers, a clear priority order. The best-organized page in the set.
- 09 vs Guinea Pig: A-. Clean comparison table, doesn't oversell either animal as "the easy one."

2. Hub and encyclopedia
The hub earns its place, it's a router with numbers, not a rewrite of the deep dives. The encyclopedia earns its place too, its history section (Aleppo 1930, Adler's coat pockets, the Gulf Hamstery) appears nowhere else. No numeric disagreement between them: adult size, lifespan, and wet-tail stats all match the deep dives word for word in places, which reads like shared source data rather than duplicated writing.

3. Could I actually keep this animal
Yes, mostly. Cage, bedding, wheel, sand bath, and diet basics are all here with real numbers. The one thing that never gets stated plainly: whether dwarf species (Winter White, Campbell's) can be housed in pairs. The set is emphatic that Syrians must be solo, but it never says the reverse for dwarfs, it only says Syrians are "the opposite" of guinea pigs, implying dwarfs might tolerate company without ever confirming it. That's a real gap for anyone buying a dwarf hamster instead of a Syrian.
Also unstated but implied: the enclosure guide says 6 inches of bedding is the "minimum," then the fun fact box says 15 to 30 inches is what actually improves welfare, and the enrichment guide's own study (10/40/80 cm) is the source for that range. The enclosure page raises the question, "why 6 inches if the real number is bigger," and the enrichment guide is where that gets answered, but the enclosure page doesn't say "see our enrichment guide for why" until its very last line.

4. Overlap and conflict across deep dives
Read twice: wet tail's symptoms and timeline (health guide and feeding guide both give the "can kill within 24-48 hours" line nearly verbatim). Read three times: the Syrian solitary-housing warning appears in the hub, the handling guide, the enrichment guide, and the guinea pig comparison. That's a lot of repetition for one fact, though it's an important one to over-communicate.
No hard numeric conflicts. The closest thing to one: the enclosure guide's fun fact converts the enrichment study's cm figures into "15 to 30 inches," which is a reasonable rounding of 40-80 cm, not an error, but it's presented as a separate claim rather than a cross-reference, so a careful reader has to do the conversion math themselves to see they agree.

5. Trust
Nothing raised real doubt. Sourcing is specific and checkable: an actual study citation (Hauzenberger, Gebhardt-Henrich and Steiger) with sample size and three named conditions, a named veterinary paper (O'Neill et al., JSAP 2022) with an exact percentage, and quotes attributed to Merck, RSPCA, and University of Bristol rather than "experts say." The sentence that most reads like an owner wrote it: "Fun fact box: Wire-gnawing is not a quirk, it is one of the standard behavioral measures used to assess housing quality in small rodents. That is why the study tracked it, and why 'my hamster chews the bars' is a husbandry readout and not a personality trait." That's someone who has actually watched a hamster gnaw bars and gotten annoyed at the "he's just bored" explanation.

6. Grade: A. Reason: specific numbers everywhere, honest about disagreement instead of papering over it, and the two heaviest welfare claims (bedding depth, wet tail) are both backed by named studies rather than assertion.
Two changes: state directly whether dwarf hamsters can be pair-housed, instead of only ruling it out for Syrians. Add a one-line cross-reference on the enclosure page pointing to the enrichment study the moment the 6-inch minimum is given, instead of waiting until the page's last line.

7. Not covered anywhere
- Whether dwarf hamster species (Winter White, Campbell's, Roborovski, Chinese) can be housed in same-sex pairs or groups. None.
- Quarantine or introduction process when bringing a new hamster home (relevant since stress and transport are named wet-tail triggers). None.
- Nail trimming specific to hamsters (the cross-species grooming guide is referenced only by title/summary, and that summary is framed around rabbits, not confirmed to cover hamster nails at all). None.
