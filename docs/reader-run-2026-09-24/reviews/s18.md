# neon-tetra

**1. Page by page**
- 00 Hub: A. Real numbers, an emergency card, a buy list. Finish it.
- 01 Encyclopedia: A. The Hindenburg shipping story alone earns the read.
- 02 Cost: A. Clear tables, honest "vet costs don't apply" line.
- 03 Handling/school size: A. Reframes "handling" as school size, which is the right call for a fish.
- 04 Health issues: A. The NTD section is careful and the "likely over-diagnosed" caveat is unusual honesty.
- 05 Tank setup: B+. Solid, but two paragraphs restate the same tank-size point back to back (see below).
- 06 Feeding: A.
- 07 Enrichment: A. The one citing an actual study, and it changes the headline number.

**2. Hub and encyclopedia**
Both earn their place. The encyclopedia stays in natural history and origin story; the hub is pure numbers and links. Neither repeats a deep dive's reasoning, they just cite its conclusion. No numbers disagree between them and the linked articles: tank size (10 gal for 6, 20 long for 10 to 15), temperature (72 to 78°F within 70 to 81°F), pH (6.0 to 7.0), and the "ten" school size all match word for word between the hub and the tank setup and enrichment articles. That level of exact agreement is the set's strongest asset.

**3. Could I actually keep this fish**
Yes, close to fully. I know the tank size, water chemistry, filter type, lighting, feeding schedule, group size, quarantine window, and the two biggest mistakes (buying two or three fish, skipping quarantine). What's missing: the cycling process itself is only named, never walked through, in this set. It's fine because the hub explicitly links "Cycle first" to the enrichment article, and the enrichment article in turn links out to a full cycling guide, so the trail holds even though the actual ammonia numbers live outside this set. A smaller gap: no article names actual compatible tankmate species, just "peaceful, similarly sized." The stocking guide referenced in the sidebar presumably covers that, but nothing in this set says so directly, so a reader has to guess that's where the answer is.

**4. Overlap and conflict**
Heavy overlap, no conflicts. The FAQ blocks repeat near-verbatim across pages, most obviously the tank-size and feeding-frequency FAQs, which appear word for word on the hub and again on the tank setup and feeding articles. The tank setup article itself repeats its own tank-size paragraph twice in a row ("10 gallons is the practical minimum..." then "A 10-gallon tank is a solid starting point..."), which reads like an edit that never got trimmed. Every number I could cross-check, price, temperature, pH, feeding frequency, quarantine length, matched exactly everywhere it appeared.

**5. Trust**
Nothing raised a real doubt. The "prices last checked September 2026" line on the cost guide is dated to the same month as today, which reads as either genuinely maintained or suspiciously convenient, but the price ranges themselves are unremarkable and plausible either way. The line that most convinced me a real keeper wrote this: "food too large is spat out, which starves the fish while the tank looks fed." That's a specific, non-obvious failure mode nobody invents without having watched it happen. The willingness to say neon tetra disease is "likely over-diagnosed" against the species' own famous reputation is a second strong signal; a padded content mill doesn't undercut its own scariest hook.

**6. Grade: A-.** Internally consistent, well-sourced (a named 2010 study with real methodology), and every page pulls its weight except the tank setup guide's one repeated paragraph. Two changes: cut the duplicated tank-size paragraph in the tank setup guide, and add a short "who not to house them with" list with actual species names instead of leaving that to an unlinked stocking guide.

**Not covered anywhere**
- Acclimating a new fish to the tank (drip method vs. floating the bag): no mention of the actual process, only that a fish needs to survive quarantine first.
- Sexing or breeding neon tetras: absent from every page in this set and not summarized in the Health and More list either.
- Named compatible tankmate species: only "peaceful, similarly sized" is given; no species list anywhere in this set.

# rabbit

**1. Page by page**
- 00 Hub: A. Dense but genuinely useful, and the emergency card is the best single element in either set.
- 01 Encyclopedia: A. The Pope Gregory myth-correction story is a real find, not filler history.
- 02 Cost: A.
- 03 Handling: A. The trancing section, backed by actual physiology, is the standout.
- 04 Health issues: A. Clear vet triage list.
- 05 Housing: A. Filename says "tank setup" but it's a full housing guide; substance is strong regardless.
- 06 Feeding: A. The life-stage table is the most complete piece of writing in either set.
- 07 Enrichment: A. Three named studies, not one.
- 09 GI stasis: A. This is the page the whole hub emergency card visibly draws from.

**2. Hub and encyclopedia**
Both earn their place, no repetition and no disagreement. The 8 sq ft plus 24 sq ft space standard, the 8 to 12 hour GI stasis window, the $150 to $600 setup cost, and the indoor lifespan of 8 to 12 years all appear identically across the hub, housing, cost, and health/GI-stasis articles. The GI stasis timing in particular is handled with real care: PetMD's 8-hour threshold and the House Rabbit Society's 12-hour threshold are both cited by name and reconciled the same way ("eight is when you call, twelve is an emergency") in three separate articles (health issues, feeding, GI stasis) and again on the hub. That kind of exact, sourced consistency across four independent pages is unusual and reads as intentional editing, not luck.

**3. Could I actually keep this rabbit**
Yes. Space, flooring, litter training, diet by life stage, toxic foods, the companion recommendation, handling technique, and the GI stasis emergency are all covered with real numbers. What's missing: nowhere does this set say how much space scales for a large-breed rabbit; the encyclopedia notes adult size runs "2.5 to 20+ lbs depending on breed" but every space and diet number afterward is given as one flat figure, as if a 3 lb and 18 lb rabbit need the same pen and the same 1/8 to 1/4 cup of pellets. A reader with a giant breed is left to guess. Second gap: the RHDV2 vaccine is mentioned twice (hub, health issues) but never with a schedule, just "ask your vet," which is a reasonable deferral but does leave a first-timer without even an approximate timeline.

**4. Overlap and conflict**
Overlap is heavy and deliberate: the GI stasis 8-to-12-hour window, the alfalfa cutoff (babies, juveniles, nursing does only), and the "hay is 80 to 85% of the diet" figure are each repeated across three or more articles, always with the same numbers. No numeric conflicts turned up anywhere I could check. The closest thing to friction is style rather than fact: the feeding guide's own FAQ calls the 8-to-12-hour rule "the window the site now uses everywhere," which is an odd bit of self-aware phrasing to leave in a public-facing FAQ, but it isn't wrong.

**5. Trust**
Nothing damaged confidence; several things built it. The correction of the Pope Gregory rabbit-domestication myth, with the actual researcher's name and a 2018 journal citation, is the kind of detail nobody pads content with, since it's easier to just repeat the popular version. The line that most convinced me a real keeper wrote this: "Awake inactivity is one of the more uncomfortable welfare measures to learn about, because a still rabbit reads as a contented rabbit to most owners." That's an observation from someone who has actually second-guessed their own calm-looking rabbit.

**6. Grade: A.** Consistent numbers across every page, real citations, and an emergency card that matches its supporting articles exactly. Two changes: scale the space and diet numbers by adult weight or breed size instead of one flat figure, and give the RHDV2 vaccine an actual age or interval instead of "ask your vet."

**Not covered anywhere**
- How space, pellet amount, and hay quantity should scale for a large or giant breed rabbit: only flat numbers are given despite the encyclopedia noting a 2.5 to 20+ lb adult size range.
- An RHDV2 vaccination schedule (age to start, booster interval): mentioned twice, never with a timeline, and not detailed in the Health and More excerpts either.
