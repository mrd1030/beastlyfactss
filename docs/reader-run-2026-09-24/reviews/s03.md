## dog-beagle

1. Page verdicts
- Hub (00): finish it. Actionable: fence height, long line, portion control, ear checks, ADAMTSL2 clearance. Grade A.
- Encyclopedia (01): finish it. Actionable mainly as context (why the leash law exists, why it is the top lab breed), not a task list. Grade B+.
- Enrichment guide (02): finish it. Actionable: scent work as the main activity, priority order, what not to do. Grade A-.

2. Hub vs encyclopedia
They split cleanly. The hub carries costs, checklist, and FAQs; the encyclopedia carries breed history (the 1500s foot-hunting hounds, the 1885 AKC registration, the lab-dog stat) and does not restate the hub's numbers wholesale. Where they do overlap, the figures agree: both cite 24.27 percent obesity, 2.54 times the odds of ear infection versus a crossbred dog, and a median age at death of 11.28 years. No disagreement found.

3. Could you keep this dog after reading the set
Mostly yes on containment, weight, and ears. You would know to buy a five foot dug-in fence, measure every meal, and check ears weekly. What is missing: no source in this set gives a cups-or-grams number, and there is no dedicated feeding or grooming article to turn "measure every meal" into a real portion. The hub tells you to do daily toothbrushing and monthly body condition scoring but not how. The enrichment guide assumes you already have "our dog enrichment guide" and "why your dog needs daily exercise" for baseline exercise, and those exist only as one-line entries in the Health and More list, not as content you get here.

4. Overlap and conflict across the deep dives
Only one deep dive exists for this breed, so the overlap is with the hub rather than between deep dives. The obesity stats, the recall warning, and the nosework fun fact all appear on the hub and are restated almost identically in the enrichment article ("plan for a beagle that will not reliably recall off a live scent" versus the hub's "treat the honest answer as no"). No numbers contradict each other; it is repetition, not conflict.

5. Trust
The set cites named researchers and studies (Duranton and Horowitz, 2019; a 19,906-dog UK cohort) rather than vague "studies show." The line that most convinced me a real Beagle owner wrote it: "A beagle walked at heel past everything interesting is a beagle doing the control condition of that study." That is a specific, slightly wry observation nobody writes without having done it.

6. Grade and two changes
Grade: A-. Reason: accurate, internally consistent, well sourced, but stops short of giving a new owner an actual feeding plan. First change: add a feeding guide with real portion guidance by weight, the way other breeds in this site get one. Second change: turn the ear-cleaning and toothbrushing checklist items into a short how-to rather than a bullet.

7. Not covered anywhere
- Exact daily food amount by weight. None.
- Step-by-step ear cleaning or tooth brushing routine. None.
- Puppy socialization plan beyond recall training. None.

## dog-french-bulldog

1. Page verdicts
- Hub (00): finish it. Actionable: AC as life support, BOAS assessment, life jacket, the aerodigestive disease finding. Grade A.
- Encyclopedia (01): finish it. Actionable as context only (the Waldorf-Astoria bat-ear vote). Grade B+.
- Feeding guide (02): finish it. Actionable: age-based meal counts, slow-feeder rationale, the 12-hour puppy rule. Grade A-.

2. Hub vs encyclopedia, and a real disagreement
They split well: hub owns health and cost, encyclopedia owns origin story. No conflict there. But the hub and the feeding guide (a deep dive) disagree on a number. Hub: "In 176 French Bulldogs with airway obstruction, every single dog had at least one gastrointestinal endoscopic abnormality." Feeding guide: "One study found 93% of affected Frenchies also had GI signs, which improved after corrective airway surgery." Both cite what sounds like the same population, one says 100 percent, the other 93 percent. That is a genuine inconsistency a careful reader would catch.

3. Could you keep this dog after reading the set
Mostly yes on heat, BOAS awareness, and feeding schedule. What is missing: the hub's Enrichment section is one short paragraph, and the actual French Bulldog enrichment guide is only a title and one-liner in the Health and More list, not included here, so exercise planning is thin. Skin fold care gets a "watch for" mention on the hub but no cleaning steps. A reader would also want more on what a scheduled cesarean costs and involves, since the hub raises it as near-universal but never says what to budget beyond the general vet-care line items.

4. Overlap and conflict across the deep dives
Only one deep dive is in this set (feeding), so the meaningful overlap is with the hub, already covered above with the 93 versus 100 percent gap. No other numeric conflicts found between the hub and the feeding guide.

5. Trust
The feeding guide is honest about disagreement among its own sources: "Adults are where sources genuinely diverge. One veterinary source says twice daily, another recommends 2 to 3 times a day... A real, unresolved difference in professional guidance, not a typo." That kind of admission is rare and reads as someone who actually checked multiple sources rather than picking one and presenting it as consensus.

6. Grade and two changes
Grade: B+. Reason: strong, specific health data, but the 93/100 percent mismatch on the GI-airway link undercuts confidence in numbers elsewhere. First change: reconcile that stat to one source. Second change: bring the actual enrichment guide's content into this set, or expand the hub's Enrichment section, since exercise planning is currently the thinnest part of the set.

7. Not covered anywhere
- Step-by-step skin fold cleaning method. None.
- What a scheduled cesarean actually costs or involves. None.
- Concrete weekly exercise plan (the hub gestures at "short play sessions" only). None in this set.

## dog-siberian-husky

1. Page verdicts
- Hub (00): finish it. Actionable: fence spec, myth-busting on anesthesia and heat, DNA tests to ask for. Grade A.
- Encyclopedia (01): finish it. Actionable as history (Balto, Togo, Seppala) rather than a task list, and genuinely interesting. Grade A-.
- Enrichment guide (02): finish it. Actionable: endurance work priority order, digging pit, heat rules. Grade A-.

2. Hub vs encyclopedia, and a real disagreement
Hub and encyclopedia split cleanly with no repeated numbers in conflict. But the hub and the enrichment deep dive disagree in framing on heat. Hub: "while Huskies visibly struggle in heat, their odds of clinical heat-related illness were 1.18 against Labradors, not statistically significant." Enrichment guide: "Heat. The most serious practical risk in the whole guide." One page says the clinical data does not support elevated risk; the other calls it the single biggest danger in the plan. They may both be defensible (owner-observed discomfort versus measured illness odds), but the set never reconciles them for the reader.

3. Could you keep this dog after reading the set
Mostly yes on fencing, exercise volume, and eye screening priority. Missing: no feeding guide exists in this set, so there is no portion guidance beyond "feed a high-quality medium breed adult food" and "avoid overfeeding." Grooming for the heavy double coat, the seasonal "blowing coat," gets no mention anywhere in the set despite being one of the most common Husky owner questions.

4. Overlap and conflict across the deep dives
Only one deep dive is present. It repeats the hub's off-leash and fencing warnings nearly word for word ("plan as though not," "engineered against, not trained against") without adding new numbers, so it is duplication rather than conflict. The heat disagreement above is the one substantive tension in the set.

5. Trust
The hub actively debunks two popular claims instead of repeating them: "the widely repeated anesthesia sensitivity is unsupported, since the largest breed-specific analysis of 55,019 anesthetics found only the German Shepherd, Chihuahua and Bulldog above the mixed-breed rate and the Husky does not feature at all." Correcting folklore against the breed's own reputation is a strong trust signal.

6. Grade and two changes
Grade: A-. Reason: rigorous sourcing and unusually willing to correct myths, but the heat-risk framing contradicts itself between hub and deep dive, and grooming and feeding are absent. First change: align the heat-risk language between the hub and the enrichment guide. Second change: add a feeding and grooming section or guide with real portions and a coat-care routine.

7. Not covered anywhere
- Daily or weekly food amount by weight. None.
- Double-coat brushing and seasonal shedding routine. None.
- Puppy socialization plan. None.

## dog-bulldog

1. Page verdicts
- Hub (00): finish it. Actionable: AC as non-negotiable, harness over collar, breeder questions about graded exercise tests. Grade A.
- Encyclopedia (01): finish it, though it repeats the hub's core stat rather than adding much beyond history. Grade B+.
- Enrichment guide (02): finish it. Actionable: floor-level games, weight as the main lever, stop-on-breathing rule. Grade A-.

2. Hub vs encyclopedia
Less separation here than in the other sets. The encyclopedia restates the hub's headline health numbers almost verbatim: "a study of 2,662 English Bulldogs found 2.04 times the odds of being diagnosed with at least one disorder... with raised odds for 24 of 43 specific disorders" appears on both pages, and both give the 7.39 versus 11.23 year life expectancy gap. The figures agree throughout, so there is no conflict, but the encyclopedia earns its place mainly through the bull-baiting and Philo-Kuon history rather than new information.

3. Could you keep this dog after reading the set
Mostly yes on the essentials: AC, harness, weight control, fold care, breathing red flags. Missing: no exact portion amount is given anywhere (the hub says "measure every meal" without a number), and skin fold cleaning is mentioned twice as important but never explained step by step. Whelping and cesarean costs are raised as near-universal for the breed but not detailed, which matters even for a buyer who is not breeding, since it explains part of the purchase price.

4. Overlap and conflict across the deep dives
Only one deep dive is present in this set. It leans on an external French Bulldog enrichment guide for shared reasoning about scent work, which is not duplication within this set, just a pointer outward. No numeric conflicts found between the hub and the enrichment guide.

5. Trust
This line reads like it came from direct observation: "A Bulldog will happily follow you into an activity it physically cannot sustain, so the owner has to be the one who stops it." The fun fact that 93 percent of 102 breeding Bulldogs shared one paternal haplotype is also specific enough that it is unlikely to be invented.

6. Grade and two changes
Grade: A-. Reason: rigorous and consistent health data, but the encyclopedia largely repeats the hub instead of adding new ground, and feeding/grooming specifics are missing. First change: trim the encyclopedia's repeated statistics and spend that space on breed history instead. Second change: add a feeding guide with real portions and a fold-cleaning how-to.

7. Not covered anywhere
- Exact food amount by weight. None.
- Step-by-step wrinkle and fold cleaning method. None.
- What a planned cesarean costs or involves. None.

## dog-german-shepherd

1. Page verdicts
- Hub (00): finish it. Actionable: DM testing context, EPI red flags, gastropexy conversation, elbow-over-hip screening priority. Grade A.
- Encyclopedia (01): finish it. Actionable as history (von Stephanitz, Horand, the first guide dog Buddy), distinct from the hub. Grade A-.
- Feeding guide (02): finish it. Actionable: age-based meal schedule, EPI diet management, bloat-linked feeding habits. Grade A-.

2. Hub vs encyclopedia
Clean split, no overlap in numbers to conflict over. Both agree on the sex-based lifespan gap (11.1 years for females, 9.7 for males). One small mismatch shows up between the hub and the feeding guide instead: the hub gives "hips run 77.2 percent normal and 20.4 percent dysplastic... elbows run 81.2 percent normal and 18.7 percent abnormal," while the feeding guide says "one study found hip dysplasia rates around 19% and elbow dysplasia around 18%." Close enough to be different studies, but presented without any note that they are not the same figures.

3. Could you keep this dog after reading the set
Mostly yes. Training, socialization reasoning, diet timeline, and the major health watch list (DM, EPI, bloat, elbows) are all covered with real numbers, and the set is honest when data does not exist, stating plainly that "no authoritative body publishes a breed-specific minute count" for exercise rather than inventing one. What is missing: grooming and shedding management gets one checklist line, "regular brushing," with no routine attached.

4. Overlap and conflict across the deep dives
Only one deep dive is present, so this compares mainly to the hub. The chicken and wheat allergy myth is handled consistently across hub FAQ and feeding guide, both pointing to the same true ranking (beef 34 percent, dairy 17, chicken 15, wheat 13, lamb 5). No contradictions there, only the dysplasia percentage gap noted above.

5. Trust
The strongest trust signal is a negative finding stated plainly: "One thing not to list: thyroid disease is not a German Shepherd problem, with the breed ranking 67th at 92.0 percent normal." Actively telling a reader what is not a concern, against the grain of most breed content that piles on risks, reads as someone working from real data rather than a template.

6. Grade and two changes
Grade: A-. Reason: thorough, honest about uncertainty, and internally consistent apart from one small stat mismatch. First change: align the hip and elbow dysplasia percentages to a single cited source. Second change: add a grooming and shedding-season routine, since "regular brushing" is the least specific line in an otherwise detailed set.

7. Not covered anywhere
- Double-coat brushing and shedding-season routine. None.
- A concrete portion amount by weight (the set explains why no fixed number exists, so this is answered as unknowable rather than missing). None.
