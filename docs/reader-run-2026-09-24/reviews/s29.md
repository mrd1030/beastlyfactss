## corydoras-catfish

**1. Page by page**

- 00 Care guide hub: A. Finish it fast, act on it immediately (buy list, first-week numbers, emergency card).
- 01 Encyclopedia: A. Finish it, the taxonomy history (Corydoras to Osteogaster) is genuinely new information, not filler.
- 02 Cost guide: A. Clear numbers, a dated price table, and a real point (price the school, not the fish).
- 03 Handling guide (thin): C. It is titled a handling guide for a fish that is never handled, so it spends its length restating the schooling rule and the surface-gulping fact from the hub almost verbatim. The only new material is the oscar contrast.
- 04 Health issues guide: A-. Actionable steps and dosing guidance for each condition.
- 05 Tank setup guide: A. The one decision that matters (substrate) is argued and given a real product.
- 06 Feeding guide: A. Seven concrete reasons a cory stops eating, with what to test first.
- 07 Enrichment guide: A. The best page in the set: it cites three actual studies, corrects a myth with nuance instead of just repeating it, and ranks priorities.

**2. Hub and encyclopedia**

Both earn their place. The hub is a router with numbers and buy links, not prose the deep dives already carry, and the encyclopedia's taxonomy section (Gill's 1858 description, the 2024 genus split) appears nowhere else in the set. Neither disagrees with a deep dive: tank size, temperature, pH, lifespan, and cost all match the tank setup and cost articles number for number. The one place the hub understates a deep dive's nuance is barbel erosion: the hub FAQ says "no controlled study establishes it," which is accurate but softer than the enrichment guide's flat "probably wrong about the reason," backed by a cited paper on substrate bacteria.

**3. Could I keep this animal, and what's missing**

Yes, largely. Between these seven pages I can size a tank, pick a substrate, cycle water parameters, budget for six fish, feed them correctly, and recognize and treat the four named health problems. Two gaps stood out. First, the article titled "Aren't Handled" never mentions the one moment you would actually touch a cory: netting one. That detail, "pectoral spines lock and tangle in mesh," sits in the enrichment guide instead, which is the wrong place to put the single fact a new keeper needs before their first net-based water change. Second, the enrichment guide warns against "over-cleaning or disturbing the substrate too aggressively," and separately says keep it clean and vacuum the surface, but no page explains how to vacuum sand without stirring it into the water column the way you would gravel. That is a real technique gap, not answered anywhere in this set.

**4. Overlap and conflict**

Read twice or more: the "not an algae eater" myth (hub, handling, feeding, enrichment, four times), the surface air-gulping fact (hub, encyclopedia, handling, tank setup, enrichment), the six-fish minimum (every article), and the identical "Jackson's Chameleon, Canary, Giant Millipede & Corydoras Catfish compared" link and blurb, pasted near-verbatim into five of the six deep dives. No numbers actually conflict. The closest thing to a disagreement is tone on barbel erosion: the health guide calls sharp substrate "the usual explanation" and treats it as one of two real factors, while enrichment guide says "no controlled study establishes it, or any competing mechanism" and treats grain shape as secondary to bacterial load. Both land on the same substrate recommendation, so it reads as one page hedging and the other one going further, not a contradiction.

**5. Trust**

The enrichment guide is what earns trust here: it names two peer-reviewed sources with working links (Vanderzwalmen et al. 2022 on substrate bacteria, Riley et al. 2019 on foraging behavior, Nelson 2014 on intestinal air breathing) and uses them to walk back the site's own barbel-erosion framing rather than just repeating it. The sentence that most convinced me a real keeper wrote this: "A group of corydoras on sand buries its snouts and works the bottom continuously. The same group on gravel picks at the surface. That difference is the enrichment." That is an observed behavioral contrast, not a repackaged fact.

**6. Grade**

B+. The core husbandry pages (tank setup, feeding, health, enrichment) are genuinely strong and internally consistent, but the handling guide is thin filler for a species that isn't handled, and the netting detail landing in the wrong article is a real usability miss.

Two changes: fold the handling guide's actual content into the hub's behavior section and replace it with something that covers the one physical-contact moment (netting, spine snagging, and what a water change actually looks like for a bottom feeder). Second, cut the repeated four-way comparison blurb down to a single link line instead of restating it in nearly every article.

**7. Not covered anywhere**

- How to vacuum a sand substrate for a cory tank without stirring sand into the water column: None found, including in the Health and More list.

Everything else the set raised (netting technique, cycling, quarantine, water testing) is answered by an article named in the Health and More sidebar list (Fish Quarantine and Treatment, How to Cycle a Fish Tank), even though this species set doesn't answer it directly itself.
