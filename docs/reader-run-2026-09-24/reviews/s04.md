## millipede

**1. Page by page**
- Hub: finish it, act on it immediately (budget, tank size, humidity). Grade A.
- Encyclopedia: finish it. Adds the import-ban backstory and the 2014 SFO seizure, not just a rehash. Grade A-.
- Cost guide: finish it, act on it (real price table, dated). Grade A.
- Handling guide: finish it, the fall-risk framing is genuinely actionable. Grade A.
- Health issues guide: finish it, clear emergency triage. Grade A.
- Tank setup guide: finish it, though it contradicts itself internally (see below). Grade B.
- Enrichment guide: finish it, honest about having no species research, but a lot of it repeats the tank setup guide's substrate section. Grade B (filler in the "priority order" recap of points already made).

**2. Hub and encyclopedia**
Both earn their place. The hub is a numbers router, the encyclopedia is natural history and does not duplicate the care numbers. But the hub disagrees with the tank setup guide about isopods. The hub says: "Isopods and springtails are fine as a cleanup crew in a display tank; leave them out of a breeding tank." The tank setup guide's fun fact box says the opposite: "Isopods can harass or weaken a giant millipede when housed together, an unexpected conflict worth knowing about." Then that same article's body, under a heading that says "Skip Isopods as Cleanup Crew," reverses again: "isopods and springtails can go in with the millipede... Keep a cleanup crew in a display tank, and leave it out of a tank you are breeding in." So the hub actually agrees with the article's body, but the article's own heading and fun fact box argue the opposite of its own text.

**3. Could I keep one after reading all six?**
Yes. Buy, house, feed, and read the emergency signs, all covered and mutually consistent (substrate depth, humidity range, temperature, calcium). One gap: nothing on sexing or breeding, despite the hub casually mentioning "a breeding tank" as if the reader already knows how to set one up or tell males from females. A page raised a question another page answered without saying so: the health guide's "chalk isn't appropriate calcium" throwaway line assumes the reader already read the tank setup guide's calcium section, no link back.

**4. Overlap and conflict across deep dives**
Read twice: substrate depth and composition (tank setup and enrichment both give near-identical paragraphs on 4-6 inches, oak/beech leaf litter, no pine or cedar), the calcium-and-molt link (tank setup, health, and enrichment all state it), and the "never dig up a buried millipede" warning (hub, handling, and enrichment all repeat it near verbatim). None of these disagree on numbers. The one real conflict is the isopod contradiction above, both a numbers-adjacent instruction disagreeing with itself in the same article.

**5. Trust**
The isopod self-contradiction is the thing that made me doubt the writing process, it reads like two different drafts were pasted together and never reconciled. The sentence that most convinced me a real keeper wrote this: "A buried millipede is not stressed by you yet. Digging it up is how it becomes so." That is a specific, slightly wry observation nobody writes without having made the mistake once.

**6. Grade: B+.** Deep, consistent husbandry numbers and an honestly-labeled research gap, undercut by one genuine internal contradiction and some enrichment-guide padding. First fix: resolve the isopod question one way and delete the wrong version. Second fix: cut the enrichment guide's "priority order" list down since it is a recap of the tank setup guide, not new information, and add one line on sexing/breeding basics since the hub name-drops a breeding tank.

**7. Not covered anywhere**
- Sexing or telling males from females apart: no answer even in the Health and More summaries.
- How to actually set up or manage a breeding tank, referenced but never explained.
- Quarantine timing specific to millipedes: the linked "Invertebrate Housekeeping" summary covers quarantine in general, so this one is answered, just not in this set.

## sugar-glider

**1. Page by page**
- Hub: finish it, front-loads the legal check and colony requirement well. Grade A.
- Encyclopedia: finish it, the 2019 PeerJ genetic-origin study is a genuinely interesting addition. Grade A.
- Cost guide: finish it, dated prices, clear pair-cost math. Grade A.
- Handling guide: finish it, the pouch-bonding framing is the most useful single idea in the set. Grade A.
- Health issues guide: finish it, the "don't just add more calcium" warning is exactly the kind of correction a new owner needs. Grade A.
- Cage setup guide: finish it, height-over-width logic is clear and specific. Grade A.
- Feeding guide: finish it, the calcium ratio and the Merck citation earn trust. Grade A.
- Enrichment guide: finish it, and it is the standout page for candor (see below). Grade A.

**2. Hub and encyclopedia**
Both earn their place, neither pads. But there is a genuine lifespan conflict. The hub and cost guide both give "10 to 15 years in captivity," the cost guide adding "commonly cited around 12 to 15." The encyclopedia gives a different number: "up to 17.8 years recorded in captivity." Those are not the same claim, and nothing flags the gap. Separately, the hub states the colony rule as flat fact: "solitary housing is a major welfare risk by veterinary consensus." The enrichment guide is more careful, noting a study "reported the solitary animals in its sample as healthy, without the self-mutilation the hobby describes as inevitable." That is not a contradiction so much as the hub skipping a nuance the deep dive is honest about.

**3. Could I keep a pair after reading all eight?**
Yes, and this is the more complete of the two sets: legal check, cage, diet ratio, bonding method, and emergency signs all connect. What's missing: sexing and neutering logistics beyond "commonly recommended," and no page explains how introductions between two gliders actually go, despite the enrichment guide saying "introductions need care and time" without describing that care. That is a question raised and left unanswered inside the same set.

**4. Overlap and conflict across deep dives**
Read twice: the bar-spacing and cage-size numbers (hub and cage setup, identical, no conflict), the calcium-to-phosphorus 2:1 ratio (feeding and health issues, identical), and the self-mutilation topic, covered twice with different confidence. The health issues guide states plainly that it happens "particularly in intact solitary males." The enrichment guide's caveat undercuts that certainty without contradicting the underlying advice. The cost guide's body says Pennsylvania is "effectively illegal," but its own FAQ downgrades that to "requires a special permit," a real wording conflict about the same state.

**5. Trust**
The enrichment guide's willingness to say "the hobby describes as inevitable" and then present a study that didn't find it is the strongest signal a real keeper or someone checking sources wrote this, most pet content would have just repeated the scarier claim. The lifespan mismatch and the Pennsylvania wording slip are what made me doubt the editing pass, not the underlying knowledge.

**6. Grade: A-.** Thorough, well-cross-linked, and unusually honest about weak evidence, docked for the lifespan conflict and a legal-status wording slip. First fix: reconcile the 10-15 vs 17.8-year lifespan claim across the encyclopedia and cost guide. Second fix: pick one description of Pennsylvania's law and use it everywhere.

**7. Not covered anywhere**
- How to actually introduce two gliders to each other: mentioned as needing "care and time" with no method given, and the Health and More summaries (vet trips, grooming, "four pets" comparisons) don't cover it either.
- Sexing and the mechanics of neutering: cost is given, the procedure and how to identify sex are not, in this set or the linked summaries.
