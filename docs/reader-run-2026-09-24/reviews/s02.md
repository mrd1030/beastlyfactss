## cat-maine-coon

1. Page by page.
- 00 Care Guide Hub: A. Finish it. Actionable cost tables, a real HCM/hip screening schedule, a breeder-question checklist, and it corrects the "5 year growth" myth with a source. Nothing here felt padded.
- 01 Encyclopedia: B. Finish it for the history (the 1878 show, Cosey's 1895 win, the "extinct" claim CFA calls exaggerated). The health paragraph mostly restates the hub rather than adding anything new.

2. Hub vs encyclopedia. The encyclopedia earns its place on history alone, that content is nowhere else in the set. But its overview paragraph and the hub's overview both carry the same genetic claim almost word for word: hub says "the severity of hip dysplasia was genetically correlated with body mass," encyclopedia says "the severity of feline hip dysplasia in the breed is genetically correlated with body mass." Not a conflict, just the same sentence twice. No numbers disagree between the two pages; both give the same 3-year growth ceiling and both correct the 3-5 year myth the same way.

3. Could I actually do this. Mostly yes: cost, food type, growth timeline, and a two-tier screening plan (HCM echo at 1/3/5, hip x-rays if breeding) are all here and consistent. What's missing is anything to act on for two things the hub itself raises and drops. It flags Maine Coons as one of the breeds most prone to wool sucking as "a stress and enrichment signal worth watching for," then never says what to do about it, and there is no deep dive in this set to answer it. It also says feline MDR1 sensitivity "changes safe dosing of several common drugs" without naming a single one, which leaves a real gap for anyone giving their cat medication.

4. Overlap and conflict across deep dives. There are no Maine Coon-specific deep dives in this set to compare against each other, so nothing to report beyond the hub/encyclopedia repetition above.

5. Trust. High. The page argues against its own DNA test upsell: "Ten of the 21 cats that actually had HCM carried no mutation whatsoever." A page trying to sell you a test does not usually volunteer that the test misses half its true positives. That is the sentence that convinced me a person who actually reads the primary studies wrote this, not someone paraphrasing a breeder pamphlet.

6. Grade: A-. Numbers-heavy, self-correcting on popular myths, and honest about the limits of genetic testing, but the set stops short of following through on two things it raises itself. Two changes: name the MDR1-affected drug classes instead of a vague warning, and add a real Maine Coon behavior/enrichment page instead of leaving wool sucking as an unresolved flag.

7. Not covered anywhere.
- What to actually do about wool sucking once you notice it.
- Which specific drugs or dosing changes the MDR1 sensitivity actually requires.

## cat-persian

1. Page by page.
- 00 Care Guide Hub: A. Finish it. The PKD success-story framing is genuinely interesting, the checklist is specific (PKD1, PRA-pd/AIPL1, BOAS), and it corrects its own doll-face claim with real CT numbers instead of taking the marketing at face value.
- 01 Encyclopedia: B. Finish it for the show-ring history (Crystal Palace 1871, the accidental 1950s peke-face mutation), though the health line at the end just previews the hub.

A Deep Dive titled "Persian Cat Enrichment" is listed in this page's sidebar navigation but its file was not part of this set, so it could not be read or reviewed here.

2. Hub vs encyclopedia. They divide labor cleanly: the encyclopedia does breed history and origin, the hub does cost and health mechanics, and the overlap is limited to a shared one-line summary of what brachycephaly causes. No numbers conflict between them; the adult size and lifespan figures in the encyclopedia are not repeated or contradicted in the hub.

3. Could I actually do this. Largely yes on diet, grooming, and the two big genetic risks. What is thinner is enrichment and daily behavior: the hub's enrichment section is four generic sentences ("low-energy," "gentle daily play"), and the one page built to go deeper on that, the Persian enrichment deep dive, is not available in this set, so a question the hub itself raises, whether "a quiet cat is automatically a content one," is never actually answered here. The set also never explains what a positive PRA-pd or ringworm-haplotype result on a kitten would mean for daily life, only that the test exists.

4. Overlap and conflict across deep dives. Not applicable, no deep dive was available to compare against the hub or encyclopedia.

5. Trust. High. The doll-face FAQ does not just answer the marketing question, it separates it by organ system: eyes and teeth improve with a flatter-to-less-flat gradient, but "researchers could not establish a correlation between skull indices and airway area," so a doll-face Persian's breathing is not assumed safe. That refusal to let one nice result cover for the rest is the most convincing sentence in the set.

6. Grade: A-. Corrects its own myths with primary data (PKD collapse, doll-face limits) rather than repeating breed folklore, but the enrichment side is thin and its own referenced deep dive was not present to check. Two changes: write real Persian-specific enrichment content instead of four generic sentences, and give the PRA-pd and ringworm-haplotype results a "what this means day to day" line.

7. Not covered anywhere.
- What actually keeping a "quiet" Persian mentally satisfied looks like day to day, beyond four generic sentences.
- What a positive PRA-pd or ringworm-susceptibility genetic result changes for how you raise the cat.

## dog-rottweiler

1. Page by page.
- 00 Care Guide Hub: A. Finish it. The neuter-timing FAQ alone is worth the page, it gives a specific hazard ratio and tells you to talk to your vet instead of defaulting to generic advice, and the bloat section corrects the common assumption that Rottweilers are top-tier bloat risks.
- 01 Encyclopedia: B+. Finish it. The near-extinction and recovery story (one female left in Rottweil by 1905) is compelling, and it front-loads the same cancer and joint numbers as the hub, which is repetition but accurate repetition.

2. Hub vs encyclopedia. The encyclopedia's overview paragraph already contains the hip/elbow dysplasia percentages and the osteosarcoma odds ratio that the hub's health section repeats in full. That is real duplication, not just a shared theme, both give "20.2 percent hip dysplastic and 36.7 percent elbow dysplastic" and the "26.67 times the odds of osteosarcoma" figure. The numbers match exactly between the two pages, no conflict, but a reader who reads both gets the same two facts twice.

3. Could I actually do this. Mostly yes on setup and cost, and the neuter-timing and gastropexy guidance is unusually direct for a breed page. What is missing is a training curriculum: the hub says training is "the safety layer" and must start early, but never says what that training actually consists of beyond "socialization classes," and it does not resolve how a 100 lb guarding dog with a 9.36 percent male aggression rate should be introduced to visitors day to day, only that a "management routine" should exist.

4. Overlap and conflict across deep dives. No breed-specific deep dive exists in this set, so there is nothing to compare beyond the hub/encyclopedia repetition above.

5. Trust. High. The bloat FAQ actively argues against the breed's own scary reputation: "it is worth being accurate: the Rottweiler is not on the top-tier predisposed lists, which name Great Danes, Saint Bernards, Weimaraners..." A page trying to sell fear would leave that line out. That correction, plus the specific 4.1-fold cruciate-rupture hazard ratio tied to early neutering, is what convinced me someone who has actually followed this breed's research wrote it.

6. Grade: A-. Specific, numerate, and willing to correct its own breed's folk reputation (bloat, dog-likeness elsewhere) rather than pad it. Two changes: spell out what the "safety layer" training actually involves session by session, and add a page on visitor/door management routines for guarding breeds.

7. Not covered anywhere.
- A concrete visitor-at-the-door management routine beyond "have one."
- What the recommended socialization and obedience curriculum actually consists of week to week.

## cat-sphynx

1. Page by page.
- 00 Care Guide Hub: A. Finish it. The ALMS1 FAQ is the standout: it tells owners a clear DNA result on the parents does not mean a safe heart, backed by a real cohort number, and the legal-status FAQ on the Netherlands ban is unusually precise about what is and is not prohibited.
- 01 Encyclopedia: B+. Finish it for the 1966 Toronto origin and the forgotten 1890s hairless line that died out with a single cat sent to the Smithsonian. It restates the HCM and legal material from the hub near-verbatim in its overview.

A Deep Dive titled "Sphynx Cat Enrichment: Warmth Is a Welfare Requirement" is listed in this page's navigation but was not part of this set and could not be reviewed.

2. Hub vs encyclopedia. The encyclopedia's overview repeats the hub's core numbers exactly: the 6.8-year UK life expectancy, the Netherlands 2026 keeping ban, and the ALMS1/HCM prevalence framing. No disagreement, but this is close to a paragraph lifted from one page's logic into the other rather than two pages doing different work.

3. Could I actually do this. On setup, warmth, sun, bathing, and ear care, yes, the set is specific and actionable. The heart disease section is honest that echocardiograms are required for life rather than a one-time test, which is useful but also means the hub never actually resolves what an owner does if a cardiologist finds early HCM, since diet and treatment specifics are not covered. The enrichment section states plainly the breed "does not thrive when left alone," but the deep dive built to expand on that welfare point was not available in this set to check whether it delivers.

4. Overlap and conflict across deep dives. Not applicable, no deep dive was available to compare.

5. Trust. Very high. The legal FAQ goes out of its way to correct sloppy reporting on its own topic: "The exemption is not for cats born before the cutoff, which is how it is usually paraphrased, but for cats already being kept before it." Correcting a common misreading of a law, rather than just repeating the misreading, is the clearest sign someone who actually read the source wrote this.

6. Grade: A. Precise on warmth, sun, and heart screening, and unusually careful with legal detail most pet sites get wrong. Two changes: add what actually happens medically and financially once HCM is diagnosed, not just how it is caught, and confirm the enrichment deep dive covers loneliness management in practice rather than restating "don't leave them alone."

7. Not covered anywhere.
- What treatment or lifestyle changes follow an actual HCM diagnosis, once found.
- Concrete day-to-day solutions for a household that cannot be home most of the day, beyond "get a companion animal."

## cat-scottish-fold

1. Page by page.
- 00 Care Guide Hub: A. Finish it. It does not flinch from the breed's core problem, one dominant mutation causes both the fold and the joint disease, and the checklist and housing section (ramps, low-entry litter box, non-slip flooring) are genuinely actionable.
- 01 Encyclopedia: B+. Finish it for the registry politics (FIFe deleting the breed's codes in 2012, GCCF refusing it since 1971) but its health paragraph is a condensed repeat of the hub's.

2. Hub vs encyclopedia. Both pages state that any cat with at least one copy of the TRPV4 variant can be affected, and both quote the 1.1 percent clinical-diagnosis figure from the same Australian study of 1,131 cats alongside the caveat that diagnosis requires radiographs most pet cats never get. No numbers disagree. The registry material is split sensibly: the hub's FAQ covers the practical "which registries refuse it and why" question in more depth than the encyclopedia, so that part earns its place rather than repeating.

3. Could I actually do this. Yes on housing, weight, and pain monitoring, this is one of the more concrete sets: specific behavioral pain signs, a screening list, and a defensible skepticism toward the "fold to straight breeding avoids it" claim. The gap is between the CFA's own breed standard and biology: the hub notes the CFA disqualifies a stiff tail while also recognizing a breed the same mutation guarantees will have some, but never spells out what that means for buying from a CFA-registered breeder specifically, since CFA recognition is treated elsewhere in the page as a point in the breed's favor.

4. Overlap and conflict across deep dives. No deep dive exists in this set, so nothing to compare beyond the hub/encyclopedia repetition above.

5. Trust. Very high. The registry FAQ lays out a genuine contradiction rather than picking a side: "Two registries are reading the same mutation and reaching opposite conclusions: one treats the folded ear as the disease marker, the other treats a stiff tail as the disqualifying sign." Naming that contradiction outright, instead of quietly reporting whichever registry looks better for the breed, is what convinced me this was written by someone who has actually followed the welfare debate.

6. Grade: A-. Direct about the core genetics, specific on home management, and willing to quote a veterinary body condemning the breed's own existence. Two changes: reconcile the CFA-recognition-as-reassurance framing with the same page's own point that the CFA standard disqualifies a trait the mutation guarantees, and add what buying from a CFA breeder actually protects against versus what it does not.

7. Not covered anywhere.
- What CFA registration or a "well-bred" CFA Fold actually screens out, given the mutation is unavoidable in the breed as sold.

## dog-border-collie

1. Page by page.
- 00 Care Guide Hub: A. Finish it. It corrects two common breed myths with real numbers, that MDR1 sensitivity is a Rough Collie problem not a Border Collie one, and that merle deafness risk is a double-merle-breeding problem, not a single-merle one.
- 01 Encyclopedia: B+. Finish it for the Old Hemp/ISDS history, though its size and lifespan figures are generic and not sourced the way the hub's health numbers are.
- 02 Enrichment Guide: A. Finish it. This is the set's best page: it reframes the standard "exercise them more" advice as the thing making the problem worse, with a real study behind it, and ends with a usable priority list.

2. Hub vs encyclopedia. Little direct repetition; the encyclopedia does history and general size/lifespan, the hub does cost, diet, and the detailed health corrections. No numbers conflict, and the encyclopedia's Chaser vocabulary anecdote does not reappear in the hub, so both add something distinct.

3. Could I actually do this. Yes, and unusually well for exercise-need dogs: the hub gives concrete daily commitments (two-plus hours, off-leash running, MDR1 and CEA tests) and the enrichment guide operationalizes it into a priority-ordered plan. One real gap: the enrichment guide repeatedly points to "our guide on why your dog needs daily exercise" and "our guide to hip and elbow dysplasia" for baseline numbers it does not itself provide, and neither of those pages is in this set, so a reader following the site's own pointers hits a dead end here even though the site clearly intends them to be reachable.

4. Overlap and conflict across deep dives. Only one breed deep dive exists, so no cross-article overlap to check. It does not conflict with the hub; both describe Border Collie collapse the same way (5 to 15 minutes of exertion, full recovery in about 30 minutes) and both correctly downgrade MDR1 as a defining risk.

5. Trust. Very high across all three pages. The enrichment guide's key takeaway is the most convincing line in the set: "Fitness adapts; the need for a task does not adapt with it, so the miles keep going up and the dog still cannot settle." That is a specific, falsifiable claim about training physiology, not a generic "give them lots of exercise" platitude, and it is the kind of thing only someone who has watched this exact failure mode in real dogs would bother to write.

6. Grade: A. Corrects two widespread myths with real numbers and pairs a solid hub with a genuinely useful behavior-specific deep dive. Two changes: link the enrichment guide's referenced exercise and dysplasia pages into this set (or stop citing them as if they are one click away), and give Border Collie collapse a clearer "what to do in the moment" checklist rather than folding it into a paragraph.

7. Not covered anywhere.
- The full baseline daily exercise numbers the enrichment guide assumes the reader already has from the (absent) daily-exercise guide.
- A step-by-step response checklist for a collapse episode beyond "stop, cool, see a vet."

## dog-golden-retriever

1. Page by page.
- 00 Care Guide Hub: A. Finish it. The cancer-longevity framing (dogs that die of cancer lived longer on average than dogs that didn't) is a genuinely counterintuitive, sourced fact rather than filler, and the pigmentary uveitis section fills a gap most Golden pages skip.
- 01 Encyclopedia: B. Finish it for the real origin story (the Nous/Guisachan records debunking the Russian-circus-dog myth), but its health line is a compressed repeat of the hub.
- 02 Feeding Guide: B+ (some filler). The feeding schedule and toxic-food list are solid and specific, but "Why Golden Retrievers Stop Eating" and "When to Worry" read as generic dog-appetite content that would work unchanged on any breed's page.

2. Hub vs encyclopedia. The encyclopedia's lifespan line already gives the 9.83-vs-6.93-year cancer figure that the hub's fun fact restates as the headline hook. Same number, same source (UC Davis, 655 dogs), no disagreement, just used twice for the same rhetorical effect.

3. Could I actually do this. Largely yes: cost, coat care, cancer expectations, and the specific age-5 eye exam recommendation are all concrete. The feeding guide raises bloat as an "emergency signs" list but the hub's FAQ on bloat downgrades Goldens as lower risk than giant breeds, and the two pages never connect that the feeding guide's own advice (slow feeder, avoid one large meal) is the direct mitigation for the risk the hub says is comparatively lower, so a reader has to piece that together themselves. The feeding guide also references three pages, the exercise guide, the French Bulldog feeding guide, and the vs. German Shepherd guide, none of which are in this set.

4. Overlap and conflict across deep dives. Only one deep dive exists here, so no cross-article conflict to check, but it does duplicate the hub's ichthyosis FAQ almost point for point (74.8 percent clear, 15 dogs with two copies, at most 3 showing signs) without adding anything the hub did not already say, unlike the feeding guide's own material which is mostly new.

5. Trust. High. The ichthyosis FAQ resists the temptation to alarm a reader over a scary-looking genetic result: "of the 15 dogs with two copies, at most 3 showed any clinical sign, so fewer than one in five affected genotypes produced visible disease." Actively talking a worried owner down from a DNA panel result, backed by the actual study numbers, is the clearest sign of someone who understands genetics and cares more about accuracy than clicks.

6. Grade: A-. Numerate and myth-correcting on cancer and ichthyosis, but the feeding guide has real generic filler in its second half and the set leans on several outside pages it does not include. Two changes: replace the generic "why dogs stop eating" section with Golden-specific detail (it currently would fit any breed), and connect the bloat-risk-level FAQ to the feeding guide's mitigation advice explicitly instead of leaving them in separate pages.

7. Not covered anywhere.
- None. Every substantive gap (exercise baseline, dysplasia detail, breed comparison) points to a page the set itself names, even though that page was not included here.
