# rat

**1. Page by page**
- Hub: would finish, actionable, everything cross-linked to a source page. A
- Encyclopedia: would finish, good history (Jack Black, the Wistar split), nothing to act on but not trying to be. A-
- Cost guide: would finish, real numbers, a table, and an honest note that housing dwarfs animal cost. A
- Handling guide: would finish, explains why bites happen instead of just warning about them. A
- Health guide: would finish, one clear priority (respiratory disease) plus a tidy secondary list. A
- Tank setup guide: would finish, the cage-vs-tank mechanism is explained, not just asserted. A
- Feeding guide: would finish, explains why hamster food fails a rat rather than just banning it. A
- Enrichment guide: would finish, names the actual study and gives a priority order instead of a shopping list. A

**2. Hub and encyclopedia**
Both earn their place. The hub is a router, not a rewrite: every number carries the source article's link, and the FAQ answers match the linked page word for word (cage size, social housing, respiratory disease). The encyclopedia stays in its lane, natural history and the pet/lab split, and does not duplicate the care numbers at all. No disagreement found: cage size, lifespan (2-3 years, some to 4), and the 95% Mycoplasma figure are identical everywhere they appear.

**3. The set as a whole**
Yes, you could buy, house, feed, and handle a pair of rats off this set alone. The unanswered thread: the feeding guide and enrichment guide both mention "scatter feeding" as an enrichment tactic, but neither says how it interacts with the twice-daily portion rule, does scattering count as one of the two meals, or extra. The cost guide flags mammary tumor removal as "one of the more common surgical costs" without a number, and no page anywhere gives a spay/neuter dollar figure despite recommending spaying before 7 months.

**4. Overlap and conflict**
The 95% Mycoplasma pulmonis statistic appears in the hub, the cost guide, the health guide, and the tank setup guide, four times, always with the same number and source (Merck). No numeric conflict anywhere; this set is unusually disciplined about repeating figures identically rather than drifting. The "mouse encyclopedia profile" link appears in five of the six deep dives as a closing aside, which reads as templated by the fourth repetition.

**5. Trust**
Nothing raised doubt. If anything, the specificity built trust: naming Willard Small's 1900 maze, the 1907 Kerplunk experiments, and the 1976 National Fancy Rat Society founding date reads like someone who actually dug into rat-fancy history rather than paraphrasing a pet-store blurb. The sentence that most convinced me a keeper wrote this: "Rats also, genuinely, sleep in hammocks, and RSPCA guidance recommends at least one hiding spot per rat." The stray "genuinely" is the tell, someone anticipating a reader's skepticism because they've watched it happen.

**6. Grade: A-**
Consistent numbers across every page, real mechanisms explained instead of asserted, and a set that could actually replace a first-timer's Google search. The two changes: give the spay/neuter and tumor-removal costs a real number instead of "some of the more common," and clarify whether scatter feeding is inside or outside the twice-daily portion.

**7. Not covered anywhere**
- Spay/neuter surgery cost, or mammary-tumor-removal cost as a number. None given in the cost guide or health guide.
- Whether scatter feeding counts toward or is separate from the twice-daily portion rule.
- How to sex a rat or introduce a new rat to an existing group. Not mentioned in this set and no Health and More entry covers it (the shared guides are heat stress, vet transport, grooming, and a rodent roundup, none address introductions).

# savannah-monitor

**1. Page by page**
- Hub: would finish, dense but organized, and the emergency card is genuinely useful. A
- Encyclopedia: would finish, the CITES export numbers (Ghana 235,903, Togo 188,110) are a level of sourcing most encyclopedia pages skip. A
- Cost guide: would finish, honest that the enclosure is a DIY build, not a purchase. A
- Handling guide: would finish, names five specific warning behaviors and what each one means. A
- Health guide: would finish, but obesity is the fourth page in this set to lead with the same fact (filler risk starting to show, though each framing differs slightly)
- Tank setup guide: would finish, the most detailed page in the set. A
- Feeding guide: would finish, the "no published portion figure, use body condition" answer is more honest than a fabricated number would be. A
- Enrichment guide: would finish, unusually upfront that its key study is genus-level, not species-specific. A

**2. Hub and encyclopedia**
Both earn their place, and neither repeats the deep dives verbatim, they compress. Checked every shared number for disagreement: basking temp (140-150F), humidity (50%), substrate depth (12-24in), UVB spec (10-12% T5 HO, 14-16in), budget ($150-300 juvenile, $300-800 adult), and lifespan (10-15, 15-20 achievable) all match exactly between the hub, the encyclopedia, and the linked deep dives. No conflict found anywhere in this set, which is notable given how many numbers it carries.

**3. The set as a whole**
Mostly yes, with one real gap: nighttime heat. The tank guide bans ceramic heat emitters, colored bulbs, and heat mats as a primary basking source, and gives a night target of 70-75F, but never says what actually holds that temperature once the halogen floods are off for the night cycle. That question is raised by the guide's own restrictions and never answered by any page in this set. Separately, the hub leans heavily on "Are Savannah Monitors Legal?" for the legal-status numbers (twelve jurisdictions, Louisiana's ban, the Arkansas list), but that article is not among the files provided in this set, so its claims can't be checked here.

**4. Overlap and conflict**
Obesity and fatty liver disease is the throughline of six of six deep dives: the cost guide, handling guide, health guide, tank guide, feeding guide, and enrichment guide all state it as the species' defining problem. That's appropriate repetition for a genuinely central fact, not padding, since each page ties it to its own topic (diet frequency, enclosure size, enrichment calories). No numeric conflicts turned up between any two pages.

**5. Trust**
The named, specific sourcing throughout, LafeberVet's behavior list, Tree of Life Exotics' stress-signal list, a named 2011 Clinical Microbiology Reviews case report, a Thailand zoo enrichment study, made this the more convincing of the two sets. The sentence that most convinced me a real keeper wrote it: "A savannah monitor on gut-loaded insects three times a week, in an enclosure big enough to walk, lives fifteen to twenty years. The same lizard on a mouse a week in a tank lives half that. The difference is entirely the plate." That's a confident synthesis, not a hedge, and it matches everything else on the page.

**6. Grade: A-**
Thorough, internally consistent, unusually well-cited for an affiliate-supported care set, and honest about where its evidence is only genus-level. Two changes: answer the nighttime heat question the tank guide's own bans create, and either include the legal guide in review or stop leaning on it so heavily from the hub.

**7. Not covered anywhere**
- What actually holds 70-75F overnight once ceramic emitters, colored bulbs, and heat mats are all ruled out as primary heat. No page in this set or the Health and More list (which covers heating and thermostats generally, but not this species' specific night-heat method) answers it.
- The full legal-status list beyond Louisiana and the Arkansas mention; the article that would cover it isn't part of this set.
- A concrete build plan or cost breakdown for the 8x4x4 DIY enclosure itself, beyond "typically a DIY build or ordered direct from a manufacturer."
