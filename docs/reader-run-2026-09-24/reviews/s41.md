## box-turtle

**1. Page by page**
- Hub: would finish, it front-loads every number I need with a source link attached. Grade A.
- Encyclopedia: would finish, the CITES and collection-decline history earns its keep. Grade A-.
- Cost guide: would finish, real dollar ranges with dated sourcing. Grade A.
- Handling guide: would finish, sexing and the shy-vs-sick distinction are both useful. Grade A-.
- Health issues guide: would finish, ties MBD, vitamin A, and respiratory infection into one causal chain. Grade A.
- Tank setup guide: would finish, gives size, temperature, humidity, substrate, and UVB as separate hard numbers. Grade A.
- Feeding guide: would finish, the age-based diet shift is the most useful single idea in the set. Grade A.
- Enrichment guide: would finish, one actual study with a blood measure instead of vibes. Grade A.

No page here felt thin or padded.

**2. Hub and encyclopedia**
Both earn their place. The hub is a router with numbers and links, the encyclopedia is wild history and taxonomy, and neither repeats the deep dives at length. I found no disagreement between the hub and any deep dive: the enclosure sizes, temperature band, humidity, and UVB numbers on the hub all match the tank setup guide word for word, and the cost and lifespan lines match the cost guide.

**3. Could I keep this animal**
Mostly yes. I could size and equip an enclosure, get UVB right, feed a diet that shifts as the turtle ages, handle it correctly, and recognize the health emergencies. Two things raised a question this set does not answer inside itself: the hub names a six month quarantine and a specific brumation protocol, but both point to guides shared across the whole reptile section (herpesvirus quarantine, tortoise brumation) rather than to anything written for a box turtle specifically. The hub says so plainly with a link, so it is not a silent gap, but it means brumation, arguably the single riskiest annual event for this species, has no box turtle specific page in this set at all.

The feeding guide also raises a question the tank setup guide answers without cross-linking it: feeding says an outdoor turtle in real sunlight can run a lighter calcium schedule because it makes its own D3, and that only makes sense once you already know from the setup guide that a box turtle can legitimately live outdoors. The two pages never point at each other on this specific point.

**4. Overlap and conflicts across deep dives**
The enclosure size, temperature, humidity, substrate, and UVB numbers appear twice each, once summarized on the tank setup guide and once repeated nearly verbatim in the hub's FAQ and first week list. That is expected duplication, not filler, since the hub is built to stand alone.

One real tension: the hub and tank setup guide say "adults daily or every other day," while the feeding guide, on its own page, treats daily as only marginally acceptable: "Daily works only on the looser of the two, and for a species where obesity and overfeeding are ordinary problems, the slower end is the safer default." That is not a hard contradiction, but a new owner reading only the hub would feed daily as a default, while the guide written specifically about feeding is nudging away from it.

A second soft tension: the tank setup guide sets substrate at "2 to 3 inches deep... for burrowing," while the enrichment guide, drawing on an actual study, argues that depth needs to be enough that the turtle can fully disappear into it, "more mulch than one small bag holds." Both can be true at once (2 to 3 inches is a floor, not the enrichment ideal) but the setup guide never says that a bioactive minimum and an enrichment target are different numbers.

**5. Trust**
Sourcing is a strength here: VCA, a named veterinary hospital, Zoo Med, and the MSD Veterinary Manual are all cited, and the cost guide dates its price check to a specific month with named retailers. The sentence that most convinced me a keeper, not a content mill, wrote this: "Kale and mustard greens turn up on the recommended list and in the goitrogen warning both. That is a quantity instruction rather than a contradiction: rotate them through, do not build the salad on them." That is the kind of nuance that only shows up when someone has actually had to answer the question themselves.

**6. Grade**
A-. This is a well-sourced, internally consistent set that only loses ground for treating a major seasonal event (brumation) and a subtle feeding tension as someone else's problem. Two changes: give this species its own short brumation section instead of relying entirely on the generic tortoise page, and soften the hub's "daily or every other day" line to match the feeding guide's actual recommendation.

**Not covered anywhere**
- A box turtle specific brumation protocol (only a generic tortoise brumation guide is linked, and it is not species-specific to Terrapene by name in what this set shows).
- None beyond that: the quarantine, hygiene, and sizing gaps I initially expected are all covered by name in the Health and More list.
