## 1. Identity

1.1 Scientific name and authority
- ITIS: "Hemigrammus erythrozonus", author "Durbin, 1909", status "valid", common name "glowlight tetra", credibility "verified - standards met". (S3) https://www.itis.gov/servlet/SingleRpt/SingleRpt?search_topic=TSN&search_value=162978
- FishBase: authority "Durbin, 1909". (S2) https://www.fishbase.se/summary/SpeciesSummary.php?id=10642
- GBIF: "Hemigrammus erythrozonus Durbin, 1909", status accepted. (S6) https://api.gbif.org/v1/species/2354398
- ETYFish: "Hemigrammus erythrozonus (Durbin 1909)". (S7) https://etyfish.org/acestrorhamphidae/
- Seriously Fish does not record an authority ("Authority not recorded", fetch-tool report). (S1)

1.2 Original description (note: sometimes misattributed to Eigenmann)
- GBIF "publishedIn": "Durbin, M. L. (1909). Reports on the expedition to British Guiana of the Indiana University and the Carnegie Museum, 1908. Report No. 2. A new genus and twelve new species of tetragonopterid characins. Annals of the Carnegie Museum. v. 6 (no. 1): 55-72." (S6)
- The author is Marion Durbin (later Ellis). ETYFish: Durbin is the maiden name of Ellis, "who described many small tetras" (fetch-tool paraphrase). (S7)
- Eigenmann (1917) is a later redescription that FWS quotes (see 11C). It is not the original description. (S4)
- Type locality: not given in any page opened. A search summary named "Erukin, Guyana" but the page was not opened, so it is NOT recorded here as sourced. FWS gives only the Eschmeyer range line "Essequibo River, Guyana." (S4)

1.3 Name meaning
- ETYFish, species: "red-banded, from érythrós (ἐρυθρός), red, and zonus, Neo-Latin adjective of zona (L.), band", referring to the "cherry-colored streak" along the sides (fetch-tool excerpt). (S7)
- ETYFish, genus Hemigrammus (Gill 1858): the name refers to the "abruptly discontinued" lateral line of H. unilineatus, ending at mid-body (fetch-tool paraphrase). (S7)
- FishBase: "Hemigrammus: Greek, hemi = half + Greek, gramma = letter, signal". No eponymy on the page. (S2)
- Practical Fishkeeping: the species name "is said to mean 'with a red zone or stripe'" (fetch-tool paraphrase). (S8)

1.4 Family (sources disagree: this is a recent reclassification, not an error; same split the ember tetra showed)
- FishBase: "Acestrorhamphidae", common name "American tetras", subfamily Pristellinae (fetch-tool report). (S2)
- ETYFish lists the species under its Acestrorhamphidae page, subfamily Pristellinae (Géry & Boutière 1964). (S7)
- Wikipedia: "the family Acestrorhamphidae, the American characins" (uncited in the part read). (S12)
- de Melo et al. (2024): "a new classification of former Characidae into four families" (Spintherobolidae, Stevardiidae, Characidae, Acestrorhamphidae); "The family Acestrorhamphidae congregates 15 subfamilies", and Pristellinae is one of them (abstract read via aggregator; the page does not itself list Hemigrammus). (S5)
- ITIS: Characidae. (S3). GBIF: Characidae. (S6). Seriously Fish: breadcrumb "Characidae". (S1). FWS (quoting ITIS 2018): "Family Characidae". (S4)
- Reading: slower-updating databases (ITIS, GBIF) and Seriously Fish still say Characidae; FishBase, ETYFish and the 2024 phylogenomic classification place it in Acestrorhamphidae. Owner to decide which family the site writes; the neon, cardinal and ember entries should be checked for consistency.

1.5 Genus status
- Seriously Fish: "Hemigrammus has long been used as something of a catch-all for small characins" (the page adds that a revision is needed; fetch-tool paraphrase). (S1)
- A search summary says Mirande (2019) found Hemigrammus polyphyletic, but that paper was not opened. Not recorded as sourced. If the owner wants this, open Mirande 2019 before citing.

1.6 Disputed synonym (do NOT repeat without checking)
- Wikipedia: "Hemigrammus gracilis is a senior synonym." (S12). This conflicts with ITIS, GBIF and FishBase, which all treat H. erythrozonus as the valid name and list no such synonym (S2, S3, S6; the GBIF record shows a status of accepted, and FishBase's synonym list was not displayed in the fetch). Catalog of Fishes was not reached. Treat as unverified.
- Practical Fishkeeping gives the true early history: first exported from Guyana in the 1930s; "an early tentative ID as Hyphessobrycon gracilis" was rejected (fetch-tool paraphrase), and the species has "played the 'change the name game' to the full" (quote). (S8)

1.7 Trade and common names
- "Glowlight tetra" (ITIS, FishBase, Seriously Fish). Retailers also write "Glow Light" and "glolite". No other widely used trade name found in the pages opened.

## 2. Origin and wild habitat

- Distribution: "Known only from the Rio Essequibo, Guyana." (S1) https://www.seriouslyfish.com/species/hemigrammus-erythrozonus/
- FishBase: "South America: Essequibo River." (S2). FWS quoting Eschmeyer et al. (2018): "Essequibo River, Guyana." and noting "Only occurrences within Guyana represent known established populations". (S4)
- DISAGREEMENT on range: Wikipedia adds "the Essequibo River and the Courantyne River in Guyana and Suriname" with no citation. (S12) The two .gov/database sources and Seriously Fish say Essequibo, Guyana only. Use Guyana.
- Habitat: "is found primarily in forested areas, in tributaries off the main river channel." (S1)
- Water: "The water here is typically stained a dark brown with tannins from decaying organic material" and, per the fetch-tool paraphrase, is often highly acidic. (S1)
- Wild chemistry: no measured temperature, pH or hardness from a field site found.
- Not introduced anywhere: "This species has not been reported as introduced or established in the United States", and no introduction elsewhere. (S4)

## 3. Size, sexing, lifespan

3.1 Adult size (sources disagree)
- Seriously Fish: maximum standard length "40mm SL" (1.6 in). (S1)
- FishBase: "Max length : 3.3 cm TL male/unsexed", "Maximum length questionable", and "Maximum length reported to reach 4 cm TL" from another reference. (S2)
- FWS, quoting Eigenmann (1917): "the size range of specimens as 21-33mm". (S4)
- Practical Fishkeeping: adults "grow to around 4cm/1.6" in length." (S8)
- Aquarium Source: "around 1.5 inches in length when fully grown." (S10). Build Your Aquarium: "about 1½ inches". (S11)
- Wikipedia: "4 to 5 cm (1.6 to 2.0 in)", larger than neon and cardinal tetras (uncited). (S12)
- Retailer specs (observations only): LiveAquaria "Maximum Size: 2"" ; That Pet Place "Max Size 1.5"; Aquatic Arts "Average adult size: 1.2 - 1.6 inches (3 - 4 cm)".
- Summary for writers: about 1.5 inches (4 cm) is the referenced maximum; wild-caught specimens measured 21 to 33 mm in 1917; 2 inches is the high end in lower-tier and retailer pages.

3.2 Sexing
- Seriously Fish: "Mature females are more rounded in the belly than males, and tend to grow a little larger." (S1)
- Practical Fishkeeping: males "slimmer with a slightly sunken belly", females larger and fuller-bodied; color does not differ by sex (fetch-tool paraphrase). (S8)
- Aquarium Source: "Females are typically a bit larger and feature a plumper body shape", differences "subtle". (S10)

3.3 Lifespan in captivity (no authoritative source found)
- Aquarium Source: "The average glowlight tetra lifespan is between two and four years when given proper care." (S10)
- Wikipedia: "two to four years", tagged citation needed. (S12). Do not use alone.
- Seriously Fish, FishBase, Practical Fishkeeping (breeding article) give none. (S1, S2, S8)
- Aquarium Co-Op's "3-5 years" is for GloFish overall, not glowlights. Do not use. (S17)

3.4 Wild lifespan: no source found.

## 5. Tank

5.1 Minimum tank size and footprint (sources disagree)
- Seriously Fish: base "60 × 38cm" (24 × 15 in); a tank of about 68 L / 18 US gal "could house a small group of these comfortably" (fetch-tool paraphrase of the volume). (S1)
- Aquarium Source: "You can keep half a dozen fish in a standard 10-gallon tank!", but recommends 20 gallons or more if possible. (S10)
- Build Your Aquarium: "Minimum tank: 20 gallons, long or tall" (fetch-tool paraphrase). (S11)
- Practical Fishkeeping Q&A: a 60 L tank is "a reasonable size" for a group of 10 glowlights (quote: "your tank is a reasonable size, leaving scope for more fish"; fetch-tool paraphrase of the context). (S9)
- Retailer specs (observations): Aquatic Arts, LiveAquaria, That Pet Place and PetSmart all list a 10 gallon minimum.
- DISAGREEMENT: 10 gal (Aquarium Source, retailers) vs about 18 gal / 60 cm base (Seriously Fish) vs 20 gal (Build Your Aquarium). The best-tier source sets the footprint at 60 x 38 cm.

5.2 Group size
- Seriously Fish: "Always buy a group of at least 6 of these, preferably 10 or more." (S1)
- Practical Fishkeeping Q&A: "A group of 10 of either of these would fill the mid-waters of your tank with activity and colour." (covers glowlight and ember tetras) (S9)
- Aquarium Source: "you should have at least six glowlight tetras". (S10)
- Build Your Aquarium: groups of at least 6, "Fewer than that causes stress, pale color, and hiding"; 10 or more better (fetch-tool paraphrase). (S11)
- PetSmart listing (retailer, observation): "prefer to swim in schools of five or more".
- Research on shoaling group size for this species: none found.

5.3 Temperature
- Seriously Fish: "24-28°C" (75-82°F). (S1)
- FishBase: "Tropical; 24°C - 28°C"; FWS notes the source marks this as "assumed to represent recommended aquarium water temperature". (S2, S4)
- Aquarium Source: "74°F to 82°F (around 77 is ideal)". (S10)
- Build Your Aquarium: 73 to 83°F. (S11)
- Retailer specs: Aquatic Arts 72-82°F; That Pet Place 75-82; LiveAquaria 72-77°F.
- Consensus ceiling: 28°C (82°F), with 83°F the highest figure seen.

5.4 pH
- Seriously Fish: "5.5-7.5"; "tends to be more colourful when kept in acidic conditions." (S1)
- Aquarium Source: "5.5 to 7.5 (aim to keep it on the acidic side)". (S10)
- FishBase: "pH range: 6.0 - 8.0". (S2)
- Build Your Aquarium: 5.5 to 7.0 preferred (fetch-tool paraphrase). (S11)
- Retailer spec: That Pet Place 6.5-7.5.
- DISAGREEMENT: lower bound 5.5 (hobby/husbandry) vs 6.0 (FishBase); upper 7.0 to 8.0.

5.5 Hardness
- Seriously Fish: "2-15dGH". (S1)
- FishBase: "dH range: 5 - 12". (S2)
- Aquarium Source: "up to 15 dGH" for general care; breeding "no higher than 6 dGH". (S10)
- Retailer spec: Aquatic Arts KH 3-10 dKH.
- KH: no reliable species figure.

5.6 Lighting
- No source gives a lighting spec for adults. Aquarium Source says the stripe "will really shine" under the right lighting (no intensity given) and that breeding eggs are "extremely sensitive to light". (S10) Practical Fishkeeping covers the breeding tank to keep out bright light: "Tetra species can lay light-sensitive eggs." (S8)

5.7 Plants, substrate, decor
- Aquarium Source: "add a wide variety of plants", "fine-leafed plants of varying heights", "some free swimming space in the center of the tank", fine sand on the bottom. (S10)
- Build Your Aquarium: floating plants, planted midground, sand or gravel both fine (fetch-tool paraphrase). (S11)
- Wild: forest tributaries stained dark with tannins (S1), which supports leaf litter, dim cover and floating plants. Not stated as a requirement by any source.
- Aquarium Source: some aquarists add "a small bag of aquarium-safe peat" to the filter for tannins. (S10)

## 6. Diet

- Seriously Fish: "Easy to feed. It will readily accept just about anything offered." and "offer regular meals of small live and frozen foods such as bloodworm, Daphnia and brine shrimp". (S1)
- FishBase: "Feeds on worms, small crustaceans and plants" (referenced to Mills and Vevers 1989; quoted by FWS). Aquarium: omnivore per retailer specs. (S2, S4)
- Aquarium Source: "stick with a standard diet of micropellets or small flakes"; occasional freshly hatched brine shrimp and freeze-dried tubifex. (S10)
- Feeding frequency: Aquarium Source: "Feed these fish several times throughout the day, but keep the feedings light." (S10). Build Your Aquarium: two to three times daily (fetch-tool paraphrase). (S11). No higher-tier source gives a frequency.
- Wild diet: FishBase line above only; no gut-content study found.

## 7. Behavior and tankmates

- Seriously Fish: "It is lively, quite colourful and peaceful." Good tankmate for "most livebearers, danios, rasboras, other tetras" and peaceful bottom dwellers; "Obviously, it isn't safe with larger species that may see it as food, such as angel fish." (S1)
- Aquarium Source: "Aggression isn't a huge problem."; leaves slow-moving or flowing-finned fish alone; "Angels are notorious for eating glowlight tetras."; named tankmates: peaceful tetras, cory catfish, cherry barbs, guppies, kuhli and clown loaches, mollies, danios, honey and sparkling gouramis. (S10)
- Practical Fishkeeping: "excellent for the community aquarium" (S8); "ideal for community tanks" (S9).
- Fin nipping: three lower-tier pages say they are not a problem. Build Your Aquarium: "Glowlight tetras are not fin nippers"; males may nip each other with no lasting harm (S11). Aquarium Source: leaves flowing fins alone (S10). A search summary cited a GloFish-focused guide (aquifarm.com) calling them "notorious fin-nippers", but that page was NOT opened. No authoritative source on nipping either way. Record as: no strong source; lower-tier pages say they do not nip.
- Betta: no opened page addresses glowlights with bettas. Gap.
- Shrimp: Build Your Aquarium says shrimp and snails are compatible (fetch-tool paraphrase). (S11) Not a strong source; fish this size may eat shrimplets.
- Swimming level: PetSmart listing (retailer): "Swim level: Middle". Aquarium Source: free swimming space in the center. (S10)

## 8. Health

8.1 Common diseases
- FishBase disease list (quoted by FWS): "Fin-rot Disease (late stage)", "White spot Disease, Parasitic infestations (protozoa, worms, etc.)", "Fin Rot (early stage)", "Bacterial Infections (general)". "No OIE listed diseases were reported." (S2, S4)
- Aquarium Source: "Ich is highly contagious and potentially lethal." (S10). Build Your Aquarium: "prone to ich", so quarantine new fish (fetch-tool paraphrase). (S11)
- Build Your Aquarium also warns against long-term aquarium salt for this species ("Moderate concentrations can stress or kill them", fetch-tool paraphrase). Low tier, unverified; VCA says salt use is "only suitable for scaled fish", and glowlights are scaled. (S11, S13)

8.2 Ich and the 86°F heat treatment (IMPORTANT for the shared ich guide link)
- VCA: "Most strains of ich cannot survive in water temperatures above 86ºF." and "Not all fish can stand the heat, though." The parasite also "replicates faster in warmer water." Heat and salt are "less effective than chemical treatment because resistant strains can develop" (fetch-tool paraphrase). (S13) https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/fish-white-spot-disease-ich
- Glowlight temperature ceilings: 28°C / 82°F (Seriously Fish, FishBase), 83°F (Build Your Aquarium). 86°F is above every range found.
- No critical thermal maximum or lethal-temperature study for H. erythrozonus found. No source says glowlights tolerate 86°F.
- Plain reading: 86°F is 3 to 4°F above the top of every opened range, and there is no measured ceiling to justify it. The method cannot be stated as safe for this species from the sources found. Do not write it the way the cardinal guide does (that guide has a measured 50% mortality figure; glowlights do not).

8.3 Neon tetra disease (Pleistophora hyphessobryconis)
- Sanders, Watral and Kent (2012): the parasite "has been described in a broad range of fish hosts", named for its type host, the neon tetra Paracheirodon innesi, and reported in aquarium fish in several families. The paper does not mention Hemigrammus or glowlight tetras. (S14) https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4117576
- Glowlight susceptibility specifically: no source found. A search returned nothing glowlight-specific.

8.4 Quarantine
- VCA: quarantine "for at least 30 days" before adding new fish (stated for ich). (S13)
- Practical Fishkeeping: "A quarantine or hospital tank is advisable for any new fish, especially online purchases." (fetch-tool paraphrase of the acclimation guide). (S15)
- The site's cardinal guide already uses 30 days.

## 9. Handling: netting, acclimation, transport

None of these are glowlight-specific; all are general small-fish guidance.
- Lights: new arrivals are kept dark; the article says "switch these off!" (S15)
- Float acclimation: add small amounts of tank water to a rolled-down bag; "Repeat this every few minutes" until it holds mostly aquarium water, "30-60 minutes"; net the fish in and leave the transport water behind, as it may carry waste or pest snail eggs (fetch-tool paraphrase). (S15)
- Drip acclimation is for sensitive species such as Caridina shrimp, about a couple of hours. (S15) Not needed for tetras by this source.
- Settling: give new arrivals an hour or two in the dark; they may take a day or two to start eating (fetch-tool paraphrase). (S15)
- Long trips: "Ammonia can rise and pH can drop" in transit (fetch-tool paraphrase). (S15)
- Bagging: "one-to three ratio" of water to air; bags in "a dark container" or wrapped in a towel or newspaper. (S16)
- Water-chemistry note for acclimation: wild-type fish are from soft acidic water (S1), tank-bred stock tolerates more (Aquatic Arts lists its stock as tank-bred; retailer observation).

## 10. Breeding basics

- Seriously Fish: "Quite easily bred, although you'll need to set up a separate tank". Water "soft and acidic in the range pH 5.5-6.5, gH 1-5, with a temperature of around 26.5 - 29 °C" (80-84°F). Eggs "will hatch in 24-36 hours, with the fry becoming free swimming a 3-4 days later." "Average brood size per female is 120-150." (S1)
- Practical Fishkeeping (John Rundle): bare tank, small sponge filter, 25-26°C, pH not above 7.2, DH not above 6.5; "More than 200 eggs can be laid by an adult pair."; "Within 24 hours the eggs will hatch."; "Parents will eat the eggs if left in."; fry live on the yolk sac about five days, then cultured infusoria, later brine shrimp nauplii and crushed dry food (fetch-tool paraphrase except where quoted). Described as a good first breeding project. (S8)
- Aquarium Source: female releases "100 to 150 eggs"; "Eggs will hatch in a day"; eggs "extremely sensitive to light"; water no higher than 6 dGH. (S10)
- DISAGREEMENT on clutch size: 120-150 average (Seriously Fish), 100-150 (Aquarium Source), more than 200 from a single selected pair (Practical Fishkeeping). Use "about 100 to 200".
- Egg predation: parents eat the eggs (S8); Build Your Aquarium agrees: egg scatterers, no parental care (fetch-tool paraphrase). (S11)

## 11. "Did you know?" candidates

A. A woman scientist named it, under her maiden name. The species is credited to "Durbin, 1909", which is the maiden name of Marion Durbin Ellis, who described many small tetras from the 1908 Indiana University and Carnegie Museum expedition to British Guiana.
- ETYFish: Durbin is the maiden name of Ellis (fetch-tool paraphrase). (S7) https://etyfish.org/acestrorhamphidae/
- GBIF publishedIn: "Durbin, M. L. (1909). Reports on the expedition to British Guiana ... Annals of the Carnegie Museum". (S6) https://api.gbif.org/v1/species/2354398
- Verify on the desktop that the Durbin in the citation and the Ellis in ETYFish are the same person before the article states it; ETYFish says so, but no second source was opened.

B. The red stripe is a gap in the pigment cells. Eigenmann's 1917 description, quoted by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, says "A broad stripe without chromatophores cherry-red in life extends from the head to the caudal". The species name itself means "red-banded".
- FWS (quoting Eigenmann 1917). (S4) https://www.fws.gov/sites/default/files/documents/Ecological-Risk-Screening-Summary-Glowlight-Tetra.pdf
- ETYFish for the name meaning. (S7) https://etyfish.org/acestrorhamphidae/
- Careful: the quote says "without chromatophores" and "cherry-red in life". Do not explain the mechanism (blood color vs reflected light) because no opened source does.

C. A name-change survivor. Exported from Guyana in the 1930s, an early guess that it was a different tetra was rejected, and Practical Fishkeeping says it "played the 'change the name game' to the full." Today databases still split on its family (see 1.4).
- Practical Fishkeeping (S8) https://www.practicalfishkeeping.co.uk/features/how-to-breed-the-glowlight-tetra/
- de Melo et al. 2024 for the family split. (S5) https://synapsesocial.com/papers/W4402170922

D. (Backup) Its eggs are light-sensitive, so breeders cover the tank. (S8, S10)

## 13. GloFish and color-morph versions

- Albino: Seriously Fish: "There is a selectively-bred albino form that has been produced for the trade". It adds, per the fetch-tool paraphrase, that care is the same as the standard form. (S1)
- Golden: Wikipedia: "Golden and albino varieties are also sold", tagged citation needed. (S12) No opened husbandry source uses "golden" as a separate morph. A search summary said one care guide lists "Golden Glowlight" as another name for the albino; that guide was not opened, so it is not recorded as sourced. Treat "golden" as at most a trade name for the albino.
- GloFish: no GloFish glowlight exists in any page opened. Aquarium Co-Op: GloFish tetras are "genetically modified black skirt tetras that glow under blue lighting" (Gymnocorymbus ternetzi), and the glowlight tetra is "not mentioned". (S17) https://www.aquariumcoop.com/blogs/aquarium/glofish. AquariumFish.com's tetra collection lists GloFish tetras separately from the Glowlight Tetra (retailer observation).
- Error to ignore: Build Your Aquarium says the glowlight's glow "comes from jellyfish DNA" and, in the same article, that glowlights are "unenhanced by chemicals or mutations". The first claim is wrong for this species (the natural stripe; GloFish are a different, modified black skirt tetra), so do not cite that page for anything about color. (S11)
- Name confusion worth a line in the article: "glowlight" (natural red stripe) vs "GloFish" (a brand of fluorescent fish).
