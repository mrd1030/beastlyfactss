## 1. Identity, size, sexing

- Seriously Fish: max size 35 mm SL (1.4 in SL). (C11) FishBase: 3.0 cm SL. (C10) Wild fish measured by Walker were 11 to 24.9 mm SL, plus one 28 mm fish (80 fish, 14 sites). (C2) Chao: "At capture, cardinal tetras are 12-35 mm SL ... unimodal at about 18 mm SL, with few specimens larger than 25 mm SL." (C1)
- Sexing: Seriously Fish: mature females "noticeably rounder-bodied" and slightly larger. (C11)
- Stripe pattern (guide's cardinal vs neon tell): Linhares et al.: "The iridescent blue strip is contrasted by the dark upper and lower red stripes that extend over the entire body of the fish." (C4) No opened source describes the neon's half-length red stripe.
- Authority: Schultz, 1956 (FishBase). (C10)

## 2. Wild habitat

- Seriously Fish: "a small characid from the rio Negro and Orinoco drainages of Brazil, Colombia and Venezuela"; "A more-or-less exclusive inhabitant of forest streams and minor tributaries containing relatively slowly-moving water"; in the Orinoco more often in clear-water morichal habitats. (C11)
- FishBase: upper Orinoco and Negro basins; "Occurs mainly in shoals in the middle water layers"; non-migratory. (C10)
- Walker (14 sites along about 450 km of the middle Rio Negro, sampled July to October 1999): pH of all collection sites "ranged between 3,35 and 5,82, this last value referring to the Rio Demini, and being the only value of pH > 5.0"; conductivity 8.03 µS/cm (Rio Caures) to 22.8 (Igarape Arixana). Habitats: low-flow areas, litter-covered stream bottoms, flooded forest floor; fish "move about in groups of a few dozen." Spawning "during the early phase of rising waters, from February to April." (C2)
- Sanchez-Bernal et al.: a blackwater fish that tolerates "ion-poor, acidic waters of this blackwater river"; uses flooded forest for feeding and reproduction; no numeric pH or temperature given. (C5)
- Linhares: store-sourced middle Rio Negro cardinals were held two months at 29.2 ± 1.1°C (about 84.6°F), pH 5.5 ± 1.3, conductivity 24.5 ± 2.7 µS, brightness 250 to 500 lux by day, fed twice daily a commercial feed with 38% crude protein. (C4) Husbandry conditions in an experiment, not a recommendation.

## 3. Lifespan

- Chao (2001): "The cardinal tetra has a life span of a year or so in the floodplain, although some may live more than 5 years in captivity." Smaller captive-reared individuals "may live much longer in the captivity, over four years." Wild cardinals "probably die after spawning." (C1)
- Seriously Fish: in the wild "considered a virtually annual fish" and "can live considerably longer in aquaria." (C11)
- Practical Fishkeeping: wild "around a year"; captive "up to five years if conditions are right." (C13)
- Animal Diversity Web: wild "about 1 year"; captivity "about 5 years, although individuals may live as long as 10 years." (C12; student-written, no reference attached to the figure.)
- Silva Ladislau et al. quote Chao et al. that a wild cardinal "would be lucky to survive a year in the wild" while a home aquarium fish might live "two to three years or more." (C6, secondary quote)
- CONTRADICTION in the cost guide: "about five years ... and some individuals reach ten." Five is supported (Chao, PFK, Seriously Fish say "more than 5" or "up to five"); ten rests only on the student-written ADW page. The "close to an annual fish in the wild" claim is supported (Chao, Seriously Fish, PFK). The cause given in the guide, that dry-season pool shrinkage strands huge numbers, is not stated in any opened source: Chao says they probably die after spawning. (A fishers' report says "the start of the dry season is when most of the fishing for piaba takes place," C6, which is a fishing season, not a die-off.)

## 4. Conservation and the Rio Negro fishery

4.1 Status
- FishBase: IUCN Least Concern (LC), assessed 4 March 2021; resilience High; fisheries and aquarium trade "highly commercial." (C10)
- ADW: "not considered threatened"; IUCN, US federal list and CITES all "No special status." (C12)
- IUCN page itself: 403. Cooke et al. 2009 is quoted only through Seriously Fish: six Rio Negro tributary populations were "recommended for conservation priority" for their genetic uniqueness. (C11)

4.2 Share of the trade (supports the cost guide's "roughly 80%")
- Chao (2001): "cardinal tetras (Paracheirodon axelrodi) averaged 80% of the total number of fishes export from Manaus"; Table 1 yearly shares ran 52.84% (1998, IBAMA) to 92.58% (1995/96, Piaba), e.g. 87.50% in 1976 and 87.92% in 1999. About 20 million ornamental fishes exported yearly from Amazonas State; at least 10,000 Amazonians take part, "including more than 1,600 fishers from the riverine communities of the middle Rio Negro"; 1998 export revenue US$2,216,620 (IBAMA report). (C1)
- Walker: "Some 12-15 million neon-tetras are exported annually, this is ca 80% of the total market of ornamental fish of the Amazon State (Chao 2001)." (C2)
- Project Piaba: "Approximately 80% of trade from artisanal fisheries in the Rio Negro is comprised of this single species." (C15)
- Sanchez-Bernal: "more than 80% of ornamental fish exported from the Negro River each year, per the article's cited sources." (C5)
- Seriously Fish: in Barcelos the species "accounts for as much as 60% of annual income." (C11, secondary)
- The cost guide's "roughly 80% of the artisanal aquarium fish trade out of the Rio Negro" matches Project Piaba's wording and Chao's export average. Better to cite Chao for the number.

4.3 How the fishery works (for context)
- Chao: main fishing areas are within 200 km of Barcelos; fishers use a dip net (rapiche) and baited traps (cacuri); a trip lasts two weeks; fish are shipped in plastic tubs holding "12-15 L of water" with "a few to 1,000 live fishes," and fishers "often apply industrial grade tetracycline to the shipping tubs." Shipping mortality between Barcelos and Manaus: "less than 5% average mortality" since 1995, against earlier claims of "greater than 50%." High season in Barcelos is October to April. (C1)
- Chao's abstract: the cardinal is "the indicator species for the ornamental fishery," and its numbers "can significantly alter the socio-economic situation of riverine communities." Also: "There is no evidence that fish diversity has been threatened, but the catch might decrease with increased fishing pressure." Authors note limits: lack of stable resources and no integrated assessment. (C1)
- Silva Ladislau et al. (89 fishers interviewed in Barcelos, January to April 2016): cardinal was the most-cited species (79 mentions, 12.5%); commercial exploitation traces to Herbert Axelrod's 1955 visit; the paper quotes Chao et al. calling the fishery possibly "the world's most benign fishery." (C6)
- The enrichment guide says the conservation benefit is "contested." No opened source states a contest. Chao's own text calls for more study; leave the "contested" wording unsourced or soften it.
- Cost guide's "Neon tetra is now almost entirely captive-bred. Cardinal is harder to breed at commercial scale": FishBase says "captive breeding is possible but difficult" and most aquarium specimens are caught in Rio Negro and Orinoco tributaries (C10); Seriously Fish: much of the trade "still relies on wild-caught fish or community projects such as Project Piaba." (C11) Counterpoints: Practical Fishkeeping (Bleher): "many are now bred worldwide, especially in eastern Europe" (C14); PFK cardinal profile (UK): "Most on sale here are captive-bred, but wild-caught fish are also available." (C13); and a US retailer sells tank-bred cardinals (section 10). The guide's "meaningful share still wild" holds; "most are wild" would need a trade statistic, which was not found.

## 5. Tank

5.1 Size and footprint
- Seriously Fish: "An aquarium with base dimensions of 60 * 30 cm (24 * 12 in) or larger is recommended" (about 54 L). (C11) FishBase: groups of 5 or more "in tanks of at least 60 cm." (C10) PFK: cardinal and neon "no smaller than 60cm (24")". (C13) The tank guide's 24 x 12 in matches.

5.2 Temperature
- Seriously Fish: 23 to 29°C (73 to 84°F). (C11) FishBase: 23 to 27°C. (C10) PFK: biotope 25 to 28°C (77 to 82°F); "cardinals and green neons: temperatures reaching 30°C (86°F)"; breeding 24°C. (C13) The tank guide's 73 to 84°F and 77 to 82°F target match Seriously Fish and PFK.
- Lab tolerance (Oliveira et al.): 100% survival at 29 and 31°C over 96 hours; high-temperature LT50 33.7°C in the table (the abstract says 33.3°C: the paper contradicts itself, so quote neither precisely); total mortality "above 35 °C"; low-temperature LT50 19.6°C (about 67.3°F) and total mortality at 15°C (59°F). Fish: wild juveniles of 0.07 ± 0.002 g from igarapes near Barcelos, held at least 4 weeks at 25 ± 1°C, tested 96 hours in 40 L chambers. No CTmax reported. (C3)
- NOTE for the health guide's "held two weeks at 86°F": the paper tests 96 hours on tiny juveniles. It does not test a two-week hold. Linhares held store fish at 29.2°C for two months (C4), which is near but below 86°F (30°C).

5.3 pH and hardness
- Seriously Fish: pH 3.5 to 7.5 "with wild specimens doing best in more acidic conditions"; 1 to 12 dGH, "toward the lower end for wild fish or breeding." (C11) FishBase: pH 4.0 to 6.0, dH 5 to 12. (C10) Wild measured: pH 3.35 to 5.82 (Walker, C2).
- The tank guide's "roughly pH 5.0 to 7.0" is narrower than Seriously Fish (3.5 to 7.5) and sits above the measured wild values (3.35 to 5.82). It is not wrong for captive stock but is not the wild range.
- Oliveira et al.: 100% survival at pH 4.0 to 8.5 over 96 hours; 96-hour LC50 2.9 (acid) and 8.8 (alkaline). Water hardness is not reported in the paper. (C3)

5.4 Ammonia and nitrite (Oliveira et al., C3)
- 96-hour LC50 for total ammonia 23.7 mg/L (NH3 + NH4+), equivalent to about 0.36 mg/L unionized NH3 at pH 7.6 ± 0.12. Table 2 shows 70% mortality at 23.7 mg/L, which is not fully consistent with an LC50 at that level.
- 96-hour LC50 for nitrite 1.1 mg/L NO2-; 100% mortality at 2.0 mg/L. "temperatures below 19.6 oC and nitrite concentrations above 1.1 mg/L NO2- may compromise fish survival."
- NUANCE for the guides that say nitrite is what the species "tolerates worst": that is true comparing 1.1 mg/L nitrite to 23.7 mg/L total ammonia, but the toxic unionized ammonia fraction at the LC50 (about 0.36 mg/L) is lower than the nitrite LC50. The ranking depends on which ammonia figure is used. Say "very sensitive to nitrite" and avoid ranking, or state both numbers.

5.5 School size
- Seriously Fish: "at least 8-10 fish," plus other schooling species for security. (C11) FishBase: 5 or more. (C10) PFK: cardinals "appreciate being in a large group." (C13) The Saxby study did not include cardinals (it used neon tetras, white cloud mountain minnows, angelfish and tiger barbs). Guides that borrow the "ten" from neons already say so.

5.6 Light and color
- Linhares et al. (fish 1.66 ± 0.16 cm, five light levels 0, 250, 500, 1,200 and 2,700 lux, exposures to 72 h): "Loss of skin color was observed when cardinal tetra was exposed to intense light. The melanic and neon stripes became pale due to a reduction in melanophores densities." The red stripe intensified. "At low light levels (0 to 250 lux), the melanophores ... proliferate in the black and neon stripes resulting in a more vibrant skin color." Authors "suggest that in nature, the paleness of the skin may represent a camouflage strategy" in bright hours, which "can help this species to visually confuse potential predators." (C4) This is the first direct support for "dim light brings out color," but for cardinals only. The camouflage point is a hypothesis; no predator was tested.
- Husbandry sources give "dim" but no lux: no opened source.

5.7 Flow and substrate
- Seriously Fish describes slowly-moving forest streams. (C11) Gentle flow and dark-substrate advice: no opened source specific to cardinals.

## 6. Diet and feeding

6.1 Wild diet
- Walker (stomach contents of 80 wild fish, 11 to 24.9 mm SL; handnet samples July to October 1999): "essentially a predator, feeding on the mesofauna that adheres to submerged litter, roots and waterplants." Counts of fish with each item: microcrustacea 38 (47.5%, mostly Cladocera, with benthic harpacticoid Copepoda), chironomid larvae 35 (43.8%), other insects 14 (17.5%), other prey 6 (7.5%), mesofauna including rotifers and thecamoebae 18 (22.5%), eggs 10 (12.5%), algae 10 (12.5%), detritus 32 (40.0%). Percentages do not sum to 100 because one stomach holds several items. "algae intake was relatively infrequent." (C2) Walker also notes that cardinals raised in captivity are larger and grow faster.
- FishBase: eats worms and small crustaceans; trophic level 3.3 (± 0.39). (C10)
- Seriously Fish: likely omnivorous, eating "small invertebrates, crustacea, filamentous algae, fallen fruit and suchlike." (C11)
- The feeding guide says "omnivores." Walker's gut-content work says essentially a predator. "Mostly small invertebrates, with some algae and detritus" is accurate to all three.
- The guide's "cladocerans, copepods, rotifers" match Walker. "Insect eggs" partly: Walker found eggs "generally of invertebrate origin." "Feed almost constantly": Practical Fishkeeping says "feed constantly throughout the day" (C13); Walker did not time feeding.

6.2 Captive diet and frequency
- Seriously Fish: dried foods can sustain them; varied live and frozen diet (bloodworm, mosquito larvae, Daphnia, Moina); "should ideally be offered small live foods on a regular basis." (C11)
- Practical Fishkeeping: "multiple small feeding in captivity is the best way to go"; foods: bloodworm, brine shrimp, black mosquito larvae, cyclops, Daphnia. (C13)
- Merck (general): "Most aquarium fish should be fed at least daily"; crush flakes for small-mouthed fish; "a wide variety of feeds." (N9 in the neon file)
- No source gives "twice daily" for cardinals as a recommendation. Linhares fed their holding stock twice daily (C4), which supports it as a practice in an experiment.

## 7. Health

7.1 Neon tetra disease in cardinals
- Merck: NTD is caused by Pleistophora hyphessobryconis, "which infects the skeletal musculature"; fish affected include tetras, angelfish, rasboras, barbs and zebrafish; microsporidia are "considered nontreatable" and "horizontal transmission in an aquarium is likely." (C8)
- Sanders et al. 2010: infects about 20 freshwater species in four orders; neon tetra is the type host; ingestion of spores infects fish (7 of 10 experimentally exposed zebrafish). Details in NEON_TETRA_RESEARCH.md section 7.1.
- The cardinal tetra is not named as a host in any opened paper. The guide's cardinal-specific statement ("cardinal tetra's own family among them") holds at the family level (Characidae).

7.2 Ich and the 86°F claim
- VCA: "Most strains of ich cannot survive in water temperatures above 86ºF." and "Not all fish can stand the heat, though." (N10)
- UF/IFAS CIR920: no heat-only method. It gives daily chemical treatments and 3 to 5 minimum treatments at 75 to 79°F, and 4 to 5 ppt salt for 7 to 10 days. (N7)
- For cardinals: Seriously Fish top of range 29°C (84°F); PFK "temperatures reaching 30°C (86°F)"; the 96-hour tolerance study shows 100% survival at 29 and 31°C. None tests a two-week hold at 86°F, and none tests whether the heat method clears ich in cardinals. The health guide's two-week hold is therefore unsupported as written. Ich quarantine of 30 days: UF/IFAS (N7).
- Cardinal tetras are wild-caught often and Seriously Fish notes wild fish are less tolerant of deteriorating water than commercially bred fish. (C11)

7.3 Fin rot, swim bladder
- Not researched from an opened page (Aquarium Co-Op is the current source for fin rot). No replacement found. The Merck bacterial diseases page was opened but no fin rot section was extracted. See Gaps.

7.4 Quarantine
- UF/IFAS CIR920: "a minimum 30-day quarantine period is recommended for new fish." (N7) The guide's "30 days, 30 to 60 for a fish worth being sure of": the 30 to 60 range was not found in an opened source. (A UF/IFAS lymphocystis fact sheet reportedly says 30 to 60 days preferred; seen only in search text, not opened.)

7.5 Transit and settling
- Chao: shipping mortality "less than 5%" Barcelos to Manaus since 1995; tetracycline applied to shipping tubs; improved care and handling would reduce captive mortality. (C1) Walker, Seriously Fish: wild fish less tolerant of deteriorating water. The guide's "losses in the first fortnight are common" has no opened source; Chao covers only the Barcelos to Manaus leg.

## 8. Handling and acclimation

- General small-fish guidance: see NEON_TETRA_RESEARCH.md section 8 (PFK acclimatising N15, PFK bagging N16).
- Cardinal-specific: the cold risk. Oliveira et al.: LT50 19.6°C, total mortality at 15°C. A bag in a cold shipping box can approach this; no source opened discusses bag temperatures. (C3)
- Soft to hard water mismatch calling for a slow method: Seriously Fish gives the 1 to 12 dGH range, "toward the lower end for wild fish," but no source says shop-to-tap mismatches require drip acclimation. Guide's inference.

## 9. Breeding basics

- FishBase: captive breeding "possible but difficult"; eggs hatch in 24 to 30 hours; fry swim freely after 3 to 4 days. (C10) Chao: "a few large eggs (300 to 500), the largest approximately 0.55 mm in diameter"; probably multiple spawning with rising water. (C1) ADW: breeding pH 5.5 to 6, about 24°C, dim light. (C12) PFK: breeding pH 5.5 to 6, 24°C (75°F). (C13)
