### Contradictions and things the owner should know first

1. **Feeding intervals are one source's, and vets disagree.** Guides (tank setup, feeding, FAQs): hatchling 5 to 7, juvenile 7 to 10, adult 10 to 14 days. ReptiFiles (1) matches exactly: "Hatchlings, every 5-7 days", "Juveniles, every 7-10 days", "Adults, every 10-14 days". PetMD (2, DVM) contradicts: "Juvenile milk snakes should be fed twice a week, while adults should be fed once a week" and "Baby snakes can be fed every other day." MSD Vet Manual (10): "Most snake species should be fed every 1-2 weeks." Ask A Vet (16): hatchlings "every 3-5 days", adults weekly. Verdict: partly supported (ReptiFiles only). The adult 10 to 14 days sits inside MSD's 1 to 2 weeks. Hatchling and juvenile figures are on the owner review list.
2. **Enclosure size.** Guides: adult minimum 48 x 24 x 24. ReptiFiles (1): "48”L x 24”W x 24”H" is the minimum for one average milksnake, larger strongly recommended. PetMD (2): "a 20-gallon tank or larger" for a medium adult and a breeder tank of at least 40 gallons for larger ones. Ask A Vet (16): adults 40 to 60 gal. The guides' number is ReptiFiles' and is the larger one; the vet source gives a much smaller minimum. Not a factual error, but the guide should not imply vets say 48 x 24 x 24.
3. **Post-meal handling window.** Feeding guide: 48 hours. MSD Vet Manual (10): "snakes should not be handled for 3 days after feeding." Ask A Vet (16): "48 hr post-feeding". Verdict: partly supported. 48 hours has only a company-blog vet behind it; the manual says 3 days. Suggest "48 to 72 hours" or "3 days" in the feeding guide. Neither PetMD nor ReptiFiles gives a number.
4. **Lifespan "20+".** ReptiFiles (1): "With good care, milksnakes can live 20 years or more." ADW (3): captive average "22.6 years". But PetMD (2): "Up to 15+ years", Smithsonian (6): "about 15 years in the wild and possibly up to 20 years in human care", Ask A Vet (16): "around 12-21 years". Verdict: partly supported. "20 years or more" is the upper end; safer wording "commonly 15 to 20 years, with ADW recording an average of 22.6 in captivity."
5. **Difficulty: see the section below.** PetMD, the only vet-authored care sheet already in the guides, labels it "Intermediate".
6. **UVB "real benefit" overstates.** Baines (14): "Species that consume whole prey can utilize vitamin D3 from their diet and do not appear to 'need' UVB for normal calcium metabolism." Merck (8) lists snakes under "no special lighting requirements" with broad-spectrum lighting still recommended. See the UVB verdict.
7. **"Arboreal more than kingsnakes get credit for" has nothing behind it and one source leans against.** ADW (3) calls the species "terricolous (ground-dwelling)" and hides under rotting logs; SD (4), MN DNR (5) and the Smithsonian (6) do not mention climbing. No opened source supports arboreal behavior.
8. **Cryptosporidium diagnosis is fine; the disinfectant claim is exactly supported.** WikiVet (12): "5% ammonia or 10% formalin", and "3% hypochlorite is not effective." No change.
9. **Mite color.** Merck (9) gives only size ("generally < 1.5 mm") and no color. The "tiny black or red" wording is carried by a clinic web page (15) with no named author. Not contradicted, but no approved source supports the red.
10. **Hatchling size.** Handling guide "6 to 10 inches": MN DNR (5) supports exactly ("The newly hatched young are 6 to 10 inches long"), but the Smithsonian (6) says Sinaloan hatchlings are "25 to 30 centimeters (10 to 12 inches)". Subspecies vary; the guide's number is the Minnesota (Eastern) one.
11. **Subspecies sizes in tank setup.** Guide: Pueblan 28 to 36, Eastern 36 to 45, Honduran and Sinaloan 40 to 48. ReptiFiles (1): L. t. triangulum "36-45”" (supported, Eastern), L. t. celaenops "14-24”", L. t. micropholis "60-72”". Smithsonian (6): Sinaloan "about 1 meter (3.5 feet)", i.e. 42 inches (inside 40 to 48). Pueblan 28 to 36 and Honduran 40 to 48: not found in any opened source (the 28 to 36 figure appeared only in a hobby site search snippet). Adult overall: MN DNR (5) "24 to 36 inches", SD (4) "24-36 inches", PetMD (2) "2 to 4 feet long, depending on species", ADW (3) "35 to 175 cm" overall and 60 to 130 cm most often in the US. The hub's "2 to 4 feet" is supported by PetMD; the 28 inch floor in tank setup is not a contradiction, it is a subspecies minimum above the species minimum.

### Difficulty: what the sources say

- PetMD (2, DVM): care-sheet difficulty "Intermediate". It adds that bites rarely break skin and that young snakes are "the most likely to attempt to bite"; handling-tolerant ("Most milk snakes are tolerant of gentle handling").
- ReptiFiles (1): "which makes them good pets for people who are new to snakes" and "Milksnakes usually tolerate handling well". No difficulty label.
- Ask A Vet (16, BVSc, company blog): "They're ideal for beginners, thanks to their manageable size, hardiness, and ease of care" and "generally docile and non-aggressive, though shy".
- Grover and Despain (7, peer reviewed, Pale milksnake): "Despite its reputation to the contrary, the Pale Milksnake is fairly easy to maintain for the experienced herpetoculturist." Also: "Occasionally, individuals may refuse to feed", many juveniles prefer lizards (so buy captive-bred, established feeders), and "Members of the genus Lampropeltis are notorious escape artists."
- Wild-caught or lizard-preferring subspecies are the hard cases; captive-bred common subspecies (Pueblan, Honduran, Sinaloan) are the easy ones. The sources split between "beginner" (ReptiFiles, Ask A Vet) and "intermediate" (PetMD vet).
- Recommendation for the owner's assistant: "Beginner/Intermediate" is the only label every opened source can live with. Set cost, handling, health, tank setup AND feeding, enrichment and the hub to "Beginner/Intermediate" so the six agree, and say "captive-bred" in the first line of the hub. Not a strong case for a flat "Beginner": the one vet-authored sheet says Intermediate.
