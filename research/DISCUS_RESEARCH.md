## Substrate section pass, 2026-11-01

Added to "Substrate": the inert-floor and calcareous warning the guide lacked. Source added to the block (now 5): Matt Clarke, "Frequently asked questions on aquarium substrates," Practical Fishkeeping (named-author article; re-opened this session): "Inert substrates and rocks don't alter the water chemistry, while non-inert, calcareous substrates do." "These contain calcium and other minerals that increase the hardness and pH." Coral sand only if you want "to make your water rock hard and increase the pH of your water to over 8.0." "Even standard pea gravel contains sufficient calcium to boost the pH and hardness." "Inert substrates are of particular importance to those keeping fishes that like soft water, such as Discus." No extension, vet or Seriously Fish page names discus for this point, which is why the Practical Fishkeeping article qualifies. Limestone is named as calcareous rock under Clarke's "rocks" line; the shared guide carries the limestone and vinegar material.

Links: one new link to /blog/aquarium-substrate-guide/ on "inert" (the shared guide is where inert versus calcareous floors and the vinegar test are explained). To hold the count at 5, the closing encyclopedia link became plain text: "The Discus encyclopedia profile has more on the species itself."

Bare bottoms: the existing bare-bottom and sand lines stay as written; their only source is Discus.com (WEAK, retailer learning center, still in the block). No non-hobby source opened this session says bare bottoms for discus, so nothing was added on bare floors.
