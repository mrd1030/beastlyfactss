## Substrate section, 2026-11-01

The guide had no substrate section; one was added under "Substrate" (138 words). Pages opened 2026-11-01:

- Seriously Fish: Betta splendens (SB51), https://www.seriouslyfish.com/species/betta-splendens/ (added to the Sources block, now 6). Extracts: "Substrates can vary from leaf litter to mud, sand, or deep sediment" (habitat); "This species fares best in a well-planted, shady aquarium with plenty of surface cover in the form of tall stem plants," "floating types such as Salvinia or Riccia spp."; "Cryptocoryne spp. are also a good choice"; "The addition of dried leaf litter offers additional cover and brings with it the growth of microbe colonies as decomposition occurs"; "These can provide a valuable secondary food source for fry, while tannins and other chemicals released by the decaying leaves are considered beneficial."
- PetMD, Maria Zayas DVM, Betta Fish Care Sheet (SB23, already in the block), https://www.petmd.com/fish/betta-fish-care-sheet. Extracts: "Substrate (optional)"; avoid "items like shells/coral/sand collected from beaches that can alter pH"; weekly "siphon substrate with a gravel vacuum; change 15 to 25% of the water" (page uses an en dash).

Notes:
- "no one material copies its home" is my reading of the varied wild substrates, not a source's words.
- "a betta does best close to neutral" restates the guide's own pH line (Water section).
- No source ties substrate texture to betta fin damage, so the section says nothing about grain.
- The guide's weekly 25 to 30% change vs PetMD's 15 to 25% is a water-change figure; not touched.
