# Leopard Gecko Research

Notes from the guide sweep. Owner decisions are dated; nothing here is cut
from a guide without the owner's say.

## 2026-11-06: sweep sources

- Cost guide: The Vet Desk and Reptile Direct came out of Sources (hobby
  sites). Zen Habitats stays in the feeding guide: approved by the owner
  (2026-11-06), the same standing as ReptiFiles.

## 2026-11-06: FunFacts

- Cost and enrichment FunFacts cut (owner): the cost one was advice the body
  already gives; the enrichment one cited unnamed "additional work".
- Health FunFact (stick tail nickname, a disease topic) replaced with the
  eyelid fact. PetMD Leopard Gecko Care Sheet: "Unlike many gecko species,
  leopard geckos have functional eyelids" and "they can blink and even close
  their eyes while sleeping".
- Handling FunFact (dropped tail twitches as a decoy) sourced to Higham and
  Russell, "Flip, flop and fly: modulated motor control and highly variable
  movement patterns of autotomized gecko tails", Biology Letters (online
  September 2009, cited as 2010). Seen through press coverage, not the paper:
  desktop check of the citation.
- PetMD also states the tail "will regrow within about 30 days", which backs
  the handling guide's regrowth line.

## 2026-11-06: tank setup

- "Diet, Briefly" cut (owner): the feeding guide owns the diet, and the
  section ended in a link-only sentence.
- The Oonincx entry had no title, year or link. Now: Oonincx et al. (2020),
  "The nocturnal leopard gecko (Eublepharis macularius) uses UVb radiation for
  vitamin D3 synthesis", Comparative Biochemistry and Physiology Part B 250:
  110506 (PubMed 32950659). Juveniles under low-level UVB had higher plasma
  25(OH)D3 than controls; growth did not differ.

## 2026-11-06: cost guide T5 sentence

The T5 HO wattage-to-length sentence (24, 39 and 54 W; copied from the bearded
dragon guide) contradicted the 14 W, 24 inch ShadeDweller the guide sells, and
its only source was the bare Zoo Med homepage. Both cut (owner). The cost guide
now cites 2 sources, under the 3 to 5 target: PetMD and the Cryptosporidium
study. Not padded; flagged for the next source pass.

## 2026-11-06: no mice for leopard geckos (owner)

The feeding guide said adults, females especially, may take a pinky mouse one
to two days old, no more than one a week. Owner (2026-11-06): no mice for
leopard geckos, the same house rule as bearded dragons (snakes and larger
reptiles are fine). The guide now says pinky mice and other rodents stay off
the menu, with insects covering protein at every age.

## 2026-11-06: sounds-off items settled in the sweep

- Handling: "hold off on regular handling until it reaches roughly 6 inches"
  waited until near adult size (adults are 6 to 9 inches); no source gives a
  size cutoff. Replaced with readiness, matching the guide's settle-in section.
- Feeding: waxworms "limit them to a couple times a week" read as a staple
  beside "occasional treats"; now "keep them to an occasional treat".
- Feeding: "adult weights of 60 to 75 grams" scoped "in that study" (seven
  geckos); the handling guide and hub carry the species range.
- Cost: the $30 floor only adds up with the optional UVB row; the annual line
  now says "with the UVB row".
- Enrichment: Rickman et al. 2025 cited from the paper, not the Lincoln news
  page (Applied Animal Behaviour Science 283: 106487,
  doi 10.1016/j.applanim.2024.106487). Abstract via the Refinement Database:
  enriched housing showed "a range of behaviours indicative of improved
  welfare", no difference on anxiety tests, and "a strong preference for the
  Naturalistic enriched enclosure" (N = 6). The guide's wording matches.
- Enrichment: the toys-do-not-replace-hides point cut from FAQ 2; the
  KeyTakeaway and What Not to Do keep it.
