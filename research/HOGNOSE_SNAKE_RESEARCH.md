### The Damm et al. numbers (settled)

Both numbers are right, for different things. From the full text (8): "Of the 91 submitted replies to the questionnaire, 63 have been rated as evaluable." and "In 34 reports, at least one symptom was mentioned post-bite." So 91 replies, 63 evaluable (the usable bite reports), 34 of those with at least one symptom, 29 with none. The Sources title "34 reports" is the paper's real title and correct as a title. The handling body's "63 usable bite reports" is correct. No change needed to either, though the body could add that 34 of the 63 had any symptom.

Other handling claims against the paper:
- "More than a third of quick bite-and-release cases caused no symptoms": supported. Paper: bite-and-release led to symptoms in about 63% of cases, so about 37% had none.
- "a snake that held on or chewed caused symptoms in about 80% of cases": supported ("extended bites and chewing each led to symptoms in 80%").
- "swelling was the most common symptom": supported ("Local edema was the most prevalent symptom after H. nasicus bites (79%)").
- "None caused systemic envenoming": NOT FOUND in the extract. The abstract says "no sequelae or fatalities occur", but Section 3.3 reports "Long-term sequelae were described in two reports" (one blistering and scabbing, one weakness lasting over a year). The authors' abstract conflicts with their own results. The guide's wording should avoid "no lasting effects" and the owner should know the paper is internally inconsistent.
- "Most of those bites came from a hungry snake or while moving one into a feeding container": not checked (the extract did not return the circumstances table).
- First aid: CONTRADICTION/TENSION with "don't pull it off". The paper says "In case of a bite, immediately removing the snake from the bite site reduces the chances of developing symptoms," and "Severity was correlated with exposure time to the bite." The paper says nothing about water. ReptiFiles (6) warns against yanking the snake off.

### Contradictions, ranked

1. Feeding interval for adults. Guide: once every two weeks, mature females every 10 to 14 days, some 14 to 21. Chicago Exotics (vet): "once weekly as they have a very fast metabolism." ReptiFiles: adults weekly, juveniles every 4 to 5 days. Only a retailer supports two weeks.
2. Prey size rule. Guide: "slightly thicker than the snake's center mass." ReptiFiles and PetPlace: no larger than the snake's head diameter.
3. "Mice are the best diet." ReptiFiles says mice are calorie-dense, not the best, rotate in other prey; the wild diet is toads.
4. Substrate depth ceiling. Guide: 3 to 6 inches. Sources: at least 3 (ReptiFiles), 4 to 5 for large females, 2 to 4 for adults (Chicago). Nothing says 6.
5. Bite release. Guide: pour cool water, "don't pull it off." Damm: prompt removal reduces symptoms, severity tracks exposure time; ReptiFiles agrees on cold water but also lists Listerine.
6. Damm abstract says "no sequelae", the results report two long-term sequelae. The guide's wording "None caused systemic envenoming" is not in the extract.
7. Floor-space rule: ReptiFiles reports it as the conventional rule it moves away from.

Not contradictions: UVB distances, temperatures, humidity, 20 and 40 gallon minimums, 5 gallon hatchlings, quarterly substrate change, 3 to 6 month quarantine all match.
