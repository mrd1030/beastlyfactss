## 1. What "livebearer" means, and the family

**These four species are all Poeciliidae.**
- FishBase classification line on all six species pages, verbatim: "Teleostei (teleosts) > Cyprinodontiformes (Rivulines, killifishes and live bearers) > Poeciliidae (Poeciliids) > Poeciliinae". (S1) Example: https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Poecilia-reticulata.html . The same line appears for P. sphenops, P. latipinna, X. maculatus, X. variatus and X. hellerii.
- FishBase family page, verbatim: the heading "Family Poeciliidae" with common name "Poeciliids"; "Distribution: low altitudes from eastern United States to northeastern Argentina; also in Africa and Madagascar." (S2) https://www.fishbase.se/summary/FamilySummary.php?Family=Poeciliidae . The Africa and Madagascar range is the FishBase family note and comes from groups it folded into the family; none of the four species is African. Say "the Americas" for the four.
- Schlupp, fetch tool: "The family is widespread from the United States of America to South America, with a center of diversity in Mexico." (S4) https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6007348
- Seriously Fish on the swordtail, verbatim: "Like other members of the viviparous family Poeciliidae". (S5) https://www.seriouslyfish.com/species/xiphophorus-hellerii/

**"Livebearer" is a common name, not one taxonomic group.**
- Helmstetter et al. 2016, fetch tool: viviparity and annualism "have each evolved in five independent instances" in the order Cyprinodontiformes (MuSSE reconstruction). The paper ties diversification to viviparity in "Goodeidae and Poeciliidae" and says Anablepidae is another livebearing family. Stochastic mapping leaves the origin within Poeciliidae uncertain, so say "several times", not "five times in the family". (S3) https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4832061
- FishBase Goodeidae page, verbatim: "Distribution: Nevada and west central Mexico. In Goodeinae, fertilization internal; viviparous". (S2) https://www.fishbase.se/summary/FamilySummary.php?Family=Goodeidae
- FishBase Anablepidae page, verbatim: "Four-eyed fishes, onesided livebearers"; "Anal fin turned into tubelike gonopodium."; "Viviparous." (S2) https://www.fishbase.se/summary/FamilySummary.php?Family=Anablepidae
- FishBase Embiotocidae (surfperches), a family in a different order that is not in Cyprinodontiformes, verbatim: "Distribution: North Pacific, coastal, rarely in freshwater."; "Viviparous."; "Intromission by the male is aided by the thickened anterior of anal fin." (S2) https://www.fishbase.se/summary/FamilySummary.php?Family=Embiotocidae . This is the cleanest "not even the same order" example.
- Wording caution: FishBase's Poeciliidae page also says "Gonopodium present or absent." So the gonopodium claim belongs to these four species (or genera), not to every poeciliid.
- Schlupp, fetch tool: the group is "characterized by internal fertilization and ovoviviparity". (S4)

## 2. How it works

- Guppy, FishBase, verbatim: "the anal fin is transformed into a gonopodium for internal fertilization." (S1) https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Poecilia-reticulata.html
- Swordtail, Seriously Fish, verbatim: "male Xiphophorus have a gonopodium, essentially a modified anal-fin, which is used for internal fertilisation of females." (S5) https://www.seriouslyfish.com/species/xiphophorus-hellerii/ . This covers both platies and swordtails, since all are Xiphophorus.
- Schlupp, fetch tool: "Males have a modified anal fin, called the gonopodium, which is used to transfer sperm". (S4)
- Seriously Fish on the Xiphophorus name: "in allusion to the presence of a gonopodium in males of this genus" (platy page, etymology). (S5) https://www.seriouslyfish.com/species/xiphophorus-maculatus/ . FishBase gives the Greek as xiphos (sword) plus pherein (to carry). (S1)
- For molly and guppy, Seriously Fish says the male "possesses a gonopodium" (P. sphenops) and "Males possess a gonopodium and a large, long, sail-like dorsal fin" (P. latipinna). (S5)

## 3. Sperm storage and the interval between broods

- Guppy, FishBase, verbatim: "Females can store sperms for later fertilization and may produce young every four weeks." (S1) https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Poecilia-reticulata.html . FishBase also gives gestation of four to six weeks for the guppy.
- Guppy, Seriously Fish, verbatim: "Excess viable sperm can be preserved in the female's oviduct for a long time. This means that the females are still able to produce young for 6 months or more if no males are present." (S5) https://www.seriouslyfish.com/species/poecilia-reticulata/
- Guppy, Gasparini et al. 2018, fetch tool: "females are able to store sperm arising from a single copulation for up to eight successive reproductive cycles", with "(each cycle approx. one month)". In their Discussion the artificial insemination work is said to have produced "up to five consecutive broods". (S6) https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5882737/ . So for the guppy "up to about eight monthly cycles" is the published ceiling and "up to five broods" is what they saw after insemination with a controlled sperm number.
- Any livebearer in the family, Schlupp, fetch tool: "females store sperm and can use stored sperm for several months to fertilize eggs". (S4)
- Swordtail, Seriously Fish: "Females are also capable of storing viable male sperm for months," (S5) https://www.seriouslyfish.com/species/xiphophorus-hellerii/ . Note: the sentence is cut off mid-clause on the live page itself (the Reproduction text breaks after "'sneakers'," and ends on this comma), so quote it only up to "for months".
- Gestation, FishBase species pages, verbatim: P. sphenops "Produces 20 to 150 young after 28 days gestation."; P. latipinna "After 28 days gestation, this species produces 10 to 100 young."; X. hellerii "Female produces 20 to 200 young after a gestation period of 24 to 30 days."; X. maculatus "After 24 to 30 days gestation, 20 to 80 young are born (Ref 7020)."; X. variatus "After 24 days gestation, up to 100 young are born." (S1)
- Gestation, Seriously Fish, verbatim: P. sphenops "Gestation takes around 8 weeks"; P. latipinna "taking around 2 months"; X. maculatus "Gestation takes around" four to six weeks. (S5) FishBase and Seriously Fish disagree on the molly (4 weeks against 8 weeks).
- Not usable for these four: Ernst et al. 2025 on Poeciliopsis gracilis gives a maximum storage of "5 months" with some females "nearly 7 months" and an interbrood interval of "18.84 ± 7.57 (SD) day". (S9, fetch tool) That is a superfetating species in another genus. Do not transfer the numbers.
- No source I opened gives a measured interbrood interval for molly, platy or swordtail beyond the FishBase gestation lines above. The "every four weeks" figure is stated for the guppy only.

Safe wording: the guppy is South American with Caribbean island populations, the others come from Mexico and Central America (the short-finned molly reaches Colombia, the sailfin molly reaches the United States Atlantic coast).

Genus range, Texas State (a search-result extract of the XGSC pages, not opened as a full page, so unverified): Xiphophorus "live in drainages in eastern Mexico, Guatemala, Belize and Honduras". Not needed in the intro. (S7)

Water, FishBase "Environment" lines, verbatim (S1): guppy "Freshwater; brackish; benthopelagic" with pH 7.0 to 8.0 and dH 9 to 19; short-finned molly "Freshwater; brackish; benthopelagic" with pH 7.5 to 8.2 and dH 11 to 30; sailfin molly "Marine; freshwater; brackish; benthopelagic"; swordtail "Freshwater; brackish; benthopelagic" with pH 7.0 to 8.0 and dH 9 to 19; southern and variable platy "Freshwater; benthopelagic" with the same pH and dH.

Water, Seriously Fish, verbatim (S5):
- Guppy: "This is a hard water fish and while it may manage in soft and/or acidic conditions, long-term maintenance should be in moderately hard or harder water."
- Short-finned molly: "this species must be maintained in moderately hard or harder water, with a basic pH." Also "all the morphs do better in slightly brackish conditions and are much more prone to disease when maintained in freshwater that is not hard and alkaline" and "can live a happy life in full marine conditions." Same page: "Salt is not necessary, as it is the "hard" minerals (calcium, magnesium) that are crucial to the long-term health of this species."
- Sailfin molly: "particularly associated with brackish habitats where algae and other phytoplankton is present in large quantities"; "often referred to as a brackish species but can live perfectly well in freshwater provided it is not soft and/or acidic."
- Platy: "X. maculatus must be maintained in moderately hard or harder water; it will not thrive in soft, acidic water." Variable platy: same wording for X. variatus.
- Swordtail wild habitat: "various types of habitat from sea level to around 1500 m altitude, including fast-flowing, rocky streams, to springs, ditches, ponds, and rivers". (Swordtails come from moving water; platies from slow canals, below.)

Habitat contrast, FishBase, verbatim (S1): swordtail "Adults are found mainly in rapidly flowing streams and rivers, preferring heavily vegetated habitats"; southern platy "warm springs, canals and ditches with typically slow-moving water, silt bottoms and weedy banks"; sailfin molly "Abundant in tidal ditches and brackish canals."

Marine caution (S2): the FishBase Poeciliidae page says poeciliids are "Reported to occur in salt waters in coastal areas", "but only one species lives in true marine waters Poeciliopsis latidens". So write "tolerate brackish and even full marine water", never "live in the sea". The marine tolerance claim for mollies rests on Seriously Fish ("full marine conditions", aquarium experience) and on FishBase listing the sailfin molly as "Marine; freshwater; brackish". No source I opened gives a salinity figure.

## 5. Superfetation and other one-liners

- Superfetation: skipped. Schlupp (S4, fetch tool) defines it ("females carry broods of different stages at any given time") and Ernst et al. (S9) show it in Poeciliopsis gracilis, but no source I opened says guppy, molly, platy or swordtail do it. Leaving it out.
- Sex reversal, FishBase on the swordtail, verbatim: "This species has a tendency to undergo sex reversal (from female to male) under certain environmental conditions". (S1) https://www.fishbase.se/summary/Xiphophorus-hellerii.html . The site's swordtail handling guide already covers this. Good "did you know" candidate, but it is swordtail only, so not for a group intro.
- Lu et al. 2020, fetch tool: the genus "allows production of fertile interspecies hybrids between species that diverged from one another several million years ago". (S8) Useful for platy and swordtail (section 6). The same sentence says the genus is "found in Mexico, Central and South America", but FishBase, Seriously Fish and the XGSC all put the genus in Mexico and northern Central America with no South American member, so do not use that range wording.
- Amazon molly (S4, fetch tool): "Amazon mollies are an all-female, clonal species of fish of hybrid origin" and "The maternal ancestor is the Atlantic molly P. mexicana". A strong molly one-liner, not needed for the intro.

## 6. Hybridization

**Platy and swordtail (both Xiphophorus): yes, well documented.**
- Texas State XGSC history page, fetch tool: Gordon's hybrids "included platyfish (X. maculatus) crossed with swordtails (X. helleri)", cited as "enhanced pigment gene expression in maculatus × helleri hybrids"; Kosswig (1928) found "the melanoma was hereditary". The page also notes platyfish were once their own genus: "Platyfish were assigned to Platypoecilus and swordtails were known as Xiphophorus." (S7) https://imls.txst.edu/history.html . The center was founded in the early 1930s (search extract), which supports "crossed for almost a century" on the site.
- Lu et al. 2020, fetch tool: "fertile interspecies hybrids" are possible in the genus, and a lab scheme crosses "X. maculatus JpA to X. hellerii". (S8) https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7573281 . The Introduction gave hybrid examples from controlled crosses only, so it does not itself show wild hybrids.
- Seriously Fish, verbatim (S5): swordtail "Some of these forms are also the result of artificial hybridisation with other members of the genus, particularly X. maculatus and X. variatus."; platy "Some of these are produced by crossbreeding with X. helleri or X. variatus."; variable platy "If wild fish are obtained care must be taken to avoid crossbreeding with domestic forms."
- Safe wording: platies and swordtails share a genus, have been crossed in laboratories and fish farms since the 1930s, and the offspring are fertile; many aquarium strains carry hybrid ancestry.

**Molly and molly (sailfin x short-finned): yes, readily in the aquarium.**
- Seriously Fish, verbatim (S5): "The 2 species will also interbreed readily in the aquarium and in order to avoid this we suggest keeping only one of them per tank." (P. sphenops page, notes naming P. latipinna.) Same sentence pattern on the P. latipinna page.
- USGS NAS, fetch tool: "Lyretail black mollies are likely a hybrid of P. latipinna and P. sphenops"; "black molly" collectively covers hybrids involving "P. latipinna, P. mexicana, P. sphenops, and P. velifera". (S10)

**Guppy and molly (both Poecilia): not established.**
- The only direct test I found is a 2017 abstract in the Journal of Ornamental Aquatics (S11, fetch tool): "The results indicated that no pregnancy and larva were seen in prospective treatments." Four treatments with three repeats, including male guppy with female molly and the reverse. This is a small, obscure journal, abstract only, authors not shown, one study. It cannot carry a flat claim either way.
- Hobby sites say the offspring are sterile or non-viable, but with no data. Not used (not an acceptable tier, and not opened beyond a search snippet).
- Seriously Fish's guppy page does not discuss hybridization (checked). FishBase does not mention it.
- Do not write that guppies and mollies interbreed, and do not write that they cannot. If the page needs the point, the honest line is: same genus does not mean they cross; mollies cross with each other, guppies are not known to cross with mollies.

## 7. Claims already on the site that these sources contradict or do not support

Checked against content/guides/platy-swordtail-molly-overview.mdx and the guppy, molly, platy and swordtail guides (grep for sperm, brood, gestation, hybrid, salt, range, water terms).

1. **Guppy sperm storage "5 to 10 months" and "up to around 8 broods"** (guppy-cost-guide.mdx lines 53 and 97, guppy-handling-guide.mdx lines 40 and 69). Gasparini et al. (S6) give up to eight monthly cycles, so "up to 8 broods" is supported; Seriously Fish says "6 months or more". Nothing I opened supports a 10 month upper figure, so "5 to 10 months" runs high. "Up to about 8 months" is what the published source supports.
2. **Guppy "A brood arrives every 21 to 30 days"** (guppy-handling-guide.mdx line 66). FishBase says "may produce young every four weeks" with gestation "four to six weeks"; Seriously Fish says gestation takes four to six weeks. 21 days is below every figure I opened.
3. **Molly "Gestation runs roughly 4 to 6 weeks", brood "roughly every 4 to 6 weeks"** (molly-handling-guide.mdx line 67, molly-cost-guide.mdx line 54). FishBase: 28 days for both molly species. Seriously Fish: "around 8 weeks" (short-finned) and "around 2 months" (sailfin). The sources disagree with each other, so "4 to 8 weeks" would be the defensible span.
4. **Swordtail water "pH 7.0 to 8.4"** (swordtail-handling-guide.mdx line 58). FishBase and Seriously Fish both give 7.0 to 8.0. The "10 to 25 dGH" matches Seriously Fish. The 8.4 upper end is not in either source.
5. **Platy "Almost every platy in the trade is a hybrid of two species, bred for color for over a century"** (platy-enrichment-guide.mdx line 74). Seriously Fish says only that "Some of these are produced by crossbreeding". The overview's wording ("hybridization with swordtails") is closer to the source. "Almost every" is stronger than anything I opened.
6. **Overview heading "The Color Range Is Entirely Man-Made" and "None of that color is natural"** (platy-swordtail-molly-overview.mdx line 49 and 51). I found no source that states this. The Texas State history page shows platyfish pigment patterns (the macromelanophore genetics studied since Kosswig 1928) were studied in wild-derived fish, which argues for "most of the range", not "none". Treat as unsupported.
7. **Overview "can tolerate salt concentrations well past normal seawater"** (line 63). My sources only reach "full marine conditions" (Seriously Fish, short-finned molly) and FishBase's "Marine; freshwater; brackish" for the sailfin molly. No salinity figure was opened. The molly health and tank guides cite research of roughly 65 parts per thousand; I did not open that paper, so I cannot confirm or contradict it.
8. **Overview swordtail "reaching 6.2 inches against a male's 5.5 including the sword"** (line 57). FishBase gives 16.0 cm for females, which is 6.3 inches, and 14.0 cm for males (5.5 inches). Minor rounding.
9. Consistent with sources, no change needed: platy and swordtail share Xiphophorus and are crossed; mollies and guppies are both Poecilia; swordtails favor faster water than platies; swordtail gestation about 4 weeks (FishBase 24 to 30 days); mollies need hard, alkaline water and tolerate brackish water; platy native range Mexico and Central America.

## Gaps

- **Guppy storage ceiling:** a search summary reported a follow-up study (Gasparini and Evans, 2018) putting typical maximum storage nearer 4 months with an upper bound of about 6 months, but I did not open it. Not recorded as verified. Only S6 and S5 are used.
- **Interbrood interval for molly, platy, swordtail:** no strong source found. The only interval stated as such is the guppy's "every four weeks" (FishBase).
- **Guppy and molly hybridization:** no peer-reviewed source of adequate standing found; see section 6.
- **Hypersaline tolerance of sailfin molly** (the ~65 ppt figure on the site): paper not opened.
- **Encyclopaedia Britannica and Eschmeyer's Catalog of Fishes:** not tried here; nothing taken from them.
- **Direct PMC download:** PubMed Central blocked direct download and the Royal Society page returned 403, so those four papers were read only through the fetch tool (S3, S4, S6, S8, S9).
- **Hobby-site claims** (sterile guppy and molly offspring, platy color history) were seen in search results only and are not used.

## Draft intro

Livebearer is a common name rather than one scientific group: fish in several unrelated families give birth to free-swimming young, from the splitfins of Mexico to the surfperches of the North Pacific. Guppies, mollies, platies and swordtails all belong to a single family, the Poeciliidae, and the four come from the Americas, the guppy from northeastern South America and the nearby Caribbean islands, the others from Mexico and Central America. Fertilization is internal, and a male uses his gonopodium, an anal fin modified into a mating organ, to transfer sperm. A female can store that sperm for months, so one mating can fertilize several broods and a tank can fill with fry long after the last male is gone. Husbandry references describe these fish as coming from hard, alkaline water, and mollies tolerate brackish water and even full marine conditions.

Sentence trace:
1. Common name, several families, surfperches: S3 (viviparity evolved independently in the order), S2 Goodeidae ("viviparous", "Nevada and west central Mexico") and Embiotocidae ("Viviparous", "North Pacific"). The phrase "free-swimming young" is the site's own plain wording for live birth, matching the overview.
2. One family, the Americas, ranges: S1 (classification line on all six pages, distribution lines), S5 (guppy "northeastern South America and the southern Caribbean"; platy and swordtail Mexico and Central America), S4 (family "widespread from the United States of America to South America").
3. Internal fertilization, gonopodium: S1 (guppy), S5 (swordtail, platy etymology), S4.
4. Sperm stored for months, several broods: S4 ("several months"), S5 (guppy "6 months or more"; swordtail "for months"), S6 (guppy, up to eight cycles). "Several broods" is S6; "long after the last male is gone" restates S5's "if no males are present".
5. Hard, alkaline water; mollies brackish and marine: S5 ("hard water fish", "moderately hard or harder water", "full marine conditions"), S1 (pH 7.0 to 8.0, dH 9 to 19; "Freshwater; brackish").

Notes for editing the draft:
- Sentence 1 leans on S3 for "unrelated families". If it should be stated more cautiously, "several" is the safe count; the paper's five origins refer to the whole order.
- Sentence 5 says "describe" on purpose: the husbandry sources give aquarium water needs and FishBase gives the environment fields, while none of the opened sources reports measured wild water chemistry for all four. It also avoids "live in the sea" (see the Poeciliidae marine caution in section 4).
- Hybridization is left out of the intro on purpose: platy and swordtail are documented, guppy and molly are not (section 6).
