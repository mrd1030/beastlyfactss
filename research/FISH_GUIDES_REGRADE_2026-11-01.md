# Fish Guides Regrade, 2026-11-01

Regrade of the 36 care guides for guppy, molly, platy, swordtail, neon tetra and cardinal tetra (cost, tank-setup, feeding, handling, health-issues, enrichment) after the research fact-check, the restore of cut content, the review pass and the stale-claims sweep. Checked against CLAUDE.md "Writing & Content Rules", docs/RULES.md, each species' research file (Contradictions, Replacement map, "Kept without a source") and research/KEPT_ITEMS_REVIEW.md. Mechanical checks ran across all 36 files: no em or en dashes in any guide, every Sources block holds 4 to 6 links, no retailer or Wikipedia entry survives in a Sources block, no guide carries more than 2 sibling links, every health-guide FunFact is about the animal and not a disease.

"Fix" rows give the exact current text and a replacement. "Gap" rows give the question a new keeper searches for that the guide leaves unanswered; every gap is marked needs research, because none of the answers was looked up for this file. Nothing here was edited in the guides.

### The 10 most important fixes, across all 36

1. **Self-reference pattern, 13 instances.** "our roundup of", "our Guppy tank setup guide covers", "Our Molly handling guide covers", "covered in full in our Guppy enrichment guide" (guppy-cost, guppy-tank-setup, molly-cost, molly-tank-setup, molly-health-issues, platy-cost, platy-tank-setup, platy-health-issues, platy-enrichment, swordtail-cost, swordtail-handling, swordtail-health-issues, swordtail-enrichment body and Sources note). RULES: "The site never talks about itself." Each becomes a sentence that says what the other page settles, with no "our" and no "covers".
2. **Comma splices standing in for a dash, roughly 30 instances.** The recurring shapes: "use a lid, guppies are capable jumpers" (guppy, molly, platy tank guides, FAQ and body), "Guppies are livebearers, females give birth to live..." (every livebearer cost and handling guide), "never refeed something previously thawed and left over, that encourages bacterial growth" (guppy, molly, platy, swordtail feeding). Each gets a period or a colon.
3. **Swordtail cost guide links the care guide hub.** "[platies](/guides/platy/)" in the hybrid paragraph. RULES: never link the care guide hub. Point it at the platy handling or cost guide, or unlink.
4. **The guppy 13-month lab figure is quoted as a lifespan in two other guides.** platy-cost ("well past a guppy's roughly 13-month average", FAQ and body) and neon-tetra-cost ("a guppy is shorter-lived still, with females averaging about 13 months in one lab study"), while the guppy cost guide itself says the count starts with the experiment, not at birth, and that a kept fish may live longer. Either say "no firm guppy figure exists" or drop the comparison.
5. **Swordtail cost guide still describes the setup table as pricing a 20-gallon long.** Body: "a 36 to 48 inch tank costs somewhat more than the table above, since a tank that long runs higher than the 20-gallon long that many starter kits use". The cost sheet now prices "tank-36in-30-40-gallon", and the FAQ says so. Replace with a sentence that says the table already prices the 36-inch tank and a 48-inch tank adds to it.
6. **Cycling time disagrees across the set.** swordtail-tank-setup says "6 to 8 weeks with a fishless cycle" (UF/IFAS FA031); the other five species' tank guides, and the site's aquarium-cycling-guide, say 4 to 6 weeks. One figure across the site, or the swordtail guide says "4 to 8 weeks" and names why.
7. **Salt dose for ich disagrees across the set.** neon-tetra-health-issues: "aquarium salt at about a tablespoon per 5 gallons" (VCA, about 1 g/L). molly-health-issues, molly-tank-setup and guppy-health-issues: "4 to 5 grams per liter (about 15 to 19 grams per gallon) for 7 to 10 days" (UF/IFAS). The two differ four to five fold. Pick one site-wide, and for soft-water tetras say whether the species tolerates it at all (needs research).
8. **The 10-gallon starting tank fights the stated minimum.** guppy-tank-setup FAQ and body: "A base of at least 18 by 12 inches, about 11 gallons, is the minimum" followed by "A 10-gallon tank is a solid starting point" (a standard 10 is 20 by 10, under both the width and the volume), and guppy-cost, guppy-handling and guppy-enrichment all recommend a 10. platy-tank-setup title "A Real 10 Gallons" and intro against its own "a standard 10 gallon is 20 by 10, so it does not meet that" 24 by 12 footprint. Owner call: either the 10 is a stated compromise ("by volume it fits five; the footprint says 20 long") in every guide, or the titles and recommendations move to the 20 long.
9. **FAQ answers over three sentences, 14 instances.** guppy-cost (lifespan), guppy-tank-setup (cycling), guppy-feeding (how often), guppy-health-issues (ich, velvet), molly-cost (forgot to plan), molly-tank-setup (tank size, salt), molly-feeding (plant food, fry), molly-handling (guppy comparison), molly-health-issues (marine salt), platy-feeding (overeat), platy-handling (do you handle), platy-health-issues (tumors, five sentences), neon-tetra-health-issues (contagious, five sentences; ich, four). RULES: 1 to 3 sentences.
10. **Molly health guide contradicts itself on shimmy.** Intro and H2: "the one condition specific to this species"; body: "livebearers such as mollies, platies, swordtails, and guppies can all show it"; closing: shimmy is "more distinctly a molly concern". Say "the condition most associated with this species" in the intro and heading and drop the "specific to" claim.

Close behind: the guppy feeding guide's garbled schedule ("Juveniles (roughly 1 to 3 to 4 months)", a fourth "Fry and juveniles" bullet duplicating the first, "every 2 to 3 hours" egg-layer advice, "well below about 72°F for more than two days" against a 63°F source floor); the guppy handling guide's "three weeks" sexing line stated twice against its own "as soon as the fins differ" and the research's 3 to 7 weeks; the swordtail feeding FunFact calling the molly's diet "mostly algae and plant material" after the molly feeding guide was corrected to "the short-finned molly is an omnivore"; the neon health guide's body never naming Pleistophora hyphessobryconis; the cardinal cost guide's "Vet Costs: Not applicable" against its health guide's "a fish vet is the first choice"; and the orphan one-line paragraph "Uneaten food is a common and avoidable driver..." at the end of the molly, platy and swordtail health guides.

### The 10 most important completeness gaps, across all 36

Every one is "needs research". Where a species guide already answers the question, it is named so the fix can be a copy.

1. **How long can the fish go without food, and what to do for a vacation.** None of the six feeding guides answers it. The guppy guide says "There's no agreed number" and stops. The most-searched feeding question after "how often".
2. **Signs of overfeeding and underfeeding.** Only the platy guide (stringy waste, hedged) and the neon guide ("hang back while others eat") give a visible tell. Guppy, molly, swordtail, cardinal feeding guides give the two-minute rule and nothing to look at on the fish.
3. **A practical portion.** "3% of body weight" and "cleared in two minutes" are the only measures. No guide translates that to a pinch, a number of micro-pellets per fish, or a photo-sized description for a group of six.
4. **A water change routine in the tank setup guides.** Only cardinal-tetra-tank-setup gives amount and frequency (25 to 30% weekly, nitrate thresholds). The molly health guide has it; the guppy, molly, platy, swordtail and neon tank guides do not.
5. **How many fish fit the recommended tank.** The livebearer tank guides give a minimum tank and no stocking number for it (platy: "about five"). Neon and cardinal say "ten" but not what else fits a 20 long beside them.
6. **Acclimation and netting in the livebearer handling guides.** Swordtail, neon and cardinal handling guides walk through float, drip and net. Guppy, molly and platy handling guides have no acclimation, no netting technique and no transport advice, though "handling" is the title.
7. **Why neons and cardinals look pale at night or in the morning.** Normal nocturnal fading is one of the most-searched tetra health worries; neither health guide says it is normal, and both describe pale color as an NTD sign without that caveat.
8. **Common conditions missing from the health lists.** Guppy: wasting and bent spine (mycobacteria), Tetrahymena ("guppy disease"), fungus. Molly, platy, swordtail: fungus, columnaris, internal parasites (white stringy feces). Cardinal: the columnaris NTD lookalike section the neon guide has. Neon: fin rot and swim bladder are one line each.
9. **Heater wattage and a lid.** Only the guppy tank guide sizes a heater (3 to 5 W per gallon). Neon and cardinal tank guides say nothing about a lid or evaporation.
10. **Hardness and tap-water remineralizing or softening, by species.** Cardinal tank guide gives pH and no GH or KH target (the handling guide has 1 to 12 dGH). Neon gives dGH but only a FunFact on RO blending; cardinal has no RO or blending advice at all. For the livebearers, "crushed coral or a buffering product" is the whole answer, with no dose or test routine.

Also absent: fry rearing time to a sellable or sexable size (guppy, molly, swordtail), maturity age for swordtails (research has 3 to 4 months, 8 to 12 months and 6 months to a year from three sources, none stated), breeding basics for the cardinal (neon has a section), quarantine or hospital tank cost in every cost guide, and the cost of RO water or remineralizer for the two tetras.
