# Kept Items Review: Would a Reader Welcome It?

Written 2026-11-01. Every item listed under "Kept without a source (owner review)" in the six research files (GUPPY, MOLLY, NEON_TETRA, CARDINAL_TETRA, PLATY, SWORDTAIL) was read in its guide, in context, and given one verdict:

- WELCOME: useful keeping advice or a fact a keeper would want; keep as is.
- WELCOME, WORDING: keep the point, fix the wording (clumsy, repeats something nearby, or sits awkwardly beside the corrected sentence).
- CONFLICT: now disagrees with a corrected figure in the same guide or in the species' other guides; the reason says which wins per the research file.
- QUESTIONABLE: a specific claim that reads as shaky even without a source (exact percentage, disease mechanism, precise timing, claim about "all sources") or filler. No cut is recommended outright; the owner decides.

Items that appear in several places (FAQ and body, or across guides) are one row unless the places need different verdicts.

### The 10 items to look at first (conflicts first)

1. **Guppy lifespan.** guppy-cost-guide.mdx FAQ and body: "On average 2 to 3 years, and 4 or 5 with excellent care" sits directly after the corrected lab figure (about 13 months average, 27 months longest). The same 2 to 3 year figure is repeated as fact in neon-tetra-cost-guide.mdx ("A guppy averages 2 to 3 years") and leaned on in platy-cost-guide.mdx ("close to a guppy's lifespan"). GUPPY_RESEARCH: no opened source supports 2 to 3; the lab figure is the only sourced one.
2. **Swordtail lifespan.** swordtail-cost-guide.mdx FAQ and body: "3 to 5 years on average... the same range as a molly or platy". The molly cost guide now says 2 to 5 and the platy cost guide 2 to 4, so "same range" is wrong on the site's own pages. SWORDTAIL_RESEARCH: only a Questionable AnAge record (5 years) exists.
3. **Platy's molly comparison.** platy-cost-guide.mdx FAQ and body: "a molly's, which commonly reaches 3 to 5 years" against the molly cost guide's corrected "About 2 to 5 years". MOLLY_RESEARCH: 2 to 5 (PetMD) wins.
4. **Swordtail 20-gallon cluster.** swordtail-cost-guide.mdx: "Retail listings give a 20-gallon minimum", the excerpt and opener "outgrows a 20-gallon starting point", and the cost-sheet table that still prices a 20-gallon long (src/lib/data/costSheets/fish.js); swordtail-tank-setup-guide.mdx: the 20 long as "a workable stopgap for a pair". All sit beside the corrected 29-gallon, 48-inch floor. SWORDTAIL_RESEARCH: Seriously Fish's 29 gallons wins for every form.
5. **"Females lack the sword entirely."** swordtail-handling-guide.mdx FAQ and body. The same guide's sexing section describes fish that look female for months and then grow a sword, and the enrichment guide's FAQ and FunFact say some females are reported to develop one. "Lack the sword, with rare exceptions" fits all three.
6. **Swordtail males against the other livebearers.** swordtail-handling-guide.mdx: "Mollies and guppies get by on a looser one-to-two-or-three ratio" (the swordtail's own floor is the same two or three); swordtail-enrichment-guide.mdx FAQ: more aggressive to each other "which is not true of the other common livebearers". The molly guides describe male sailfin mollies as aggressive to one another and say an all-male molly group needs space and numbers. No source compares the species.
7. **Molly "typical brood 20 to 60".** molly-cost-guide.mdx body, molly-handling-guide.mdx FAQ and FunFact: the restored "typical 20 to 60" sits beside the corrected "roughly 20 to 35 fry in lab conditions", so the reader gets two typicals. The handling guide adds "noticeably larger broods than guppies" (guppy guide: 20 to 40), and platy-handling-guide.mdx ranks platy broods between the two. MOLLY_RESEARCH: lab means 21 to 35 win; the 10 to 100+ range is sourced and can stay; none of the rankings has a source.
8. **Guppy brood timing.** guppy-handling-guide.mdx: "A brood arrives every 21 to 30 days, about 25 on average at 75 to 81°F, and a female can release up to about 50" immediately after the corrected "3 to 6 weeks... 20 to 40 young, with large broods reaching 50 or more". GUPPY_RESEARCH: 3 to 6 weeks is the safe wording; "about 25 on average" has no source.
9. **Guppy dropsy outlook.** guppy-health-issues-guide.mdx body and FAQ: "advanced internal or organ failure... hardest condition on this list... poor outlook" directly after the corrected "the outlook depends on what is behind the swelling" (Merck: a bacterial case may respond to antibiotics). Merck wins; the pinecone-stage hedge can stay.
10. **Cardinal dry-season stranding.** cardinal-tetra-cost-guide.mdx Lifespan: the restored stranding sentence sits in the same paragraph as Chao's "wild fish probably die after spawning" and offers a different cause. CARDINAL_TETRA_RESEARCH: Chao wins (spawning is February to April, rising water, not the dry season).

Other conflicts, lower down the tables: guppy Koi priced in two tiers in one paragraph; guppy "fertility falls after age 2" beside a 13-month average life; molly shimmy "raise toward 76 to 80°F" against the sailfin's corrected 79°F ceiling; swordtail "runs a stronger filter" against the tank guide's corrected "doesn't need especially strong filtration"; platy "about as forgiving as community fish get" against the enrichment guide's corrected "mass-bred stock is often weak".

Two patterns run through the tables. First, the restore pass often put the old sentence straight after the corrected sentence that replaced it, so many guides now say the same thing twice in a row (the guppy handling ratio line, the guppy tank FunFact, the molly vet paragraph, the molly vegetable sentence are the clearest). Second, a family of cross-species comparisons (lifespan, brood size, male aggression, activity, plant share, salt tolerance "better than most freshwater fish") has no source anywhere and is where most of the conflicts come from.
