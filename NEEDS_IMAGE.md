## 2026-08-10 run: 5 facts drafted, awaiting images

Ready for the site owner to generate photos and add to facts.js.

1. **Cassowary** (Birds) - The Dagger Toe. Southern cassowaries carry a dagger-like claw on their inner toe that can grow over 4 inches long, sharp enough to inflict severe, potentially lethal wounds if the bird feels cornered. Despite the fearsome reputation, they are shy forest dwelling fruit-eaters that would much rather vanish into the undergrowth than confront a person. Visual hook: close-up of a cassowary's foot showing the long dagger-like inner claw against leaf litter.
2. **Border Collie** (Dogs & Cats) - A Thousand-Word Vocabulary. A border collie named Chaser learned the names of 1,022 different toys over three years of patient training and could pick a specific one out of a huge pile just by hearing its name. She could even work out the name of a brand new toy by noticing it was the only object in the group she didn't already have a name for. Visual hook: a border collie sitting in a semicircle of dozens of different toys, nose pointed at one specific toy.
3. **Pronghorn** (Mammals) - Built to Outrun a Ghost. The pronghorn can run close to 55 miles per hour, making it the second fastest land animal on Earth after the cheetah. Scientists believe it evolved that speed to outrun the American cheetah, a predator that has been extinct for thousands of years, so it is still built to escape a chase that no longer happens. Visual hook: a pronghorn at full sprint across open grassland, legs blurred with speed.
4. **Lionfish** (Ocean) - Eighteen Venomous Spines. Lionfish carry eighteen venomous spines along their fins, delivering a sting that is intensely painful but rarely fatal to humans. Native to the Indo-Pacific, they were first spotted off Florida in 1985, likely released from home aquariums, and have since become one of the most damaging invasive species in the Atlantic and Caribbean, preying on more than 70 different reef species. Visual hook: a lionfish's fanned, striped venomous spines lit up against a dark reef background.
5. **Coconut Crab** (Weird & Wonderful) - Grip Like a Lion's Bite. The coconut crab is the largest land dwelling arthropod alive, and its claws can pinch with a force that rivals a lion's bite. Researchers measured the biggest crabs pinching with over 3,000 newtons of force, easily enough to crack open a coconut shell. Visual hook: a coconut crab's oversized claw gripping a whole coconut on a sandy beach.
