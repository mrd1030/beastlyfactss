# 10 Surprising Axolotl Facts

Axolotls look like smiling cartoon characters with their feathery gills and permanent grin, but they are some of the most biologically impressive animals alive. Here are ten facts that show why they’re basically real-life superheroes in tiny bodies.

1. They can regenerate almost any body part. Axolotls can regrow limbs, tails, spinal cords, and even parts of their brain and heart without scarring. This ability makes them incredibly valuable for scientific research.

2. They stay in their larval form for life. Most axolotls never fully metamorphose. They keep their external gills and cute “baby” face even as adults. This is called neoteny and is one of their most unique traits.

3. Their gills are external and feathery. The fluffy structures on the sides of their heads are gills used for breathing underwater. They can also absorb oxygen through their skin when water conditions are good.

4. They can be triggered to “grow up.” Adding a small amount of iodine to their water can cause an axolotl to metamorphose into a land form with lungs and no external gills. It rarely happens naturally.

5. Their smile is permanent. That wide, friendly grin isn’t an expression of happiness. It’s simply the natural shape of their mouth. They always look cheerful even when they’re just floating around.

6. They are used heavily in medical research. Because of their incredible regeneration abilities, axolotls are studied worldwide to understand healing, organ regrowth, and genetic processes that could one day help humans. That work had to wait on an enormous genome. When it was fully sequenced in 2018, the axolotl genome came to 32 billion base pairs, more than ten times the size of the human genome and the largest genome decoded at the time.

7. They eat by suction. Axolotls don’t chew their food. They open their mouths and suck prey in like a vacuum. Worms and small insects disappear in one quick gulp.

8. They can regrow their spinal cord. If damaged, an axolotl’s spinal cord can repair and reconnect itself. This is another reason they are so important for studying nervous system recovery.

9. Wild axolotls live in a very small area. They are native only to a few lakes near Mexico City, mainly Lake Xochimilco. Pollution and habitat loss have made them critically endangered in the wild. Surveys put the population at about 6,000 axolotls per square kilometer in 1998 and just 36 by 2014, a 99 percent drop. Farm pesticides and introduced carp and tilapia, which eat axolotls and compete for food, drove most of it.

10. They come in many colors in captivity. While wild axolotls are usually dark, captive ones can be white, pink, or golden. The leucistic variety with pink gills is especially popular. A leucistic axolotl does not glow on its own. The ones that glow green under blue or UV light are GFP axolotls, genetically modified lines first bred in labs for regeneration research. They carry green fluorescent protein, a gene borrowed from the crystal jelly, a shallow-water cousin of the [glowing jellies of the deep sea](/blog/weirdest-creatures-deep-sea-facts-that-will-blow-your-mind/) and the animal that gave biology one of its most useful tools.

</FunFact>
