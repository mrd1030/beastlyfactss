# 10 Surprising Rabbit Facts

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Rabbits might look like simple fluffy pets, but they have some surprising abilities and behaviors that catch even experienced owners off guard. Here are ten facts that show just how interesting these little animals really are.

1. Their teeth never stop growing. Rabbits need constant chewing on <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4h9WW2u" product="Timothy hay (Oxbow Western Timothy Hay, 90 oz)">hay</AffiliateLink> and safe wood to keep their teeth at the right length. Without enough to gnaw on, their teeth can overgrow and cause serious health issues. Rabbit teeth grow about 1 centimeter a month, and an incisor with nothing opposite to wear against can grow as much as 1 millimeter a day. Wild rabbits keep up by chewing grass, roots and ground cover all day.

2. They can see almost completely around them. With eyes on the sides of their head, rabbits have nearly 360-degree vision. This helps them spot predators quickly, though they do have a small blind spot right in front of their face.

3. A “binky” is their happy dance. When a rabbit suddenly jumps, twists, and kicks its feet in the air, it’s a clear sign of joy and excitement. You’ll usually see it when they feel safe and playful.

4. They can make a purring-like sound. When rabbits are relaxed and content, they often grind their teeth softly or make little clucking noises. It’s their version of purring and usually happens during gentle petting.

5. They are very sensitive to heat. Rabbits can overheat easily because they don’t sweat much. They rely on their ears to release heat, so they need shade, cool surfaces, and frozen water bottles on warm days. For a rabbit, the risk of [heat stress](/blog/small-mammal-temperature-heat-stress-guide/) climbs once a room passes about 80°F, a temperature plenty of homes reach in summer.

6. Their digestive system is delicate. Stress, diet changes, or not eating enough can quickly lead to a dangerous condition called GI stasis. This is why consistent routines and plenty of hay are so important. Part of that system runs through the rabbit twice. The cecum produces soft, mucus-coated droppings called cecotropes, and the rabbit eats them straight from the anus, which is why you rarely see them. They carry microbial protein, B vitamins and fatty acids the first pass could not absorb, with protein around 28 to 30 percent.

7. Their ears can move independently. Rabbits can swivel each ear separately to listen in two different directions at the same time. It looks funny but is an excellent survival adaptation.

8. Many rabbits can be litter trained. With patience and the right setup, a lot of rabbits learn to use a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3SPggIn" product="Litter box">litter box</AffiliateLink> reliably. This is one reason more people are successfully keeping them as indoor pets.

9. Baby rabbits are born completely helpless. Unlike hares, rabbit kits are born blind, deaf, and without fur. They stay hidden in a nest while their mother visits only briefly a couple of times a day to feed them.

10. They thump their feet as a warning. When a rabbit stomps its back foot hard, it’s usually signaling danger or that something startled them. In the wild this alerts the whole group to freeze or run.

<FunFact>
  Rabbits sometimes sleep with their eyes slightly open. It can look a little strange the first time you notice it, but it is completely normal behavior for them.
</FunFact>
