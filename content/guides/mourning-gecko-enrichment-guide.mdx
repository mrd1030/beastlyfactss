# Mourning Gecko Enrichment: The Only One You Should Keep in Groups

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Every other gecko guide on this site has a paragraph about whether the animals can be housed together. This species starts from a colony.

<KeyTakeaway>
Mourning geckos are parthenogenetic. There are no males, reproduction happens without them, and they live in colonies. That makes the social question, which is contested for almost every other reptile on this site, already settled here, and it makes group size an enrichment decision rather than a risk.
</KeyTakeaway>

For everything else about them, see the [Mourning Gecko care guide](/guides/mourning-gecko/).

## What the Research Says

Nothing on *Lepidodactylus lugubris* enrichment specifically. Two borrowed threads apply.

**Social housing.** A 2024 study on tokay geckos, covered in our [Tokay Gecko enrichment guide](/blog/tokay-gecko-enrichment-guide/), measured enclosure use, basking and hiding as animals moved from single to pair housing and concluded **pair housing improves tokay welfare**, with socially deprived lizards more likely to move and hide before feeding. Different genus, much larger animal, so it is a supporting signal, not proof.

**Enrichment type.** The leopard gecko work ranked thermal and feeding enrichment highest and visual enrichment lowest, which is the general steer for geckos.

<FunFact>
Parthenogenesis is why this species spread across the Pacific so successfully. A single animal arriving somewhere can found an entire population, which is also why one gecko in your enclosure quietly becomes several.
</FunFact>

## Colony Housing

The default, never the experiment. A group gives you the social behavior that makes this species interesting to watch, and it is how they live.

Provide enough vertical surface and cover that individuals can space themselves out. Crowding a colony into a small enclosure is the failure mode, not the grouping itself.

## Vertical Space, Densely Planted

Small arboreal geckos that live on leaves and vertical surfaces. A tall enclosure gives them far more usable area than a wide one, and an <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4vlb6Bp" product="18x18x24 glass terrarium">18x18x24 terrarium</AffiliateLink> is a sensible footprint for a small colony.

Fill it. <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4grZYPq" product="Live terrarium plants">Live planting</AffiliateLink> does three jobs at once here: usable surface, visual cover so animals can avoid each other, and humidity. Empty height is wasted height. Our [tank setup guide](/blog/mourning-gecko-tank-setup-guide/) covers dimensions and humidity.

## Both Halves of the Diet

A prepared <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00KO450UC?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Pangea Watermelon Complete Gecko Diet, 8 oz">fruit-based gecko diet</AffiliateLink> in shallow dishes placed at several heights, so feeding is distributed instead of a scrum at one point. Other flavors and sizes are available.

Then the half a dish cannot deliver: small live insects, fruit flies or micro crickets, released into the planting so they have to be hunted. Feeding enrichment ranked second in the closest gecko study, and for a tiny insectivore the chase is most of it. Our [feeding guide](/blog/mourning-gecko-feeding-guide/) covers proportions.

## Cover at Every Level

Cork slabs run vertically, leaf litter, and planting dense enough that a gecko can be out of sight anywhere in the enclosure and not only at the bottom. In a colony this matters more, because it lets individuals choose distance from each other.

## Priority Order

1. A colony over a single animal
2. Vertical space, densely planted
3. Cover available at every height
4. Live prey to hunt, alongside a prepared diet
5. Feeding stations distributed, not pooled
6. A low UVB gradient
7. Novelty, last

## What Not to Do

Do not crowd a colony into a small enclosure and conclude group housing is stressful, since space and cover are what make grouping work. Do not build height and leave it empty. Do not rely on a dish alone, because that removes the hunting half of the behavior. Do not handle much, since these are small, fast and easily injured, covered in our [handling guide](/blog/mourning-gecko-handling-guide/). And do not be surprised by eggs, because parthenogenesis means a colony grows on its own.
