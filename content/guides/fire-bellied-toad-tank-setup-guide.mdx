# Fire-Bellied Toad Enclosure Setup: Half Fish Tank, Half Terrarium

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Fire-bellied toads need a genuinely different kind of setup than most amphibians on this site, real aquatic filtration matters here in a way it doesn't for a more purely terrestrial species. For the full care picture, see our full [Fire-Bellied Toad care guide](/guides/fire-bellied-toad/), or our [cost guide](/blog/fire-bellied-toad-cost-guide/) for what all of this runs.

## Enclosure Size

A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4pjiyeT" product="10 gallon tank">10 gallon tank</AffiliateLink> works for one to three toads, this is a social species that can be kept in a small group of its own kind. Larger is better if you're keeping more. A tightly fitting screen lid is essential, fire-bellied toads are strong jumpers and genuine escape artists.

## The Semi-Aquatic Layout

Build roughly half land, half water, a true paludarium setup, or alternatively an all-water base with floating cork bark or flat rocks providing land access points. Water should be deep enough for your toads to genuinely swim, several inches, with easy access in and out. This semi-aquatic middle ground is worth comparing against a fully aquatic species like the [axolotl](/blog/axolotl-tank-setup-guide/), which never leaves the water and needs an entirely submerged, chilled tank rather than a part-land setup.

## Temperature

65 to 75°F, some sources allow up to about 78°F. Most rooms at normal temperature need no supplemental heat at all. If you do need a heat source because your space runs cold, a low-wattage bulb or a thermostat-controlled under-tank heater works, avoid overheating this species entirely.

## Humidity

High, and largely self-maintaining given the water section already built into the enclosure. Light misting helps if needed, but the aquatic portion does most of the work here.

<FunFact>
Fire-bellied toads are strong jumpers and genuine escape artists, exactly why a tightly fitting screen lid matters as much here as the water section itself.
</FunFact>

## Substrate

<AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CY23K32S?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="VCEPJH Loose Coconut Fiber Substrate, 2 Pack">Coconut fiber</AffiliateLink> or a naturalistic soil blend for the land portion. For the water section, use no substrate at all, or large smooth river rock or slate, never small gravel, which can be swallowed and cause impaction. Skip mealworms and other hard-shelled feeders too, for the same reason.

## Water Quality: The Part That Actually Matters Most

This is genuinely the defining requirement for this species, more important than temperature, substrate, or lighting combined. Always dechlorinate tap water with a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3Rg5gTT" product="Water conditioner">reptile-safe conditioner</AffiliateLink> before it goes in the tank. Run a small <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3T8xyAd" product="Sponge filter">filter</AffiliateLink> in the water section, or commit to frequent partial water changes, up to half the water weekly if you're not filtering, to keep ammonia under control. Poor water quality is directly responsible for the red leg syndrome covered in our [health issues guide](/blog/fire-bellied-toad-health-issues-guide/), treating this water section with the same care you'd give a fish tank is really the whole game here.

## Lighting

Low-level UVB offers some benefit though isn't strictly required for this species. If used, a low-output bulb in the 5 to 7% range positioned 8 to 13 inches above the substrate works well.

## The Non-Obvious Requirement: Never House With Other Species

This species' skin toxins, covered in our [handling guide](/blog/fire-bellied-toad-handling-guide/), mean fire-bellied toads should only ever be housed with their own kind, never mixed with other amphibian or reptile species in a shared enclosure.
