# Fire-Bellied Toad Enclosure Setup: Half Fish Tank, Half Terrarium

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Fire-bellied toads need a different kind of setup than most amphibians, real aquatic filtration matters here in a way it doesn't for a more purely terrestrial species.

<Figure src="/assets/images/fire-bellied-toad-tank-setup-guide-1.jpg" alt="Labeled diagram of a fire-bellied toad paludarium showing the screen lid, land and water sections, sponge filter, and smooth river rock" caption="Half fish tank, half terrarium, water quality matters more here than temperature, substrate, or lighting combined." shareable />

## Enclosure Size

A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4pjiyeT" product="10 gallon tank">10 gallon tank</AffiliateLink> works for one to three toads, this is a social species that can be kept in a small group of its own kind. Larger is better if you're keeping more. A tightly fitting screen lid is essential, fire-bellied toads are strong jumpers and genuine escape artists.

## The Semi-Aquatic Layout

Build roughly half land, half water, a true paludarium setup, or alternatively an all-water base with floating cork bark or flat rocks providing land access points. Water should be deep enough for your toads to swim, sloping gradually to about 3 inches, deeper if you want, with easy access in and out. PetMD's sheet puts the water at 1 to 2 inches, which is the floor for a land-heavy layout; this is a toad that swims, so give it the 3. This semi-aquatic middle ground is worth comparing against a fully aquatic species like the [axolotl](/blog/axolotl-tank-setup-guide/), which never leaves the water and needs an entirely submerged, chilled tank rather than a part-land setup.

## Temperature

75 to 78°F through the day, dropping at night. Treat 65°F as the floor and 82°F as the hard ceiling. Most rooms at normal temperature need no supplemental heat at all. If you do need a heat source because your space runs cold, a low-wattage bulb or a thermostat-controlled under-tank heater works, avoid overheating this species entirely.

## Humidity

High, and largely self-maintaining given the water section already built into the enclosure. Light misting helps if needed, but the aquatic portion does most of the work here.

<FunFact>
This toad needs no heater in most rooms. Its ceiling is 82°F, so a warm summer room is closer to a danger for it than a cool one.
</FunFact>

## Substrate

<AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CY23K32S?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="VCEPJH Loose Coconut Fiber Substrate, 2 Pack">Coconut fiber</AffiliateLink> or a naturalistic soil blend for the land portion. For the water section, use no substrate at all, or large smooth river rock or slate, never small gravel, which can be swallowed and cause impaction. Skip mealworms and other hard-shelled feeders too, for the same reason: [impaction](/blog/fire-bellied-toad-health-issues-guide/) is what both bans are about.

## Diet Basics

Live crickets make up the majority of the diet, with waxworms, earthworms, blackworms, small silkworms, dubia roach nymphs and black soldier fly larvae substituted in every few feedings for variety. Skip mealworms and the other hard-shelled feeders for the impaction reason above.

Two to six food items per toad every two to three days works well for an adult, with PetMD's once or twice a week the lean end of that same range, and juveniles are fed daily in smaller quantities. A practical ceiling either way is what the toad clears in about 15 minutes. Gut-load feeders for at least 24 hours before they go in, then dust them with a calcium and vitamin D supplement at every feeding session, adding a multivitamin once or twice a week.

## Water Quality: The Part That Matters Most

This is the defining requirement for this species, more important than temperature, substrate, or lighting combined. Always dechlorinate tap water with a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3Rg5gTT" product="Water conditioner">reptile-safe conditioner</AffiliateLink> before it goes in the tank. Run a small <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3T8xyAd" product="Sponge filter">filter</AffiliateLink> in the water section, or commit to frequent partial water changes, up to half the water weekly if you're not filtering, to keep ammonia under control. Poor water quality is directly responsible for red leg syndrome, and treating this water section with the same care you'd give a fish tank is really the whole game here.

## Lighting

Low-level UVB offers some benefit though isn't strictly required for this species. If used, a low-output bulb in the 5 to 7% range positioned 8 to 13 inches above the substrate works well.

## The Non-Obvious Requirement: Never House With Other Species

This species' skin toxins mean fire-bellied toads should only ever be housed with their own kind, never mixed with other amphibian or reptile species in a shared enclosure.
