# Is It Legal to Own a Quaker Parakeet? And How to Handle One

Quaker parakeets are intelligent, social, genuinely gifted talkers, and also the one bird on this site where a real legal check needs to happen before anything else. For the full care picture, see our complete [Quaker Parakeet care guide](/guides/quaker-parakeet/).

## The legal picture, state by state

Quakers are restricted or banned in a meaningful number of US states, and the reasoning is concrete: escaped birds form invasive feral colonies that damage crops and build large stick nests on power infrastructure, a real, documented problem, nests were linked to over 1,000 power outages in Florida in a single year, costing hundreds of thousands of dollars.

- **Commonly cited outright bans**: California, Georgia, Hawaii, Kansas, Kentucky, Maine, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, Tennessee, and Wyoming.
- **Ban with exceptions**: Colorado (only if owned before 1990), Connecticut (own but don't breed or sell), Wisconsin (special permit required).
- **Legal with conditions**: New Jersey (permit), Virginia (own but don't breed or sell), New York (identification banding/registration), Ohio (flight feathers must be clipped).

Sources genuinely disagree on the exact count, some put outright bans around 10 states, others frame it differently. **This list is a starting point, not a substitute for checking directly with your state's wildlife or agriculture agency before buying**, laws in this area do change, and Lafeber notes that birds have been confiscated and euthanized in states where they're illegal to own.

## Handling and temperament

Quakers are playful, clever, and often described as genuine "clowns," typically bonding strongly to one person while remaining reasonable with the rest of the household. They're assertive, not shy, and will clearly signal displeasure, sometimes with a bite, if they feel threatened. Expect real territoriality around the cage, especially during spring breeding season, worth supervising closely around children during that window.

## An unusually good talker for the price

This is a genuine standout trait worth knowing. A Quaker's talking ability and intelligence is often compared favorably to birds many times more expensive, [African grey](/blog/african-grey-parrot-handling-guide/) and Amazon parrots among them. Hand-fed Quakers can build large vocabularies, and some start talking before they're even fully weaned.

## The nest-building instinct, unlike any other parrot

Here's a genuinely unusual behavioral fact. The Quaker is the only parrot species that builds its own nest rather than nesting in a tree cavity, and in the wild, these twig-woven nests can become massive multi-chambered structures shared by many pairs. Pet Quakers retain this instinct fully, they'll shred and weave any suitable material. **Reserve shredding toys for play areas rather than the cage itself**, heavy nest-style shredding material inside the cage can trigger hormonal, territorial behavior you don't want to encourage.

<FunFact>
Wild Quaker colonies build genuine "apartment complexes" out of sticks, with each breeding pair maintaining its own chamber inside a shared structure that can house dozens of pairs. It's the only nest-building behavior of its kind among parrots, and a caged pet Quaker will attempt the exact same thing with whatever material you leave within reach.
</FunFact>

## First-timer mistakes

- Buying before checking state legality.
- Providing a permanent nest box "because they seem to want one," this reliably triggers unwanted hormonal aggression.
- Under-socializing a bird this intelligent, boredom shows up fast as plucking and screaming, covered in the [health issues guide](/blog/quaker-parakeet-health-issues-guide/).
