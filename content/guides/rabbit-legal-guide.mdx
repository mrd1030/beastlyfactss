# Is It Legal to Own a Rabbit? The Two States Worth Reading

Every domestic rabbit alive, from a four pound Netherland Dwarf to a twenty pound Flemish Giant, is the same species: Oryctolagus cuniculus, the European rabbit. That fact is uncontroversial to a biologist and quietly awkward in law, because two states wrote rules naming that species without saying what they meant to do about the ones living in houses.

## Minnesota Prohibits the Species Your Rabbit Belongs To

Minnesota designates the European rabbit, Oryctolagus cuniculus, a prohibited invasive species by rule, and section 84D.05 then bars possessing, importing, purchasing, selling, transporting or introducing one except under a permit from the commissioner. The DNR restates it on its own invasive species page, listing the European rabbit among the prohibited mammals and describing possession as a misdemeanour.

Nothing in the designation says wild, feral, or non-domesticated. There is no strain qualifier of the kind that would separate a pet from a population loose on a headland.

Two things are worth saying plainly here, and they are different things. The first is what the rule says, which is the above. The second is what the state does, which is not something a rule text can tell you, and there is no evidence in the regulation itself of house rabbits being pursued. Those are separate questions, and a keeper who wants certainty should ask the DNR rather than infer from the silence in either direction.

## Nevada's Row Argues With Itself

Nevada's NAC 503.110(1)(d)(5) has two columns and they do not agree. The scientific classification column names Oryctolagus cuniculus. The common name column says Wild European Rabbit.

If the binomial governs, every pet rabbit in Nevada is inside the rule. If the common name governs, none of them are. The section never says whether domesticated races are carved out, and that omission is conspicuous because the same body of rules does carve them out elsewhere: the chinchilla, mink, rat, mouse, turkey and duck entries all carry domesticated races wording. The rabbit entry does not.

That looks less like a decision about rabbits and more like a row nobody revisited.

## The Rules That Reach Rabbit Keepers

For almost everyone, none of the above will ever matter, and the restrictions that do bite are the ones this map does not cover.

Municipal codes cap animal numbers. Zoning sometimes classes rabbits as livestock rather than pets, which changes what is allowed on a residential lot. Leases and HOA terms routinely name rabbits. A keeper in one of the 50 unrestricted jurisdictions who hits a problem has almost always hit a city ordinance or a tenancy clause rather than a state wildlife rule.

Rabbits also live eight to twelve years, which is longer than most buyers expect and longer than many tenancies last. That gap does more to end rabbit ownership than any statute. The [guinea pig's legal position](/blog/guinea-pig-legal-guide/) is almost identical for the same reason: a domesticated animal that no state saw a need to write about.
