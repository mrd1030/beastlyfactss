# Bristlenose Pleco Enrichment: Wood Is Not Optional, For Murkier Reasons Than You Think

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Every care sheet says plecos eat wood to digest it. The honest version is that the requirement is solid and the explanation is shakier.

<KeyTakeaway>
Bristlenose plecos kept without driftwood develop digestive problems and lose color, and providing it is treated as a requirement across the hobby. What is less certain is the usual explanation: research on other wood-rasping loricariid catfish, close relatives of bristlenose in the same family, suggests **they are not true wood digesters**, and that any benefit likely comes from **microbes colonizing the wood's surface** rather than from the wood itself or from fermentation inside the gut.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Research Settles, and What It Does Not

Two things, pointing in slightly different directions, and both worth having.

**The requirement holds.** Bristlenose plecos rasp submerged wood constantly, and keepers consistently report **digestive problems and faded color** in animals kept without it. This is the settled part.

**The mechanism is less settled.** A 2009 study of wood-eating Panaque and Hypostomus catfish, loricariids in the same family as bristlenose though a different genus, found they poorly digest wood cellulose and pass wood through the gut in under four hours, concluding they are **not true wood digesters** in the way termites or beavers are ([German and Bittong, 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2762538/)). A 2020 follow-up on wood-eating Panaque and Panaqolus catfish checked the usual fallback explanation, gut microbes fermenting the wood, and found little evidence of that either. The likelier account, per that study, is that any nutritional benefit comes from **microbes and fungi already growing on the wood's surface**, not fermentation inside the gut, with the wood itself contributing **less nutritionally than long assumed** ([McCauley et al., 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7391310/)). Neither study looked at bristlenose itself, but both cover close relatives in the same family.

There is also the surface itself. A large piece of submerged wood grows biofilm and algae continuously, and grazing that surface is a good deal of what a pleco does with its day, whatever the wood is doing internally.

<FunFact>
This is a nice case of a care recommendation being right for possibly the wrong reason. The hobby arrived at "plecos need wood" from the wood-digestion story, and the recommendation survives the story being questioned, because the outcome without wood is bad regardless of why.
</FunFact>

## Driftwood, and Enough of It

The item. Large <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eR0Z2l" product="Driftwood">driftwood</AffiliateLink> pieces, ideally softer woods, which plecos rasp more readily.

Size matters more than variety. One small ornament piece in a tank with two plecos is not a wood provision, it is a decoration they will strip in a fortnight. Give a substantial piece, or several.

It will tint the water with tannins, which is normal and harmless. Our [tank setup guide](/blog/bristlenose-pleco-tank-setup-guide/) covers the setup.

## They Are Not an Algae Solution

The myth that starves them, and the reason people think plecos are hardy right up until one dies.

A bristlenose will clear the algae in a tank efficiently. Then the algae is gone and the fish is living on scraps. <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00025JZAY?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Hikari Tropical Sinking Wafers for Catfish, Loaches and Bottom Feeders, 3.88 oz">Sinking wafers</AffiliateLink> and blanched vegetables, courgette and cucumber especially, fed deliberately and after lights out if faster fish take everything. Other sizes are available.

Watch the belly. A pleco with a sunken abdomen is not being fed enough, and it is the clearest single indicator in this species. Our [feeding guide](/blog/bristlenose-pleco-feeding-guide/) covers amounts.

## Caves and Daytime Shade

Nocturnal fish that spend the day wedged into something. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4dsuNBx" product="Resin Hide Cave Aquarium Decoration">cave</AffiliateLink> gives that, and males use caves as spawning sites, which is why a cave per fish is sensible when you keep more than one.

Add general shade: wood overhangs, broad-leaved plants, and a light that is not blasting the whole floor. A pleco visible in the open all day is often a pleco with nowhere better to be.

## Grazing Surface

Beyond the wood, anything that grows biofilm. Rock, plant leaves, the back glass left unscrubbed.

Scrubbing every surface in the tank every week removes a genuine food source. Leave the back and sides, clean the front.

## Water and Oxygen

Plecos produce a lot of waste and need real filtration and current. Good flow and surface agitation matter, since a heavily stocked warm tank with a large catfish in it runs low on oxygen faster than people expect.

Our [health issues guide](/blog/bristlenose-pleco-health-issues-guide/) covers what a pleco in trouble looks like, which is subtle in a fish that sits still anyway.

## Priority Order

1. Substantial driftwood, replaced as it is consumed
2. Deliberate feeding: sinking foods and vegetables, after lights out
3. A cave per fish, and daytime shade
4. Grazing surface left unscrubbed
5. Filtration and flow suited to a messy fish
6. Floor area over height
7. Tannins and dimmer light

## What Not to Do

Do not keep a bristlenose without wood. Do not buy one as an algae solution and stop feeding it. Do not scrub every surface in the tank. Do not keep two males with one cave. Do not ignore a sunken belly. And see our [handling guide](/blog/bristlenose-pleco-handling-guide/) before netting one, since the pectoral spines lock into mesh and the odontodes catch.
