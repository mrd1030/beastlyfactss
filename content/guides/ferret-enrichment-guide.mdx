# Ferret Enrichment: Lying Awake With Their Eyes Open

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Most boredom advice is vague. The ferret research produced a specific, observable behavior you can look for tonight.

<KeyTakeaway>
Ferrets denied playtime spent more time lying awake with their eyes open, screeched more, and sat and stood less than after playtime. Survey work separately found the enrichments ferrets most enjoy are digging, tunnels, human interaction and exploration, in that company.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Research Says

Two 2022 surveys cover ferret housing and enrichment in some depth. The most commonly reported enrichments were **hammocks, tunnels and human interaction**, and the things ferrets were reported to most enjoy were **digging, tunnels, human interaction and exploration**. Multi-level cages promote exploratory behavior and reduce stress, and cages should provide separate feeding and elimination areas.

The behavioral finding is the one worth building around: **ferrets without playtime spent more time lying awake with their eyes open, screeched more, and sat and stood less** than after playtime.

<FunFact>
Lying awake with eyes open is easy to misread as a relaxed animal resting. A ferret that has just had a proper play session sleeps hard. One that has not is awake, still, and doing nothing, which looks like calm and is not.
</FunFact>

## Tunnels and Connected Routes

Tunnels appear in both the "commonly provided" list and the "most enjoyed" list, which is a rare alignment. Ferrets are built for tunnels: long, flexible, and evolved to pursue prey through burrows.

Connected tubes, fabric tunnels and routes between levels change how much of the space actually gets used. ## A Place to Dig

Digging is on the enjoyment list, and a ferret with no outlet for it will find one: houseplants, litter, carpet edges, the corner of a sofa. A tub of uncooked rice, dried beans, or soil, deep enough to submerge in, redirects the behavior instead of suppressing it.

## Scheduled Play, Not Ambient Access

The playtime study is about a scheduled session, not about a bigger cage. Ferrets are crepuscular and sleep a great deal, so the window where enrichment lands is narrower than it is for a diurnal animal. Several hours out of cage daily, during their active period, does more than any amount of cage furniture.

Human interaction shows up repeatedly in the surveys as a core enrichment. For a single ferret it is not optional.

## Priority Order

1. Several hours of daily out-of-cage time during their active window
2. Human interaction, or a companion ferret, or both
3. Tunnels and connected routes
4. A dig box
5. Hammocks, sleep sacks and enclosed sleeping spots
6. Multi-level cage space with real connections between levels
7. Foraging and puzzle feeding
8. Novel objects, rotated

## What Not to Do

Do not read a still, awake ferret as a content one. Do not rely on cage size instead of out-of-cage time, since the playtime research is about sessions rather than square footage. Do not use latex or foam rubber toys, which ferrets chew through and which cause intestinal blockages. Do not suppress digging without redirecting it. And do not leave a single ferret with cage furniture and no interaction, because human contact is in the evidence, not in the nice-to-have column.
