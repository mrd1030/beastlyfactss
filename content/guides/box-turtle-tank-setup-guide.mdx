# Box Turtle Enclosure Setup: Land, Water, and Real Space Requirements

<AffiliateDisclosure />

A box turtle enclosure needs to genuinely support both the land-dwelling and water-access sides of this species' semi-terrestrial life, floor space, burrowing depth, and a real basking setup all matter here. For what all of it costs, see our [cost guide](/blog/box-turtle-cost-guide/).

## Enclosure Size

36 by 18 inches is a workable minimum, but larger is considerably better, closer to 5.5 feet by 3 feet for an eastern box turtle, or around 4 feet by 2 feet for the somewhat smaller ornate box turtle. Floor space matters far more than height, this is a ground-dwelling, burrowing species. An <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CJB82285?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="REPTI ZOO 50 Gallon PVC Reptile Enclosure, 36x18x18 inch">enclosure at this minimum size</AffiliateLink> is a reasonable starting point, though an outdoor pen is genuinely excellent where your climate allows, with walls buried deep enough and secured well enough to keep predators out.

## Temperature

Provide a clear warm basking zone with a gradient down to a cooler area, using an indoor heat source. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4bqzQAS" product="Basking bulb (standard, 75W)">basking bulb</AffiliateLink> set in a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/44eEEFH" product="Basking dome/fixture">basking dome fixture</AffiliateLink> covers this well. This species shouldn't be kept outdoors once temperatures drop below about 70°F.

## Humidity

Relatively humid, 60 to 80%. Mist daily and provide a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eKyN0W" product="Large soak-able water dish">shallow water dish</AffiliateLink> large enough for your turtle to genuinely soak in, this ties directly to the semi-terrestrial lifestyle covered in our [handling guide](/blog/box-turtle-handling-guide/). Hatchlings and young turtles dehydrate especially easily and need consistently moist substrate, not just an occasional misting.

## Substrate

Wood chips or a topsoil and <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CY23K32S?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="VCEPJH Loose Coconut Fiber Substrate, 2 Pack">coconut fiber</AffiliateLink> mix, at least 2 to 3 inches deep to support burrowing. Avoid gravel, sand, additive-laden potting soil, crushed walnut shells, and cat litter entirely, all carry real impaction risk for a species that will investigate and sometimes ingest its substrate.

<FunFact>
An outdoor pen, walls buried deep and secured well against predators, is considered genuinely excellent housing for a box turtle where climate allows, arguably better than any indoor enclosure this species could be given.
</FunFact>

## Lighting

UVB is essential, not optional. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3TiqeC4" product='UVB - Arcadia Forest 6% (ProT5 kit, 36"/39W)'>T5 HO bulb (6% strength or the equivalent Zoo Med ReptiSun 5.0 T5 HO)</AffiliateLink> in a reflective fixture, targeting a basking-area UV index of 3.0 to 4.0. Run a longer photoperiod in summer, around 14 hours, tapering to about 10 hours in winter.

## The Non-Obvious Requirements

Both land and shallow water access, genuinely non-negotiable for this species, a dry-only or water-only setup doesn't match how box turtles actually live. And a 4 to 5 inch opaque barrier along the front glass helps curb the stress-driven pacing that clear-walled enclosures can cause, this species can see through glass in a way that creates real, ongoing stress if left unaddressed.

## Diet: Genuinely Omnivorous

Unlike the strictly herbivorous [tortoises](/blog/sulcata-tortoise-tank-setup-guide/) covered elsewhere on this site, box turtles eat a real mix, roughly half animal matter and half plant matter. Earthworms, insects, and occasional lean protein alongside leafy greens and vegetables make up a balanced diet for this species.
