# Common Rat Health Issues (Respiratory Disease Is the One to Know)

<VetDisclaimer />
<AffiliateDisclosure />

Hardy for their size. One condition is the baseline.

## Chronic respiratory disease (Mycoplasma pulmonis)

This is the one. A northwestern US survey found 95% of pet ratteries positive. Lab colonies stay cleaner. Pet homes do not.

**Watch for:** sneezing, sniffling, labored or noisy breathing, a rough coat, lethargy, weight loss, head tilt, red-brown porphyrin at the eyes and nose.

No cure. Antibiotics ease a flare. Once it is chronic they rarely live past 2 years. Ammonia in the cage drives flares. Same-week vet, not wait-and-see.

<FunFact>
Ninety-five percent of those surveyed ratteries already had the bacterium. Clean air is how you change the odds, not how you invent a rare diagnosis.
</FunFact>

## Mammary tumors

Most common tumor. Males and females. Tissue from chin to tail. Most are benign fibroadenomas and can be removed. They often return. Spay a female before 7 months. Feel the belly and chest when you handle them.

## Pituitary tumors

Common, especially in females. High-calorie feeding raises the risk. Head tilt, depression, a behavior change, sometimes sudden death. No good treatment. Diet is the lever. A new tilt can also be an inner ear problem. Get the exam.

## Chronic kidney disease

Older rats. Fatal. No effective treatment. Wellness checks matter more as they age so you can decide on comfort with facts.

## Also common

**Zymbal's gland tumors** at the ear base, usually after 18 months. Often too invasive to clear. Firm mass, head shaking, bleeding from the ear.

**Bumblefoot** from wire floors or damp bedding. ## When to see a vet

Now: labored or noisy breathing, a sneeze that does not stop, a new lump. Same week: a head tilt, a behavior change, lethargy.
