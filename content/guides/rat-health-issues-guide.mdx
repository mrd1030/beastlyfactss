# Common Rat Health Issues (Respiratory Disease Is the One to Know)

<VetDisclaimer />
<AffiliateDisclosure />

Rats are hardy for their size, but one condition affects nearly all of them to some degree, and knowing what it looks like matters more here than with most small pets.

## Chronic Respiratory Disease (Mycoplasma pulmonis)

This is the condition every rat owner needs to recognize. Merck Veterinary Manual calls chronic respiratory disease, caused by the bacterium Mycoplasma pulmonis, the most common health problem in rats, and it isn't a rare or unlucky diagnosis: while the disease rarely shows up in laboratory colonies, a survey of pet ratteries in the northwestern US found virtually all of them, 95%, positive for it. How severely it actually presents varies with environmental and individual factors, intracage ammonia levels chief among them, along with concurrent infections, genetics, and diet.

**Watch for:** sneezing, sniffling, labored or noisy breathing, a rough or ruffled coat, lethargy, weight loss, head tilt, and reddish-brown porphyrin staining around the eyes and nose.

There's no cure. Antibiotics can ease symptoms during a flare-up, but Merck is direct about the long-term outlook: rats with the chronic form of this disease rarely live longer than 2 years. That single fact is why housing quality matters so much for this species, ammonia buildup from an infrequently cleaned or poorly ventilated cage is a documented driver of flare-ups. Any of the signs above is worth a same-week vet visit rather than a wait-and-see approach.

## Mammary Tumors

The most common tumor type in rats, and genuinely common, affecting both males and females, not just unspayed females, because rat mammary tissue extends widely under the skin from chin to tail. **Any new lump warrants a vet visit**, but Merck notes the odds favor a benign, operable outcome: most are fibroadenomas rather than cancer, and surgical removal is usually possible when caught early, though a second lump reappearing months later isn't unusual. Spaying a female rat before 7 months of age meaningfully lowers her lifetime risk, per Merck guidance, and checking along the belly and chest for new lumps during regular handling is a reasonable habit for any rat, regardless of sex.

## Pituitary Tumors

Common in rats, especially females, per Merck, and directly linked to diet: the risk of developing one rises with high-calorie feeding, which is one more reason portion control isn't just about waistline. **Watch for** head tilt, depression, and behavior change, sometimes progressing to sudden death. There's no effective treatment, which makes prevention through diet the more useful lever here than treatment after the fact.

## Chronic Kidney Disease (Progressive Nephrosis)

A condition of older rats that Merck describes as always fatal, with no effective treatment once it develops. It's a normal part of aging in this species rather than a preventable husbandry failure, which is part of why routine wellness checks matter more as a rat gets older, catching decline early at least allows for supportive care and quality-of-life decisions made with more information.

## Also Common: Zymbal's Gland Tumors, Bumblefoot, and Mites

A few less-headline conditions round out the list. **Zymbal's gland tumors** develop near the base of the ear, mostly in rats over 18 months old, and unlike mammary fibroadenomas, they're often too invasive to fully remove, watch for a firm mass near the ear, head shaking, or bleeding from the ear. **Bumblefoot (pododermatitis)** is a bacterial foot infection usually caused by time spent on wire flooring or damp, poorly cleaned bedding, more common in sedentary or older rats, a reasonable argument for solid-floored shelves and a soft <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07N658NL6?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Niteangel Hanging Hammock Nap Sack Swing Bag Pet Sleeper for Ferret Rat Sugar Glider and Other Small Animals">hammock</AffiliateLink> to rest in instead. **Mites** are normally present in small numbers on any rat and only become a visible problem when stress or illness weakens a rat's ability to keep them in check through normal grooming.

## The Husbandry Pattern Worth Remembering

Ammonia buildup from infrequent cleaning is directly linked to chronic respiratory disease, the single biggest threat this species faces. A high-calorie diet raises pituitary tumor risk. And time spent on wire flooring or damp bedding contributes to bumblefoot. All three are within an owner's control, and given how much chronic respiratory disease alone can shorten a rat's already-short life, getting the basics right carries real weight. ## When to See a Vet

See a vet promptly for labored or noisy breathing, sneezing that doesn't resolve, or any new lump, don't wait to see if it clears up on its own given how quickly a small animal can decline. Book a routine visit for a head tilt, sudden behavior change, or general lethargy.

Mice share the same Mycoplasma pulmonis respiratory risk as rats, worth knowing if you're keeping both, and the [mouse encyclopedia profile](/encyclopedia/animal/mouse/) covers how close the two species are.
