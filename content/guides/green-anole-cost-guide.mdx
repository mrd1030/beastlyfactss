# How Much Does a Green Anole Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Green anoles are one of the cheapest reptiles you can buy, and that low price is exactly why this species is so often underestimated and under-housed.

For the full care picture beyond budgeting, see our [full Green Anole care guide](/guides/green-anole/).

## The Lizard: $5 to $30

Most green anoles sell for under $15, some as low as $5. There's no real morph market for this species, it's a wild-type animal throughout, though rarer anole species from other parts of the world cost considerably more. Those are different animals entirely, not color variants of the green anole.

That 24x24x24 inch front-opening terrarium is the largest single expense by far relative to the animal's price, and it's a genuinely new addition to the gear we recommend for this species. Beyond the enclosure, a full setup also needs a 6500K LED for general illumination and branches or live or artificial plants to round things out, adding a bit more on top of the priced gear above. Altogether, that's how a first-time setup lands at roughly $390 to $470, and it's covered in detail in our [tank setup guide](/blog/green-anole-tank-setup-guide/).

## Ongoing Costs: Roughly $20 to $35 a Month

Feeder insects plus periodic substrate and supplement replacement make up the monthly total, modest. <FunFact>
Casually kept green anoles are often reported living just 3 to 4 years, but ReptiFiles states plainly that with genuinely appropriate care, this pet can live up to 10 years and possibly longer. The gap between those two numbers is almost entirely a setup problem, not a species limitation.
</FunFact>

## Vet Costs

A routine exotic exam runs $50 to $100. Emergency visits start around $150 and can reach $300 or more before diagnostics. Worth sitting with directly, a single vet visit can easily cost several times more than the animal itself.

## Lifespan: Longer Than the Reputation Suggests

Casually kept green anoles are often reported living just 3 to 4 years, but that's a reflection of common under-care, not the species' actual potential. With genuinely appropriate care, ReptiFiles states plainly that this pet can live up to 10 years and possibly longer. The cheap price tag is exactly what drives poor setups for this species, people assume a low-cost animal doesn't need much investment, when the opposite is closer to true.

## The Honest Budgeting Takeaway

The animal's price has almost nothing to do with what it costs to keep one well. A proper enclosure, UVB, and consistent humidity, all covered in the [tank setup guide](/blog/green-anole-tank-setup-guide/), cost roughly twenty to thirty times the lizard's own price tag, and skipping that investment is exactly why this species' reputation for a short lifespan persists.
