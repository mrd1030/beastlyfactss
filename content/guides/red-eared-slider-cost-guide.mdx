# How Much Does a Red-Eared Slider Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Red-eared sliders are sold cheap and small, which is exactly what makes their real cost so easy to underestimate. The animal itself is inexpensive. What it grows into is not.

## The turtle: $10 to $200+

Juvenile or hatchling normal sliders commonly run $10 to $40 at pet stores or from breeders, with many listings clustering around $15 to $30. Adults often cost $40 to $100 or more depending on size and condition. Morphs, albino, pastel, hypo, high-red, typically run $50 to $200 or more. These are among the most commonly sold pet turtles in the country. Availability is never the issue. Pick captive-bred over wild-caught.

Federal law restricts the sale of turtles under 4 inches, not ownership. The [state-by-state rules](/blog/red-eared-slider-legal-guide/) are where the restrictions on keeping one come from.

<FunFact>
Red-eared sliders live 20 to 30 years with good care. A handful of less documented claims put the ceiling at 40 to 70. Even the conservative end is a pet you will still be feeding in your 40s or 50s if you bring one home today.
</FunFact>

## Vet costs

A reptile or exotic vet is required for this species, not optional. Routine wellness exams often run $80 to $180, with fecal checks, bloodwork, or imaging adding more. Emergency visits start around $150 to $400 for the exam alone. Shell repairs, abscess treatment, or hospitalization can reach several hundred to over $1,000. In some areas, finding a qualified reptile vet is a bigger obstacle than the cost. Locate one before you need it.

## The budgeting number is the lifespan

20 to 30 years with good care is the consensus range, with 20 to 25 cited as typical. This is a multi-decade pet, not unlike a koi, another species people buy small and cheap without registering what it grows into. See the [koi cost guide](/blog/koi-cost-guide/). The setup you build for a palm-sized hatchling needs to work for an animal that will need a 75 to 100-plus gallon home. Plan for that turtle, not the one in front of you.
