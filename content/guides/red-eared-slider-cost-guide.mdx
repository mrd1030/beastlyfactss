# How Much Does a Red-Eared Slider Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Red-eared sliders are sold cheap and small, which is exactly what makes their real cost so easy to underestimate. The animal itself is inexpensive. What it grows into is not.

## The Turtle: $10 to $200+

Juvenile or hatchling normal sliders commonly run $10 to $40 at pet stores or from breeders, with many listings clustering around $15 to $30. Adults or larger specimens often cost $40 to $100 or more depending on size and condition. Morphs or color variants, albino, pastel, hypo, high-red, are meaningfully more expensive, typically $50 to $200 or more, with rarer genetics commanding a real premium. These are among the most commonly sold pet turtles in the country, so availability is never the issue. Whatever you choose, pick captive-bred over wild-caught.

Federal law actually restricts the sale of turtles under 4 inches, not ownership, and the [state-by-state rules](/blog/red-eared-slider-legal-guide/) are where the restrictions on keeping one come from.

<FunFact>
Most sources agree red-eared sliders live 20 to 30 years with good care, and the oldest documented captive slider reached 41.3. Even taking the conservative end of that range, this is a pet you'll likely still be feeding in your 40s or 50s if you bring one home today.

## Vet Costs

A reptile or exotic vet is required for this species, not optional. Routine wellness exams often run $80 to $180, with fecal checks, bloodwork, or imaging adding significantly more. Emergency or treatment visits start around $150 to $400 for the exam alone, and shell repairs, abscess treatment, or hospitalization can reach several hundred to over $1,000 depending on severity. In some areas, finding a qualified reptile vet is a bigger obstacle than the cost itself, it's worth locating one before you actually need it.

## The Budgeting Number Is the Lifespan

20 to 30 years with good care is the consensus range, with 20 to 25 cited as typical. Either way, this is a multi-decade pet, not unlike a koi, another species people buy small and cheap without quite registering what it grows into, and the [koi cost guide](/blog/koi-cost-guide/) shows the same shape of budget at pond scale. The setup you build for a palm-sized hatchling needs to work for an animal that will eventually need a 75 to 100-plus gallon home, so plan for that turtle, not the one currently in front of you.
