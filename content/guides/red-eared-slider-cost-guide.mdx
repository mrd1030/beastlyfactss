# How Much Does a Red-Eared Slider Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Red-eared sliders are sold cheap and small, which is exactly what makes their real cost so easy to underestimate. The animal itself is genuinely inexpensive. What it grows into is not. For the full care picture beyond budgeting, see our complete [Red-Eared Slider care guide](/guides/red-eared-slider/).

## The Turtle: $10 to $200+

Juvenile or hatchling normal sliders commonly run $10 to $40 at pet stores or from breeders, with many listings clustering around $15 to $30. Adults or larger specimens often cost $40 to $100 or more depending on size and condition. Morphs or color variants, albino, pastel, hypo, high-red, are meaningfully more expensive, typically $50 to $200 or more, with rarer genetics commanding a real premium. These are among the most commonly sold pet turtles in the country, so availability is never the issue. Whatever you choose, pick captive-bred over wild-caught.

Federal law actually restricts the sale of turtles under 4 inches, not ownership, more on that distinction in our [Red-Eared Slider legal guide](/blog/red-eared-slider-legal-guide/) if you want the details.

<FunFact>
Most sources agree red-eared sliders live 20 to 30 years with good care, but a handful of far less consistently documented claims put the ceiling as high as 40 to 70 years. Even taking the conservative end of that range, this is a pet you'll likely still be feeding in your 40s or 50s if you bring one home today.
</FunFact>

## Vet Costs

A reptile or exotic vet is genuinely required for this species, not optional. Routine wellness exams often run $80 to $180, with fecal checks, bloodwork, or imaging adding significantly more. Emergency or treatment visits start around $150 to $400 for the exam alone, and shell repairs, abscess treatment, or hospitalization can reach several hundred to over $1,000 depending on severity. In some areas, finding a qualified reptile vet is a bigger obstacle than the cost itself, it's worth locating one before you actually need it. Our [Red-Eared Slider health issues guide](/blog/red-eared-slider-health-issues-guide/) covers the specific problems that tend to drive these vet visits.

## The Real Budgeting Number Is the Lifespan

20 to 30 years with good care is the consensus range, with 20 to 25 cited as typical. Either way, this is a multi-decade pet, not unlike a koi, another species people buy small and cheap without quite registering what it grows into, see our [Koi cost guide](/blog/koi-cost-guide/) for that comparison. The setup you build for a palm-sized hatchling needs to work for an animal that will eventually need a 75 to 100-plus gallon home, so plan for that turtle, not the one currently in front of you.
