# How Much Does Platy Fish Ownership Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Platies are one of the more affordable fish on this entire site, cheap to buy, cheap to keep, and easy to house in a modest setup.

## The Fish: %%animal:platy%%, More for Premium Strains

Standard varieties, reds, blues, tuxedos, wags, and most of the common patterns, run %%animal:platy%% each, with a six-pack landing around $30. Less common varieties like tiger platies run toward the top of that range, around $6. Whichever variety you pick, the day-to-day care is identical underneath the color.

<FunFact>
The named varieties sold today, from solid reds to wag-tailed bicolors, are the product of generations of selective breeding. Wild southern platies are far from plain, though: the species is one of the most color-variable fish known, with black spotting and blotches in many patterns and, in some fish, red, orange or yellow pigment.
</FunFact>

Platies are genuinely unfussy eaters, so food costs stay modest, add water conditioner, occasional filter media, and a little electricity for the heater and filter, and full monthly upkeep for a 10-gallon platy tank commonly lands around $15 to $20.

## Vet Costs

Like most aquarium fish, platies rarely see a vet in any traditional sense. Most platy health issues, ich, fin rot, dropsy, and the occasional bout of "shimmy," get managed at home with water quality correction and over-the-counter medication rather than a clinic visit. Dedicated aquatic vets exist but are uncommon and rarely necessary for a beginner fish kept in water that is hard enough.

## Lifespan

2 to 4 years, with 3 to 4 achievable under stable conditions and excellent, consistent water quality pushing a fish toward 5. That's well past a [guppy's](/blog/guppy-cost-guide/) lab-study average of about 13 months, and a little shorter than a [molly's](/blog/molly-cost-guide/) 2 to 5 years on a bigger setup.

## The Cost to Plan For: More Platies Than You Started With

This is the detail that catches new owners off guard, and it's the same one that trips up new guppy and molly owners. Platies are livebearers, females give birth to live, free-swimming fry rather than laying eggs, and a single mating goes a long way. Female platies store sperm in the folds of their ovarian tissue for months at a time, which means one encounter with a male can fertilize several separate broods without him ever being present again. A brood runs 20 to 80 fry, and the interval between broods measures about 30 to 36 days, after a gestation of roughly 24 to 30 days.

Put males and females in the same tank with no plan, and you'll have meaningfully more platies within a couple of months than you started with, which means more tank space and more of every ongoing cost listed above.
