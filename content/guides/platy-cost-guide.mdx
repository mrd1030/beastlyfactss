# How Much Does Platy Fish Ownership Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Platies are one of the more affordable fish on this entire site, cheap to buy, cheap to keep, and easy to house in a modest setup.

## The Fish: %%animal:platy%%, More for Premium Strains

Standard varieties, reds, blues, tuxedos, wags, and most of the common patterns, run [%%animal:platy%% each](https://www.thatpetplace.com/aquarium-livestock/freshwater-fish/livebearers/live-platies). Selectively bred strains with less common patterns can cost more. Whichever variety you pick, the day-to-day care is identical underneath the color.

<FunFact>
The named varieties sold today, from solid reds to wag-tailed bicolors, are the product of generations of selective breeding. Wild southern platies are far from plain, though: the species is one of the most color-variable fish known, with black spotting and blotches in many patterns and, in some fish, red, orange or yellow pigment.
</FunFact>

Platies are genuinely unfussy eaters, so food costs stay modest, add water conditioner, occasional filter media, and a little electricity for the heater and filter, and full monthly upkeep for a 10-gallon platy tank commonly lands around $15 to $20.

## Vet Costs

Like most aquarium fish, platies rarely see a vet in any traditional sense. Most platy health issues, ich, fin rot, dropsy, and the occasional bout of "shimmy," get managed at home with water quality correction and over-the-counter medication rather than a clinic visit. Dedicated aquatic vets exist but are uncommon and rarely necessary for fish at this price.

## The Cost to Plan For: More Platies Than You Started With

This is the detail that catches new owners off guard, and it's the same one that trips up new [guppy](/blog/guppy-cost-guide/) and [molly](/blog/molly-cost-guide/) owners. Platies are livebearers, females give birth to live, free-swimming fry rather than laying eggs, and a single mating goes a long way. Female platies store sperm in the folds of their ovarian tissue for months at a time, which means one encounter with a male can fertilize several separate broods without him ever being present again. A brood runs 20 to 80 young, and a new one can arrive about every 30 to 36 days.

Put males and females in the same tank with no plan, and you'll have meaningfully more platies within a couple of months than you started with, which means more tank space and more of every ongoing cost listed above.
