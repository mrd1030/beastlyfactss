# How Much Does a Madagascar Hissing Cockroach Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Madagascar hissing cockroaches are about as affordable as a pet gets, both to buy and to keep.

## The Cockroach: $3 to $65

Individual nymphs run around $3 each. A breeding pair runs about $24, and a starter colony of 25 runs around $65. Because this same species is also sold widely as a feeder insect for reptiles, availability is wide and prices stay low. As pets, hissers are valued for their size, calm temperament, long life and the hiss; as feeders, for their body size and how well they gut-load. The low price reflects how widely they are bred.

## How Many to Buy

A single roach is not how this species lives. Wild hissers live in mixed groups of roughly 10, and a captive group should be kept together too, which is why the per-animal price matters less than the group price. The three common starting points:

- **An all-male group.** No breeding, so the group never outgrows its tank. Males hold territories and fight over them, so an all-male group needs a hide per male and a few spare.
- **A breeding pair, about $24.** Cheap to start, but not a pair for long. Both sexes are mature at about 7 months, a female carries her eggs internally for about 2 months, then gives birth to 15 to 40 nymphs, and breeding runs year-round in a warm tank.
- **A starter colony of 25, about $65.** The quickest route to a full display, and the one that needs the most room from day one.

Healthy colonies run at 2 to 10 adults per gallon of enclosure, so a mixed group's real cost is the tank it grows into. A colony past that density needs a bigger tank, a second one, or separate-sex housing to stop the births.

A tank or plastic tub in the 5 to 20 gallon range with a secure, screened lid is the enclosure baseline, paired with coconut fiber substrate, a hide or two, and a water dish. The optional heat mat and thermostat only earns its keep if you're breeding a colony, and a mat without a thermostat is an overheating risk that can even melt a plastic tub.

## Ongoing Costs: A Few Dollars a Month

Fresh vegetables and fruit, occasional commercial roach or dog food, and modest substrate replacement make up the entire monthly cost. This is about as low-maintenance as a pet gets financially.

## Vet Costs

Effectively none. Invertebrates are rarely taken to a vet, and very few vets treat them at all. Care here is entirely husbandry-based: correct humidity and substrate do the work.

## Lifespan: 2 to 5 Years

2 to 5 years is the typical range, most commonly 2 to 3, with 4 to 5 achievable under excellent care. That is long-lived for an invertebrate at this price, especially compared to something like a [praying mantis](/blog/praying-mantis-cost-guide/), which lives only a fraction as long.

## The Honest Value Proposition

If cost is a real factor in choosing a pet, this is close to the floor. A complete setup for a small group commonly costs less than a single vet visit for most pets, and the ongoing cost is close to negligible. What you're trading for that affordability is a display and observation pet rather than a handling-focused one. The [Madagascar Hissing Cockroach encyclopedia profile](/encyclopedia/animal/hissing-cockroach/) has the full natural history.

The hiss itself is a misleading first impression, one of four set side by side in [Four Animals, Four First Impressions That Are Wrong](/blog/ackie-milksnake-mhc-angelfish-overview/), alongside the ackie monitor, milk snake, and angelfish.
