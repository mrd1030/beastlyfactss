# How Much Does a Western Hognose Snake Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Western hognose snakes are one of the more affordable pet snakes out there, with a low ongoing cost that makes them one of the cheaper long-term reptiles to keep once the initial setup is built.

## The Snake Itself: %%animal:hognose-snake%%+, Depending on Morph

<ComparisonTable
  headers={["Morph", "Price Range"]}
  rows={[
    ["Normal, wild-type", "$50 - $100"],
    ["Albino", "$150 - $200"],
    ["Anaconda", "$175 - $500"],
    ["Rare combinations (coral, snow, and similar)", "$1,000+"],
  ]}

Females typically cost $50 to $200 more than males across every morph tier, since they grow larger and are more sought after for breeding.

## Upfront Setup: %%setup:hognose-snake%% With the First Vet Exam

<CostTable guide="hognose-snake" section="necessities" />

Highly recommended, not counted in the total: a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B076GXJFJG?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Solarmeter Model 6.5R Reptile UV Index Meter">UV index meter</AffiliateLink>, about $240 to $265. It is the only way to check that the UVB tube sits at the right height and to know when the tube needs replacing; UV cards do not measure UVB.

A 2x2x2 foot or 36x18x18 inch PVC or glass enclosure is the biggest single line item, and it is sized for a female, whose minimum is a 40 gallon equivalent of 36x18x16 inches. A male alone can live in a 20 gallon equivalent of about 30x13x13 inches, so a single male can go in a smaller box. For the heat source, the table prices a low-wattage halogen basking bulb in a fixture over the substrate, run on a dimming thermostat, which is the setup the [enclosure setup guide](/blog/hognose-snake-tank-setup-guide/) recommends over an under-tank heat mat. The thermostat is the non-negotiable part of it. A heat mat appears only as night heat, for a room that falls below 60°F.

Every row above the vet exam adds up to $390 to $850. Without the optional UVB and night heat, it is $280 to $710. A T5 tube's wattage is not its length: a 24-watt tube is about 22 inches long, a 39-watt tube about 34 inches and a 54-watt tube about 46 inches, so go by the length in inches when you match it to two-thirds of the enclosure. The first vet exam and fecal test bring the full setup to %%setup:hognose-snake%%, before the snake itself. The feeding tub, snake hook and gram scale are small lines, but each one does a job: the tub keeps substrate out of the meal, the hook lifts a hungry snake without a hand that smells of mouse, and the scale catches weight loss before it shows.

Those lines total %%annual:hognose-snake%% a year, about $15 to $35 a month. Frozen and thawed rodents are the largest of them, even fed infrequently, since hognoses don't eat as often as some other snakes. Electricity for the heat and the annual wellness check make up most of the rest.

## Vet Costs

A routine reptile wellness exam runs roughly $50 to $100, with a fecal test around $25 to $50. Radiographs, if needed, run $100 to $250 per view. Treating a respiratory infection or, less commonly, an impaction can run into the hundreds.

<FunFact>
A snow or coral morph hognose can cost more than ten times what a normal, wild-type hognose costs. Genetically and behaviorally, though, it's the identical animal underneath, needing the exact same enclosure, heat, and diet. The price gap is entirely about pattern and color rarity, not care difficulty.
</FunFact>

## Lifespan and the Long Game

10 to 15 years is the commonly cited lifespan range for a Western hognose, with 15 to 20 years reported in captivity. That's a long commitment for a snake that stays small and costs little to maintain day to day, worth keeping in mind even though the monthly numbers look modest.

## The Value Proposition

Among the beginner snakes, hognoses land on the affordable end for both setup and ongoing cost, broadly comparable to a [corn snake](/blog/corn-snake-cost-guide/) and cheaper to run than a [ball python](/blog/ball-python-cost-guide/) at the higher size tiers.

The fuller side by side, beyond just the price tags, lives in our [Corn Snake vs. Hognose Snake guide](/blog/corn-snake-vs-hognose-snake-guide/), which covers temperament and handling differences too. The morph market adds real price variation if you want a specific look, but a normal, wild-type hognose is one of the most budget-friendly reptiles you can responsibly keep.
