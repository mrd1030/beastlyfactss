# How Much Does a Western Hognose Snake Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Western hognose snakes are one of the cheaper long-term reptiles to keep once the initial setup is built.

## The Snake Itself: %%animal:hognose-snake%%+, Depending on Morph

<ComparisonTable
  headers={["Morph", "Price Range"]}
  rows={[
    ["Normal, wild-type", "$50 - $100"],
    ["Albino", "$150 - $200"],
    ["Anaconda", "$175 - $500"],
    ["Rare combinations (coral, snow, and similar)", "$1,000+"],
  ]}
/>

Females typically cost $50 to $200 more than males across every morph tier, since they grow larger and are more sought after for breeding.

## Upfront Setup: %%setup:hognose-snake%% With the First Vet Exam

<CostTable guide="hognose-snake" section="necessities" />

A 2x2x2 foot or 36x18x18 inch PVC or glass enclosure is the biggest single line item, and it is sized for a female, whose minimum is a 40 gallon equivalent of 36x18x16 inches. A male alone can live in a 20 gallon equivalent of about 30x13x13 inches, so a single male can go in a smaller box. For the heat source, the table prices a low-wattage halogen basking bulb in a fixture over the substrate, run on a dimming thermostat, which is the setup the [enclosure setup guide](/blog/hognose-snake-tank-setup-guide/) recommends over an under-tank heat mat. The thermostat is the non-negotiable part of it. A heat mat appears only as night heat, for a room that falls below 60°F.

Highly recommended, not counted in the total: a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B076GXJFJG?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Solarmeter Model 6.5R Reptile UV Index Meter">UV index meter</AffiliateLink>, about $240 to $265. It is the only way to check that the UVB tube sits at the right height and to know when the tube needs replacing; UV cards do not measure UVB.

Every row above the vet exam adds up to $390 to $850. Without the optional UVB and night heat, it is $280 to $710. The first vet exam and fecal test bring the full setup to %%setup:hognose-snake%%, before the snake itself. The feeding tub, snake hook and gram scale are small lines, but each one does a job: the tub keeps substrate out of the meal, the hook lifts a hungry snake without a hand that smells of mouse, and the scale catches weight loss before it shows.

Those lines total %%annual:hognose-snake%% a year, about $15 to $35 a month. Frozen and thawed rodents are the largest of them, since [an adult hognose eats about once a week](/blog/hognose-snake-feeding-guide/). Electricity for the heat and the annual wellness check make up most of the rest.

## Vet Costs

A routine reptile wellness exam runs roughly $50 to $100, with a fecal test around $25 to $50. Radiographs, if needed, run $100 to $250 per view. Treating a respiratory infection or, less commonly, an impaction can run into the hundreds.

<FunFact>
A snow or coral morph hognose can cost more than ten times what a normal, wild-type hognose costs. Genetically and behaviorally, though, it's the identical animal underneath, needing the exact same enclosure, heat, and diet. The price gap is entirely about pattern and color rarity, not care difficulty.
</FunFact>

## Lifespan and the Long Game

A Western hognose lives 10 to 20 years in captivity. That's a long commitment for a snake that stays small and costs little to maintain day to day, worth keeping in mind even though the monthly numbers look modest.

## The Morph Is the Only Big Number

A hognose costs the same to run whatever its pattern, so the morph is the one line where the budget swings by hundreds. Pick a normal, wild-type animal and put the money into the enclosure, and this is one of the most budget-friendly reptiles you can responsibly keep, close to a [corn snake](/blog/corn-snake-cost-guide/).
