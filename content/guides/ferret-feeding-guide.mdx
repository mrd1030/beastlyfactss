# Ferret Feeding: Schedule, Diet, and Why They Stop Eating

Ferrets run on a faster gut than a cat or a dog. That changes the bowl and the clock.

## Feeding schedule

Leave high-quality dry kibble out 24 hours a day. The gut has no cecum. Food is through in about 3 to 4 hours. They want to eat every 4 to 6 hours on their own. Portion the bowl only if the ferret is putting on fat.

Kits wean onto solids around 4 weeks. Adult weight around 4 months. A ferret on insulinoma is sometimes switched to 4 to 6 small meals a day. That is a medical plan, not the default.

## What ferrets eat

Obligate carnivores. Ferret-specific dry kibble, about 32 to 40% protein. Read the guaranteed analysis on the bag you actually buy. High-quality kitten food is a stand-in if ferret kibble is missing. Adult cat food is not. Keep it dry. Soft or semi-moist food is worse for teeth.

Whole prey, cooked chicken, or cooked eggs are optional extras. Raw meat has to be human-grade. Salmonella and Campylobacter are real. Water always. A sipper beats an open bowl; they play in bowls.

## Safe treats and foods to avoid

Safe in small amounts: freeze-dried single-ingredient meats, cooked egg, cooked chicken, a fatty-acid supplement like Ferretone.

Never: chocolate. Sugar, raisins, honey except as emergency gum-rub on the way to a vet. Dairy. Bread, pasta, rice. Peas and legumes. Long-term cat or dog food. Fruit and vegetables.

## Why ferrets stop eating

1. **A missed meal.** The gut empties fast, so a skip looks worse than it would in a cat.
2. **Stress.** Rehoming, a cage change, handling.
3. **A new kibble.** Switch slowly.
4. **Dental pain.** Wants to eat, cannot chew.
5. **A swallowed foreign object.** Rubber and foam.
6. **Insulinoma.** Common in middle-aged and older ferrets. The [health issues guide](/blog/ferret-health-issues-guide/) has the list.
7. **Ulcers, gut infections, or systemic illness.** Adrenal, kidney, liver, heart.

## When to worry

Same day. A healthy adult can drop blood sugar inside a single day of not eating. Young, senior, or already sick animals crash faster.

Watch for loose or absent stool, blood in urine, sneezing or breathing trouble, trouble chewing, bald patches, hunching, lethargy. For a suspected low-sugar crash, weakness, wobbling, a glazed stare, drooling, or collapse, rub a small amount of Karo syrup or honey on the gums on the way to the vet. That is first aid, not a substitute.
