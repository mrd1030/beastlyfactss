# Madagascar Hissing Cockroach Feeding: Schedule, Diet, and Why They Stop Eating

Hissers are nocturnal, which changes how you should judge whether feeding is happening at all, and their diet looks a little different from what most keepers expect.

## Feeding Schedule

The schedule to follow: keep a dry protein source available at all times, high-quality dry dog food, cat food, or a commercial roach chow, and add fresh produce 2 to 3 times a week in a quantity the colony finishes within 24 to 48 hours so leftovers don't mold. Always-available dry food means no roach goes short between produce days, and that matters: a colony that runs short of food starts cannibalizing its own members. Offer dry food and produce in separate dishes so the moisture from the produce doesn't spoil the dry food, and check the dishes every few days, since appetite shifts with temperature, breeding and life stage.

The lighter end of the range is dry food topped up about once a week and fruit every couple of weeks, the routine long used for classroom colonies. It keeps a small pet group going, and it is the one to drop to if uneaten produce keeps going moldy before it is eaten. It is a fallback. The always-available schedule is the default.

There's no distinct feeding schedule tied to life stage. Newly hatched nymphs are independent immediately and feed themselves from birth. The one documented age-related difference is that breeding females need reliable access to protein-rich food to produce healthy broods.

## What Hissing Cockroaches Eat

High-quality dry dog food is the most consistently recommended staple, several sources rate it as having a better overall nutritional makeup for hissers than cat or bird food, though those work as substitutes. A commercial roach or insect chow is an accepted alternative. Their wild diet is decaying plant matter and fallen, overripe fruit, and captive feeding is built to approximate that.

Offer fresh produce a few times a week: romaine lettuce and other dark leafy greens, dandelion greens, carrots, squash, sweet potato, apples, bananas, oranges, and grapes. Rinse produce and use pesticide-free when possible, chop into manageable pieces, and remove anything uneaten within a day or two, mold and fruit flies are the main colony-health risk from feeding.

Water needs care too. Most sources favor a water source that can't drown a roach, a cotton ball or sponge saturated with water in a shallow dish, or a commercial water gel, changed regularly, since an open dish poses a real drowning risk to small nymphs.

## Safe Treats and Foods to Avoid

Safe: apple slices, banana, orange, romaine lettuce, dandelion greens, carrots, grapes, sweet potato, and squash.

Use caution with: iceberg lettuce, nutritionally much weaker than romaine, any produce treated with pesticides, and letting spoiled or moldy leftovers sit past a day or two. Skip diets that are too protein-heavy, hissers are naturally protein-sparing and don't need a meat- or pellet-heavy feed the way some other insects do.

## Why Hissing Cockroaches Stop Eating

1. **A nocturnal feeding schedule you're just not witnessing.** Hissers are light-averse and most active after dark, feeding is often genuinely happening overnight even when it looks like nothing's being eaten.
2. **Approaching a molt.** Nymphs go through about six molts over roughly six to seven months to reach adulthood, and commonly go quiet and eat less beforehand. They need [elevated humidity](/blog/madagascar-hissing-cockroach-tank-setup-guide/) during this window to shed successfully.
3. **Stress from a new environment.** Newly acquired hissers commonly need an adjustment period before settling into visible normal feeding.
4. **Isolation.** Hissers are normally kept in groups, and a solitary individual can become insecure and hide rather than feed visibly.
5. **An enclosure that's too cool.** Temperatures dropping much below about 70°F cause lethargy and slow activity, including feeding. This resolves once the correct 75 to 85°F range is restored.
6. **Illness.** Genuine sickness signs include lying in one place without hissing when touched, lethargy beyond normal sluggishness, a dull exoskeleton, weight loss, discharge from the mouth or eyes, and abnormal feces.

Unlike many temperate reptiles, hissers are a tropical species with no documented seasonal dormancy or brumation period in captivity, so that common exotic-pet explanation doesn't apply here.

## When to Worry

A hisser can go about a week without food, and a nymph approaching a molt may eat little for part of that.

What should prompt real concern: inactivity or lying in one place, failure to hiss when handled, lethargy beyond normal pre-molt sluggishness, a dull-looking exoskeleton, visible weight loss, discharge from the mouth or eyes, visible sores, and abnormal feces. A visibly sick hisser commonly survives only about 2 to 3 days once symptoms are obvious, so act quickly: check temperature and humidity and consult an exotics-experienced vet as soon as several symptoms stack up.

For a colony bred as feeders, the diet above does double duty: what the roach eats in its last day or two is what the reptile gets, and [gut-loading timing](/blog/gut-loading-feeder-insects-guide/) decides whether that nutrition is still there when the roach is eaten.
