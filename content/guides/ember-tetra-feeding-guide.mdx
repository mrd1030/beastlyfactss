# Ember Tetra Feeding: Diet, Food Size, and How Often to Feed

<AffiliateDisclosure />

An ember tetra grows to about 0.8 inch (2 cm), and its mouth is built to match. Everything about feeding one follows from that: the food has to be tiny, it has to sink slowly enough to be caught, and each portion has to be small enough for the school to clear.

## Feeding Schedule by Life Stage

Fry eat little and often, juveniles step down toward the adult routine, and adults settle at one or two meals a day.

Where the adult routine allows two meals, make one of them live or frozen food. Young fish get the extra meals because they eat more for their size: up to 5% of body weight a day, against about 3% for an adult.

The portion matters more than the count. Each meal is a small pinch sized to the school, and the way to judge it is to watch the fish eat. If food drifts past them and settles on the substrate untouched, the next pinch is smaller. Food that sits uneaten breaks down into ammonia, the same waste the [nitrogen cycle](/blog/aquarium-cycling-guide/) in a mature tank has to clear.

Embers scatter their eggs and give no parental care, and adults will eat both eggs and newly hatched fry, so fry are fed in a separate tank.

## What Embers Eat, in the Wild and in the Tank

In the backwaters and oxbow lakes of Brazil's Araguaia River basin, the ember tetra is thought to be a micropredator, picking tiny invertebrates and other zooplankton out of the water. The captive diet is built to fit that: small food, with live or frozen prey on the menu every day.

Build the daily diet on a quality dried food sized for very small fish. A slow-sinking <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B004PBB5TK?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Hikari Tropical Semi-Floating Micro Pellets, 0.77 oz">micro-pellet</AffiliateLink> works, and so do flake and granules once they are crushed down to a fine crumb. Around that staple go small live and frozen foods: baby brine shrimp (Artemia nauplii), daphnia, cyclops and moina.

<KeyTakeaway>
Embers have little mouths and feed best on tiny, slow-sinking food. Choose the smallest micro-pellet grade, crush flake fine, and keep live and frozen foods at the size of baby brine shrimp and daphnia.
</KeyTakeaway>

Live or frozen fare belongs in the routine every day. Baby brine shrimp and daphnia are small enough to swallow whole, and they are the closest match a keeper can buy to the zooplankton a wild ember probably hunts.

<FunFact source="The ETYFish Project">
The genus name Hyphessobrycon joins Greek words for "a little smaller" and "to bite" or "eat greedily". The scientist who named the genus never explained the "smaller", which probably refers to the size of the fish.
</FunFact>

Live food carries a risk the dried staple does not. Cyclops and daphnia are common intermediate hosts of Camallanus, a blood-red intestinal worm, and fish fed those crustaceans can end up heavily infected.

Dried food goes stale: use an opened container kept at room temperature within about 2 months. Dry food stored in the freezer keeps about 6 months, frozen foods such as brine shrimp about a year, and thawed food is never refrozen.

## Foods to Avoid

- **Live tubifex worms.** In a study of shop-bought tubifex, 6 of 7 samples held infected worms, and 5 already had parasite spores in the water at the time of purchase. The worms sold under the name turned out to be several different species.
- **Anything too big to swallow.** A pellet or flake an ember cannot get down sinks uneaten and rots.
- **Rich, starchy or fatty diets.** Fatty liver disease is a common problem in captive fish, and diets high in carbohydrate or fat are among its causes. Poor nutrition in general brings slow growth, deformities and a weaker immune system.

## Why Embers Stop Eating

1. **Faster or bigger tankmates.** An ember loses out at feeding time to anything rowdy or much bigger than itself, so in a mixed tank the faster fish can take most of a meal while the embers hang back. Watching feeding time tells you whether the embers are getting fed at all. Choosing companions that pass [the checks two species need before sharing a tank](/blog/aquarium-stocking-and-tankmates-guide/), similar size and a calm temperament above all, is what keeps feeding time fair.
2. **Poor water quality.** In a new tank that has not finished cycling, ammonia or nitrite can reach toxic levels, and the fish turn lethargic, stop eating, and often die. Test for both first when appetite drops.
3. **Internal parasites.** Some intestinal parasites cause weight loss and loss of appetite.
4. **Illness of any kind.** A sick fish often has a poor appetite, which is also why medicated food can be hard to get into one.

## When to Worry

A well-fed ember school is outgoing, especially in a large group, and adult males show the most intense color, especially in spawning condition. The signs that need action:

- **Several fish off their food at once.** That points to the water. Test ammonia and nitrite, and keep testing until neither is measurable.
- **One fish thinning while the rest eat.** Weight loss and refusing food are signs of some internal parasites, which are among the [ember tetra health problems](/blog/ember-tetra-health-issues-guide/) that need a diagnosis before any medication goes in.
- **A red thread at the vent.** Camallanus shows as blood-red worms protruding from the vent, most likely in a tank fed live cyclops or daphnia.

## When You Go Away

A trip away needs a person to feed the fish or an automatic feeder, and each has a way to go wrong.

For a sitter, pre-portion every meal before you leave, for example in a labeled pill organizer with one compartment per feeding, so they cannot overfeed. Show them once, and leave your number. A well-meant extra pinch ends up rotting on the substrate like any other leftover.

For an automatic feeder, set it up and run it for several days before you leave, with a food it dispenses well, checking that it is not jamming or overfeeding.

A fish this small lives on what fits in its mouth, and the keeper who sizes every food to that mouth has most of ember tetra feeding solved.
