# Ember Tetra Feeding: Diet, Food Size, and How Often to Feed

<AffiliateDisclosure />

An ember tetra grows to about 0.8 inch (2 cm), and its mouth is built to match. Everything about feeding one follows from that: the food has to be tiny, it has to sink slowly enough to be caught, and each portion has to be small enough for the school to clear.

## What Embers Eat in the Wild

In the backwaters and oxbow lakes of Brazil's Araguaia River basin, the ember tetra is probably a micropredator, picking tiny invertebrates and other zooplankton out of the water. No one has examined the gut contents of wild embers, so that description is a reasoned guess about a fish this size. The captive diet below is built to fit it: small food, with live or frozen prey on the menu every day.

Like almost any fish, embers take small prey wherever they find it. They leave adult dwarf shrimp alone but will pick off baby shrimp, and dense plants are what give shrimplets somewhere to hide, one more reason the [ember tetra tank](/blog/ember-tetra-tank-setup-guide/) is planted heavily.

## The Staple Diet

Build the daily diet on a quality dried food sized for very small fish. A slow-sinking <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B004PBB5TK?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Hikari Tropical Semi-Floating Micro Pellets, 0.77 oz">micro-pellet</AffiliateLink> works, and so do flake and granules once they are crushed down to a fine crumb. Around that staple go small live and frozen foods: baby brine shrimp (Artemia nauplii), daphnia, cyclops and moina.

<KeyTakeaway>
Embers have little mouths and feed best on tiny, slow-sinking food. Choose the smallest micro-pellet grade, crush flake fine, and keep live and frozen foods at the size of baby brine shrimp and daphnia.
</KeyTakeaway>

## Live and Frozen Foods

For this species, live and frozen foods belong in the regular routine: small live or frozen fare every day, alongside the dried staple. Baby brine shrimp, daphnia, cyclops and moina are all small enough for an ember to swallow whole, and they are the closest match a keeper can buy to the zooplankton a wild ember probably hunts.

<FunFact>
The genus name Hyphessobrycon joins Greek words for "a little smaller" and "to bite" or "eat greedily". The scientist who named the genus never explained the "smaller", which probably refers to the size of the fish.
</FunFact>

## How Often to Feed, and How Much

Feed adults once or twice a day. Where the routine allows two meals, make one of them live or frozen food.

The portion matters more than the count. Each meal is a small pinch sized to the school, and the way to judge it is to watch the fish eat. If food drifts past them and settles on the substrate untouched, the next pinch is smaller. Food that sits uneaten breaks down into ammonia, the same waste the [nitrogen cycle](/blog/aquarium-cycling-guide/) in a mature tank has to clear.

Watching also tells you whether the embers are getting fed at all. They are very peaceful and do not compete well with boisterous or much larger tankmates, so in a mixed tank the faster fish can take most of a meal while the embers hang back. Choosing companions that pass [the checks two species need before sharing a tank](/blog/aquarium-stocking-and-tankmates-guide/), similar size and a calm temperament above all, is what keeps feeding time fair.

## Feeding Fry

Embers scatter their eggs and give no parental care, and adults will eat both eggs and newly hatched fry. Fry raised in a separate tank start on Paramecium or a dry fry food of 5 to 50 micron grade.

A fish this small lives on what fits in its mouth, and the keeper who sizes every food to that mouth has most of ember tetra feeding solved. The [ember tetra encyclopedia profile](/encyclopedia/animal/ember-tetra/) has the natural history.
