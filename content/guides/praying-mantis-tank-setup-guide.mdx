# Praying Mantis Enclosure Setup: Height, Humidity, and a Mesh Lid

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Mantis enclosures are small, cheap, and simple to set up correctly, once you understand why height matters more than any other dimension for this species. This covers the full setup, for what all of it costs, see our [Praying Mantis cost guide](/blog/praying-mantis-cost-guide/), or the complete [Praying Mantis care guide](/guides/praying-mantis/) for everything else.

## Enclosure Size

The rule of thumb: length and width should each be at least twice the mantis's body length, and height should be at least three times its length. That height requirement isn't arbitrary, it's directly tied to molting, a mantis needs enough vertical clearance to hang freely and shed its exoskeleton without touching the bottom or sides. A reasonable adult <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0041P5PBE?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Exo Terra Glass Nano Terrarium Kit, 8 x 8 x 12 in">enclosure</AffiliateLink> runs around 8 inches long, 8 inches wide, and 12 inches tall as a minimum. Nymphs do well in something much smaller, a vented 32-ounce deli cup works fine at that stage.

## Temperature

Most commonly kept species, Carolina and Chinese mantises among them, do fine at normal room temperature. A small heat mat is only necessary if your home runs genuinely cool. Never place a mantis enclosure in direct sunlight, the confined space heats up fast enough to be fatal.

## Humidity

Somewhere in the 60 to 80% range depending on the species, tropical species like Ghost and Orchid mantises need the higher end of that range, while more temperate species need less. Achieve this with <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4vPKMjX" product="Fine mist spray bottle">light misting</AffiliateLink> once or twice daily. This is genuinely a balancing act: too dry raises mismolt risk, too wet invites mold and the internal infection covered in our [Praying Mantis health issues guide](/blog/praying-mantis-health-issues-guide/). Watch your specific mantis's behavior and adjust rather than chasing an exact number blindly.

## Substrate

<AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CY23K32S?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="VCEPJH Loose Coconut Fiber Substrate">Coconut fiber</AffiliateLink>, plain soil, bark, or even paper towel all work well. Substrate here serves two purposes: giving the mantis stable footing and helping hold humidity at the level you're aiming for.

## Lighting

No special lighting or UVB is needed. A normal household light cycle, roughly 12 hours of light a day, is sufficient.

<FunFact>
Height is the one dimension a mantis enclosure genuinely can't skimp on. A mantis needs roughly three times its own body length in vertical clearance just to hang freely and shed its exoskeleton cleanly, far more than the horizontal space it needs day to day.
</FunFact>

## The Requirement That Actually Matters Most: A Mesh Lid and Real Climbing Space

Use a mesh or ventilated lid, never solid glass or plastic, airflow is essential to avoiding the mold and infection risk that comes with excess humidity. Provide <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3QQNV3L" product="Climbing branch (Mopani wood)">branches or mesh material</AffiliateLink> on the sides for the mantis to climb and, critically, to hang upside-down from when it molts. This is genuinely one branch or panel doing double duty, not two separate purchases, the same surface the mantis climbs day to day is what it hangs from during a molt. What actually matters for a clean molt is the enclosure's vertical clearance above that perch, not a second dedicated object. Leave genuine clearance above the highest perch, roughly twice the mantis's body height, so it has room to shed cleanly without anything obstructing the process.

A pair of <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DCJXXMCT?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Entomology Forceps Set (fine-tip)">fine-tip feeding tongs</AffiliateLink> is also worth having on hand, they make it easy to place feeder insects precisely without disturbing the branch or substrate, and to pull a live insect back out quickly if you need to clear the enclosure before a molt. Our [Praying Mantis handling guide](/blog/praying-mantis-handling-guide/) covers exactly why that matters.

The overall approach, low fuss, no complex lighting, but real attention to height and moisture, mirrors a lot of what works for an [emperor scorpion enclosure](/blog/emperor-scorpion-tank-setup-guide/) too, both husbandry systems live or die on getting humidity and a safe molting space right.
