# Bristlenose Pleco Feeding: Diet, Driftwood, and the Algae Myth

<AffiliateDisclosure />

A bristlenose pleco is sold as a living algae solution, and for a young fish in a new tank, that's fair enough. As it grows, that reputation quietly stops being true. What it actually needs is a real diet, plus one piece of tank furniture that doubles as food.

## Feeding Schedule

Feed once a day, ideally after lights-out since this is a nocturnal species that does most of its grazing overnight. Offer a wafer or vegetable portion the fish clears within a few hours, and remove anything still untouched after 12 hours rather than letting it foul the water. Dropping food near a favorite cave or driftwood perch helps a shy individual find it without competing in open water.

## What a Bristlenose Pleco Eats

Build the diet around <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00025JZAY?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Hikari Tropical Sinking Wafers for Catfish, Loaches and Bottom Feeders, 3.88 oz">sinking algae wafers</AffiliateLink> formulated for catfish and other bottom feeders. Supplement several times a week with blanched zucchini, cucumber, spinach, or sweet potato, and add a small amount of protein, frozen bloodworms or brine shrimp, once a week or so. A rough target worth aiming for is around 85% plant matter to 15% protein, this species stays a primarily plant-based grazer even as an adult, unlike a more omnivorous bottom feeder such as the [corydoras catfish](/blog/corydoras-catfish-feeding-guide/).

## Portions: What 85/15 Looks Like in a Week

A percentage is hard to measure at the tank, so turn it into nights. A workable week for one adult is algae wafers on four nights, blanched vegetables on two, and a protein treat on one. One protein night in seven lands close to the 15% target without weighing anything.

- **Wafer nights:** one wafer per adult pleco each evening, adjusted by how fast it disappears. A wafer still sitting there in the morning means the next one should be smaller.
- **Vegetable nights:** a round of zucchini or a slice of cucumber, blanched for 30 to 60 seconds so it sinks, held down on a vegetable clip or under a plant weight.
- **Protein night:** frozen bloodworms, sinking shrimp pellets or blanched prawn. A single cube of frozen bloodworm, split across the tank, is plenty for two or three adults.

## The Driftwood Question: Diet, Not Just Decor

<AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eR0Z2l" product="Driftwood">Driftwood</AffiliateLink> is standard advice for a bristlenose pleco for a real reason: it rasps wood constantly and instinctively, and most care guides tie this behavior to healthy digestion, fiber and the biofilm colonizing the wood's surface both appear to matter. A tank without any driftwood at all is associated with digestive problems even in an otherwise well-fed fish. [How much wood counts as enough](/blog/bristlenose-pleco-enrichment-guide/) is the part that gets skipped: one small ornament piece is a decoration, not a provision.

<FunFact>
The hard science on wood-digesting fish is actually clearest in a different group of plecos entirely. Panaque and the Hypostomus cochliodon group are the catfish confirmed to rely on wood almost exclusively, and even there, researchers now think the fish are mostly extracting nutrition from microbes living on the wood rather than the wood itself. A bristlenose is a grazer first, but it still gnaws driftwood on instinct and does measurably better with a piece in the tank.
</FunFact>

## The Algae Myth, and Why It's Easy to Believe

This is the single most common and most damaging feeding mistake with this species. A juvenile bristlenose in a new tank really can look like it's living entirely off algae film, which is exactly what makes the myth so convincing. Tank algae rarely grows fast enough to keep pace with a full-grown adult, and a bristlenose left to fend for itself on algae and other fish's leftovers gradually loses condition rather than failing suddenly, which makes the problem easy to miss until it's fairly advanced.

## Why a Bristlenose Pleco Stops Eating or Loses Weight

1. **Malnutrition from an algae-only diet.** A sunken belly is the clearest visible sign, and it's the most common cause on this list by far.
2. **Poor water quality.** Elevated ammonia or nitrite causes real stress and appetite loss, worth testing for directly rather than guessing.
3. **Missing driftwood.** Digestive trouble from a bare tank can look like reduced appetite even when food intake hasn't actually changed.
4. **Stress or illness.** Both suppress appetite, and either is worth ruling out once diet and water quality check out.

## When to Worry

A sunken, concave belly alongside normal or increased feeding activity points toward a diet that's heavier on algae than it should be, add wafers and vegetables deliberately rather than assuming the tank is handling it. If appetite loss shows up alongside lethargy, clamped fins, or visible sores, test water parameters immediately, poor water quality is one of the most common and most fixable causes behind a pleco going off food.
