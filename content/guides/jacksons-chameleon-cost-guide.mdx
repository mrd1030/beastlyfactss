# How Much Does a Jackson's Chameleon Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Jackson's chameleons sit in a comparable price range to the [veiled chameleon](/blog/veiled-chameleon-cost-guide/) already covered on this site, but the two species diverge in some genuinely important ways worth understanding before you buy. This species is also part of our recent [Jackson's Chameleon, Canary, Giant Millipede & Corydoras Catfish compared](/blog/jacksonschameleon-canary-millipede-corydoras-overview/) roundup, worth a look if you're weighing very different kinds of pets against each other.

## The Chameleon: $50 to $250

Common animals typically run $90 to $150, with prized, highly colored specimens of the primary subspecies reaching $250. Wild-caught Hawaiian animals sometimes show up cheaper, but they're frequently parasitized, captive-bred is strongly worth the higher price for this species.

<FunFact>
Wild-caught Jackson's chameleons sometimes turn up from Hawaii at a lower price than captive-bred animals. The catch is they're frequently parasitized, which is exactly why paying more for a captive-bred animal is worth it for this species specifically.
</FunFact>

## Ongoing Costs: Roughly $50 to $100 a Month

Feeder insects, supplements, live plants, and electricity for misting and lighting equipment make up the monthly total, bringing annual costs to somewhere around $1,200 including occasional larger expenses. ## Vet Costs

A routine exam commonly runs $50 to $100 or more. Emergencies, gout, metabolic bone disease, or birth complications among them, can run into the hundreds.

## Lifespan: Genuinely Different by Sex

This is where Jackson's chameleons diverge sharply from the veiled chameleon already covered on this site, and for a completely different reason than that species' sex-based lifespan gap. Males commonly live 8 to 10 years. Females live considerably shorter, typically 3 to 5 years, tied to the physical toll of giving birth to live young repeatedly, more on this in the [handling guide](/blog/jacksons-chameleon-handling-guide/).

## The Comparison Worth Understanding: Jackson's vs. Veiled

Both species need minimal handling and modest but real ongoing investment. The genuine differences are biological rather than financial, Jackson's chameleons give live birth rather than laying eggs, and come from a cooler, higher-altitude climate in East Africa, meaning their temperature needs run noticeably cooler than the [veiled chameleon's](/blog/veiled-chameleon-tank-setup-guide/), covered in full in the tank setup guide. If you're choosing between the two, temperature requirements and reproductive biology, not cost, are really what should drive the decision.
