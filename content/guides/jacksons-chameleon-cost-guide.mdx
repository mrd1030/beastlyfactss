# How Much Does a Jackson's Chameleon Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Jackson's chameleons sit in a comparable price range to the veiled chameleon, but the two species diverge in some important ways worth understanding before you buy.

## The Chameleon: $50 to $250

Common animals typically run $90 to $150, with prized, highly colored specimens of the primary subspecies reaching $250. Wild-caught Hawaiian animals sometimes show up cheaper, but Hawaii bars commercial export to the mainland and they're frequently parasitized, so captive-bred is strongly worth the higher price for this species.

## Where a Jackson's Comes From

Most Jackson's chameleons for sale in the US trace back to Hawaii, where the species was introduced from Kenya and Tanzania in 1972 and now runs feral. Hawaii treats it as injurious wildlife: it is illegal to move one between islands or to export one commercially to the mainland, with fines up to $200,000 and possible prison time. So a legal Hawaiian animal does not reach the mainland trade. A wild-caught Hawaiian Jackson's on the mainland today either left before the rule or broke it, and the rule has not stopped the flow: most animals sold in the US still arrive that way. The Hawaiian population also descends from roughly 36 founders, and the inbreeding shows as smaller bodies and crooked horns.

Captive-bred animals are the ones worth the higher price. They come in small numbers, from breeders who hold their babies until 4 to 6 months old before selling, because losses in this species tend to cluster in that window. Captive-bred lines from African stock cost more again and bring better genetics. A tiny baby offered cheap is a warning sign: it is often pulled from an importer's holding facility rather than raised by a breeder. Before paying, ask the seller how old the animal is, whether it was born in captivity, and whether a fecal test has been done, since wild-caught animals are frequently parasitized. The healthy-animal checks and the quarantine that follows any new reptile are laid out in the [reptile quarantine guide](/blog/reptile-quarantine-guide/).

## Ongoing Costs: Roughly $50 to $100 a Month

Feeder insects, supplements, live plants, and electricity for misting and lighting equipment make up the monthly total, bringing annual costs to somewhere around $1,200 including occasional larger expenses. A calcium supplement is part of that recurring spend, and [getting the dose right matters](/blog/jacksons-chameleon-health-issues-guide/), since this is a species where over-supplementing causes its own problems.

## Vet Costs

A routine exam commonly runs $50 to $100 or more. Emergencies, gout, metabolic bone disease, or birth complications among them, can run into the hundreds.

## Lifespan: Different by Sex

This is where Jackson's chameleons diverge sharply from the veiled chameleon, and for a completely different reason than that species' sex-based lifespan gap. Males commonly live 8 to 10 years. Females live considerably shorter, typically 3 to 5 years, tied to the physical toll of giving birth to live young repeatedly.

## The Comparison Worth Understanding: Jackson's vs. Veiled

Both species need minimal handling and modest but real ongoing investment. The genuine differences are biological rather than financial, Jackson's chameleons give live birth rather than laying eggs, and come from a cooler, higher-altitude climate in East Africa, meaning their temperature needs run noticeably cooler than the [veiled chameleon's](/blog/veiled-chameleon-tank-setup-guide/). If you're choosing between the two, temperature requirements and reproductive biology, not cost, are really what should drive the decision.
