# Four Pets Decided Before You Ever Set Up the Tank

Each of these has one decision that matters more than anything in the care sheet, and it happens before or at the moment of purchase, not after. Get that one thing right and the rest of the care is manageable. Get it wrong, and no amount of good husbandry fixes it later.

## Sulcata Tortoise: The Decision Is Your Outdoor Space, Years From Now

A hatchling sulcata is small, cheap, and easy. An adult is 80 to 110 pounds, lives 50 to 70 years, 70 or more with good care, and needs real outdoor space within just a few years of growth. The decision that matters isn't what enclosure to buy today, it's whether you'll have the yard, the shelter budget, and the multi-decade plan this species actually requires.

The wild animal explains the yard. The sulcata, or African spurred tortoise, is the largest mainland tortoise in the world, outsized only by the island giants of Aldabra and the Galápagos, and it survives surface heat near 120°F by digging dens up to 10 feet deep. A pet keeps that instinct, which is why fencing has to go down as well as up. It also matures slowly, at about 15 years, so most of a sulcata's life is spent at full adult size.

## Hedgehog: The Decision Is Your Zip Code

Before cage size, before diet, before anything else: is a hedgehog even legal where you live? Banned outright in several states, permit-only in New Jersey, and sometimes banned at the city level even where the state allows it. This is the one pet of the four where the first real decision is a legal one, not a husbandry one.

Once the law is settled, the rest of the decision is about the animal's schedule. African pygmy hedgehogs are nocturnal and prefer a quiet, dim room, and they are solitary, housed one to an enclosure because males in particular fight. The commitment is short by mammal standards, a mean lifespan of 3 to 5 years, and it carries one health risk worth knowing before purchase: wobbly hedgehog syndrome, a progressive paralysis that affects about 3% of captive African hedgehogs in North America.

## Lovebird: The Decision Is One Bird or Two

A single lovebird bonds to you. A pair bonds to each other. Same species, same price range, genuinely different pet depending on which you choose, and it's a choice made at purchase, not something you can undo easily once two birds have bonded to one another instead of to you.

The same small bird brings a big attitude either way. Lovebirds are only 5 to 6.5 inches long, yet they will chase much larger birds and even the family dog or cat, and they turn territorial and aggressive when frightened, so a lovebird is a poor candidate for a mixed aviary with a [budgie](/blog/budgie-handling-guide/). One bird or two, the cage minimum is 2 feet wide by 2 feet tall by 3 feet long, and the bird or birds will be in it for 10 to 15 years, up to 20.

## Guppy: The Decision Is Males, Females, or Both

The cheapest pet of the four, and the one most likely to multiply past what you planned for. A female stores sperm for months and can produce broods long after a male is gone. Decide upfront: separate sexes, males only, or a plan for the fry, because "both, and see what happens" isn't really a plan.

The arithmetic is fast. A female guppy produces a brood roughly once a month and is receptive again within a day or two of giving birth. In one study of mated females, broods averaged 12 to 14 fry, and a female's lifetime total came to around 100 offspring. A single female in a community tank is a population, not a fish.

Line up the timelines and the spread is almost absurd: a guppy might not see its third birthday, while a well-kept sulcata tortoise can outlive the person who bought it by decades. Two pets sold in the same kind of shop, on completely opposite ends of the commitment scale.

## The Common Thread

None of these four fail because of bad daily care. They fail because of a decision made once, at the very start, that nobody flagged as important at the time: the growth trajectory, the legal status, the pairing, the sex ratio. Make that one call correctly, and each of these turns out to be a genuinely manageable, rewarding pet.
