# Stick Insect Enrichment: Fresh Leaves and Room to Hang

<AffiliateDisclosure />

This is the shortest enrichment guide on the site, and that is the honest answer rather than a gap. For everything else about them, see our complete [Stick Insect care guide](/guides/stick-insect/).

<KeyTakeaway>
A stick insect's behavioural repertoire is standing on a plant, eating it, swaying, and periodically moulting. Two of those four have hard requirements: **fresh live host plant** and **clear vertical space of at least three times body length** to moult in. Get those right and there is very little left to add.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Research Actually Says

Nothing on phasmids. The borrow is from the tarantula housing study, covered in full in our [Tarantula enrichment guide](/blog/tarantula-enrichment-guide/).

Tarantulas given an enclosure roughly six times larger with six times as much structure used **77 percent larger home ranges**, showed **150 percent greater odds of being active in a novel environment** afterwards, were **no more active overall**, and expressed **no preference** between the two housings.

That is a long way from a leaf-eating insect and it is the only controlled invertebrate housing experiment available. Its most useful contribution here is a warning against overreach: an invertebrate that does very little is not necessarily an invertebrate that needs more to do.

<FunFact>
Many stick insects reproduce parthenogenetically, so a single female bought as a pet will quietly produce fertile eggs with no male involved. That is worth knowing before the enclosure fills up, since a group of stick insects becomes a larger group of stick insects without any decision from you.
</FunFact>

## Height, for Moulting

The one hard number. A tall <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0041P5PBE?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Exo Terra Glass Nano Terrarium Kit, 8 x 8 x 12 in">nano terrarium</AffiliateLink> or a taller mesh enclosure, with clear vertical drop below the ceiling of at least three times the insect's body length.

The ceiling has to be grippable. Mesh or screen, not smooth glass. A stick insect that loses its grip mid-moult is usually finished, and the fix costs nothing.

Bear in mind adults of some species are long. The rule scales with the animal, so a large species needs a genuinely tall enclosure. Our [tank setup guide](/blog/stick-insect-tank-setup-guide/) covers dimensions by species, and our [moulting guide](/blog/invertebrate-molting-guide/) covers the process.

## Live Host Plant, Fresh

The feeding half and the enrichment half at once. Species vary, and bramble suits most of the commonly kept ones.

Live cut stems standing in water beat picked leaves in a pile, because they stay fresh for days and give the insects something to climb and hang on. Block the opening of the water container with cotton wool or foil, since insects fall in and drown otherwise, and this is a routine avoidable loss.

Replace before the leaves dry. A stick insect will not eat crisped foliage, and this is the commonest reason a group stops thriving.

## Humidity and Ventilation

Moulting fails in dry air as reliably as it fails in a short enclosure. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4vPKMjX" product="Fine mist spray bottle">fine mist spray bottle</AffiliateLink> once or twice a day, with the enclosure drying between mistings and real airflow.

Stagnant damp is the other failure mode and causes mould and disease. Species differ, and every one of them wants ventilation. Our [health issues guide](/blog/stick-insect-health-issues-guide/) covers both ends.

## Something on the Floor

A layer of <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CY23K32S?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="VCEPJH Loose Coconut Fiber Substrate, 2 Pack Natural Coco Coir Reptile Bedding">coconut fibre substrate</AffiliateLink> holds humidity and gives eggs somewhere to sit, and other sizes and volumes are available.

Keep it modest. This is a canopy animal and the floor is where eggs land rather than where the insect lives.

## A Group, With Space

They are not predatory or territorial and most species are kept in groups happily. The limits are space and food supply rather than temperament.

Overcrowding shows as damaged legs and antennae, and stick insects knock pieces off each other during moults in a crowded enclosure. If limbs are going missing, thin the group or enlarge the enclosure.

## Priority Order

1. Clear vertical space of at least three times body length
2. A grippable mesh ceiling
3. Fresh live host plant, replaced before it dries
4. Water container openings blocked so nothing drowns
5. Humidity with real ventilation
6. Enough space that a group is not knocking each other apart
7. Nothing else, and that is genuinely the list

## What Not to Do

Do not use a short enclosure, which is the mistake that causes deformities and deaths. Do not leave a smooth ceiling. Do not leave an open water container in with them. Do not feed dried or wilted foliage. Do not seal the enclosure to hold humidity. Do not overcrowd. And see our [handling guide](/blog/stick-insect-handling-guide/) before picking one up, since legs come off far more easily than people expect and do not always grow back.
