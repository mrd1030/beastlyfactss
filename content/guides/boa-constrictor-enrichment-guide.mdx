# Boa Enrichment: A Big Snake in a Box Is Still a Snake in a Box

The rack argument is usually had about ball pythons. It applies harder to boas, who get bigger, climb more, and have no research of their own. For everything else about them, see our complete [Boa Constrictor care guide](/guides/boa-constrictor/).

<KeyTakeaway>
Nothing has been published on boa enrichment. The closest evidence is the ball python housing study, which compared 35 snakes in racks against furnished terrariums and concluded racks are not a welfare alternative. Boas are larger and more arboreal, which makes that conclusion harder to argue with here, not easier.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Research Actually Says

Nothing on *Boa constrictor*. The nearest evidence, covered in full in our [Ball Python enrichment guide](/blog/ball-python-enrichment-guide/), compared **35 ball pythons in a conventional rack system against furnished terrariums** containing substrate, hiding places, climbing opportunities, a water basin large enough for bathing, elevated basking spots and living plants. The authors concluded that from a welfare perspective, rack systems are not an alternative to furnished terrariums.

A 2025 study on eastern indigo snakes looked at the effects of light and novel enrichment on behavior and physiology, and Hutchings reviewed the spatial needs of captive snakes generally.

<FunFact>
The furnished enclosures in the ball python study deliberately included climbing structure, for a species usually described as terrestrial. Boas climb considerably more than ball pythons do, particularly as juveniles, so the part of that study that transfers best is the part it was least designed to test.
</FunFact>

## Climbing, Which Boas Actually Need

Wild boas use trees regularly and juveniles are notably arboreal. Most commercial boa setups give them a floor and nothing else.

Boas are heavy and not agile, so stability is the requirement: something they can brace coils against that will not shift or roll.

## Enclosure Size, Which Gets Worse With Age

The uncomfortable part of keeping this species. A boa that outgrows its enclosure usually gets moved into a tub, because tubs are what exist at that size and are what fit in a room.

That is the scenario the housing research argues against, and the argument gets stronger as the snake gets bigger rather than weaker. If you cannot house an adult properly, that is a decision to make before buying rather than after. Our [tank setup guide](/blog/boa-constrictor-tank-setup-guide/) covers adult dimensions honestly.

## Cover Across the Floor

Two hides is a starting point. Cork, leaf litter and planting that break the whole floor into cover mean the snake can be anywhere without being exposed, rather than choosing between two boxes.

## Water Big Enough to Get Into

The furnished condition in the ball python study included a bathing basin, and for a snake this size that means a serious container. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eKyN0W" product="Large soak-able water dish">dish the animal can fully enter</AffiliateLink> supports shedding and hydration and is entirely self-directed. Our [health issues guide](/blog/boa-constrictor-health-issues-guide/) covers what a bad shed indicates.

## Priority Order

1. An enclosure sized for the adult, decided before purchase
2. Heavy stable climbing structure
3. Cover across the entire floor
4. A bathing-sized water container
5. Scent novelty and varied feeding presentation
6. Rearrangement, last

## What Not to Do

Do not plan to move an adult boa into a tub, since that is the housing the only comparable study argues against. Do not provide climbing structure that shifts under weight, which teaches the snake not to use it. Do not treat feeding response as a reason to keep the enclosure sparse, and see our [handling guide](/blog/boa-constrictor-handling-guide/) for safe interaction with a large constrictor. Do not present ball python findings as boa findings. And check our [legal guide](/blog/boa-constrictor-legal-guide/), since large constrictors are restricted in more places than people expect.
