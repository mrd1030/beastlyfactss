# Mouse Enrichment: Give Them Far More Nesting Material Than You Think

<AffiliateDisclosure />

The mouse is the most studied mammal in the world, and the single most useful finding for a pet keeper is about a bag of shredded paper.

<KeyTakeaway>
A mouse in a room you find comfortable is below its own comfort range. Given enough nesting material it builds its way out of the problem, and the research puts the useful amount at **six to ten grams**, which is considerably more than most pet cages ever contain.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Research Says

Brianna Gaskill's work on laboratory mice is the relevant thread, and it translates directly to a pet cage.

**Mice are cold at room temperature.** Standard laboratory housing runs at 20 to 24 degrees Celsius. A mouse's lower critical temperature is around 30, and given a free choice mice select **26 to 29 degrees**. Your living room is not a neutral environment for them.

**Nesting material is how they solve it.** Given material, mice shift from seeking out warm spots to building. That switch happens **between six and ten grams** of material, and the recommendation that came out of the work is no less than six grams, up to ten where the ambient temperature is low.

**Nest quality is a readable signal.** Nest scoring is used to detect thermal stress, illness, pain and aggression between cage mates. A mouse that suddenly stops building well is telling you something.

<FunFact>
The elegant part of this result is that it hands the problem to the animal. Rather than fixing the room at one temperature, you give the mouse the raw material and it constructs the microclimate it wants. That is choice and control, which is what enrichment is supposed to mean.
</FunFact>

## Nesting Material, Generously

The item with the strongest evidence behind it and the easiest one to get wrong by underdoing it.

Plain <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4voH9Ap" product="Small-pet paper bedding">paper bedding</AffiliateLink> plus shreddable plain paper and cardboard gives a mouse enough to build a proper domed nest instead of a scrape. Other sizes and volumes are available. Add more than looks necessary, and let them rearrange it.

Never use fluffy commercial nesting fiber, the cotton wool style product. It is an entanglement and ingestion hazard and it is still widely sold. Plain paper does the job better.

## Read the Nest

Free, and genuinely useful. Learn what your mouse's normal nest looks like, then treat a sudden decline as a prompt to check the animal.

That is how laboratories use it, and it works just as well for one mouse in a bedroom.

## Climbing and Levels

Mice climb constantly and use vertical space far more than most people expect. Ropes, branches, ladders, multiple levels and things to get on top of turn a floor into a three dimensional space.

Height is only useful if it is filled. An empty tall cage is a floor with wasted air above it.

## Foraging Instead of a Bowl

Scatter feeding is the single easiest change. Mice forage, and pouring the day's food into a dish removes all of it.

Scatter dry food through the substrate, hide it in cardboard tubes and paper parcels, put part of it behind a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DPKP3JBX?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Hamiledyi 3 Pack Guinea Pig Foraging Toys, wooden enrichment puzzle">wooden foraging puzzle</AffiliateLink>, and vary where it goes. It costs nothing and turns eating from a thirty second event into most of an evening.

## Chewing

Continuously growing incisors. <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0G41RJPZJ?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Rabbit Chew Toys, Toys for Guinea Pigs and Hamsters, Natural Chew Sticks and Balls Set">Chew sticks</AffiliateLink>, untreated wood and cardboard cover it, and cardboard doubles as nest material.

## Company, With One Caveat

Female mice are social and should be kept in same-sex groups. They do noticeably better with company and it is not a close call.

Adult males are a different problem. They frequently fight seriously, and solitary housing is the common answer. That is a genuine welfare compromise rather than a preference, and it is a reason to think carefully before choosing males.

## Priority Order

1. Six to ten grams of plain, shreddable nesting material
2. A hideout to build the nest inside
3. Female group housing
4. Scatter feeding, never a bowl
5. Climbing structure and levels, filled
6. Chewing material
7. Novelty and rearrangement, last, since a destroyed nest is a cost

## What Not to Do

Do not provide a token pinch of bedding when the tested amount is six to ten grams. Do not use fluffy commercial nesting fiber, which is an entanglement risk. Do not house a female alone. Do not assume adult males can be introduced. Do not strip the whole cage at once during cleaning, since removing the nest resets the thermal work the mouse has done.
