# How to Handle a Sulcata Tortoise (While You Still Can)

Sulcata tortoises are personable and curious, many hand-feed readily and clearly recognize their keeper over time. There's an honest caveat worth stating upfront though: real handling, picking the animal up, is only practical while it's still a juvenile.

## The Size Problem

An adult sulcata weighs 80 to 110 pounds, and males can reach 200. That's well beyond what most people can comfortably or safely lift. Interacting with an adult sulcata means spending time in its space, hand-feeding, and letting it approach and investigate you, not picking it up the way you might a smaller tortoise. Our [Sulcata Tortoise cost guide](/blog/sulcata-tortoise-cost-guide/) covers just how much space and infrastructure that adult body size actually demands.

Everything else about keeping this species is in our complete [Sulcata Tortoise care guide](/guides/sulcata-tortoise/).

## Handling a Juvenile Correctly

Keep hatchlings and young juveniles low to the ground when you do pick them up. Sulcatas push off powerfully with their strong legs, and a fall, even a short one, risks cracking the shell. Never flip a tortoise onto its back.

<FunFact>
A hatchling sulcata is small enough to hold in one hand, but an adult can weigh 80 to 110 pounds, males occasionally reach 200, and can live 70 to 100 years. Almost nothing else sold as a pocket-sized pet grows into something so large, or outlives its owner so routinely.
</FunFact>

## First-Timer Mistakes

- Underestimating adult size when choosing an enclosure or planning long-term space. This is the single biggest reason sulcatas end up surrendered to rescues.
- Housing two mature males together. They're territorial, and adult males will fight, causing real shell injuries and chronic stress.
- Treating a young tortoise's manageable size as representative of what's coming. Growth in the first 5 to 10 years is rapid.

## Behavior Worth Knowing About

Sulcatas are strong, dedicated burrowers, expect extensive digging and tunneling wherever they're housed, our [Sulcata Tortoise enclosure setup guide](/blog/sulcata-tortoise-tank-setup-guide/) covers how to plan substrate depth around that instinct. They also ram and bulldoze objects, and sometimes each other, as a dominance display, this is normal behavior, not aggression toward you specifically.

## Telling Males From Females

Sexing a sulcata by eye isn't reliable until it's several years old and around 10 to 15 inches in shell length, younger juveniles show no dependable external differences. At that size, check the plastron, the bottom shell: concave on males, flat on females. The tail is the other reliable marker, longer with a spur at the tip on males, short and blunt on females. Compare more than one trait rather than relying on just one, individual features can be misleading on their own.

## How This Compares to Smaller Tortoises

A Russian or Hermann's tortoise can live comfortably in a large indoor tortoise table for its entire life. A sulcata is a genuinely different category of commitment, requiring an 8-by-8-foot indoor minimum as a juvenile and several hundred square feet outdoors as an adult. If you're drawn to a pet you can pick up and interact with hands-on for its whole life, rather than just its first few years, a hedgehog is a much closer match, see our [Hedgehog handling guide](/blog/hedgehog-handling-guide/) for what that looks like. If you're drawn to tortoises specifically but not prepared for this scale, a smaller species is worth strong consideration instead.
