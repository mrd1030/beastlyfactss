# Tarantula Feeding: Schedule, Diet, and Why They Stop Eating

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Weeks without food is often premolt. That is the rule on this species.

## Feeding schedule by life stage

- **Slings (under about 2 inches):** every 2 to 3 days. Pinhead crickets, fruit flies, prey no larger than the abdomen.
- **Juveniles (about 2 to 4 inches):** every 7 days to start. If the abdomen stays fat, stretch toward 14 days. If it looks thin, stay nearer 4 to 7.
- **Subadults and adults:** every 1 to 2 weeks. A Chilean rose hair may only need four or five crickets a month. A fast tropical species can eat more than once a week.
- **Mature adult males:** after the final molt many eat little or nothing. That is the end-of-life pattern, not a mystery illness.

Prey no larger than the abdomen. Pull uneaten live prey inside 24 hours. A cricket will chew a soft new molt.

## What tarantulas eat

Live insects. Crickets as the default. Dubia or discoid for larger spiders. Mealworms and superworms as extras; crush the head on the large ones. Waxworms rarely. Largest species can take a pinky. Not required.

Gut-load a day or two. Do not dust with calcium or vitamins.

They inject enzymes, liquefy the prey, and leave the empty shell. Offer prey live and whole.

## Safe treats and foods to avoid

Staples: crickets, dubia, discoid, mealworms, superworms with the head crushed.

Treats: waxworms.

Never: wild-caught insects, fireflies, human food.

## Why tarantulas stop eating

1. **Premolt.** Dark, plump abdomen, less movement. Slings a few weeks. Adults several months.
2. **Post-molt.** Soft fangs. Wait 3 to 7 days for slings and juveniles, 7 to 14 for adults.
3. **Mature male drive.** After the last molt.
4. **A new enclosure.**
5. **Species habit.** Rose hairs fast for no obvious reason.
6. **Prey size or type.**
7. **Illness, injury, dehydration.** Death curl: legs tucked under. That is not premolt.

## When to worry

A plump, moving spider refusing food for weeks is premolt. A juvenile off food more than 6 to 8 weeks with a shrinking abdomen needs a look. Adults in premolt can go months.

The emergency is a shriveled abdomen and legs that will not hold the body up. Shallow water now. Check water and humidity first. See an exotics vet if refusal comes with lethargy, injury, no drinking, a death curl, or that juvenile window with weight loss.

They cannot be dusted, so gut-loading is the only nutrition path. The [gut-loading guide](/blog/gut-loading-feeder-insects-guide/) is that page.
