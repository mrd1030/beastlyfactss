# Common Sulcata Tortoise Health Issues and What Causes Them

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Sulcata tortoises are hardy animals, and most of what shows up on this list traces back to a small number of husbandry factors, several of which are genuinely debated even among specialists, worth knowing about rather than treating as settled fact. ## Shell Pyramiding

The most common issue keepers actually encounter. Watch for scutes that grow raised and cone-shaped instead of lying smooth and flat. This is permanent once it happens in a growing tortoise, prevention is the only real strategy.

What causes it is genuinely debated. One well-known study found that low humidity during growth was the key driver, with dietary protein playing only a minor role (Wiesner & Iben, 2003). A separate study found that supplemental night heat and faster growth rates caused pyramiding even when humidity stayed constant, suggesting growth rate itself matters too (Heinrich & Heinrich, 2016). The practical takeaway either way: humid hides for growing tortoises and a properly paced diet both matter, and getting both right is your best defense.

<FunFact>
Two separate studies on sulcata shell pyramiding reached genuinely different conclusions, one pointed to low humidity as the key driver, the other to growth rate and supplemental night heat. Specialists still don't fully agree, which is exactly why prevention, not a single fix, is the standard advice.
</FunFact>

## Shell Rot

A bacterial or fungal infection causing discoloration, a foul smell, loose scutes, and sometimes visible fluid, from substrate that's too wet or general poor hygiene. Left unaddressed it can become systemic, causing lethargy and appetite loss. Always see a vet. Keeping substrate dry rather than damp, covered in our [Sulcata Tortoise enclosure setup guide](/blog/sulcata-tortoise-tank-setup-guide/), is most of the prevention here.

## Shell Fractures

The reptile equivalent of a broken bone, and it needs the same urgency. Immediate vet care and pain management matter here, don't wait to see if it improves.

## Outdoor-Specific Risks Worth Knowing

A tortoise housed outdoors faces hazards that don't come up indoors. Escape through digging, fencing needs to be buried at least 12 inches deep, and <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4aCGvb1" product="Escape-proof outdoor fencing">escape-proof fencing</AffiliateLink> built to that spec from the start saves you from finding out the hard way. Overheating on concrete in direct sun with no shade available is another real risk, and yard chemicals, fertilizers, pesticides, and snail bait are all toxic and worth eliminating entirely from any area your tortoise has access to.

## The Husbandry Pattern Worth Remembering

Low humidity during the growing years is strongly linked to pyramiding, the single most common cosmetic and structural issue in this species. Wet substrate causes shell rot. Missing UVB and calcium cause metabolic bone disease. And an under-prepared outdoor space introduces real physical hazards.
