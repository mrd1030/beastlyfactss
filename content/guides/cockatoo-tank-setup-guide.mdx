# Cockatoo Cage Setup: Size, Material, and Daily Enrichment That Actually Matters

<AffiliateDisclosure />

A cockatoo's cage needs to support a large, powerful, intelligent, and emotionally demanding bird, and getting the size, material, and daily enrichment right matters as much as anything else in this species' care.

<Figure src="/assets/images/cockatoo-tank-setup-guide-1.jpg" alt="Labeled diagram of a cockatoo cage showing stainless steel construction, a rooftop play stand, and massive daily enrichment" caption="Getting the size, material, and daily enrichment right matters as much as anything else in this species' care." shareable />

## Cage Size

36 inches wide by 24 inches deep by 48 inches tall is a commonly cited minimum, with many avian vets recommending closer to 48 by 48 by 60 inches as a genuinely comfortable size. Bigger is always better with this species. A cage top that opens up to connect with a play stand is an ideal setup, giving your bird more usable space throughout the day.

## Bar Spacing and Security

Three-quarters of an inch to 1 inch bar spacing for large cockatoos. Secure, genuinely escape-proof locks matter more here than with most birds, cockatoos are notorious escape artists with the intelligence and strength to work out standard latches.

## Lighting

Full-spectrum or UV lighting is commonly recommended for indoor parrots. Just as important: 10 to 12 hours of genuine darkness and quiet for sleep every night. Sleep deprivation in this species measurably worsens screaming and other behavioral issues, this isn't a minor detail to skip.

## Bathing

A cockatoo sheds powder down every day, and bathing is what keeps it on the bird. Offer a bath daily, in the morning so the feathers are dry by night: lukewarm water from a plant mister as a gentle rain, or the bird on a suction-cup perch at the back of the shower. A daily mist or shower is the step that stops powder from spreading through the house, which matters for the people breathing it as much as for the bird, and the rest of the [feather dust](/blog/bird-feather-dust-air-quality-guide/) routine builds on it. A cockatoo that will not bathe on its own still gets a gentle spritz once or twice a week.
