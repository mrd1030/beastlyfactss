# How Much Does a White's Tree Frog Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

White's tree frogs, sometimes called dumpy tree frogs for their round, waxy-skinned build, are one of the more affordable and forgiving amphibians to keep, both to buy and to maintain long term. For everything else about day-to-day care, see our complete [White's Tree Frog care guide](/guides/whites-tree-frog/).

## The Frog Itself: $20 to $60

Standard animals run $20 to $60, with blue and other designer color morphs commanding a bit more. Captive-bred animals are widely available and are the better choice over wild-caught.

## Vet Costs

An exotic or amphibian-experienced vet exam commonly runs $50 to $150, based on published exotic-clinic pricing. A dedicated exotic emergency visit fee alone often starts around $250 at specialty hospitals, before any treatment. Amphibian veterinary care has real limitations, prevention through correct husbandry does more work here than treatment ever will, a theme that comes up throughout this species' care and is covered in full in our [White's Tree Frog health issues guide](/blog/whites-tree-frog-health-issues-guide/).

## Lifespan: Longer Than Most People Expect

The Smithsonian's National Zoo states the average lifespan is about 16 years, with one individual recorded living 21 years in human care. Other sources cite a slightly more conservative 10 to 15-plus years with excellent care. Either way, this is a genuinely long-term amphibian commitment, closer to a decade and a half than the year or two some people assume for a frog.

## The Bottom Line

White's tree frogs are one of the more budget-friendly, low-maintenance amphibians available, cheap to acquire, modest to maintain, and hardy compared to many frogs and toads. The main financial risk isn't the routine cost, it's the long runway. Budget for 10 to 15-plus years of ongoing care, not just the first year's setup.
