# How Much Does a White's Tree Frog Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

White's tree frogs, sometimes called dumpy tree frogs for their round, waxy-skinned build, are one of the more affordable and forgiving amphibians to keep, both to buy and to maintain long term.

## The Frog Itself: $20 to $60

Standard animals run $20 to $60, with blue and other designer color morphs commanding a bit more. Captive-bred animals are widely available and are the better choice over wild-caught, which often carry parasites and disease and adjust less well to captivity.

## Telling Sex and Color Apart Before You Buy

**Sex.** Most frogs for sale are juveniles, and a juvenile usually cannot be sexed: the adult features may not have appeared yet, and the species takes about two years to mature. In an adult, look under the chin. A male has a greyish, wrinkled vocal sac there; a female's throat is plain white. Males are also slimmer and smaller, about 3 inches against a female's 4, and in breeding condition a male grows a dark nuptial pad at the base of each thumb for gripping a female. A frog heard calling is a male. For a single pet none of this matters, since both sexes cost and live the same, but for a pair it decides whether the keeper ends up with eggs.

**Color.** A normal White's tree frog already ranges from light blue-green through emerald to almost grey, with scattered white or gold spots along the sides, and it can change shade somewhat. Its skin carries blue and green pigment under a yellow layer, and where that yellow layer is thin or worn the frog looks blue. That is why a bluish frog in a store is not proof of a blue morph: one shade on one day proves little, and a frog paying a morph price should come from a breeder who can say what its parents were.

## Vet Costs

An exotic or amphibian-experienced vet exam commonly runs $50 to $150, based on published exotic-clinic pricing. A dedicated exotic emergency visit fee alone often starts around $250 at specialty hospitals, before any treatment. Amphibian veterinary care has real limitations, and prevention through correct husbandry does more work here than treatment ever will.

## Lifespan: Longer Than Most People Expect

The average lifespan is about 16 years, with one individual recorded living 21 years in human care, and a more conservative 10 to 15-plus years is also widely cited for excellent care. Either way, this is a genuinely long-term amphibian commitment, closer to a decade and a half than the year or two some people assume for a frog.

## The Bottom Line

White's tree frogs are one of the more budget-friendly, low-maintenance amphibians available, cheap to acquire, modest to maintain, and hardy compared to many frogs and toads. The main financial risk isn't the routine cost, it's the long runway. Budget for 10 to 15-plus years of ongoing care, not just the first year's setup.
