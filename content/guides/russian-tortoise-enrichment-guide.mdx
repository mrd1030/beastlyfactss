# Russian Tortoise Enrichment: Digging, Grazing, and Exploration

Russian tortoises are active diggers and grazers by nature, and enrichment here is mostly about giving that behavior somewhere real to happen. For everything this doesn't cover, see our complete [Russian Tortoise care guide](/guides/russian-tortoise/).

<KeyTakeaway>
Digging opportunity isn't just enrichment for this species, it's close to a welfare necessity. Burrows are how Russian tortoises buffer temperature and humidity extremes in the wild, and a tank with no diggable zone is missing something they actively need.
</KeyTakeaway>

## Burrowing and Substrate

Provide a deep, diggable zone, a 50/50 mix of topsoil and coconut coir or a sandy loam blend, ideally 6 to 12-plus inches in at least one part of the enclosure, or a dedicated dig box if the main substrate can't go that deep. Pre-formed or partially buried hides, terracotta half-pots, wooden hide boxes filled with loose substrate, or cork flats, give a starting point the tortoise can expand on its own. In outdoor pens, bury walls at least 12 inches deep, Russian tortoises are genuinely persistent diggers and will tunnel toward an escape if given the chance.

## Foraging and Grazing

Scatter feed rather than relying on a single bowl, placing dandelion, plantain, clover, endive, or other safe weeds in multiple spots so the tortoise has to search, closer to natural grazing behavior. A crumpled towel or simple forage mat with greens tucked into the folds adds a bit more of the same. Rotate forage locations daily or every other day, and if you have outdoor access, pots of safe rotating weeds work well too.

## Spatial Exploration

<FunFact>
Russian tortoises are better climbers than they look. Low platforms, flat stones, and gentle slopes get used, not just walked past, alongside the hides and visual barriers that break up an otherwise flat, open space.
</FunFact>

Place multiple hides oriented in different directions, one for the warm side and one for the cool or humid side, plus large hollow logs, cork flats, and flat stones that create routes and visual breaks rather than just decoration. Rearrange non-critical decor occasionally, roughly every 1 to 4 weeks depending on the individual, without moving the hides themselves.

## Outdoor Time

Whenever local temperature and security allow, supervised or fully secure outdoor pens are the richest enrichment available for this species: real sunlight, variable terrain, actual soil, and edible vegetation all at once. Many long-term keepers treat outdoor housing as the standard to aim for rather than an occasional bonus.
