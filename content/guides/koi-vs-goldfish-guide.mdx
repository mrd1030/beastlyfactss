# Koi vs. Goldfish: Which Pond Fish Actually Fits Your Space

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Both are colorful, popular ornamental carp relatives that get grouped together constantly, sometimes even assumed to be the same fish in different colors. They aren't, and the differences, species, adult size, and whether a pond is required, matter a lot more than which one looks prettier in a display tank.

## They Aren't the Same Fish, Just Related Ones

This is worth clearing up first because the confusion is so common. Koi and goldfish are genuinely different species in different genera, koi descend from the Amur carp, goldfish from the Prussian carp, and both are members of the minnow family, selectively bred for color over centuries. The two can be crossed, mostly by artificial spawning for research, but the result is no route back to either fish: in one study every first-generation hybrid male examined was sterile, and only some hybrid females produced eggs, and only after hormone injection. It's a case of two separate domestication stories, one in Japan and one in China, that happened to converge on similarly bright, patterned ornamental fish.

<KeyTakeaway>
Choose Goldfish if you want to start in an aquarium rather than build a pond first. You get a flexible fish, at home in a large tank or an outdoor pond, without koi's demand for hundreds of gallons per fish from day one.
</KeyTakeaway>

## Telling Them Apart at the Pond Edge

Size is the obvious difference in adults, since koi are the one commonly cultured carp relative that passes 24 inches, against the 4 to 8 inches most goldfish reach. It is also the least useful one at the store, where a young koi and a big comet goldfish can be the same length. Look at the mouth instead. Koi carry barbels, two pairs of whisker-like feelers at the corners of the mouth, and goldfish have none. The head and body back it up: a koi has a boxy head on a long, slender body, and a goldfish has a shorter, rounder one. The barbel check works at any age, which makes it the one to use before a "goldfish" goes into a small tank and turns out to need a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B000G0GDJ6?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Firestone 45mil EPDM Rubber Pond Liner 15ft x 20ft">pond</AffiliateLink>.

<FunFact>
Koi and goldfish are both genuinely cold-hardy and can overwinter in an outdoor pond in most temperate climates, slowing their metabolism and settling near the bottom once water temperatures drop, rather than needing to be brought indoors the way most ornamental fish do.
</FunFact>

## Sharing a Pond

Koi and goldfish can share a pond. They take the same water chemistry and the same <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0002DK91U?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Kaytee Koi's Choice Koi Floating Fish Food, 10 lbs">diet</AffiliateLink>, and they get nearly all of the same diseases. The exceptions are a few herpesviruses, and one of them is the reason a mixed pond needs a rule.

Koi herpesvirus kills koi and common carp, and goldfish are typically unaffected by it. That is exactly the problem. A goldfish can become infected, show no signs at all, and pass the virus to the koi. So the cheap goldfish added to a koi pond as an afterthought gets the same quarantine a new koi would, in [a separate hospital system](/blog/fish-quarantine-and-treatment-guide/) for at least 30 days at 75°F, and a goldfish that shared a pond with sick koi never moves to another koi pond. KHV is a koi disease that a goldfish can carry in without anyone noticing.

<KeyTakeaway>
A Koi is the better fit if you have, or are ready to build, real outdoor pond space and want a fish that can become a decades-long fixture of your yard. Go in with the space and budget question settled first, this isn't a fish you can downsize into later.
</KeyTakeaway>

## Bottom Line

Pick a goldfish if you want a flexible, aquarium-or-pond-capable fish with a lower upfront cost. Pick a koi only if you have real outdoor pond space and are ready for a fish that can genuinely outlive the pond's original owner. They may get confused for each other constantly, but they're different species with very different space requirements. The [koi](/encyclopedia/animal/koi/) and [goldfish](/encyclopedia/animal/goldfish/) encyclopedia profiles cover the two separate carp lineages behind the mix-up.
