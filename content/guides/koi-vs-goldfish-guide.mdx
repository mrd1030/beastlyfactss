# Koi vs. Goldfish: Which Pond Fish Actually Fits Your Space

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Both are colorful, popular ornamental carp relatives that get grouped together constantly, sometimes even assumed to be the same fish in different colors. They aren't, and the differences, species, adult size, and whether a pond is required, matter a lot more than which one looks prettier in a display tank.

## They Aren't the Same Fish, Just Related Ones

This is worth clearing up first because the confusion is so common. Koi and goldfish are genuinely different species in different genera, koi descend from the Amur carp, goldfish from the Prussian carp, and despite both being members of the minnow family and both selectively bred for color over centuries, they can't interbreed to produce viable offspring. It's a case of two separate domestication stories, one in Japan and one in China, that happened to converge on similarly bright, patterned ornamental fish.

<KeyTakeaway>
Choose Goldfish if you want to start in an aquarium rather than build a pond first. You get a flexible fish, at home in a large tank or an outdoor pond, without koi's demand for hundreds of gallons per fish from day one.
</KeyTakeaway>

## Adult Size Determines Everything Else

A typical adult koi reaches 24 to 25 inches, with well-kept individuals in a spacious pond approaching 3 feet. Most goldfish stay considerably smaller, 4 to 8 inches for most varieties, though pond-raised common and comet goldfish can grow larger than the compact fancy varieties bred for aquarium life. That size gap is the reason koi essentially require an outdoor <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B000G0GDJ6?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Firestone 45mil EPDM Rubber Pond Liner 15ft x 20ft">pond setup</AffiliateLink>, roughly 250 gallons per fish and [sized off waste volume](/blog/koi-tank-setup-guide/), while goldfish can realistically live out a full life in a properly sized aquarium.

<FunFact>
Koi and goldfish are both genuinely cold-hardy and can overwinter in an outdoor pond in most temperate climates, slowing their metabolism and settling near the bottom once water temperatures drop, rather than needing to be brought indoors the way most ornamental fish do.
</FunFact>

## Both Are a Longer Commitment Than People Expect, Just Different Scales

A goldfish's 10 to 15 year typical lifespan already surprises people used to thinking of goldfish as short-lived. A koi's is on another level entirely: 25 to 50 years typically, with some individuals reported reaching 100, a timeline that can genuinely span a person's entire adult life. Feeding either one a proper <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0002DK91U?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Kaytee Koi's Choice Koi Floating Fish Food, 10 lbs">species-appropriate diet</AffiliateLink> matters more than it might seem given how long you're likely to be doing it.

<KeyTakeaway>
A Koi is the better fit if you have, or are ready to build, real outdoor pond space and want a fish that can become a decades-long fixture of your yard. Go in with the space and budget question settled first, this isn't a fish you can downsize into later.
</KeyTakeaway>

## Bottom Line

Pick a goldfish if you want a flexible, aquarium-or-pond-capable fish with a lower upfront cost. Pick a koi only if you have real outdoor pond space and are ready for a fish that can genuinely outlive the pond's original owner. They may get confused for each other constantly, but they're different species with very different space requirements. The [koi](/encyclopedia/animal/koi/) and [goldfish](/encyclopedia/animal/goldfish/) encyclopedia profiles cover the two separate carp lineages behind the mix-up.
