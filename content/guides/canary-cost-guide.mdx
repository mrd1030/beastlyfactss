# How Much Does a Canary Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Canaries are one of the more affordable pet birds you can buy, and the cost scales up meaningfully once you get into specialized song and show breeds. For everything beyond budgeting, see our full [Canary care guide](/guides/canary/).

## The Bird: $25 to $500

Common yellow canaries from pet stores run $25 to $60. Red Factor canaries, bred specifically for color, run $80 to $150. Specialized song breeds, American Singers, German Rollers, and Spanish Timbrados among them, run $100 to $250 or more, with prize singers and exhibition quality birds reaching $200 to $500.

Males typically cost 20 to 50% more than females, since only males sing. That price gap is a direct reflection of demand, most buyers want a singing bird specifically, and our [Canary handling guide](/blog/canary-handling-guide/) covers exactly why that distinction matters so much with this species.

A quality flight cage runs $120 to $220 on its own, and it's worth spending real money here. Canaries fly horizontally rather than climbing the way parrots do, so a wide, long cage suits this species far better than a tall, narrow one built with a climbing bird in mind. Our [Canary cage setup guide](/blog/canary-tank-setup-guide/) covers the shape and sizing details in full.

## Ongoing Costs: Roughly $10 to $30 a Month

Seed or pellet food, fresh produce, cage liners, and occasional toy replacement make up the monthly total, genuinely modest for a pet bird compared to larger parrot species.

## Vet Costs

An annual avian wellness exam, worth budgeting for even though it's easy to skip, runs $50 to $100 or more. Mite treatment or a respiratory illness can add meaningfully to that if it comes up, and our [Canary health issues guide](/blog/canary-health-issues-guide/) covers the conditions most likely to drive an unplanned vet visit.

## Lifespan

10 to 15 years with good care, sometimes cited more conservatively at 8 to 12. A genuinely long-term commitment for a bird often bought on impulse for its song.

## The Cost Decision That Actually Matters Most: Are You Buying for the Song?

If you specifically want a singing bird, make sure you're buying a confirmed male, this is worth verifying with the seller directly, since females cost less but won't sing the way you might be expecting.

<FunFact>
Only male canaries develop full song. Females mostly produce simple chirps, so a "quiet" canary bought for its singing potential is very often a hen rather than a bird with a quiet personality, worth confirming sex before you buy if song is the whole point.
</FunFact>

This is the single most common source of buyer disappointment with this species, and it's an easy thing to get right upfront simply by asking the right question before you buy.
