# Guppy Enrichment: The Sex Ratio Is a Welfare Decision

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Most stocking advice for guppies is about color. The research is about what happens to the fish that are not colorful.

<KeyTakeaway>
Male guppies pursue females close to continuously, and the costs of that harassment have been measured: **reduced feeding and a forced change in where females live**. One study found effects carrying into the next generation. The fix is boring and effective: **several females per male**, or an all-male tank.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Research Says

Sexual harassment in guppies and related livebearers is a well-studied area, and it maps directly onto how people stock tanks.

**Direct costs to females.** Across nine livebearer species that included the guppy, females spent **significantly less time feeding** with a male present than with another female. In wild Trinidadian guppies, male harassment **drives females into habitats they otherwise do not prefer** and leads the sexes to segregate.

**Costs to the next generation.** In one guppy study, lifetime fecundity did not differ between treatments, but offspring did. Females under **high sexual harassment produced daughters with smaller bodies** and **sons with shorter gonopodia** that were less attractive to females and less successful.

That last finding is the one worth carrying. The harassed females still produced offspring, so a keeper counting fry would see nothing wrong. The cost showed up in the fish that came out.

<FunFact>
The guppy is one of the most-studied fish in evolutionary biology, largely because those short generations and obvious sexual selection make it a natural laboratory. The side effect is that we know more about the social costs of a badly stocked guppy tank than about almost any other aquarium fish.
</FunFact>

## Sex Ratio, First

The single decision that matters most, and it is free.

**Several females to each male**, and more females is better still. In harassment experiments, tanks with one male per female put the females under more pressure than tanks with one male per roughly six females, and those two ratios match what is found in the wild.

**Or keep males only.** It avoids the harassment question entirely, avoids the endless fry, and keeps the color, since males are the colorful sex. For a lot of keepers this is the better answer and it rarely gets suggested.

What does not work is stocking on looks, which produces a male-heavy tank, which is the arrangement the research describes.

## Cover, as a Social Tool

Dense <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B008HXF16K?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="SubstrateSource Anubias Nana Live Aquarium Plant, 1 Pot">live planting</AffiliateLink>, including floating plants, offers females somewhere to get out of a male's line of sight, the same refuge-seeking that wild females show under harassment.

Cover in a livebearer tank is not just decoration or fry protection. Plant heavily along the sides and back and leave swimming space through the middle.

Floating plants do double duty, since fry hide in the roots.

## Space and Numbers

Guppies are active and social and do better in groups than in pairs. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4pjiyeT" product="10 gallon tank">10 gallon tank</AffiliateLink> is a reasonable starting point for a small group, and bigger is easier because a livebearer tank does not stay the size you stocked it.

Plan for the fry. A mixed-sex guppy tank produces a new brood roughly monthly, and overstocking is how water quality collapses.

## Water Stability

Guppies suffer in unstable water.

A [cycled tank](/blog/aquarium-cycling-guide/), <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3Rg5gTT" product="Water conditioner">water conditioner</AffiliateLink> on everything that goes in, and small regular changes.

## Feeding

Spread food across the surface rather than in one spot, so the females that are being chased still get some.

That is the practical link back to the research: a female feeds less with a male near, and food arriving in one place makes it worse. Vary the diet with frozen and live foods.

## Priority Order

1. Several females per male, or an all-male tank
2. Dense planting and floating cover for females to retreat into
3. A group rather than a pair, with space for the fry
4. Food spread across the surface
5. Stable, cycled water
6. A plan for the population
7. Varied diet

## What Not to Do

Do not stock a guppy tank on which fish look best, which produces the male-heavy ratio the research warns about. Do not keep a single male with a single female. Do not keep a bare tank with no cover for females. Do not feed in one corner. Do not let the population run unmanaged.
