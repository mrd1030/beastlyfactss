# Guppy Enrichment: The Sex Ratio Is a Welfare Decision

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Most stocking advice for guppies is about color. The research is about what happens to the fish that are not colorful.

<KeyTakeaway>
Male guppies pursue females close to continuously, and the costs of that harassment have been measured: **energy expenditure, reduced foraging, and increased vigilance**. One study found effects carrying into the next generation. The fix is boring and effective: **at least two to three females per male**, or an all-male tank.
</KeyTakeaway>

For everything else about them, see the [Guppy care guide](/guides/guppy/).

## What the Research Says

Sexual harassment in guppies and related livebearers is a well-studied area, and it maps directly onto how people stock tanks.

**Direct costs to females.** A number of studies identify direct costs of sexual harassment including **energy expenditure and reduced foraging ability**. In the closely related *Poecilia mexicana*, males **attempt to mate almost constantly** while females are largely non-responsive. Females flee, become **more vigilant in the presence of males**, and have **hampered feeding opportunities** as a result.

**Costs to the next generation.** In one guppy study, lifetime fecundity did not differ between treatments, but offspring did. Females under **high sexual harassment produced daughters with smaller bodies** and **sons with shorter gonopodia** that were less attractive to females and less successful.

That last finding is the one worth carrying. The harassed females still produced offspring, so a keeper counting fry would see nothing wrong. The cost showed up in the fish that came out.

<FunFact>
The guppy is one of the most-studied fish in evolutionary biology, largely because those short generations and obvious sexual selection make it a natural laboratory. The side effect is that we know more about the social costs of a badly stocked guppy tank than about almost any other aquarium fish.
</FunFact>

## Sex Ratio, First

The single decision that matters most, and it is free.

**Two to three females per male minimum**, and more females is better still. Six or more females per male is a common recommendation and there is nothing wrong with it.

**Or keep males only.** It avoids the harassment question entirely, avoids the endless fry, and keeps the color, since males are the colorful sex. For a lot of keepers this is the better answer and it rarely gets suggested.

What does not work is stocking on looks, which produces a male-heavy tank, which is the arrangement the research describes.

## Cover, as a Social Tool

Dense <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4grZYPq" product="Live terrarium plants">live planting</AffiliateLink>, including floating plants, gives females somewhere to break line of sight and stop being pursued.

That is what the cover is for here. In a livebearer tank it is not decoration and it is not just fry protection, it is the mechanism that lets a female get a rest. Plant heavily along the sides and back and leave swimming space through the middle. Our [tank setup guide](/blog/guppy-tank-setup-guide/) covers the layout.

Floating plants do double duty, since fry hide in the roots and survive at a rate you can actually manage.

## Space and Numbers

Guppies are active and social and do better in groups than in pairs. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4pjiyeT" product="10 gallon tank">10 gallon tank</AffiliateLink> is a reasonable starting point for a small group, and bigger is easier because a livebearer tank does not stay the size you stocked it.

Plan for the fry. A mixed-sex guppy tank produces a new brood roughly monthly, and overstocking is how water quality collapses.

## Water Stability

Modern guppies are less hardy than their reputation, having been bred hard for color and fin, and they suffer in unstable water.

A cycled tank, <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3Rg5gTT" product="Water conditioner">water conditioner</AffiliateLink> on everything that goes in, and small regular changes. Our [health issues guide](/blog/guppy-health-issues-guide/) covers the conditions that come with heavily line-bred stock.

## Feeding

Fast, greedy surface feeders. Spread food across the surface rather than one spot, so the females that are being chased still get some.

That is the practical link back to the research: a harassed female feeds less, and food arriving in one place makes it worse. Vary the diet with frozen and live foods. Our [feeding guide](/blog/guppy-feeding-guide/) covers it.

## Priority Order

1. At least two to three females per male, or an all-male tank
2. Dense planting and floating cover for females to break line of sight in
3. A group rather than a pair, with space for the fry
4. Food spread across the surface
5. Stable, cycled water
6. A plan for the population
7. Varied diet

## What Not to Do

Do not stock a guppy tank on which fish look best, which produces the male-heavy ratio the research warns about. Do not keep a single male with a single female. Do not keep a bare tank with no cover for females. Do not feed in one corner. Do not let the population run unmanaged. And see our [handling guide](/blog/guppy-handling-guide/) before moving a heavily pregnant female, which is a common and avoidable cause of stress-induced loss.
