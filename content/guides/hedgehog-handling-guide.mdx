# How to Handle a Hedgehog

Hedgehogs are solitary, nocturnal, and defensive by default, and understanding their first-contact reflexes matters more with this species than with almost any other small pet. For everything else about day-to-day care, see our full [Hedgehog care guide](/guides/hedgehog/).

## What Happens When You First Pick One Up

Huffing, clicking or popping sounds, and balling up tightly are all normal defensive reflexes, not aggression, and not a sign you're doing anything wrong. A hedgehog carries around 5,000 to 7,000 quills, and while they're smooth and unbarbed, unlike a porcupine's, so they don't embed in skin, a balled-up hedgehog full of raised quills can still genuinely poke and hurt bare hands.

## The Correct Technique

Scoop from underneath and the sides using flat or cupped hands, like a shovel rather than a grip, and let the ball unroll on its own timeline. Never force a ball open. This is both ineffective and a real welfare issue, it teaches the hedgehog that handling means being restrained against its will, which makes future sessions harder, not easier. A towel that carries your scent can help a nervous hedgehog feel more secure during early handling.

## Self-Anointing: Not What It Looks Like

<FunFact>
Hedgehogs foam at the mouth and spread saliva over their own quills when they encounter a new or interesting smell, a behavior called self-anointing. It looks genuinely alarming the first time you see it, twisting to coat their own spines in frothy saliva, but it's completely normal and not a seizure or a sign of poisoning. One practical note worth remembering: if your hedgehog self-anoints after contact with something that could be irritating, wash your hands before touching your own eyes or mouth.
</FunFact>

## Quilling

Between 2 and 6 months old, hedgehogs shed their baby quills and grow adult ones, and they're often grumpier and ball up more during this period. This is temporary, typically resolving within about a month, and not a sign of a training setback.

## First-Timer Mistakes

- Forcing a ball open instead of waiting it out
- Handling roughly during the quilling phase and reading normal irritability as a personality trait
- Skipping scent-based trust-building, like a worn towel and regular gentle contact, in favor of jumping straight to full handling

## How This Compares to Other Small Pets

Hedgehogs are often marketed alongside hamsters and guinea pigs as similar small mammals, but the comparison doesn't hold up well. Their environmental needs, especially strict temperature control, are closer to a reptile's, like a [sulcata tortoise's](/blog/sulcata-tortoise-handling-guide/), than a typical pocket pet's, and they're genuinely less cuddly, tolerating handling rather than seeking it out the way a hand-raised [lovebird](/blog/lovebird-handling-guide/) actively does. Knowing that going in sets more realistic expectations than the marketing usually does.
