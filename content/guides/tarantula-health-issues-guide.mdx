# Common Tarantula Health Issues (Prevention Matters More Than Treatment)

<VetDisclaimer />
<AffiliateDisclosure />

Veterinary care for tarantulas is limited, many areas simply don't have a vet who treats invertebrates. That reshapes how tarantula health works in practice: prevention through correct husbandry does most of the heavy lifting, and knowing what's normal versus what's actually wrong matters more than knowing what to do once something's gone wrong.

Get the enclosure right first.

## Dehydration

The most common issue keepers run into. Look for a shriveled, wrinkled abdomen, lethargy, and what's sometimes called a death curl, the legs curling in tightly underneath the body. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/44hAzR9" product="Small/shallow water dish">shallow water dish</AffiliateLink> and humidity in the range the species needs, checked with a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3QHrM81" product="Digital thermometer/hygrometer combo">thermometer/hygrometer combo</AffiliateLink>, prevent most cases. Too much ventilation and a missing water dish are the usual causes, and a spider that has stopped eating loses the water its prey carried too. Tarantulas survive long fasts but die quickly without water.

The fix is a large, shallow, open dish of water straight away. A dehydrated spider lowers the front of its body into the water and drinks for minutes or even hours, the abdomen starts to fill out within a few hours, and activity usually picks up overnight. Afterward, cut back ventilation a little and keep part of the substrate damp.

## Molting Complications

A stuck or incomplete molt can be fatal. The best prevention is maintaining proper humidity and, critically, never disturbing or feeding your tarantula during a molt. What a normal molt looks like, from the premolt fast to the empty skin, is laid out in the [invertebrate molting guide](/blog/invertebrate-molting-guide/). Once a molt is complete, wait about a week for the new exoskeleton to fully harden before offering food again.

Slow is not the same as stuck. The molt itself runs anywhere from 15 minutes to several hours, and a long, quiet stretch inside that window is normal. Stuck is a spider half in and half out of the old skin for hours with no progress, struggling that goes nowhere, or legs left twisted at the end. That is the point to call an experienced keeper or an exotic vet, never to pull at the old skin yourself.

## Falls and Trauma

A ruptured abdomen or visible hemolymph (the tarantula equivalent of blood) leaking from an injury is a real emergency. Some keepers can stem a minor leak with a small amount of cornstarch applied to the wound, but anything beyond minor bleeding needs specialist attention if you can find it, and often the outcome depends on how quickly you can act. Most falls happen during attempted handling, which is the main argument for leaving this species in its enclosure.

<FunFact>
Hemolymph doesn't stay confined to vessels the way mammal blood does, it flows freely through the body cavity and bathes the internal organs directly. That's part of why even a moderate abdominal rupture can turn fatal so quickly, there's no closed circulatory system holding things together while the injury is dealt with.
</FunFact>

## Oral Nematodes

Parasitic nematodes are microscopic worms that arrive mostly on wild-caught spiders and can spread through a whole collection. The spider behaves normally until late in the infection, which is why the signs matter:

- A white, sticky mass around the mouthparts, the clearest sign
- Palps held permanently tucked under the fangs
- A wet sternum from drooling, beyond normal cleaning
- Long stretches beside the water dish, restlessness, or unusual amounts of silk
- A sweet smell from the enclosure
- Loss of appetite

Isolate the spider from the rest of the collection at once, with its own tools. Captive-bred spiders are much less likely to carry them, and a vet can remove the worms under anesthesia where one is available.

## Mites and Mold

Both are husbandry issues rather than illnesses in the traditional sense. Improving ventilation and general enclosure sanitation resolves most mite cases.

Mold on the spider itself is more serious. It shows as a white or yellowish plume on the carapace, the underside of the abdomen, or the leg tips, and once it reaches the book lungs and the organs it is usually fatal. It hits spiders kept damp with poor airflow, especially burrowing species that spend their lives out of sight. Caught early, the first step is a move into a clean, dry, well-ventilated container with a water dish, away from the spores.

## Dyskinetic Syndrome (DKS)

A poorly understood condition causing a loss of motor coordination. Unfortunately it's often fatal, and there isn't a well-established treatment.

## The Thing That Isn't a Health Problem

A tarantula that stops eating for weeks is very often just entering a pre-molt period, this is completely normal behavior, not a sign of illness. Don't panic or assume something is wrong based on appetite alone.
