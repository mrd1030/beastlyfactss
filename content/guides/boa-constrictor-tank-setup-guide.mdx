# Boa Constrictor Enclosure Setup: Sizing for the Adult It Will Become

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Boa constrictor housing scales up significantly as the snake grows, and building for the adult size from a reasonably early point saves you from repeated upgrades. This covers the full setup, for what all of it costs, see our [Boa Constrictor cost guide](/blog/boa-constrictor-cost-guide/), or the complete [Boa Constrictor care guide](/guides/boa-constrictor/) for everything else.

## Enclosure Size

A useful working rule: enclosure length plus width should equal or exceed the snake's length, and height should be at least half the snake's length. In practice, adults typically need somewhere around 6 to 8 feet long, 2 to 3 feet wide, and 3 to 4 feet tall, roughly 10 square feet of floor space at minimum. A <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0H3WT574V?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Reptile Habitats 6x2x2 (180 Gallon) PVC Enclosure V3">6ft+ adult PVC enclosure</AffiliateLink> is a solid way to build for that final size from the start rather than upgrading twice. Boas are semi-arboreal, so unlike a purely ground-dwelling snake, both floor space and height genuinely matter here. House boas individually, never together.

<FunFact>
A boa constrictor's enclosure needs both floor space and height in a way that few other pet snakes do. Because boas are semi-arboreal, the rule of thumb isn't just length plus width matching the snake's length, height has to reach at least half that length too. A purely ground-dwelling snake of the same length can get away with a much shorter enclosure.
</FunFact>

## Temperature

Basking area 88 to 92°F, occasionally up to 95°F, ambient warm side 80 to 85°F, cool side 75 to 80°F, nighttime 70 to 80°F. A heat source is required, and it needs to be genuinely inaccessible to direct contact, no exposed heating elements a snake this size and strength could reach. A <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0GFD5T3H7?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="2 in 1 Reptile Dimming Thermostat (PT02T)">thermostat</AffiliateLink> is essential, not optional, for a heat source powering an enclosure this large.

## Humidity

60 to 70%, higher during shedding cycles. Achieve this with a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eKyN0W" product="Large soak-able water dish">large water bowl</AffiliateLink>, regular misting, moisture-retentive substrate, and a dedicated humid hide. A <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07MTPKDG5?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Exo Terra LED Thermometer and Hygrometer Bundle">thermometer and hygrometer</AffiliateLink> keeps this confirmed rather than guessed at, since both temperature and humidity in an enclosure this large can vary a lot between the warm end and the cool end.

## Substrate

Coconut husk or fiber, cypress mulch, or a reptile-specific soil mix all work well. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4vNgpe1" product="Cypress mulch substrate">cypress mulch or coconut fiber substrate</AffiliateLink> covers most setups, though a true bioactive setup needs a proper soil-based mix rather than relying on cypress or coco husk alone. Whatever you choose, avoid letting it stay persistently wet, that's a direct path to the scale rot covered in our [Boa Constrictor health issues guide](/blog/boa-constrictor-health-issues-guide/).

## Lighting

Not strictly required, but increasingly recommended: a low level of UVB alongside a consistent day and night cycle offers real benefits even though boas have historically been kept without it.

## The Non-Obvious Requirement: Two Hides, Not One

Provide a hide on the warm side and a separate hide on the cool side, <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B092HDK2S4?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Zoomed Medium Habba Hut Reptile Hide">two snug hides</AffiliateLink> sized to fit a coiled boa work well for this. This lets your boa thermoregulate, moving between temperature zones as needed, while still having secure cover available wherever it settles. A front-opening enclosure with genuinely good ventilation is strongly preferred over a top-opening tank, both for your own ease of access with an animal this size and for the snake's air quality.
