# Koi Pond Setup: Built for Waste Volume, Not Just Water Volume

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Koi husbandry is water management at scale. Get the volume, filtration, and depth right, and koi are hardy, low-drama fish.

<Figure src="/assets/images/koi-tank-setup-guide-1.jpg" alt="Labeled diagram of an outdoor koi pond showing predator netting, filtration, aeration, and real depth" caption="Koi husbandry is water management at scale, get the volume, filtration, and depth right and this is a low-drama fish." shareable />

## Size

Roughly 250 gallons per adult koi, or about 10 gallons per inch of body length. In practice, 1,000 gallons is the frequently cited minimum practical pond volume, and it only comfortably supports a few fish, larger is strongly preferred for water stability and healthy growth. Juveniles can start in something smaller, even a 29-gallon tank temporarily, but they outgrow that fast.

Indoor aquariums can technically house koi, but matching the volume and industrial-grade filtration an adult needs indoors is expensive and impractical for most keepers. Outdoor ponds are the standard setup for adult koi for good reason. The [red-eared slider](/blog/red-eared-slider-tank-setup-guide/) follows the same arc, sold small into a tank it outgrows within a couple of years, which is the shape of the mistake rather than anything particular to fish.

## Depth

Minimum 3 feet, ideally 3 to 5 feet or more.

<FunFact>
Pond depth for koi isn't about the fish's size at all, it's about surviving the seasons. Depth matters for temperature stability, protection from predators, and survival through winter in climates that freeze. A pond that's too shallow can freeze solid or overheat, both genuinely dangerous for the fish, which is why "how deep" ends up mattering more than "how wide" in a lot of pond designs.
</FunFact>

## Temperature

Koi are hardy coldwater fish, comfortable in a 64 to 75°F range, with 59 to 77°F as the outer limits. They tolerate wider extremes than that range suggests, but rapid swings, more than about 2°F a day, are the real risk, not the absolute temperature. No heater is needed in a temperate outdoor pond, a de-icer keeps a section ice-free for gas exchange in cold climates. Feeding and metabolism drop sharply as water cools, which is why koi eat far less in winter.

## Substrate

Smooth-sided pebbles or gravel in a shallow layer are acceptable, and a bare-bottom pond is the easier build, simpler to clean and free of anaerobic pockets and pump damage. Avoid sharp or jagged material, which can injure mouths and gills during foraging or damage the pond liner, and skip very fine material that clogs equipment.

## Lighting

No UVB lighting is required, koi don't rely on it for vitamin D the way many reptiles do. Natural daylight is preferred for outdoor ponds, with some shade to limit algae growth and temperature spikes. A UV clarifier or sterilizer is common and genuinely useful, but it's a water-treatment device for controlling algae and pathogens, not lighting for the fish themselves.

## The Part That Determines Success: Filtration

Koi are heavy waste producers, and this is where most beginner setups fall short. Mechanical and biological filtration needs to be robust, an <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B004DL0Y4E?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="TotalPond Complete Pond Filter with UV Clarifier">oversized filtration system paired with a UV clarifier</AffiliateLink> is the right call here, not overkill, with strong aeration and oxygenation alongside it. An <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0078LTKM8?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Aquascape 75000 Pond Air 2 (Double Outlet Aeration Kit)">aeration kit or waterfall pump</AffiliateLink> keeps oxygen levels stable, especially in summer heat. Open swimming space matters too, koi are active, so keep dense decor limited.

Filter sizing runs on the same [turnover math](/blog/aquarium-filtration-guide/) as an aquarium, the water volume times the passes per hour you want, and koi sit on the heavy-bioload side of it, so size from the pump's gallons-per-hour rating and the pond's real volume, and ignore the pond size printed on the box. The pond also gets cycled before any fish go in. A [fishless cycle](/blog/aquarium-cycling-guide/) is finished when a full dose of ammonia reads zero within 24 hours, nitrite reads zero, and nitrate has started to build, which takes four to six weeks in warm water and longer in a cold pond. From then on, ammonia and nitrite read zero on every test.

## Sizing the Pump, Filter, and Liner

**Turnover.** The floor is the whole pond passing through the filter at least once every two hours. That suits a lightly stocked water garden; a koi pond is usually run at one to two full turnovers an hour, paired with good aeration, because koi carry a far heavier ammonia load than a goldfish or planted pond. The closer the pond runs to one adult per 250 gallons, the closer to two turnovers it should sit.

**Rated flow against delivered flow.** The number on the pump box is not what reaches the filter. Every foot the water climbs to a waterfall or a raised filter, and every fitting and length of hose, cuts it: a pump rated 3,000 GPH can deliver only 2,000 GPH at 6 feet of head. Buy a pump rated about 1.5 times the flow you need delivered.

The filter then has to be rated for the delivered flow and for a koi load, which is why a filter sold for a pond far larger than yours is the right purchase.

**Liner.** A liner has to run down one wall, across the floor, up the other wall, and past the edge far enough to anchor. The sizing math is (length + 2 x depth + 1 foot) by (width + 2 x depth + 1 foot), measured at the widest and deepest points. A 16 by 11 foot pond dug 3 feet deep needs a liner of 16 + 6 + 1 = 23 feet by 11 + 6 + 1 = 18 feet. Round up to the next stock size, never down, and lay underlayment beneath it: the soft fabric keeps rocks and roots from puncturing the liner from below.

## Other Non-Obvious Requirements

- **Predator protection:** herons, raccoons, and similar predators are real threats. An <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B076KQSFDZ?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="AlpineReach Koi Pond Netting Kit 15 x 20 ft">appropriately sized netting kit</AffiliateLink> and adequate depth both matter here.
- **Easy maintenance access:** you'll be netting and cleaning regularly, design for it.
- **A quarantine system:** essential for any new arrival before it joins the main pond, and the [hospital tank guide](/blog/fish-quarantine-and-treatment-guide/) covers how to run one.
- **Careful plant selection:** koi eat many plants, choose varieties that can handle it.
- **Winter preparation:** de-icers, reduced feeding schedules, and depth all matter more as temperatures drop in cold climates.
