# How Much Does a Savannah Monitor Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Savannah monitors are cheap to buy, and that low price is part of a genuine problem with how this species gets marketed and, too often, cared for.

## The Monitor: $150 to $800

Juveniles run $150 to $300, and adults $300 to $800. Part of why this species stays so affordable is that many are wild-caught or farmed in bulk for the pet trade, which keeps supply high and price low, but also means a real share of savannah monitors sold arrive stressed, parasitized, and with a shorter life expectancy than a captive-bred animal would have.

The enclosure dominates this cost. An adult needs an 8x4x4 foot custom build, and it's worth being upfront here: the enclosure above is an honest partial-match substitute, it's actually 8x2x2ft, real 8x4x4ft PVC or wood enclosures for an adult savannah monitor generally aren't sold ready-made on Amazon. True adult-sized housing for this species is typically a DIY build or ordered direct from a manufacturer. By the time you add a cluster of halogen basking bulbs, <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3TipZXE" product="UVB - Arcadia Desert 12% (ProT5 kit, 22in/24W)">T5 HO UVB</AffiliateLink>, 12 to 24-plus inches of substrate, thermostats, and a water basin large enough to fully submerge the animal, total setup cost commonly runs well past $500, sometimes exceeding $1,000.

## Ongoing Costs: Roughly $30 to $50 a Month

Food, a mix of insects and lean protein, plus meaningful electricity for the high-wattage heating this species needs, make up the monthly total.

<FunFact>
Many pet savannah monitors die in their early teens or younger, well short of the 15 to 20 years this species can reach under excellent husbandry, from fatty liver disease, kidney failure, or gout, nearly all tied directly to overfeeding.
</FunFact>

## Vet Costs

A routine annual exam runs $50 to $100. Exotic vet workups for obesity, parasites, or metabolic bone disease can run into the hundreds, and given how common these issues are in this species, it's worth budgeting for rather than hoping to avoid.

## Lifespan: The Number the "Cheap and Easy" Marketing Leaves Out

Commonly cited at 10 to 15 years, with 15 to 20 achievable under excellent husbandry. Here's the honest part: many pet savannah monitors die well before that, in their early teens or younger, from fatty liver disease, kidney failure, or gout, nearly all tied directly to overfeeding. This species' actual lifespan potential and its typical real-world lifespan are two different numbers, and the gap between them is almost entirely about diet and enclosure size.

## The Honest Budgeting Takeaway

The low purchase price of a savannah monitor doesn't reflect what responsible care actually costs. A properly sized adult enclosure and correct feeding, not overfeeding, are what determine whether this animal lives its full potential lifespan or a fraction of it.
