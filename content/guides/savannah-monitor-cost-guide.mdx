# How Much Does a Savannah Monitor Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Savannah monitors are cheap to buy, and that low price is part of a genuine problem with how this species gets marketed and, too often, cared for.

## The Monitor: %%animal:savannah-monitor%%

Juveniles run $150 to $300, and adults $300 to $800. Part of why this species stays so affordable is that most sold in the pet trade are wild-caught or farmed in bulk, which keeps supply high and price low, but also means a real share of savannah monitors sold arrive stressed, parasitized, and with a shorter life expectancy than a captive-bred animal would have.

### Buying One

A captive-bred animal is worth tracking down and worth paying more for, because the cheap ones are cheap for the reasons above. Ask the seller directly whether the animal was bred in captivity or imported, and get the answer in writing if you are paying captive-bred money. Whatever the source, it gets a fecal exam in its first weeks: pinworms, coccidia, and flagellated protozoa are common in this species, and the $50 to $100 routine exam below is where that test starts. The [reptile quarantine guide](/blog/reptile-quarantine-guide/) covers keeping a new arrival apart from other reptiles and choosing a vet, and the name of a vet who sees large monitors belongs on the shopping list before the animal comes home.

## Upfront Setup: %%setup:savannah-monitor%%

<CostTable guide="savannah-monitor" section="necessities" />

The enclosure dominates this cost. An adult needs an 8x4x4 foot enclosure, and it's worth being upfront here: real 8x4x4ft PVC or wood enclosures for an adult savannah monitor generally aren't sold ready-made. The table prices one ordered direct from a maker, %%price:argentine-tegu-full-8x4x4ft-pvc-enclosure%%; the other route is a DIY build.

### What an 8x4x4 Build Involves

A DIY build is a piece of furniture, and it has to do four jobs at once:

1. **Hold the soil.** The base carries 12 to 24 inches of packed, damp soil, so it needs a sealed, waterproof floor and a front dam or lip tall enough to keep that depth in when the doors open. Deep soil is heavy, and the floor and whatever it sits on have to carry it.
2. **Contain a strong digger and climber.** Doors that latch, and a top secure enough to hold the heat and light fixtures without the monitor pushing it loose.
3. **Let UVB in.** UVB does not pass through glass or plastic, so the lamp section of the top is mesh. On an animal with claws like this one, that mesh should be plastic-coated welded wire, firmly attached, not window screen.
4. **Be cleanable.** Every surface sealed so it can be scrubbed and dried during a deep clean.

Budget the build around those four jobs, then add the equipment. The equipment in the table, a cluster of halogen basking bulbs, T5 HO UVB, 12 to 24-plus inches of substrate, a thermostat, and a water basin large enough to fully submerge the animal, goes in whichever way the enclosure is built.

## Ongoing Costs: Roughly %%monthly:savannah-monitor%% a Month

Food, a mix of insects and lean protein, plus meaningful electricity for the high-wattage heating this species needs, make up the monthly total.

<FunFact>
The $150 juvenile is cheap because it was farmed or caught in bulk. The 8 by 4 by 4 foot enclosure it will need is not sold ready-made, and that build is the real purchase price.
</FunFact>

## Vet Costs

A routine annual exam runs $50 to $100. Exotic vet workups for obesity, parasites, or metabolic bone disease can run into the hundreds, and given how common these issues are in this species, it's worth budgeting for rather than hoping to avoid.

## Lifespan: The Number the "Cheap and Easy" Marketing Leaves Out

Commonly cited at 10 to 15 years, with 15 to 20 achievable under excellent husbandry. Here's the honest part: many pet savannah monitors die well before that, in their early teens or younger, from fatty liver disease and the other consequences of overfeeding. This species' actual lifespan potential and its typical real-world lifespan are two different numbers, and the gap between them is almost entirely about diet and enclosure size.

## The Honest Budgeting Takeaway

The low purchase price of a savannah monitor doesn't reflect what responsible care costs. A properly sized adult enclosure and correct feeding are what determine whether this animal lives its full potential lifespan or a fraction of it.
