# Red-Eared Slider Tank Setup: Plan for the Adult, Not the Hatchling

<AffiliateDisclosure />

The setup that suits a quarter-sized hatchling looks nothing like what an adult red-eared slider actually needs. Plan for the second one from day one. For everything else about keeping this species, see our complete [Red-Eared Slider care guide](/guides/red-eared-slider/).

## Size

Roughly 10 gallons of water per inch of shell length is the rule of thumb. In practice, that means a practical adult minimum of 75 to 100 gallons for one turtle, with many experienced keepers recommending 100 to 125 gallons or more for better water quality and swimming room. Females grow noticeably larger than males, commonly 10 to 12 inches or more compared to 7 to 9 inches for males, and need proportionally more space.

Juveniles can start in a smaller temporary setup, 20 to 40 gallons, but they outgrow it quickly. A secure, predator-proof outdoor pond with adequate depth and basking access is an excellent option for adults in suitable climates.

## Temperature

Water temperature should sit at 75 to 80°F, some sources accept up to 86°F, but stability matters more than hitting an exact number. Basking surface temperature should reach 90 to 95°F, some sources allow slightly higher. Ambient air on the cool side runs mid-70s to low 80s during the day, with a nighttime drop into the mid-60s to mid-70s being acceptable.

A clear thermal gradient, achieved with a quality <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eQY1L5" product="Submersible aquarium heater">submersible heater</AffiliateLink> sized to your actual water volume plus a separate <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/44eEEFH" product="Basking dome/fixture">basking heat lamp and dome fixture</AffiliateLink>, lets the turtle regulate its own temperature by choosing where to be. A UVB fixture alone won't create adequate basking warmth, the two do different jobs.

## Humidity

Not a primary concern the way it is for terrestrial reptiles, since this is a fully aquatic species outside of basking time. What matters is a completely dry basking area where the turtle can fully dry off. High ambient humidity without that dry zone contributes to shell problems covered in our [Red-Eared Slider health issues guide](/blog/red-eared-slider-health-issues-guide/), but normal household humidity is otherwise fine.

## Substrate

Many keepers prefer bare bottom, it's the easiest to clean and carries no ingestion risk. Large river rock or gravel bigger than the turtle's head is an acceptable alternative. Avoid fine sand or small gravel entirely, both can be ingested, and skip anything that traps waste and makes cleaning difficult.

## Lighting

UVB is mandatory for this species. Red-eared sliders fall into Ferguson Zone 3, aim for a UV index of roughly 3.0 to 4.0 at the basking surface. Linear T5 HO fluorescent bulbs, like a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0FF9GHCDC?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Zoo Med ReptiSun T5 HO Terrarium Hood with 10.0 Bulb 24in L">full-length T5 HO fixture</AffiliateLink> spanning a good portion of the enclosure, are strongly preferred over compact coil bulbs.

Run the light 10 to 12 hours a day on a timer, and pair it with a separate heat lamp, UVB fixtures alone don't create adequate basking warmth. Replace UVB bulbs on schedule, typically every 6 to 12 months, even if they still visibly light up, output degrades well before the bulb looks dim.

<FunFact>
Red-eared sliders are established as an invasive species on every continent except Antarctica, largely thanks to decades of pet owners releasing turtles they could no longer house. It's one more reason that secure, predator-proof outdoor pond setup matters as much for keeping other wildlife out as it does for keeping your turtle in.
</FunFact>

## The Details That Catch First-Time Keepers Off Guard

Filtration needs to be genuinely powerful, sliders produce a lot of waste, and over-filtering, using a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4vNGY2v" product="Canister filter (Fluval 407, up to 100 gal)">canister filter rated well above your actual tank volume</AffiliateLink>, is a common and reasonable recommendation, not overkill. This is the same lesson keepers of other heavy-waste aquatic pets learn the hard way, koi included, see our [Koi tank setup guide](/blog/koi-tank-setup-guide/) for how that plays out in a pond setting.

The <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0D9NPKV3K?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Turtle Topper Basking Platform for Aquatic Turtles, 40 Breeder/75 Gallon">basking platform</AffiliateLink> must allow full drying, not just a place to climb partway out of the water. Water depth should be at least 1.5 to 2 times the shell length, enough for the turtle to right itself if flipped, but not so deep that a weak or sick animal struggles to reach the surface. A secure lid matters, red-eared sliders are surprisingly capable climbers and genuine escape artists. Wash your hands thoroughly after any contact with the turtle or its water, Salmonella risk is real. Gravid females need a nesting option, without a suitable place to lay, a female risks egg-binding, a serious complication.
