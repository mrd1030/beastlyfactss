# Rat Enrichment: Company First, Then Variety

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Rats have a deep enrichment literature behind them, most of it from laboratories, and the findings most useful to a pet keeper cost almost nothing.

<KeyTakeaway>
Company comes first. In a survey of pet rat keepers, rats in smaller groups fought more, and rats whose keepers spent more time with them got on better with their cage mates. Paper-based enrichment in the cage went with more exploring and friendlier behavior. Beyond that, cover seven kinds of enrichment: nesting material, digging substrate, a foraging device, climbing structure, a tube, a hideaway and a suspended area.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Research Says

The deep background is a 2022 systematic review in *Frontiers in Veterinary Science*, led by Paul Mieske, covering environmental enrichment for laboratory rats and mice. It found both benefit similarly from enriched living environments, with no evidence that housing conditions affect the welfare of different strains differently.

The more directly useful paper is a 2026 exploratory study of **pet** rats by Stephanie Schneidewind and Ines Windschnurer, a survey of German-speaking rat keepers. Rats given **paper-based enrichment** in the cage explored more when let out to roam and were friendlier toward their cage mates. The time a keeper spent directly with the rats predicted friendlier behavior too, and rats kept in **smaller groups** showed more aggression. The authors read the results as support for a complex cage, suitable enrichment, proper social housing and regular kind contact.

<FunFact>
Plain shredded paper, tissue and cardboard cost next to nothing, and in the pet rat survey paper-based enrichment went with both more exploring and friendlier behavior toward cage mates.
</FunFact>

## The Seven Categories

Work through them rather than buying more of what you already have.

**Nesting material.** Paper, fleece strips, tissue. Rats build, and material to build with is one of the highest-value cheap items in rodent welfare generally.

**Digging substrate.** A tub or a deep corner of loose material they can excavate.

**Foraging device.** Something food has to be worked out of. **Climbing structure.** Ropes, ladders, branches. Rats climb constantly and a cage with only floors is half a cage.

**Suspended area.** Hammocks and platforms. Rats sleep off the ground given the option.

## Social Housing Comes Before All Seven

None of the above substitutes for another rat. These are intensely social animals and single housing is a welfare problem that enrichment does not solve. Two is the minimum, a small same-sex group is better, and a cage that fits a group is the first purchase: [the minimum floor size and the bar spacing that keeps a rat inside it](/blog/rat-tank-setup-guide/) are what to check before buying. ## Training, Which Rats Are Unusually Good At

Rats learn target training, recall and short trick sequences quickly, and a clicker and target stick is the whole kit. A few minutes daily is cognitive enrichment, exercise and relationship-building at once, and it is free. Time the sessions to the rat's clock: rats are mostly nocturnal or crepuscular, awake around dusk and dawn, though they shift toward their keeper's schedule over time, so early evening usually finds a rat awake and ready to work. This is a species where the interaction genuinely is enrichment and not a stressor, which is not true of most small pets, [mice](/blog/mouse-enrichment-guide/) included.

## The Wheel, in Proportion

A <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01AFI3ERM?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Exotic Nutrition Silent Runner 12 inch">correctly sized solid-surface wheel</AffiliateLink> is a fine addition. Solid surface, never wire or mesh, which injures feet, and large enough that the back stays flat. It is one category of seven, not the centerpiece.

## Priority Order

1. Companions, before anything else
2. A cage large enough for a group with multiple levels
3. Coverage across all seven enrichment categories, not depth in one
4. Scatter feeding instead of a bowl
5. Daily out-of-cage time and training
6. A correctly sized solid wheel
7. Rotation and novelty, ongoing instead of last

## What Not to Do

Do not keep a single rat. Do not buy a better version of a category you already cover while leaving three uncovered. Do not use a wire or mesh wheel, or one small enough to arch the spine. Do not use cedar or pine shavings, which cause respiratory problems in a species already prone to them. And do not treat scatter feeding as a substitute for a proper ration.
