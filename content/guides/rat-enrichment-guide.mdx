# Rat Enrichment: It Is the Number of Types That Matters

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Rats have more enrichment research behind them than almost any other pet species, and the most useful finding for a pet keeper is not about any particular product.

<KeyTakeaway>
In pet rats, a greater total *number of enrichment types* was associated with more frequent digging, nesting and climbing. Not one magic item: nesting material, digging substrate, a foraging device, climbing structure, a tube, a hideaway and a suspended area, counted as categories. Variety is the measured variable.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Research Says

The deep background is a 2022 systematic review in *Frontiers in Veterinary Science*, led by Paul Mieske, covering environmental enrichment for laboratory rats and mice. It found both benefit similarly from enriched living environments, with no evidence that housing conditions affect the welfare of different strains differently.

The more directly useful paper is Stephanie Schneidewind and Ines Windschnurer's 2026 exploratory study of **pet** rats, a survey of German-speaking rat keepers published in *Applied Animal Behaviour Science*. It found that a greater total number of enrichment types, listed as nesting material, digging substrate, foraging device, climbing structure, tube, hideaway and suspended area, was associated with **more frequent digging, nesting and climbing**, all natural behaviors.

<FunFact>
That reframes the shopping list. The question is not "is this the best rat toy" but "how many of the seven categories does this cage currently cover". A cage with one excellent item in it and nothing else scores worse than one with seven adequate ones.
</FunFact>

## The Seven Categories

Work through them rather than buying more of what you already have.

**Nesting material.** Paper, fleece strips, tissue. Rats build, and material to build with is one of the highest-value cheap items in rodent welfare generally.

**Digging substrate.** A tub or a deep corner of loose material they can excavate.

**Foraging device.** Something food has to be worked out of. **Climbing structure.** Ropes, ladders, branches. Rats climb constantly and a cage with only floors is half a cage.

**Suspended area.** Hammocks and platforms. Rats sleep off the ground given the option.

## Social Housing Comes Before All Seven

None of the above substitutes for another rat. These are intensely social animals and single housing is a welfare problem that enrichment does not solve. Two is the minimum, a small same-sex group is better, and a cage that fits a group is the first purchase: [the minimum floor size and the bar spacing that keeps a rat inside it](/blog/rat-tank-setup-guide/) are what to check before buying. ## Training, Which Rats Are Unusually Good At

Rats learn target training, recall and short trick sequences quickly, and a clicker and target stick is the whole kit. A few minutes daily is cognitive enrichment, exercise and relationship-building at once, and it is free. This is a species where the interaction genuinely is enrichment and not a stressor, which is not true of most small pets, [mice](/blog/mouse-enrichment-guide/) included.

## The Wheel, in Proportion

Solid surface, never wire or mesh, which injures feet, and large enough that the back stays flat. It is one category of seven, not the centerpiece.

## Priority Order

1. Companions, before anything else
2. A cage large enough for a group with multiple levels
3. Coverage across all seven enrichment categories, not depth in one
4. Scatter feeding instead of a bowl
5. Daily out-of-cage time and training
6. A correctly sized solid wheel
7. Rotation and novelty, ongoing instead of last

## What Not to Do

Do not keep a single rat. Do not buy a better version of a category you already cover while leaving three uncovered, since the study measured breadth. Do not use a wire or mesh wheel, or one small enough to arch the spine. Do not use cedar or pine shavings, which cause respiratory problems in a species already prone to them. And do not treat scatter feeding as a substitute for a proper ration.
