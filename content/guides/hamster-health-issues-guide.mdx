# Common Hamster Health Issues (Wet Tail Is the One to Know)

## Wet Tail (Proliferative Ileitis)

This is the condition every hamster owner needs to recognize immediately. A large veterinary study tracking causes of death in pet hamsters found wet tail to be both the most common diagnosed condition and the leading specific cause of death, present in roughly 7 to 8% of hamsters studied. It's bacterial, tied to stress, and hits young Syrians between 3 and 10 weeks old hardest.

**Watch for:** watery diarrhea, a wet or matted rear end, lethargy, and a hunched posture.

This is a same-day emergency. Wet tail can be fatal within 24 to 48 hours without treatment. Don't wait to see if it improves, get to a vet immediately. It's typically triggered by stress, transport, overcrowding, poor hygiene, or a sudden diet change, which is worth keeping in mind when bringing a new hamster home or making any changes to its routine.

## Tumors

Common in older hamsters, particularly females, and can grow quickly given how fast a hamster's metabolism runs. This is one of the more frequent causes of death in aging hamsters and always warrants a vet visit.

## Diabetes (More Common in Dwarf Species)

Excessive drinking and urination alongside weight loss. Manageable with a vet-guided low-sugar diet. Sugary treats, honey sticks, and fruit-heavy diets are the typical culprits, worth avoiding as a matter of course rather than waiting for symptoms. ## The Husbandry Pattern Worth Remembering

Stress and poor hygiene are directly linked to wet tail, the single biggest threat this species faces. Dusty or aromatic bedding causes respiratory problems. And a sugar-heavy diet raises diabetes risk in dwarf species specifically. All three are fully within an owner's control, and given how short a hamster's life already is, getting the basics right matters even more than it would with a longer-lived pet. Rough handling and a stressful environment are also part of that picture, see our [Hamster handling guide](/blog/hamster-handling-guide/) for the approach that keeps a hamster calm rather than defensive.

## When to See a Vet

See a vet immediately for watery diarrhea or a matted, wet rear end, don't wait on this one. Book a routine visit promptly for dropped food or drooling, sudden weight loss, a lump or growth, or labored breathing.
