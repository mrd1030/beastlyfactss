# Common Canary Health Issues

<VetDisclaimer />

Canaries face a handful of conditions common enough in this species to know before they show up, several of them mite-related and easy to overlook until song or breathing is already affected.

## Air-Sac Mites

A genuinely canary-relevant parasite living in the respiratory tract. Watch for open-mouth breathing, clicking or wheezing sounds, and, tellingly, a loss of song in a previously singing male. Always see an avian vet, this needs targeted treatment and won't resolve without it. Caused by exposure to an already-infected bird, and worsened by stress or overcrowding.

<FunFact>
A previously singing male canary going quiet is one of the more telling early signs of air-sac mites. The loss of song can show up before more obvious breathing symptoms do, which makes it worth taking seriously rather than dismissing as a mood change.
</FunFact>

## Scaly Face and Leg Mites

Crusty, roughened buildup around the beak, eyes, legs, and feet. See a vet, this is genuinely treatable, especially if caught early rather than left to progress.

## Feather and Red Mites

Bald patches and visible nighttime itching, since these mites feed at night rather than during the day, which is part of why they're easy to miss initially. A related sign, unexplained lethargy that gets worse over time, can point to anemia from a heavy infestation. Treatment needs to cover both the bird and its full environment, cage, perches, and surrounding area, mites left in the cage will simply reinfest a treated bird otherwise.

## Respiratory Infection

Ruffled feathers, discharge, and labored breathing. Caused by poor air quality, a dirty cage, household fumes, or drafts. Always see a vet.

## Other Conditions Worth Knowing

Cataracts and feather cysts (sometimes needing surgical removal) both occur in this species. Feather-picking can signal mites, a diet deficiency, boredom, or general stress, worth a vet visit to rule out a medical cause before assuming it's purely behavioral.

## The Early Warning Signs Worth Watching For

A canary that stops singing, shows fluffed or dull feathers, or becomes generally lethargic is signaling something's wrong, well before more specific symptoms might appear. Given how many of this species' issues trace back to mites and air quality, both genuinely preventable, paying attention to these early, general signs matters more than waiting for a specific symptom to confirm what's going on.
