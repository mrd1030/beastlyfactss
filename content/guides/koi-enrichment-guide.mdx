# Koi Enrichment: Volume, Depth, and Somewhere to Overwinter

<AffiliateDisclosure />

A koi is a decades-long commitment to an animal that will reach the length of your arm. The enrichment question is mostly a construction question.

<KeyTakeaway>
Koi reach around two feet, live for decades, shoal, and learn who feeds them. Nearly every welfare problem they have traces to a pond that was **built for the fish in the bag**. Volume, depth and a stable winter refuge come first, and everything on this page after that is comparatively easy.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Research Says

There is no controlled koi enrichment study to point at. Koi are carp, among the most-studied fish in aquaculture, and the useful literature is about **space, stocking density, water quality and social behavior**, not enrichment as a category.

The group size work on aquarium fish, done on [neon tetra](/blog/neon-tetra-enrichment-guide/), found effects were **species-specific**, which is a reason not to import shoal-size numbers from a tetra into a pond. What is clear about koi is that they are social, they feed together, and they learn routines and people.

So this guide is built on natural history and on the physical requirements of a very large, very long-lived fish, and it says so instead of dressing that up.

<FunFact>
Koi genuinely do learn individual people, and long-term keepers describe fish that surface for one household member and ignore another. For a fish sold as pond decoration, that is a considerable amount of animal.
</FunFact>

## Volume, Then Depth

The whole thing. Adult koi need thousands of gallons rather than hundreds, and a pond built to the fish you bought is a pond you will be rebuilding.

**Depth matters separately from volume.** At least three to four feet in part of the pond in most temperate climates, so the bottom does not freeze and stays thermally stable through winter. That deep zone is where the fish overwinter, and a shallow pond is a pond where a hard winter is an event and not a season.

## Filtration and Aeration for the Adults

Size the system for the fish the koi will become, not the ones swimming now.

The dangerous window is summer, when warm water holds less oxygen and a heavy fish load is producing the most waste. ## A Group

Social shoaling fish that feed and move together. A single koi is a poorer arrangement than a group, and the constraint is pond volume, not compatibility.

Stock for the adults. The commonest route to an overstocked pond is buying a sensible number of small fish.

## Structure and Shade

Open water for swimming, plus shaded areas and some cover. Shade keeps summer temperatures down and gives fish somewhere to be out of direct sun, and cover matters for a pond fish with herons above it.

Plants help with shade, cover and water quality, and koi will eat and uproot them. Protect planting in baskets or give it a shallow zone the fish cannot fully reach.

## Feeding as Interaction

The enrichment koi keepers already do without calling it that. They learn a routine, they surface for it, and most will take food from a hand.

Vary where and when feeding happens, scatter food across the surface, never one corner, and use foods that float and take time to work at. In cold water, feeding drops away entirely, and pushing food into fish that cannot digest it is a common winter mistake.

## Winter, Deliberately

Koi overwinter in the deep zone at low metabolic rate. What they need is depth, a hole kept open in the ice for gas exchange, no feeding once the water is cold, and to be left alone.

Do not break ice by hitting it, which sends a shockwave through the water. An aerator or a floating heater keeps a hole open properly.

## Priority Order

1. Volume and depth sized for two-foot adults
2. Filtration and aeration rated for the adult fish load
3. A deep zone that stays thermally stable through winter
4. A group, never a single fish
5. Shade, cover and predator netting
6. Feeding as interaction, varied in place and timing
7. Correct winter handling, which mostly means leaving them be

## What Not to Do

Do not build a pond for the fish you are buying. Do not size filtration for the current stocking. Do not build shallow and hope. Do not feed in cold water. Do not smash ice to open a hole. Do not keep one koi alone. And weigh the [koi versus goldfish comparison](/blog/koi-vs-goldfish-guide/) before deciding, since goldfish are the right answer for a lot of ponds that get koi.
