# Common Axolotl Health Issues (Most Trace Back to Temperature)

<VetDisclaimer />
<AffiliateDisclosure />

Axolotls are hardy in the right conditions and surprisingly fragile outside them. A large share of what goes wrong traces back to water that's too warm, which makes temperature control the single most important thing to get right.

## Fungal Infection

Shows up as a white or grey, cotton-like growth on the gills or body. It's typically triggered by warm water, poor water quality, stress, or an existing injury that gave the fungus an opening. Treatment starts with correcting water quality and cooling the tank down with the help of a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4wCcGzE" product="Aquarium chiller, 1/3 HP compressor (79 gal capacity)">chiller</AffiliateLink>, and salt baths can help. Severe cases need a vet-prescribed antifungal.

## Impaction

Swallowed gravel or small decor. Look for bloating, floating instead of resting on the bottom, appetite loss, and an absence of waste. Mild cases sometimes resolve with fasting and cooler water under a vet's guidance, but a blockage low in the gut can need surgery.

## Ammonia and Nitrite Burns

Red, inflamed patches on the skin or gills, usually from an uncycled or poorly maintained tank. Water changes and a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3Rg5gTT" product="Water conditioner">dechlorinator</AffiliateLink> correct this, but the underlying fix is making sure your tank is properly cycled and regularly checked with a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4p1T7yd" product="Water test kit">water test kit</AffiliateLink> before you rely on it. The targets are 0 ppm ammonia, 0 ppm nitrite and nitrate under 10 ppm, and the [water change routine for cold, clean axolotl water](/blog/why-axolotls-need-cold-clean-water/) is what holds them there.

<FunFact>
An axolotl's gills are a surprisingly good early warning system. They tend to curl or angle forward under heat stress well before more obvious signs like appetite loss ever show up, which makes them worth checking daily, not just when something already looks wrong.
</FunFact>

## Bacterial Infection

Red streaks, reddened skin, open sores and a swollen, fluid-filled body (ascites) point to bacteria, most often *Aeromonas* or *Pseudomonas*, which take hold when the water runs warm or dirty. This needs a vet and prescription treatment; water changes alone will not clear it.

## Obesity

Obesity is a recognized problem in pet axolotls, and it comes from feeding an adult on a juvenile's schedule. A young axolotl eats daily; an adult needs food only every 2 to 3 days.

## Heat Stress

This is the condition that ties back to temperature most directly, and it's worth taking seriously. Watch for curled or forward-facing gill tips, appetite loss, floating, and pale patches on the skin. Extended exposure above roughly 72°F starts to suppress the immune system. Above about 75°F, axolotls become sluggish, float uncontrollably, and pick up bacterial and fungal infections, and the heat itself can be fatal. Gills curled forward can also mean too much current instead of heat, so check the filter output as well as the thermometer.

## When to See a Vet

Find an aquatic or exotic-amphibian vet before you need one, general small-animal clinics often aren't equipped for this species. Ask a practice directly whether it sees axolotls, and use the Association of Reptile and Amphibian Veterinarians' member directory to find candidates. Bring a sample of tank water in a clean container to the visit, along with your latest test readings. See a vet promptly for fungal growth that hasn't improved within 48 hours of correcting water quality, suspected impaction, red streaking or open wounds that suggest a bacterial infection, or appetite loss lasting 5 days or more.

Most of what shows up on this list traces back to a setup issue and not to bad luck, which is worth remembering the next time the water looks a little warmer than usual. Overfeeding and the wrong food are common triggers too. Check the [Axolotl encyclopedia profile](/encyclopedia/animal/axolotl/) for more.
