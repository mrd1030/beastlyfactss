# Leopard Gecko vs. Crested Gecko: Which Beginner Gecko Fits You Better

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Both are near the very top of every "best first reptile" list, and for good reason - but the actual day-to-day care looks quite different between them. The real differences come down to whether you're willing to keep live insects, what shape of enclosure you have room for, and how much you can commit to gentle handling.

## Diet Is the Biggest Practical Difference

Leopard geckos are strict insectivores - crickets, mealworms, or dubia roaches are the actual diet, not a supplement to something else, which means committing to <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4uGamHo" product="Dubia Roach Colony Starter Kit">keeping a feeder insect colony</AffiliateLink> or making [regular pet store runs](/blog/leopard-gecko-cost-guide/) indefinitely. Crested geckos are the rare reptile that can thrive almost entirely on a commercial powdered diet mixed with water, with insects offered only now and then for enrichment; how much to mix and how often, by age, is set in the [Crested Gecko feeding guide](/blog/crested-gecko-feeding-guide/). That is what makes the crested gecko the easier of the two to feed. For anyone who doesn't want live bugs in the house at all, this single fact usually settles the decision on its own.

<FunFact>
Crested geckos were believed extinct after their last confirmed sighting in the late 1800s, until a small wild population was rediscovered in New Caledonia in 1994 - meaning the crested gecko now living happily on a fruit-mix diet in millions of homes worldwide was, for most of the 20th century, thought to be gone for good.
</FunFact>

## Heating Requirements Point Opposite Directions

Leopard geckos need a genuine warm zone, typically from an <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/44hAxJ1" product="Under-tank heat mat + thermostat kit">under-tank heat mat</AffiliateLink> running around 88 to 92 degrees F, since they're ground-dwelling and thermoregulate against warm ground in the wild. Crested geckos need far less: an enclosure around 72 to 78 degrees F with a low-wattage bulb on a thermostat making a gentle 82 to 85 degree F basking spot, which the [Crested Gecko tank setup guide](/blog/crested-gecko-tank-setup-guide/) sizes by enclosure. The danger runs the other way for them: long stretches above 80 degrees F are already too warm, and past 85 they overheat. If you want the lighter heating setup, that favors a crested gecko.

<KeyTakeaway>
Choose a Leopard Gecko if you're comfortable keeping live feeder insects and want a calmer, ground-dwelling pet. You'll need to run a belly-heat mat year-round, but as a tradeoff, a dropped tail grows back.
</KeyTakeaway>

## Enclosure Shape Isn't Interchangeable

A leopard gecko is a ground-dweller and needs floor space more than height, with minimal climbing decor required. A crested gecko is arboreal - it lives in the trees in the wild - and needs <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4vlb6Bp" product="18x18x24 in glass terrarium (arboreal)">a tall enclosure</AffiliateLink> with <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3QQNV3L" product="Climbing branch (Mopani wood)">branches, vines, and plants to climb through</AffiliateLink>. A leftover wide, short tank suits a leopard gecko fine and would waste most of a crested gecko's usable space, and vice versa.

## Tail Loss Isn't Equally Forgiving

Both species can drop their tails defensively if grabbed or badly startled. A leopard gecko's tail grows back, usually shorter and less detailed than the original but functional. A crested gecko's tail does not regrow at all - it's gone permanently, and most keepers consider a "frog butt" (tail-less) crested gecko just as good a pet, but it does mean handling a crested gecko calls for a bit more care and patience, especially early on.

<KeyTakeaway>
A Crested Gecko is the better fit if you'd rather skip live insects and run only a small basking bulb instead of a belly-heat mat. Just handle it gently, tail loss is permanent for this species, and it's a startlingly good jumper that can go from your hand to across the room in an instant.
</KeyTakeaway>

## Bottom Line

The crested gecko is the easier of the two to feed and heat; the leopard gecko is the easier one to hold. Pick a leopard gecko if you don't mind keeping live feeder insects and want a calmer, ground-dwelling pet that tolerates handling well. Pick a crested gecko if you'd rather skip live insects entirely, want a small basking bulb instead of a belly-heat mat, and have room for a tall, planted enclosure - just handle it gently, since a dropped tail won't come back. The [leopard gecko](/encyclopedia/animal/leopard-gecko/) and [crested gecko](/encyclopedia/animal/crested-gecko/) encyclopedia profiles cover the arid and forest origins behind those differences.
