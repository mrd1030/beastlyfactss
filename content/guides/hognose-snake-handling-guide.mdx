# How to Handle a Western Hognose Snake (And Why the Drama Is Mostly Bluff)

The show looks like a fight. It is the opposite.

## The puff-adder act

Inflates, flattens a cobra-like hood, hisses, throws closed-mouth mock strikes. If that fails, some roll belly up, mouth open, tongue out, sometimes musk. Flip one and it often rolls again.

That is bluff. A snake doing this is trying not to bite. Real aggression is almost unheard of.

<FunFact>
Flip a playing-dead hognose right side up and it will often flop back onto its back. The sequence is hardwired.
</FunFact>

## Venom

Rear-fanged. Mild. Not medically significant for a healthy person. The documented case was a keeper bitten while offering a mouse: local swelling, bruising, mild cellulitis, no systemic effects, about five months to heal. Handle thoughtfully around feeding. A bite that breaks skin still goes to a doctor. This is not the animal venomous-reptile laws are written for.

## Pickup

Wash prey scent off. Not right after a meal. Wait out the hood. Lift from mid-body. Steady. A constant hood at every routine check usually means the enclosure is short on cover or depth. The [enrichment guide](/blog/hognose-snake-enrichment-guide/) is that fix.

## First-timer mistakes

- Panicking at the hood and dropping the snake
- Handling after a meal
- Hands that still smell like prey
