# Rat Feeding: Diet, Portions, and Why Hamster Food Isn't Rat Food

A bag labeled "Hamster and Gerbil Food" looks close enough for a rat too, but the two formulas are built to different specs for a reason, and RSPCA guidance is blunt about not swapping one for the other.

## The Staple Diet

A nutritionally complete, rat-specific pelleted diet or lab block should be the base of every meal. RSPCA guidance on this is unambiguous: always feed food designed specifically for rats, rather than pellets made for rabbits, guinea pigs, hamsters, or other herbivores. Real product formulations back that up. Oxbow's Essentials Adult Rat Food guarantees a minimum 15% crude protein, 4% crude fat, and a fiber range of just 2 to 5%, while the company's own Garden Select Hamster & Gerbil Food runs a meaningfully higher 10 to 15% fiber alongside 16% protein, a formula built for a more herbivore-leaning digestive system than a rat has. Oxbow's separate Mouse & Young Rat Food, guaranteeing a minimum 18% crude protein, is formulated specifically for growing rats under 6 months and for pregnant or nursing females, and is explicitly labeled as not intended for adult rats. The general pattern holds even across brands: a rat's nutritional needs shift by life stage in a way a single all-purpose "small pet" bag doesn't account for.

## Feeding Frequency and Portions

Feed twice daily, morning and evening, per RSPCA guidance, adjusting the amount so a rat finishes what's offered and holds a healthy weight rather than free-feeding an unlimited bowl. Supplement daily with small amounts of fresh vegetables, and offer fruit, grains, and seeds more sparingly. Keep all of that supplemental food, vegetables, fruit, grains, and seeds combined, under about 10% of the total diet, with the rat-specific pellet or lab block doing most of the nutritional work.

## Foods to Avoid

RSPCA guidance specifically flags onion, citrus fruit, walnuts, rhubarb, grapes, raisins, and chocolate as foods to avoid entirely. Also skip caffeine, alcohol, raw beans, unripe tomato, green or raw potato, and anything salty, sugary, or heavily processed, none of it is worth the risk for an animal this small. Fresh water should always be available in a bottle rather than an open bowl, and RSPCA guidance specifically recommends more than one bottle per cage so a blockage or a spot of competition between cage mates never leaves a rat without access.

## Rats Are True Omnivores

This is where rats differ from several other small pets on this site. Chinchillas and guinea pigs are strict herbivores built around a hindgut-fermentation system that fresh meat would actively disrupt. Rats are not built that way. In the wild, brown rats eat insects, small vertebrates, and carrion alongside grain, seeds, and plant matter, and research on wild Norway rat diet has found they actually prefer meat when given the option between the two. A pet rat can safely have small amounts of cooked lean meat, egg, or legumes a couple of times a week as a genuine, species-appropriate protein source rather than an indulgence, something that would be entirely inappropriate to offer a chinchilla or a guinea pig on the same shelf.

## The Mistakes That Matter

Reaching for whatever small-pet pellet is already in the house, hamster food, gerbil food, rabbit food, is the single most common feeding mistake with rats, and it's an easy one to make since the bags all look similar on a store shelf. It isn't a trivial substitution: those formulas are built around a more fiber-forward, herbivore-leaning digestive system, not the one a rat has, and a mismatch here shows up over time as poor coat condition and a diet that isn't doing what the label implies.

Overfeeding fatty extras is the second big one. Merck Veterinary Manual specifically notes that obesity is common in pet rats and that calorie restriction, without compromising overall nutrition, is one of the few interventions actually shown to extend their lifespan, which makes weighing out a measured daily portion a meaningful habit rather than a fussy one.
