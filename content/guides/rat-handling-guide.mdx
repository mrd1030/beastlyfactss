# How to Handle a Rat (And Why It's the Least Likely Small Pet to Bite)

Hands-on. Trainable. A real bite is uncommon. Most of what looks like one is something else.

## Hands-on on purpose

Handled regularly from a young age they come looking for contact. Food rewards teach a name, a recall, and simple tricks. That is the same learning that put rats in maze labs for a century. Willard Small built the first rat maze at Clark in 1900. Watson and Carr's Kerplunk work in 1907 used them because they learn a route and keep it.

## Why bites are rare

Fear, not a hunt. A scared rat usually runs. Allogrooming is a fast nibble they use on each other and sometimes on fingers. Poor close-up eyesight means a finger that smells like food gets checked with the mouth.

## Pickup

A few days to settle. Approach slow. Both hands: one under the chest, one under the hips. Hold close to your body, not at arm's length.

**Never pick a rat up by the tail.** The skin shears off. That is degloving, or tail slip. Scoop from underneath. Tell children and guests.

<FunFact>
The first rat maze at Clark was modeled on Hampton Court. A pet that answers to its name is using the same memory.
</FunFact>

## First-timer mistakes

- Reading allogrooming as a fight
- Lifting by the tail
- Handling the hour they come home
- Expecting a poorly socialized adult to act like a hand-raised pup

Daily handling and supervised floor time are welfare, not a bonus. The [mouse encyclopedia](/encyclopedia/animal/mouse/) is the harder-to-tame comparison. The [rat encyclopedia](/encyclopedia/animal/rat/) is the species page.
