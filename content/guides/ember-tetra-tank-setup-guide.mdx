# Ember Tetra Tank Setup: A 10-Gallon School, Soft Water, and Dense Plants

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Ember tetras are a small South American characin from the Araguaia River basin in central Brazil, first described from the Rio das Mortes. They are thought to live in minor tributaries, backwaters and oxbow lakes rather than main river channels, in soft, weakly acidic water with dense plants and leaf litter. An adult reaches about 0.8 inch (2 cm), and the whole tank is built around that small fish living in a group.

## Tank Size

<AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4pjiyeT" product="10 gallon tank">A 10-gallon tank</AffiliateLink> is the size for a school of 10. The footprint guidance for this species is a base of at least 18 x 12 inches (45 x 30 cm), set for the recommended purchase of 8 to 10 fish, and a standard 10-gallon covers it. Aim for 10 or more.

A 5-gallon nano is sometimes suggested, but only with a smaller school. The school is the point of keeping embers, so the extra 5 gallons buy the group of 10 the species is kept in. The closest group-size evidence comes from [a study on neon tetras](/blog/aquarium-stocking-and-tankmates-guide/), where groups of 10 shoaled the most and showed less aggression and darting than smaller groups.

Tankmates have to be small and calm. Embers are very peaceful, and boisterous or much larger fish crowd them out. They leave adult dwarf shrimp alone but will eat shrimplets.

## Temperature

75 to 82°F (24 to 28°C) is the range, held by <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eQY1L5" product="Submersible aquarium heater">a submersible heater</AffiliateLink>. 82°F is the ceiling, so the heater sits inside the range with room to spare above it.

That band overlaps a [neon tetra tank](/blog/neon-tetra-tank-setup-guide/), usually run at 72 to 78°F, only between 75 and 78°F. At the top it stops short of a [cardinal tetra](/blog/cardinal-tetra-tank-setup-guide/), which tolerates water reaching 86°F. An ember tank treats 82°F as its limit.

## Water Chemistry

Embers want soft water that runs from acidic to neutral: pH 5.0 to 7.0, with a general hardness of 1 to 10 dGH. That is the soft, weakly acidic water of the Araguaia backwaters, and it is the same pH range a cardinal tank is run at, so the two species differ on temperature far more than on chemistry.

Test the source water before the tank is filled. Hard tap water comes down by [blending it with RO water](/blog/freshwater-ph-gh-kh-guide/), which lowers GH and KH together so the pH follows and then holds. A bottled pH-down product forces the number for a while and leaves the hardness that set it untouched. Every top-off and water change is dechlorinated with a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3Rg5gTT" product="Water conditioner">water conditioner</AffiliateLink> and mixed the same way the tank was first filled.

## Filtration

Keep the flow gentle. A little water movement does no harm, and a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3T8xyAd" product="Sponge filter">sponge filter</AffiliateLink> fits that brief. It is also [the gentlest intake for a tank that holds shrimp](/blog/aquarium-filtration-guide/), which matters when dwarf shrimp share the tank. A hang-on-back works too when its output is baffled or turned down.

## Plants and a Dark Substrate

Plant the tank heavily and lay a dark substrate under it. Dense stems and leaves give a school of 0.8-inch fish the kind of cover the backwaters they come from are full of.

Floating plants earn a place on the surface too. Leaf litter on the substrate is optional: as the leaves decay, colonies of microbes establish on them.

In a clear, sunlit stretch of the Araguaia basin, a shoal of embers was hard to spot from above, and when cloud covered the sun their dark backs let them fade into the shade. The shoal shifted position every few minutes. The reason was hunting in the tall stands of Eichhornia nearby: pike cichlids.
</FunFact>

## Cycle the Tank Before the School Goes In

An ember tank is [cycled before any fish are added](/blog/aquarium-cycling-guide/): ammonia and nitrite both read zero, and nitrate has started to build. Adding the whole school of 10 at once is one of the two safe ways to stock a newly cycled tank, as long as the water is tested every day until the readings settle.

New fish spend 30 days in [a bare quarantine tank](/blog/fish-quarantine-and-treatment-guide/) before they join a display tank that already holds fish or shrimp.

The [ember tetra encyclopedia profile](/encyclopedia/animal/ember-tetra/) has more on the species itself.
