# How to Handle a Blue-Tongued Skink

Docile once settled. How fast that happens depends on where it came from.

## The display

Hiss, flatten, gape, blue tongue. Theater. A settled animal rarely follows through. A scared one can bite.

<FunFact>
The tongue flash is meant to look worse than the animal is.
</FunFact>

## Origin

Captive-bred Northern: calmer, faster to tame. Wild-caught Indonesian: no prior people, starts defensive. Tanimbar lines run feistier.

## The wait

2 to 3 weeks. Eating first. Food interaction, then handling. Gloves on a feisty wild-caught animal at the start. The [feeding guide](/blog/blue-tongue-skink-feeding-guide/) is the settle window.

## Pickup

Come in from the side. Hand under the body. Support along the forearm, tail included. Never the tail. A fracture plane runs through each tail vertebra. It can drop. The regrown tail is shorter cartilage. Wash hands so they do not smell like food.

Huff: slow down. Flattened hiss: not today. Settled walk with tongue flicks: keep going. Short bursts and freezes: nervous.

Start at 5 minutes. Add a minute when it stays still. Cap around 15 minutes. Several times a week for a settled animal. A new wild-caught Indonesian stays at the short end.

## First-timer mistakes

- Too soon
- Head pokes
- Food lunge read as a fight
- Grab from above
- Lift by the tail
