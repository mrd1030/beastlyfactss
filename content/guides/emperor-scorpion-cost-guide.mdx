# How Much Does an Emperor Scorpion Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Emperor scorpions are one of the more affordable exotic pets to keep, both to buy and to maintain, and the "scary invertebrate" reputation doesn't come with a scary price tag. For the full care picture beyond budgeting, see our complete [Emperor Scorpion care guide](/guides/emperor-scorpion/).

## The Scorpion: $25 to $200

The typical range for captive-bred specimens is $25 to $100. Juveniles or smaller individuals often run $25 to $50, with larger adults or proven breeders toward the higher end, $60 to $100. Specialty size or locality variants can reach $100 to $200 in some listings, and pet store pricing commonly falls in the $30 to $80 range.

One thing worth flagging directly: some sources list dramatically higher prices, $800 or more, but that figure doesn't match current breeder, retailer, or hobbyist reporting and appears to be an outlier, not a realistic expectation. True color or size morphs exist but aren't nearly as extensively bred in this species as they are in many popular reptiles, so don't expect the kind of morph-driven price spread you'd see with a ball python.

A secure glass or plastic enclosure, minimum around 10 gallons, roughly 20x10x12 inches, is the starting point, and larger is better for groups or enrichment. No specialized UVB lighting is needed here, which keeps this setup meaningfully cheaper than a comparable reptile enclosure. An adult eating 3 to 6 appropriately sized prey items a week keeps monthly feeding costs modest, and substrate refresh or misting supplies add just a few dollars on top. There's no ongoing lighting cost to speak of, since this species doesn't need UVB.

## Vet Costs

Exotic vets experienced with invertebrates are uncommon, and most routine issues are managed by keepers directly through husbandry correction rather than a vet visit. When professional care is genuinely needed, a failed molt, a serious injury, or infection, expect exotic vet exam fees in the $80 to $200-plus range, with limited treatment options available for many invertebrate conditions. Prevention through correct humidity and substrate does far more work here than veterinary care ever will, which our [Emperor Scorpion health issues guide](/blog/emperor-scorpion-health-issues-guide/) covers in detail.

Emperor scorpions typically live just 6 to 8 years, which makes them one of the lightest long-term commitments among popular exotic pets, nowhere near the decades a tortoise, parrot, or even a koi can rack up.
</FunFact>

## Lifespan: Shorter Than You Might Expect

6 to 8 years with good care is the most commonly cited range, with some sources noting anywhere from 2 to 8 years depending on conditions. Females tend to live longer than males. This is a genuine multi-year commitment, but nowhere near the decades-long timeline of many reptiles, birds, or even koi, which makes the emperor scorpion one of the lighter long-term commitments among exotic pets.
