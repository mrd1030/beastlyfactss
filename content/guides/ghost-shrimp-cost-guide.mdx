# How Much Does Ghost Shrimp Ownership Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Ghost shrimp are some of the cheapest livestock sold in the aquarium hobby, and the reason isn't about the animal, it's about what most of the supply chain is built for. A large share of every ghost shrimp shipment is raised to be fed to other fish, not kept as a pet, and that split identity is what explains the price tag.

## The Shrimp Themselves: Often Under a Dollar

Bought in bulk as feeder stock, ghost shrimp commonly sell for well under a dollar each. Bought individually as pets from a dedicated aquarium retailer, [The Shrimp Farm](https://www.theshrimpfarm.com/posts/shrimp-caresheet-ghost-shrimp-palaemonetes-sp/) puts the more typical range at $1 to $3 per shrimp. That's a wide spread for the same animal, and it's worth understanding why before you buy: a feeder-tank shrimp and a pet-tank shrimp can be genetically identical and still have had very different lives before they reached the counter.

Part of the reason the price sits so low across the board is genus-wide hardiness rather than any one retailer's discount. [Quality Marine](https://www.qualitymarine.com/news/ghost-shrimp-palaemonetes-sp/) describes ghost shrimp as filling two roles in the trade at once, "a live feed as well as a cleanup crew member," and a shrimp cheap and tough enough to ship in bulk as food for other animals is also, incidentally, a cheap and tough started pet.

<FunFact>
Ghost shrimp are sold on the same store shelf as cherry shrimp and Amano shrimp, but they aren't close relatives of either. Ghost shrimp belong to the family Palaemonidae, while cherry shrimp (genus Neocaridina) and Amano shrimp (genus Caridina) both belong to the separate family Atyidae, confirmed as distinct lineages in different superfamilies by [Wikipedia's taxonomic overview of Atyidae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atyidae). All three ended up in the same aquarium aisle by convergent lifestyle, small and hardy freshwater scavengers, rather than by shared ancestry: each family independently invaded fresh water from marine ancestors at a different point in history.
</FunFact>

## Vet Costs

Not applicable in the traditional sense. There's essentially no practical veterinary care available for a freshwater invertebrate this small, so husbandry and prevention do all the work here.

## Lifespan and Budgeting

Roughly one year is the number most sources converge on for a home aquarium. [The Shrimp Farm](https://www.theshrimpfarm.com/posts/shrimp-caresheet-ghost-shrimp-palaemonetes-sp/) puts it plainly: "on average, ghost shrimp live for one year given ideal water conditions." [FishLab](https://fishlab.com/ghost-shrimp/) agrees, noting they "tend to only live for about a year" on average, with some individuals reaching closer to two years under good, stable conditions. [Animal Diversity Web](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Palaemonetes_paludosus/) reports a similar one-year cycle in the wild, 6 to 13 months.

That's genuinely shorter than an Amano shrimp's typical 2 to 3 years. It's a less clean comparison against cherry shrimp, whose commonly cited 1 to 2 year range overlaps closely enough with ghost shrimp's own that "ghost shrimp always dies first" doesn't hold up consistently across sources, the honest takeaway is that ghost shrimp sits at the shorter end of a range most small dwarf shrimp share, not in a league of its own. Either way, budget for equipment that keeps water stable over that span rather than a multi-year investment.

## Why the Sticker Price Doesn't Tell the Whole Story

A price this low creates an obvious impression: this is a low-investment animal, full stop. That's true of the equipment, but it undersells a real distinction in the animal itself. [Quality Marine](https://www.qualitymarine.com/news/ghost-shrimp-palaemonetes-sp/) is upfront that most ghost shrimp move through the trade as live food, and feeder-stock tanks aren't run with the same standards as tanks meant to hold healthy pets, they're built for volume and turnover, not for minimizing stress or disease on individual animals. Buying from a source that's clearly selling shrimp as pets rather than pulling them out of a crowded feeder tank costs a little more per shrimp and is worth it.

Ghost shrimp are sold right alongside [cherry shrimp](/guides/cherry-shrimp/) and [Amano shrimp](/guides/amano-shrimp/) in most stores despite being a different animal, and they're also a common budget tankmate for [betta fish](/guides/betta-fish/) tanks, worth knowing before you assume "cheap cleanup crew" is the whole story.
