# Degu vs. Chinchilla: Which Long-Lived Rodent Fits Your Schedule

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Both are South American rodents known for living unusually long lives and needing a dust bath instead of water, which makes them easy to lump together. The differences come down to when each one is actually awake, how sensitive one of them is to sugar, and just how much longer the other one lives.

## One Is Awake When You Are

Degus are genuinely unusual among small pet rodents for being diurnal, most active from morning through early evening, so there's a real chance of watching one out and busy during a normal day. Chinchillas run on the opposite schedule, nocturnal to crepuscular, mostly asleep during the day and active once evening comes. If you specifically want a small pet you can watch being active during normal waking hours, that alone tends to settle the decision.

<KeyTakeaway>
Choose a Degu if you want a small pet that's active during the day rather than at night. Just take the sugar restriction seriously, degus have a real genetic intolerance to sugar, and fruit or other sugary treats that are fine for many small pets can push a degu toward diabetes.
</KeyTakeaway>

## Sugar Sensitivity Isn't a Minor Detail for a Degu

This is a sharper distinction than most small-pet diet differences. Degus have a documented genetic intolerance to sugar and are highly prone to diabetes, so fruit, honey, seeds, and other sugary items need to be avoided almost entirely rather than offered as an occasional treat. Chinchillas don't carry that same acute sensitivity, though a hay-heavy, low-sugar diet is still the right baseline for both species.

<FunFact>
Degus are one of the more vocal small pet rodents, with researchers documenting a wide range of distinct calls used for different social situations, enough that degu vocalizations have become a genuine subject of bioacoustics research. Chinchillas communicate too, but with a smaller, quieter vocal range that most owners barely notice day to day.
</FunFact>

## Dust Baths Are the One Real Similarity

Both species groom the same unusual way: by rolling in <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4vgxhss" product="Chinchilla dust (Oxbow Poof)">chinchilla bathing dust</AffiliateLink>, a powdered pumice that soaks up oil and dirt from the coat, never in water. The schedule matches too. Two or three sessions a week, then the tray comes out, since dust bathing too often irritates a chinchilla's eyes and overuse causes problems in degus as well. It's one of the few care routines that carries over unchanged between the two.

## Social Needs Are Both Real, but Not Equal

Degus are colonial animals in the wild, and that social need carries over strongly into captivity: isolated degus are at real, documented risk of loneliness, depression, and self-harming behaviors like overgrooming, which is why pairs sharing a proper <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3T761Pz" product="Small-pet hideout/igloo">hideout</AffiliateLink> are strongly recommended. Chinchillas can also benefit from a companion and often do well paired, but the welfare consequences of keeping one alone are less severe than they are for a degu.

<KeyTakeaway>
A Chinchilla is the better fit if a much longer lifespan and a quieter, lower-stakes solo-housing option matter more to you. In exchange, you'll be working around a nocturnal schedule rather than a daytime-active one.
</KeyTakeaway>

## The Lifespan Gap Is the Biggest Factor

A degu typically lives 5 to 9 years in captivity. A chinchilla commonly lives 10 to 20 years, roughly double, making it one of the longest-lived rodents in the pet trade. That's worth weighing as seriously as activity pattern or diet when deciding between the two.

## Bottom Line

Pick a degu if you want a genuinely daytime-active small pet and can commit to a same-sex pair and a strict low-sugar diet. Pick a chinchilla if a much longer lifespan matters more to you and you're fine working around a nocturnal schedule. Both need dust baths instead of water, that part of the routine is identical either way. The [degu](/encyclopedia/animal/degu/) and [chinchilla](/encyclopedia/animal/chinchilla/) encyclopedia profiles cover the shared Chilean origins behind the dust bath.
