# Pacman Frog Tank Setup: Small Footprint, One Big Rule About Substrate

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Pacman frogs are easy to house correctly, they're sedentary burrowers that don't need much space. The setup mistakes that cause problems for this species are specific and avoidable.

<Figure src="/assets/images/pacman-frog-tank-setup-guide-1.jpg" alt="Labeled diagram of a Pacman frog enclosure showing deep coco fiber substrate, a side-mounted heat mat, and daily misting" caption="This species is a sedentary burrower that doesn't need much space, the setup mistakes that actually cause problems are specific and avoidable." shareable />

## Enclosure Size

A <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B09MQBB6CP?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Reptile Growth Reptile Terrarium, 10 Gallon Tank 20x10x11 for Invertebrates">10 to 20 gallon terrarium</AffiliateLink> works well for an adult, since this species spends the overwhelming majority of its time buried and motionless rather than moving around. A 24x18x18 inch enclosure is an ideal, but floor space matters far more than height here, this isn't a climbing species.

Always house one frog per enclosure. Pacman frogs are cannibalistic, and cohabitation isn't a workable option regardless of enclosure size.

## Temperature

Daytime around 75 to 85°F, with a slightly warmer basking area near 83°F, dropping to 65 to 75°F at night. Provide heat with a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/44hAxJ1" product="Under-tank heat mat + thermostat kit">side-mounted under-tank heat mat on a thermostat</AffiliateLink>, or a low-wattage overhead bulb.

Never place a heat mat under the tank. Pacman frogs burrow to the substrate floor, and a mat positioned underneath risks burning a frog that's buried directly on top of it.

## Humidity

60 to 80% is the target range, maintained through moisture-retentive substrate and daily misting. Let it drop back toward the lower end of that range between mistings rather than keeping the enclosure constantly waterlogged. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3QHrM81" product="Digital thermometer/hygrometer combo">digital hygrometer</AffiliateLink> takes the guesswork out of tracking this.

## Substrate: The Detail That Matters Most

Use 3 to 4 inches of <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CY23K32S?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="VCEPJH Loose Coconut Fiber Substrate">coco fiber or coco husk</AffiliateLink>, kept moist enough for burrowing, with a light layer of sphagnum moss or leaf litter on top if you like.

Avoid gravel, rocks, bark chips, and charcoal entirely. These aren't just uncomfortable, they're genuinely abrasive to a Pacman frog's delicate skin and, more seriously, a real cause of this species' impaction risk, since it swallows whatever's nearby when it strikes at prey. Change the substrate every 2 to 3 months as waste builds up acidity, unless you're running a bioactive setup with a cleanup crew.

## Lighting

No special lighting is required. A standard 12-hour light, 12-hour dark cycle using a basic LED works fine. Low-level UVB is optional and can support vitamin D synthesis, but isn't necessary if the diet is properly supplemented.

## Furnishings

Keep it simple: a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/44hAzR9" product="Small/shallow water dish">shallow, easy-to-clean water dish</AffiliateLink> that won't risk drowning a burrowed frog, and a basic hide or some leaf litter for a sense of security. This species doesn't need, or use, elaborate decor.

The dish and the misting bottle both get tap water treated with a conditioner that neutralizes chlorine and chloramine, which municipal water carries at levels harmless to people and lethal through a frog's permeable skin. A liquid test kit confirms the treatment worked instead of assuming it, the step that [dechlorinating water for amphibians](/blog/amphibian-quarantine-and-water-guide/) hinges on.

## Cleaning Routine

A Pacman frog sits in the same few inches of substrate for days, so waste stays where it lands. Every day, pull out uneaten food and droppings, and empty, clean, and refill the water dish, which the frog often soaks and defecates in. Once a month, clean and disinfect the whole enclosure with an amphibian-safe habitat cleaner or a 3% bleach solution, rinsed off completely and dried before the frog goes back in. The full substrate change every 2 to 3 months sits on top of that.

Even a healthy frog can carry Salmonella, so do the washing in a laundry sink or bathtub, never a kitchen sink, and wash your hands with soap afterward.

The setup looks nothing like a [White's tree frog's](/blog/whites-tree-frog-tank-setup-guide/): a tall, climbing enclosure for an arboreal frog against a shallow tub of soil for one that barely moves.
