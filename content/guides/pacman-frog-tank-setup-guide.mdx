# Pacman Frog Tank Setup: Small Footprint, One Big Rule About Substrate

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Pacman frogs are genuinely easy to house correctly, they're sedentary burrowers that don't need much space. The setup mistakes that actually cause problems for this species are specific and avoidable. For the full care picture beyond the enclosure, see our complete [Pacman Frog care guide](/guides/pacman-frog/).

## Enclosure Size

A <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B09MQBB6CP?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Reptile Growth Reptile Terrarium, 10 Gallon Tank 20x10x11 for Invertebrates">10 to 20 gallon terrarium</AffiliateLink> works well for an adult, since this species spends the overwhelming majority of its time buried and motionless rather than moving around. Some specialist sources recommend a 24x18x18 inch enclosure as an ideal, but floor space matters far more than height here, this isn't a climbing species.

Always house one frog per enclosure. Pacman frogs are cannibalistic, and cohabitation isn't a workable option regardless of enclosure size, covered in more detail in our [Pacman Frog handling guide](/blog/pacman-frog-handling-guide/).

## Temperature

Daytime around 75 to 85°F, with a slightly warmer basking area near 83°F, dropping to 65 to 75°F at night. Provide heat with a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/44hAxJ1" product="Under-tank heat mat + thermostat kit">side-mounted under-tank heat mat on a thermostat</AffiliateLink>, or a low-wattage overhead bulb.

Never place a heat mat under the tank. Pacman frogs burrow to the substrate floor, and a mat positioned underneath risks burning a frog that's buried directly on top of it.

## Humidity

60 to 80% is the target range, maintained through moisture-retentive substrate and daily misting. Let it drop back toward the lower end of that range between mistings rather than keeping the enclosure constantly waterlogged. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3QHrM81" product="Digital thermometer/hygrometer combo">digital hygrometer</AffiliateLink> takes the guesswork out of tracking this.

## Substrate: The Detail That Actually Matters Most

Use 3 to 4 inches of <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CY23K32S?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="VCEPJH Loose Coconut Fiber Substrate">coco fiber or coco husk</AffiliateLink>, kept moist enough for burrowing, with a light layer of sphagnum moss or leaf litter on top if you like.

Avoid gravel, rocks, bark chips, and charcoal entirely. These aren't just uncomfortable, they're genuinely abrasive to a Pacman frog's delicate skin and, more seriously, a real cause of the impaction risk covered in our [Pacman Frog health issues guide](/blog/pacman-frog-health-issues-guide/), since this species swallows whatever's nearby when it strikes at prey. Change the substrate every 2 to 3 months as waste builds up acidity, unless you're running a bioactive setup with a cleanup crew.

<FunFact>
A Pacman frog's bite force has been compared, pound for pound, to some predatory dinosaurs relative to body size. It's a strange thing to picture from an animal that spends nearly all day sitting motionless under a few inches of coco fiber, waiting.
</FunFact>

## Lighting

No special lighting is required. A standard 12-hour light, 12-hour dark cycle using a basic LED works fine. Low-level UVB is optional and can support vitamin D synthesis, but isn't necessary if the diet is properly supplemented, see our [Pacman Frog feeding guide](/blog/pacman-frog-feeding-guide/) for gut-loading and calcium dusting specifics.

## Furnishings

Keep it simple: a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/44hAzR9" product="Small/shallow water dish">shallow, easy-to-clean water dish</AffiliateLink> that won't risk drowning a burrowed frog, and a basic hide or some leaf litter for a sense of security. This species doesn't need, or use, elaborate decor.
