# Is It Legal to Own a Gerbil? The California Ban Explained

Fifty of 52 US jurisdictions place no restriction whatsoever on keeping a gerbil. The two that do are the two where a desert rodent could plausibly live outdoors if it got out, which is the whole logic of both rules and worth stating plainly before the regulatory detail, because the detail is genuinely odd.

## California Restricts the Order, Then Exempts Almost Nothing

California's restricted species regulation works by taxonomic group. Order Rodentia is restricted, and the rule then carves out a short list of exceptions for the rodents people actually keep. Hamsters, guinea pigs, domestic rats and mice make it through. Gerbils do not.

The strange part is that the order's own heading names them. A reader working down the page sees the word gerbil, then finds no matching exception underneath it, and the absence is the answer. It is an easy regulation to misread in exactly one direction, which is why so much internet advice on this is confidently wrong.

What the restriction means in practice is broader than a sales ban. Restricted status under § 671 covers possession, import and sale, so a household moving to California with a gerbil is not in a different position from someone trying to buy one there.

## Hawaii Listed Two Genera

Hawaii took the more conventional route and named the animals. Both Meriones unguiculatus, the Mongolian gerbil that makes up essentially the entire pet trade, and Gerbillus gerbillus sit on Part A of the Restricted Animal List.

Part A is designated for research and exhibition, so the effect is the same one it has for every other species on it. Institutions can hold the animal, private keepers cannot, and captive-bred origin does not create an exception. Hawaii's reasoning is consistent across its whole list: an island ecosystem with no native land mammals treats any established rodent population as a serious problem.

## Everywhere Else, There Is Simply No Rule

This is worth saying clearly because the California ban gets repeated often enough that keepers elsewhere start to wonder. Outside those two jurisdictions, no state checked places a permit, a registration or a condition on gerbils. They sit in the same unregulated category as hamsters and domestic mice across the rest of the country.

Anyone comparing small rodents on legal grounds will find the differences are narrower than expected. The comparison that actually matters for most households is about care rather than law, and the [degu and gerbil overview](/blog/degu-gerbil-overview/) covers where those two diverge.
