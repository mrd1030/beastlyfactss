# Kingsnake Enrichment: Colubrid Evidence, Honestly Labeled

<AffiliateDisclosure />

There is no kingsnake enrichment study. What there is instead is good research on a close relative, which is worth using and worth labeling.

<KeyTakeaway>
The corn snake enclosure-size work is the nearest available evidence, and it is a short borrow: both are North American colubrids of similar size and habit. Given room to stretch out fully, corn snakes did, and preferred the larger enclosure. Assume the same applies here, and know that it is an assumption.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Colubrid Research Shows

Nothing on *Lampropeltis californiae*. The colubrid evidence, covered in full in our [Corn Snake enrichment guide](/blog/corn-snake-enrichment-guide/), is:

**Enclosure size.** Twelve corn snakes were housed in enclosures either two thirds of their body length or longer than their body length. Given the chance to stretch out completely, they did, and they preferred the larger enclosure.

**Enrichment.** A second paper found added complexity affected behavior and welfare measures. A third found enrichment changed how well the snakes discriminated familiar from unfamiliar human odor.

**Ratsnakes.** Almli and Burghardt found enriched-housed ratsnakes showed different behavioral profiles and performed differently on problem solving, feeding and field tasks.

<FunFact>
"Snakes feel secure in small spaces" is the most repeated claim in snake keeping and it has been tested once, in a close relative of this animal, with a preference test. The snakes chose the larger enclosure.
</FunFact>

## Cover Everywhere, Not Two Hides

More space only helps if the snake will cross it. A kingsnake in a large bare enclosure uses two hides and nothing else, which then gets misread as proof it wanted a smaller box.

## Climbing, Despite the Reputation

Kingsnakes climb more than their terrestrial reputation suggests. A firmly braced branch or a stack of cork adds a second usable level. Stability is the requirement rather than height.

## Scent, the Free One

Chemosensory animals get more from novel scent than from novel objects. A shed from another enclosure, a scent-marked piece of cork, or prey dragged across the substrate before offering all invite investigation. The odor-discrimination finding in corn snakes at least suggests the channel is worth using.

## Priority Order

1. An enclosure longer than the snake
2. Cover distributed across the whole floor
3. A stable climbing structure
4. Scent novelty
5. Varied feeding presentation
6. Rearrangement, last

## What Not to Do

Do not house two kingsnakes together, ever. This species eats other snakes, and that is a safety issue rather than a welfare preference. Do not use the small-spaces claim to justify a small enclosure. Do not add space without adding cover. Do not treat handling as enrichment. And do not present corn snake findings as kingsnake findings, which is the labeling this guide exists to demonstrate.

An enclosure longer than the snake is the first purchase, and every item above assumes it.
