# How Much Does Amano Shrimp Ownership Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Amano shrimp cost more than a basic cherry shrimp, and selective breeding has almost nothing to do with why.

## The Shrimp: $5 to $12 Each

[Aquatic Arts](https://aquaticarts.com/products/amano-shrimp) prices standard amano shrimp at $6.39 to $7.99 each individually, dropping to roughly $4.50 apiece when bought as a 10-pack, with a larger-size variant that has sold for $11.99 a shrimp. [A-Z Animals](https://a-z-animals.com/blog/freshwater-shrimp-prices-purchase-cost-colors-more/) prices a group of five at $27.50, close to $5.50 each, a similar picture.

That's a real step up from a lot of other dwarf shrimp, but the comparison to cherry shrimp is more specific than "amano costs more." The same A-Z Animals pricing table lists cherry shrimp at a near-identical $25 for five, but only for the Fire Red color grade. Plain, common-grade cherry shrimp routinely sells for less than that at big chain pet stores, while premium cherry shrimp grades like Painted Fire Red or Bloody Mary can cost as much as or more than an amano shrimp. The honest version isn't "amano costs more than cherry shrimp" full stop, it's that amano shrimp carry a price floor a mass-bred cherry shrimp simply doesn't have, set by collection and shipping rather than selective breeding investment.

<FunFact>
Takashi Amano, the aquascaper this shrimp is named after, wasn't showing off a brand-new discovery when he made it famous. According to a history of his company, Aqua Design Amano, he was already advocating the use of what was then called Caridina japonica against algae in his own tanks as early as 1983, the year after he founded ADA. Decades of that advocacy turned a regional Japanese river shrimp into one of the most requested livestock items in the global aquarium trade, and demand at that scale, resting on a species that still can't be farmed commercially, is a real part of why the price has stayed well above a basic freshwater-bred shrimp ever since.
</FunFact>

A 10-gallon tank is a genuinely practical minimum, more comfortable than the 5-gallon a small group can technically survive in, and it gives shrimp room to forage. The sponge filter isn't optional either: a standard filter intake is a real hazard for this species. Add substrate and a bit of decor and most first setups land around $60 to $150 before the shrimp themselves.

## A Quarantine Tank: About $44 to $75

Nearly every amano shrimp sold is a wild-collected adult, which is the case for holding new arrivals apart for two to four weeks before they join a display tank. The hospital tank does not need to be elaborate: a bare 10-gallon tank, a sponge filter, and a heater, kept free of copper, which is toxic to invertebrates at the doses fish medications use.

## Vet Costs

Not applicable in the traditional sense, the same as most aquarium livestock. When something goes wrong with an amano shrimp, it's almost always a water chemistry problem rather than something a vet visit would treat.

## Lifespan

2 to 3 years is the commonly cited range for amano shrimp in captivity, according to [Shrimp Science](https://www.shrimpscience.com/species/amano-shrimp/), with shrimp kept toward the cooler end of their comfortable temperature range sometimes living longer still. That's a real budgeting variable: a cooler, more stable tank costs a little more to run consistently than a warm one, but it's the cheaper choice across the shrimp's full lifespan once you count how much less often you're restocking.

## Why This Costs More Than Cherry Shrimp

The driver isn't fancy color grading, it's biology. Amano larvae need brackish or marine water to develop, so a home colony essentially never happens by accident, unlike [cherry shrimp](/encyclopedia/animal/cherry-shrimp/), which build a self-sustaining colony in plain freshwater with zero effort from the keeper. Nearly the entire amano shrimp supply chain runs through wild collection instead, and that collection and international shipping cost is what sets the price floor.
