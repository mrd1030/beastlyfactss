# African Grey Parrot Cage Setup: Size, Placement, and Enrichment Done Right

<AffiliateDisclosure />

An African grey's cage needs to support a genuinely large, intelligent, long-lived bird, and getting the size and placement right matters as much as anything else in this species' care. For what all of it costs, see our [cost guide](/blog/african-grey-parrot-cost-guide/).

## Cage Size

36 inches wide by 24 inches deep by 48 inches tall is a commonly cited minimum, with some veterinary guidance recommending closer to 40 by 30 by 60 inches. Whatever size you land on, your bird needs to be able to fully extend and flap its wings without touching the bars in any direction. Bar spacing should be no more than 1 inch.

## Temperature

Standard household temperatures, roughly 65 to 80F, work well. Avoid drafts and cold spots, but no special heating or cooling is needed indoors.

## Placement Matters More Than It Might Seem

Excellent ventilation without direct drafts is important. Remove fresh food promptly rather than letting it sit, this directly ties into the aspergillosis risk covered in our [health issues guide](/blog/african-grey-parrot-health-issues-guide/). Keep the cage well away from the kitchen entirely, fumes from overheated non-stick cookware are genuinely deadly to birds, and keep the bird safely separated from household cats or dogs.

## Substrate and Cage Lining

A paper liner on the cage bottom makes cleaning straightforward. Avoid any hardware with zinc coating, chewing on it is a real heavy-metal poisoning risk.

## Lighting

Full-spectrum or UV lighting supports healthy vitamin D3 and calcium metabolism, directly relevant to preventing hypocalcemia. Maintain 10 to 12 hours of genuine darkness for sleep every night.

<FunFact>
A bored, under-slept, under-stimulated African grey reliably develops plucking and excessive screaming, which is part of why 10 to 12 hours of genuine nightly darkness and daily foraging enrichment are treated as welfare essentials for this species, not accessories.
</FunFact>
