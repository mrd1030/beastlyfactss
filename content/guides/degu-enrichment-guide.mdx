# Degu Enrichment: The Dust Bath Is a Message Board

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Most care sheets file the sand bath under grooming. The research on this species says it is doing something considerably more interesting.

<KeyTakeaway>
Degus dust bathe **at a higher rate on sand a familiar degu has already used**, and dust bathing plays a role in male-to-male communication. In the wild, colony bathing sites are marked with urine and anal gland secretions. The sand tray is a shared scent board and not a shower.
</KeyTakeaway>

For everything else about keeping them, see the [Degu care guide](/guides/degu/).

## What the Research Says

Degus get more research attention than most exotic pets because they are used as a model for diabetes and Alzheimer's disease. Two threads matter to a keeper.

**Dust bathing is communication.** Work on *Octodon degus* found dust bathing is **involved in communication during male-to-male interactions**, though not female-to-female, and that **both sexes dust bathe at a higher rate on soil previously used by a familiar conspecific**. Wild colony dust baths are scent-marked.

**They are colonial and need company.** Colony management guidance for research degus is explicit that housing has to reflect their group-living ecology, and that in the absence of social interaction and physical stimulation animals may become aggressive or self-mutilate. At least two together is the baseline.

<FunFact>
The familiarity effect is the strange part. It is not that degus prefer clean sand or dirty sand, it is that they respond to sand carrying a specific animal's signature. A freshly sterilized tray every time is, from the degu's point of view, wiping the board.
</FunFact>

## Social Housing, Non-Negotiable

The starting point, never an enhancement. Degus are colonial, and a single degu is missing the thing the species is built around.

Two minimum, same-sex or neutered, and ideally littermates or animals introduced young. Introductions of unfamiliar adults need care. Isolation is linked to aggression and self-mutilation in this species, which is a stronger statement than the equivalent for most rodents.

## The Sand Bath, Offered Properly

Given what it is doing, offer it in sessions to the group rather than as a permanent fixture. <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B09PZ1772S?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Supreme Petfoods Science Selective Bathing Sand for Hamsters, Gerbils, Chinchillas and Degus">Bathing sand</AffiliateLink> two or three times a week, twenty minutes or so at a time, is the usual schedule and other sizes are available.

Use sand for degus, not dust. Leave it in permanently and it becomes a toilet, and sift and top it up instead of replacing everything every session, since the research suggests the scent history is part of what makes it worth using.

Our [tank setup guide](/blog/degu-tank-setup-guide/) covers where to site it in a multi-level cage.

## Height and Levels

Active, agile, diurnal climbers. A tall cage with multiple solid levels, ramps, ledges and branches gives them far more usable space than floor area alone.

Solid shelves, not wire, since wire flooring causes foot problems. Fill the height. Buying it and leaving it open wastes it.

## A Wheel, Solid and Large

Degus run, and a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4gmXgL1" product="Solid exercise wheel (chinchilla)">solid-surface exercise wheel</AffiliateLink> gets used heavily. Solid surface and large diameter both matter: an open-rung wheel is a tail and limb injury waiting to happen, and a small one forces an arched spine.

This is one of the few pieces of equipment for this species that reliably earns its space.

## Chewing, Which They Do to Everything

Open-rooted teeth and a serious appetite for destruction. <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0F6V5ZZR6?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="PINVNBY 9 Pcs Lava Blocks for Chinchillas, Hamster Chew Stones">Mineral and lava chew blocks</AffiliateLink>, untreated wood, cardboard and apple or willow branches all get worked through.

Assume anything plastic in the cage will be chewed eventually, and choose accordingly. Our [health issues guide](/blog/degu-health-issues-guide/) covers dental disease, which is common and progressive in this species.

## Foraging Without Sugar

The constraint that makes degus different. They are highly prone to diabetes and cannot handle sugar, so fruit and most commercial treats are out.

Build foraging on safe material instead: scatter their pellets and dried herbs through hay and substrate, hide portions in cardboard, paper parcels and a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DPKP3JBX?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Hamiledyi 3 Pack Guinea Pig Foraging Toys, wooden enrichment puzzle">wooden foraging puzzle</AffiliateLink>, and let them work for the ordinary diet rather than for treats. Our [feeding guide](/blog/degu-feeding-guide/) covers what is safe, and it is a shorter list than for most rodents.

## Priority Order

1. At least two degus, and preferably more
2. A tall cage with solid multi-level structure
3. Sand baths in sessions, shared, not sterilized every time
4. A large solid-surface wheel
5. Constant chewing material
6. Sugar-free scatter and puzzle foraging
7. Rearrangement and novelty, last

## What Not to Do

Do not keep a degu alone. Do not treat the sand bath as purely hygiene, which is the framing the research complicates. Do not leave sand in permanently. Do not use an open-rung wheel or wire shelving. Do not feed fruit, sugary treats or standard rodent mixes to a diabetes-prone species. And see our [handling guide](/blog/degu-handling-guide/) before grabbing one by the tail, since degus can shed the tail sheath and it does not grow back.
