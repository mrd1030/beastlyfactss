# Uromastyx Enrichment: Heat and a Burrow Beat Every Object You Can Buy

Two items on this page matter more than everything else combined, and neither of them is a toy. For everything else about them, see our complete [Uromastyx care guide](/guides/uromastyx/).

<KeyTakeaway>
A uromastyx needs a basking surface hotter than almost anything else kept as a pet, a genuinely cool end to retreat to, and enough substrate to dig a real burrow. Get those three right and the species behaves. Get them wrong and no amount of added decoration compensates.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Research Actually Says

Nothing on *Uromastyx*. Two borrowed threads, both useful.

**Thermal ranked first.** Bashaw and colleagues tested five types of environmental enrichment on leopard geckos and found **thermal and feeding enrichment produced the strongest responses**, while mirror-based visual enrichment was the weakest. Covered in full in our [Leopard Gecko enrichment guide](/blog/leopard-gecko-enrichment-guide/).

**Burrowing substrate was preferred.** Nagabaskaran, Skinner and Miller gave western hognose snakes a choice between an enriched side with burrowing substrate and a standard side, with **shelters and a thermal gradient on both**. The snakes preferred the enriched side and the preference **strengthened over time**. Covered in our [Hognose Snake enrichment guide](/blog/hognose-snake-enrichment-guide/).

A snake and a gecko is an odd pair to build a desert lizard's guide on. What they share with uromastyx is the two behaviours this species is defined by: choosing a temperature, and going underground.

<FunFact>
Uromastyx digest on heat. A basking site that is too cool does not simply make them less active, it leaves plant material sitting in a gut that cannot process it, which is why thermal enrichment for this genus is a digestive question as much as a behavioural one.
</FunFact>

## The Hot End, and a Real Cool End

The single highest-value item. Uromastyx bask on surfaces far hotter than most keepers expect, and the mistake is usually not the lamp, it is the absence of anywhere properly cool at the other end.

A gradient means the animal can pick, across the full range, without giving up cover to do it. A shallow enclosure with one hot zone and a slightly less hot zone is not a gradient. Our [tank setup guide](/blog/uromastyx-tank-setup-guide/) covers the actual temperatures and the UVB requirement, which for a desert basker is high.

## Substrate Deep Enough to Dig In

The second item, and the one most often skipped because it makes the enclosure harder to clean. Uromastyx excavate burrows in the wild and use them for both heat and cold refuge.

A deep bed of <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0002DIZKC?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Zoo Med ReptiSand, 10 Pounds, Desert White">burrowing sand</AffiliateLink> or a sand and soil blend that holds a tunnel shape lets that happen. Other sizes and volumes are available, and a large enclosure needs more than one bag. The depth is the point: substrate an animal can scratch at is not substrate an animal can dig into.

If you would rather keep the main floor easy to inspect, a large dig box gives the same behaviour in a contained area, the same approach we take in our [Bearded Dragon enrichment guide](/blog/bearded-dragon-enrichment-guide/).

## Foraging for a Herbivore

Feeding ranked second in the leopard gecko study, and for a plant eater it is easy to do well. Scatter greens rather than piling them, offer food in more than one place, rotate what appears across the week, and sow a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0H3R2RXFJ?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Tortoise Forage Seed Mix, oat, rye and alfalfa blend">forage seed mix</AffiliateLink> in a tray so some of the diet is living plant the animal crops itself.

Seed and pollen material suits their natural diet and is worth working through rather than pouring out. A <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CHD73YNF?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="CTWPets 100% Bee Pollen Powder (Reptile Food Topper)">bee pollen topper</AffiliateLink> scattered across greens gives them something to pick at, and a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0D82DPB1D?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Repashy SuperVeggie, 6 oz Jar">plant-eater supplement</AffiliateLink> handles the calcium side that a herbivorous reptile under artificial light needs covered deliberately. Our [feeding guide](/blog/uromastyx-feeding-guide/) covers proportions and what to avoid.

## Rock, Height and Line of Sight

Stacked slate and rock give a basking platform, a surface that holds heat, and somewhere to climb onto and survey from. Anchor it, because a uromastyx will dig underneath a rock stack and a collapse onto a burrowing lizard is a serious injury.

Cover at both ends, so the animal never has to choose between being at the right temperature and feeling secure.

## Priority Order

1. A genuinely hot basking surface with a genuinely cool end
2. Strong UVB across the basking zone
3. Substrate deep enough to excavate, or a large dig box
4. Scattered, varied plant foraging
5. Anchored rock and height
6. Cover available at both ends of the gradient
7. Novelty, last, since visual enrichment ranked bottom in the closest study

## What Not to Do

Do not run a hot enclosure with no cool end, which is the most common version of getting the gradient wrong. Do not give shallow substrate to an obligate burrower. Do not build a diet on insects for a herbivore, covered in our [health issues guide](/blog/uromastyx-health-issues-guide/). Do not raise humidity in a desert species, since damp is a far bigger risk here than dry. Do not stack unanchored rock over a digging animal. And see our [handling guide](/blog/uromastyx-handling-guide/) before treating handling as enrichment for a species that mostly wants to be left on its rock.
