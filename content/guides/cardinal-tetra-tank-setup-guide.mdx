# Cardinal Tetra Tank Setup: Soft Water, Warmer Temps, and a Blackwater Feel

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Cardinal tetras are native to the upper Orinoco basin and the Negro River in the Amazon basin, the same kind of soft, dim, tannin-stained blackwater country neon tetra comes from. This species just runs a few degrees warmer and is usually kept in a slightly bigger school.

## Tank Size

<AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4pjiyeT" product="10 gallon tank">A 10-gallon tank</AffiliateLink> is too small for the school of 10 this species needs. The recommended base footprint is roughly 60x30cm, close to 24x12 inches, which lines up with a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eSbKl2" product="20 gallon long tank">20-gallon long tank</AffiliateLink>, and that is the practical minimum for the school of 10 this species is kept in. Schooling behavior plays out horizontally, so favor a longer tank over a taller one.

## Temperature

73 to 84°F is the range for this species, with 77 to 82°F as the everyday target, and cardinal tetra tolerates water reaching 30°C (86°F), warmer than the roughly 25°C (77°F) ceiling neon tetra is comfortable with. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eQY1L5" product="Submersible aquarium heater">heater</AffiliateLink> is necessary for any tank this size, and keeping the temperature stable matters as much as hitting the right number.

In lab tolerance testing, deaths climbed once the water fell below 19°C (66°F), so a failed heater in a cool room is a bigger threat to this fish than a warm summer day.

## Water Chemistry

Cardinal tetras want soft, acidic water, and wild-caught specimens do best toward the more acidic end. Aim for roughly pH 5.0 to 7.0, toward the lower end where possible, and it's worth testing your source water before assuming your setup is automatically suitable. Ammonia and nitrite both need to read zero before stocking.

Hold that chemistry with a [weekly 25 to 30% change](/blog/aquarium-water-changes-guide/), testing nitrate first: under 20 ppm the routine is right, and past 40 ppm the next change is 50%. The new water goes in dechlorinated, at tank temperature and close to the tank's chemistry, which in a soft-water tank means mixing the change water the same way the tank was first filled.

## Filtration

Keep the flow gentle. This species' native habitat is slow-moving forest streams full of fallen branches and leaf litter, not open water with strong current. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3T8xyAd" product="Sponge filter">sponge filter</AffiliateLink> is a common, effective choice, and a hang-on-back filter works too if you can baffle or reduce its output.

## Substrate

Fine gravel or sand, and a darker color makes cardinal tetra's red and blue stripe stand out more against the background, the same principle that applies to the closely related [neon tetra](/blog/neon-tetra-tank-setup-guide/).

## Lighting

Dim to moderate. This species comes from shaded, densely vegetated streams, and bright, intense lighting stresses fish built for filtered, dappled light rather than open water.

## The Non-Obvious Requirement: Recreate Blackwater Conditions

This is the detail that takes a cardinal tetra tank from adequate to great. <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eR0Z2l" product="Driftwood">Driftwood</AffiliateLink> and Indian almond leaves release natural tannins that stain the water and lower pH, mimicking the blackwater habitat this species evolved in, the same fallen branches and leaf litter that define its native streams.

Pair this with dense midground live plants to dim the light further and give the school somewhere to retreat. Always quarantine new fish before adding them, given the disease risk this species carries, and [a bare hospital tank](/blog/fish-quarantine-and-treatment-guide/) is what that means, not a spare corner of the display tank. Keep them well fed on a proper micro-pellet food sized for their small mouths. Always use a water conditioner on tap water before it goes in the tank, dechlorinating and neutralizing chloramine and heavy metals that are otherwise harmful to fish.

The [Cardinal tetra encyclopedia profile](/encyclopedia/animal/cardinal-tetra/) has more on the species itself.
