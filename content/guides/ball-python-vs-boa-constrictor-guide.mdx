# Ball Python vs. Boa Constrictor: Which Snake Actually Fits Your Space

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Both are calm, non-venomous constrictors that get recommended constantly to new snake keepers, and at hatchling size they can look deceptively similar in a display tank. The difference shows up later: one of these snakes stays a manageable size for life, and the other becomes large.

## Adult Size Is the Whole Story

A ball python's growth is famously modest: most adults settle in at 3 to 5 feet and are done growing inside a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4f8WVcx" product="4x2x2 ft PVC enclosure">4x2x2 ft enclosure</AffiliateLink>, the current adult standard, for the rest of their life. A boa constrictor keeps growing well past that point, commonly reaching 6 to 10 feet with some individuals exceeding 12, and an adult weight that can approach 60 pounds, eventually needing an enclosure <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0H3WT574V?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Reptile Habitats 6x2x2 (180 Gallon) PVC Enclosure V3">6 to 8 feet long and 2 to 3 feet wide</AffiliateLink>. Everything downstream of that size gap, enclosure footprint, feeding portions, and the physical strength involved in handling, scales with it.

<KeyTakeaway>
Choose a Ball Python if you want a constrictor that stays a manageable, easy-to-house size for its entire adult life. The tradeoff is feeding reliability, this species is genuinely prone to skipping meals for weeks at a time, especially around sheds or a stressful move.
</KeyTakeaway>

## Eggs vs. Live Birth

This is a clean, genuine biological difference between two snakes that otherwise get compared constantly as similar-looking beginner constrictors. Ball pythons are oviparous: a female lays a clutch of eggs and coils around them to incubate, a behavior that gives the species its name. Boa constrictors skip the egg stage entirely and give birth to live young after a gestation period of roughly three to four months, one of the reasons boas are grouped with a smaller set of live-bearing snake families rather than the largely egg-laying majority.

<FunFact>
A boa constrictor litter isn't small: a female can give birth to a few dozen live young in a single litter, each one already 15 to 20 inches long and fully capable of hunting on its own from day one, no parental care required after birth.
</FunFact>

## Feeding Habits Point in Different Directions

Ball pythons have a well-earned reputation for feeding strikes, sometimes going weeks or months without eating, often around shedding, breeding season, or the stress of a new environment. It's rarely dangerous for a healthy adult, but it catches new owners off guard. Boa constrictors have the opposite reputation: steadier, more consistently enthusiastic eaters, which for many keepers is one of the species' most appealing traits, though no individual snake is entirely immune to an occasional off period.

## Handling Changes as the Snake Grows

A ball python never grows large enough to pose a real handling challenge, an adult is comfortably managed solo for its entire life. A boa constrictor is a different story once it reaches full adult size: an 8-to-10-foot, 50-plus-pound boa is genuinely strong, and many experienced keepers recommend a second person present for handling sessions with a large adult, both for the snake's safety and the handler's.

<KeyTakeaway>
A Boa Constrictor is the better fit if you want a steadier eater and don't mind the extra size. Plan around that size deliberately though, a full-grown adult needs a large enclosure and, for handling sessions, often a second set of hands.
</KeyTakeaway>

## Both Are a Multi-Decade Commitment

A ball python commonly lives 20 to 30 years in captivity. A boa constrictor lives about as long on average, with documented individuals reaching well into their 30s and at least one confirmed case just past 40. Whichever one you choose, this isn't a short-term pet.

## Bottom Line

Pick a ball python if you want a constrictor that stays a manageable size for life and can shrug off the occasional skipped meal. Pick a boa constrictor if a steadier eater matters more to you than final size, and you're ready to house and handle a genuinely large snake once it's grown. Both are long-lived, rewarding constrictors, they just ask for very different amounts of space.
