# Milk Snake Enclosure Setup: Heat Under Cover, Moisture on a Dial

<AffiliateDisclosure />

In the wild a milk snake warms itself from under rocks and logs, seldom out in the open. The enclosure is built to give it the same life under cover: a warm end, a cool end, a damp hide for shedding, and a lid it cannot push open.

<Figure src="/assets/images/milk-snake-tank-setup-guide-1.jpg" alt="Labeled diagram of a milk snake enclosure showing an escape-proof lid, a halogen basking bulb, and a humid hide" caption="The three parts of a milk snake enclosure that matter most: a lid that holds an escape artist, a halogen basking bulb over the warm end, and a humid hide for shedding." shareable />

## Enclosure Size

Most milk snakes stay at or under 48 inches long, which puts the minimum adult enclosure at 48 inches long by 24 inches wide by 24 inches tall. Within that ceiling, a Pueblan reaches 28 to 36 inches, an Eastern 36 to 45, and a Honduran or Sinaloan 40 to 48. A secure, escape-proof lid matters more with this species than some others, because milk snakes are accomplished escape artists. A <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0D1B332MY?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="120 Gallon Wooden Reptile Terrarium, 48 x 24 x 24 in">wood or PVC vivarium</AffiliateLink> is generally a better choice than glass for this species. Both hold heat and humidity more effectively, which matters given how much this species' health depends on getting that balance right.

## Temperature

Basking surface 85 to 90°F, cool side 75 to 80°F, nighttime 70 to 75°F. A <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B000YS3H0I?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Exo Terra Sun Glo Halogen Basking Spot Lamp, 50W">halogen flood bulb</AffiliateLink> positioned over a basking stone is the commonly recommended heat source for this species, generally preferred over colored or red bulbs. A thermostat-controlled heat mat can supplement the warm hide if needed, though it is the supplement and not the main heater. [Where the probe sits](/blog/reptile-heating-thermostats-guide/) decides what that thermostat is holding.

A ceramic heat emitter is the other common heater for this species, and the choice between the two comes down to light. The halogen is the daytime basking source because it gives light and heat together and runs smoothly on a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08R29S2WB?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Exo Terra Dimming and Pulse Proportional Thermostat for Reptile Terrariums">dimming thermostat</AffiliateLink>. A ceramic emitter gives heat with no light, so it is the night heater for a room that falls below 70°F, never the basking lamp.

## Humidity

40 to 60%, monitored with a digital probe hygrometer. Provide a dedicated humid hide lined with moist sphagnum moss, which is what prevents retained shed, and mist as needed to maintain the range.

## Substrate

Aspen bedding and <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4vNgpe1" product="Cypress mulch substrate">cypress mulch</AffiliateLink> both work well and support natural burrowing. Avoid substrate that stays constantly wet, since it is a direct path to scale rot, and skip cedar and pine entirely: both are toxic to reptiles.

## Hides and Cover

A wild milk snake spends the winter tucked into burrows dug by other animals and cracks in rock, and the enclosure copies that life under cover: one hide on the warm end, one on the cool end, the humid hide, and enough [cork bark and clutter](/blog/milk-snake-enrichment-guide/) between them that the snake can cross without being exposed.

## Cleaning

Remove feces and urates daily, scooping out the substrate they touched and replacing it. Scrub the water bowl weekly with a veterinary disinfectant such as F10SC, left on for the [full contact time a reptile-safe disinfectant needs](/blog/reptile-salmonella-hygiene-guide/), and replace all of the substrate every 3 to 4 months, washing the hides and decor at the same time. A bowl the snake has fouled between scrubs gets emptied, rinsed and refilled that day.

## Lighting

UVB is optional. A snake that eats whole prey gets vitamin D3 from its food and does not need UVB for calcium, but appropriate UVB is now widely recommended for every reptile on welfare grounds. A low-output option like <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00M9ONY1W?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Zoo Med 5.0 T5-HO 14&quot; Terrarium Hood with ReptiSun T5 HO 5.0 UVB Lamp">a 5% T5 hood</AffiliateLink> works well, run on a standard 12-hour photoperiod.

## Diet Basics

Milk snakes eat [frozen/thawed rodents](/blog/milk-snake-feeding-guide/): hatchlings every 5 to 7 days, juveniles every 7 to 10, adults every 10 to 14. Size the prey no wider than the snake's head, or roughly 10% of its body weight. Frozen and thawed is both safer for the snake and generally considered the more humane choice than live. Keep a water bowl large enough for the snake to curl up in and soak, topped up with fresh water.

## The Non-Obvious Requirement: Never Cohabitate

Milk snakes are cannibalistic, and this is a firm, no-exceptions rule: one snake per enclosure, always. It is one of the few places this setup diverges from a corn snake's.

## Hold the Moisture Dial

Heat, light and size are set once, and moisture is the part of a milk snake enclosure that needs watching for as long as the snake lives in it. A humid hide that stays damp over a floor that never stays wet keeps the snake clear of both retained shed and scale rot, and the hygrometer is how a keeper knows the balance is holding.
