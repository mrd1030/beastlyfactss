# Milk Snake Tank Setup: Corn Snake Basics, Plus a Few Species-Specific Twists

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Milk snake care closely mirrors the [corn snake's](/blog/corn-snake-tank-setup-guide/), familiar territory if you've kept one before, with a couple of details specific to this species worth getting right. This covers the setup itself, for costs and ongoing supplies, see our [Milk Snake cost guide](/blog/milk-snake-cost-guide/), or the full [Milk Snake care guide](/guides/milk-snake/) for diet and handling.

## Enclosure Size

Most milk snakes stay at or under 48 inches long, which puts the minimum adult enclosure at 48 inches long by 24 inches wide by 24 inches tall. Larger subspecies, Honduran milk snakes especially, can reach 4 to 5 feet and need a correspondingly bigger setup. A secure, genuinely escape-proof lid matters more with this species than some others, milk snakes are accomplished escape artists.

## Temperature

Basking surface 85 to 90°F, cool side 70 to 80°F, nighttime not below about 66°F. A <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B000YS3H0I?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Exo Terra Sun Glo Halogen Basking Spot Lamp, 50W">halogen flood bulb</AffiliateLink> positioned over a basking stone is the commonly recommended heat source for this species, generally preferred over ceramic, colored, or red bulbs. A thermostat-controlled heat mat can supplement the warm hide if needed.

<FunFact>
Not every care sheet agrees on how to heat a milk snake. Some general reptile care sheets suggest a ceramic heat emitter instead, a genuine point of disagreement in the hobby, though halogen basking bulbs positioned over a basking stone remain the more specialist-endorsed choice for this species.
</FunFact>

## Humidity

40 to 60%, monitored with a digital probe hygrometer. Provide a dedicated humid hide with moist <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4bpnvNm" product="Sphagnum moss (for moist hide)">Sphagnum moss for moist hide</AffiliateLink>, this is what actually prevents the retained shed covered in our [Milk Snake health issues guide](/blog/milk-snake-health-issues-guide/), and mist as needed to maintain the range.

## Substrate

<AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4vNgpe1" product="Cypress mulch substrate">Aspen or cypress mulch substrate</AffiliateLink> both work well and support natural burrowing. Avoid substrate that stays constantly wet, this is a direct path to scale rot, and skip cedar and pine entirely, both are toxic to reptiles.

## Lighting

Not strictly required, but low-level UVB offers real benefit according to current husbandry guidance. A low-output option like <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00M9ONY1W?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Zoo Med 5.0 T5-HO 14&quot; Terrarium Hood with ReptiSun T5 HO 5.0 UVB Lamp">Optional low-level UVB (5%)</AffiliateLink> works well, run on a standard 12-hour photoperiod.

## The Non-Obvious Requirement: Never Cohabitate

This belongs in the setup conversation as much as the handling one. Milk snakes are cannibalistic, and this is a firm, no-exceptions rule, one snake per enclosure, always. A <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0D1B332MY?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="120 Gallon Wooden Reptile Terrarium, 48 x 24 x 24 in">wood or PVC vivarium</AffiliateLink> is generally a better choice than glass for this species, both hold heat and humidity more effectively, which matters given how much this species' health depends on getting that balance right.
