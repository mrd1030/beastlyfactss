# Green Cheek Conure Cage Setup: What Your Bird Actually Needs

<AffiliateDisclosure />

A conure's cage needs to support a genuinely active, intelligent bird, not just contain one. This covers the full setup, for costs and ongoing supplies, see our [Green Cheek Conure cost guide](/blog/conure-cost-guide/), or the complete [Green Cheek Conure care guide](/guides/conure/) for everything else.

## Cage Size

24x24x30 inches is the minimum most sources list for a single adult, but treat that as a floor, not a target. Bigger is always better, aim for 30 to 36 inches or more in multiple dimensions, or a genuine flight cage if you can manage it. Your bird should be able to fully stretch and flap its wings without touching the sides.

Bar spacing should be 1/2 to 5/8 inch maximum to prevent head entrapment or escape. Horizontal space for climbing and play matters more than extreme height, a wide <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4wuT808" product="Large bird flight cage">flight-style cage</AffiliateLink> is worth the extra footprint over a tall, narrow one.

## Temperature

Normal household temperatures of 65 to 80°F work well. Avoid drafts, direct air-conditioning vents, and sudden temperature swings. No supplemental heating is needed in a typical indoor environment.

## Humidity

Average household humidity is generally fine. Extremely dry air, common in winter when heating runs constantly, can contribute to dry skin or respiratory irritation, a room humidifier helps if your air runs very dry. This species doesn't have the specialized high-humidity needs some tropical reptiles do, the [Red-Eared Slider tank setup guide](/blog/red-eared-slider-tank-setup-guide/) is a good example of just how different those needs can be by comparison.

## Cage Bottom and Substrate

Most keepers use a grate with paper, newspaper, or a cage liner underneath for easy daily cleaning. Avoid loose substrates like corn cob or wood chips, both can be ingested or harbor mold. Clean droppings and food debris daily, this is a small habit that prevents a lot of downstream health issues covered in our [Green Cheek Conure health issues guide](/blog/conure-health-issues-guide/).

## Lighting

Full-spectrum UV lighting made for birds (UVB 5.0 or a similar avian-specific bulb) is recommended by multiple care sources for 10 to 12 hours daily, supporting vitamin D synthesis, especially important for indoor birds with limited natural sunlight. Maintain a consistent day and night photoperiod, covering the cage at night gives 10 to 12 hours of real darkness.

Supervised outdoor time in an escape-proof carrier or cage is a nice addition when weather allows, never leave a bird outside unattended, and avoid direct midday sun.

<FunFact>
A "Pineapple" green cheek conure gets its name from a genuine genetic combination, the Yellow-Sided and Cinnamon mutations together, producing a mostly yellow-olive body with an orange face instead of the wild type green and red. It's the same species and the same cage setup needs as a normal green conure, the difference is purely cosmetic.
</FunFact>

## Furnishings and Daily Needs

Varied <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4pbY2ge" product="Bird perches (varied diameter)">perches</AffiliateLink>, multiple diameters and textures, natural branches, rope, and a concrete or grooming perch, exercise the feet and help prevent pressure sores. Skip sandpaper-covered perches entirely.

Foraging and destruction opportunities matter just as much: this species needs to chew and work for food, so rotate <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4wMu5Wu" product="Hanging Sola Ball Chew/Forage Toys, 2 Pack (BBjinronjy)">foraging toys</AffiliateLink> and <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B095G51MZG?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="KATUMO Bird Toys, Parakeet Swing Parrot Ladder Cockatiel Perch Conure Hanging Bell Toys for Parakeet Conure Cockatiel Mynah Love Bird Small Birds">general toys</AffiliateLink> frequently, and provide safe wood, cardboard, paper, and foraging puzzles. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eNZiCK" product="Sugar glider bonding/sleeping pouch">snuggle pouch or bird tent</AffiliateLink> gives your conure somewhere secure to burrow, something this species genuinely loves.

Out-of-cage time, several hours of supervised free time daily, isn't optional, it's essential for both physical and mental health. Social needs matter too, strong daily human interaction, or a compatible companion bird, keeps a conure well adjusted, isolation reliably leads to behavioral problems, more on that in our [Green Cheek Conure handling guide](/blog/conure-handling-guide/).

**Safety:** never use non-stick or Teflon cookware near the bird, the fumes are highly toxic. Avoid running ceiling fans during free-flight time, secure windows and doors, and stick to bird-safe plants and household items. For placement, a family living area helps the bird feel like part of the flock, but keep one side of the cage against a wall for security, and stay well away from the kitchen, both the fumes and the heat are risks there.
