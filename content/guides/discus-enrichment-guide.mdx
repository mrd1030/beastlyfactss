# Discus Enrichment: A Nervous Cichlid Needs a Group and a Quiet Room

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Discus have a reputation as difficult fish, and most of the difficulty is water chemistry. The behavioral side gets less attention and it is more tractable.

<KeyTakeaway>
Discus establish a pecking order, and in a group of three or four the lowest-ranked fish takes all of it with nowhere to escape to. **Six or more** spreads the pressure. Then give them **height**, **stable warm water**, and a **tank that is not in a corridor**, because these are genuinely nervous animals.
</KeyTakeaway>

For everything else about them, the [Discus care guide](/guides/discus/) has it.

## What the Research Says

Nothing on discus. The nearest relevant study is Saxby and colleagues' 2010 work on group size in four home aquarium species, covered in full in our [Neon Tetra enrichment guide](/blog/neon-tetra-enrichment-guide/).

That study found the effect of group size on welfare was **species-specific**. Groups of ten improved welfare in neon tetras, and there was **no evidence of a group size effect for angelfish**, the other large cichlid tested, which our [Angelfish enrichment guide](/blog/angelfish-enrichment-guide/) covers.

That is a useful warning against assuming shoal logic applies to a cichlid. The discus case is different again, and the argument is not about shoaling but about **hierarchy**: with too few fish, rank falls entirely on one individual.

<FunFact>
Discus are among the very few fish that feed their fry directly, with the young grazing a mucus secretion off the parents' flanks for the first weeks. It is one of the more remarkable parental behaviors in freshwater fish and it only appears in tanks stable enough for them to spawn at all.
</FunFact>

## Six or More

The number, and the reason is the pecking order. A group of three gives the bottom fish nothing but the bottom.

Six or more spreads the aggression thinly enough that no individual is permanently targeted. In a large tank, more is better still. Our [tank setup guide](/blog/discus-tank-setup-guide/) covers stocking density.

Watch for the singled-out fish. A discus that hangs dark-colored at the surface in a corner is the one to act on, and the answer is usually more fish or more tank rather than removing the aggressor.

## Height

Deep-bodied laterally compressed fish, like angelfish. Vertical space is what runs out first.

A tall tank such as a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08BJB7FX4?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Tetra Glass Aquarium, 55 Gallons">55 gallon</AffiliateLink> is a starting point for a small group, and a proper group of six adults needs considerably more. Buy the height.

## Stable, Warm, Clean Water

The requirement that makes this an advanced fish. Warm, soft, very clean, and above all **stable**.

A large <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4vNGY2v" product="Canister filter (Fluval 407, up to 100 gal)">canister filter</AffiliateLink> and a serious water change routine, plus a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4p1T7yd" product="Water test kit">water test kit</AffiliateLink> used regularly, not after something goes wrong.

Swings are what harms them. A parameter that is slightly off and steady beats one that is perfect on average and moves. Our [health issues guide](/blog/discus-health-issues-guide/) covers the conditions that follow instability.

## Put the Tank Somewhere Quiet

An enrichment decision that costs nothing and is almost never mentioned. Discus are nervous fish, and a tank in a busy corridor with people passing and looming over it produces animals that live at the back.

A low-traffic wall, approached from the side rather than above, and a background on the rear glass. Feed at the same times so the routine becomes predictable. Fish that trust the room use the whole tank.

## Vertical Structure and Cover

Tall plant stems and upright <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eR0Z2l" product="Driftwood">driftwood</AffiliateLink> give them structure to orient to and break sight lines between individuals, which matters given the hierarchy.

Leave open water. Discus cruise instead of darting, and they need somewhere to do it.

## Feeding

Slow, deliberate feeders that lose out to anything faster. That is a large part of why community tanks go wrong for them.

Feed several small meals, never one large one, spread across the tank, and give them time. Vary the diet. Our [feeding guide](/blog/discus-feeding-guide/) covers what a growing discus needs, which is more than most keepers provide.

## Priority Order

1. Six or more fish
2. A tall tank sized for adults
3. Stable, warm, clean water with a serious change routine
4. A quiet, low-traffic tank position with a background
5. Vertical structure that breaks sight lines, with open cruising water
6. Several small feeds spread across the tank
7. Tank mates chosen for temperature and temperament, or none

## What Not to Do

Do not keep three or four and wonder why one is being picked on. Do not buy a long shallow tank for a deep-bodied fish. Do not chase perfect parameters at the cost of stability. Do not put the tank in a corridor. Do not keep them with boisterous or nippy fish, or with anything that eats faster than they do. And see our [handling guide](/blog/discus-handling-guide/) before moving fish that have finally settled.
