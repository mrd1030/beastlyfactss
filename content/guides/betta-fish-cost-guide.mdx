# How Much Does a Betta Fish Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Betta fish have a reputation as a starter pet you can keep in almost nothing, which is exactly the misconception worth correcting. A proper setup costs more than the bowl-and-fish combo suggests, but it's still one of the more affordable pets you can keep once it's built.

## The Fish Itself: %%animal:betta-fish%%

Most bettas run %%animal:betta-fish%%, with rare varieties climbing to about $55. Males are typically priced higher than females, mostly down to their showier fins and color, the traits that make bettas so popular in the first place.

## Picking a Healthy Betta at the Store

The price tag says nothing about the fish's condition, and the cup it waits in says less than it seems to. Bettas are sold in small cups or bottles without filtration or aeration, and they tolerate that better than most fish would. A 2023 BMC Zoology study by Kajimura and colleagues put the 96-hour lethal level of un-ionized ammonia for bettas at 7.7 mg per liter, and the authors suggest that generations of shipping in small bags that hold about 90% air and a little water may have selected for that tolerance. A betta can sit in fouled water for days and still be alive. Whether it is healthy is a question for the fish itself.

What a healthy betta shows:

- Bright, vibrant color.
- Intact fins, held open.
- Normal activity: swimming around the cup, not hiding or lying on the bottom all the time.
- A good appetite, if the store will let you watch it eat.

What to walk away from:

- A fish that is not eating, or that is lethargic or swimming abnormally.
- Frayed fins, or fins that look worse on a second visit.
- New spots, growths, or unusual changes in appearance, including the gold dust of velvet or the salt-grain spots of ich.
- Fins pressed flat against the body, one of the [body signs that mark a sick fish from outside the tank](/blog/spotting-a-sick-fish-guide/).

A sick $5 fish is the most expensive betta in the store once the medication and a hospital container are added to the bill. Paying a few dollars more for a lively fish in cleaner water is the cheapest insurance in betta keeping.

That works out to roughly $120 to $300 a year, or $10 to $25 a month. Food alone is inexpensive, often just $30 to $50 for the entire year, and the bigger recurring costs are conditioner, replacement filter media, test reagent refills, and the electricity draw from running a heater continuously, which lands somewhere between $30 and $100 a year depending on your climate and how cold the room gets. A year that needs a course of medication runs higher than the top of that range. Keeping water parameters stable also cuts down on health problems that would otherwise add cost.

## Vet Care Is Rare, But It Exists

Aquatic veterinarians do exist for serious cases, but few betta owners ever need one. Most betta health issues are managed through water quality correction, which is part of what keeps this pet's lifetime cost relatively low.

## The Lesson on Cost

The cheap bowl-and-fish setup bettas are often sold with isn't a complete, humane setup, it's missing a heater and proper filtration, both of which matter for a genuinely tropical fish. Spending the extra money upfront on a real 5-gallon-plus tank with a heater is the difference between a fish that struggles and one that thrives, and it doesn't meaningfully change the long-term cost picture either way.

Against the goldfish, the other usual first fish, a betta starts at [5 heated gallons where a goldfish starts at 20 unheated](/blog/betta-fish-vs-goldfish-guide/): a smaller tank, traded for a heater that runs all year. And beside the guinea pig, cockatiel and tarantula, the betta's [one rule worth knowing first](/blog/guinea-pig-cockatiel-betta-tarantula-overview/) is the same one this budget is built on: it needs a real tank.
