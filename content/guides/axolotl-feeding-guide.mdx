# Axolotl Feeding: Schedule, Diet, and the Substrate Mistake That Matters Most

Axolotl feeding is straightforward once you understand one central fact about this species: they gulp their food whole, which shapes nearly every rule on this page, including the most important one about what's on the tank floor. For the rest of their care needs, see our complete [Axolotl care guide](/guides/axolotl/).

## The Staple Diet

Axolotls are carnivores that eat a variety of invertebrate prey, no plant matter is needed in this species' diet at all. The gold-standard staple is earthworms or nightcrawlers, high in protein, well-balanced, and easy for an axolotl to digest, paired with a quality <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4tR5M7U" product="Soft Sinking Pellets for Axolotls">sinking pellet formulated specifically for axolotls</AffiliateLink> or carnivorous amphibians. Frozen bloodworms, brine shrimp, and daphnia work well for hatchlings and juveniles or as occasional variety, but shouldn't be the entire diet on their own.

## Feeding Frequency and Portions

Size each meal to roughly your axolotl's head, and remove anything uneaten after 15 to 30 minutes to protect water quality. Frequency slows down considerably as your axolotl grows:

- **Hatchlings (0 to 3 months):** live food 2 to 3 times daily.
- **Juveniles (3 to 6 months):** about once a day.
- **Sub-adults (6 to 12 months):** every 1 to 3 days.
- **Adults (12-plus months, over about 7.5 inches):** every 2 to 3 days. Adults have slower metabolisms and genuinely need that extra time to digest food properly in cool water, more on keeping that water at the right temperature in our [tank setup guide](/blog/axolotl-tank-setup-guide/).

## Foods to Avoid

Skip feeder fish, they carry real parasite risk, and many common feeder species contain an enzyme that causes a vitamin B deficiency over time. Avoid mammalian meat like beef heart or chicken, axolotls can't properly digest mammalian fat and protein. Hard-shelled feeders, adult mealworms, crickets, and crayfish, are genuinely risky too, their exoskeletons are difficult to digest and can cause internal injury.

<KeyTakeaway>
This is the detail that matters most: never use gravel or small rocks as substrate. Because axolotls gulp their food rather than carefully selecting it, they readily swallow whatever's nearby, and swallowed gravel causes intestinal blockage that can require surgery and can be fatal. Fine sand under about 1mm, or a bare-bottom tank, is the safe choice.
</KeyTakeaway>

## Supplementation

None generally needed. A varied diet of worms and formulated pellets covers this species' nutritional needs without additional vitamins or minerals.

## The Mistakes That Actually Matter

Gravel substrate is genuinely the most consequential mistake on this list, it's directly responsible for the impaction that sends more pet axolotls to emergency surgery than almost anything else, and it's entirely avoidable with the right substrate choice.

<FunFact>
The second common mistake is continuing juvenile feeding frequency into adulthood, an adult fed daily the way a juvenile would be becomes obese and stresses water quality. And feeding the wrong food, feeder fish or mammalian meat instead of worms and proper pellets, causes real digestive and nutritional problems even without an acute impaction event.
</FunFact>
