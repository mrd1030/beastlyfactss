# How Much Does a Pet Rat Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Rats are cheap to buy, but because they must be kept in pairs or small groups, the real budget starts once you multiply that by two and add a genuinely large multi-level cage. For the full care picture beyond budgeting, see our complete [Rat care guide](/guides/rat/), or our companion guides on [feeding](/blog/rat-feeding-guide/), [handling](/blog/rat-handling-guide/), [health issues](/blog/rat-health-issues-guide/), and [enclosure setup](/blog/rat-tank-setup-guide/).

## The Rat(s): $10 to $20 Each, Budgeted as a Pair

A single pet rat typically runs $10 to $20 at a pet store, with rescue adoption often landing at the low end of that or less. The number that actually matters, though, is the multiple: rats are highly social, and RSPCA guidance is explicit that pet rats should never be kept alone, recommending a pair or trio of bonded rats in a same-sex group instead. That means the realistic starting price for this species is $20 to $40 for two animals, not $10 to $20 for one, before a single piece of housing is bought.

The cage is the number that swings this whole budget. PDSA's guidance for rat housing is blunt about it: large cages built for ferrets or chinchillas are usually the right size, and a basic multi-level starter cage like the <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B001W8MDKO?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Prevue Pet Products Rolling Small Animal Cage, Model 495">Prevue rolling cage</AffiliateLink> runs around $200, while a genuinely spacious option like the Critter Nation double unit, which is literally the same MidWest cage line sold under different branding for ferrets, runs $280 to $360. More on why size and cage type matter this much in our [Rat enclosure setup guide](/blog/rat-tank-setup-guide/). Add a wheel, hammocks, hideouts, bedding, dishes, and chews, and most first-time pair setups land around $250 to $550 total, a wide range driven almost entirely by which cage you choose.

Food and bedding make up most of the monthly total. Bedding is the bigger of the two recurring costs since a rat cage needs cleaning at least twice a week, not because rats are messier than other small pets, but because ammonia buildup from infrequent cleaning is one of the biggest drivers of the chronic respiratory disease covered in our [Rat health issues guide](/blog/rat-health-issues-guide/). See our [feeding guide](/blog/rat-feeding-guide/) for exactly what that pellet and produce budget should look like.

## Vet Costs

Budget $60 to $110 for a routine exotic-vet wellness exam, and expect that figure to roughly double once you're accounting for a full pair rather than a single rat. Emergency and surgical costs run higher: mammary tumor removal is one of the more common surgical costs in this species, and because rats are small, health problems can progress quickly, so prompt vet visits matter more than they might for a larger animal. Our [Rat health issues guide](/blog/rat-health-issues-guide/) covers the specific conditions worth budgeting for.

## Lifespan

Merck Veterinary Manual puts the average pet rat lifespan at 18 to 36 months. Most care organizations, including PDSA, describe it more simply as 2 to 3 years, with some individuals reaching 4.

<FunFact>
That lifespan window isn't just genetics, it's disease-driven in a very specific way. Chronic respiratory disease from the bacterium Mycoplasma pulmonis is so widespread in pet rats that one survey of pet ratteries in the northwestern US found virtually all of them, 95 percent, positive for it, and Merck notes that rats with the chronic form of the disease rarely live longer than 2 years. A clean, well-ventilated cage genuinely changes the odds here, more in our [Rat enclosure setup guide](/blog/rat-tank-setup-guide/).
</FunFact>

A short lifespan cuts both ways financially: less time to spread that big cage purchase across, but also a real, bounded commitment rather than an open-ended one. If you're weighing a rat against a smaller, less space-hungry rodent, our [Mouse encyclopedia profile](/encyclopedia/animal/mouse/) covers a species with a lower buy-in and a smaller footprint, though mice bring their own odor and handling tradeoffs.

## The Honest Budgeting Takeaway

Here's the pattern worth remembering: the rats themselves are genuinely inexpensive, but this is a species where the housing cost dwarfs the animal cost more than almost anywhere else on this site, because "properly housed" for a social, intelligent rodent means a large multi-level cage, not a small bin. Skipping that step to save money is the single most common way rat setups go wrong.
