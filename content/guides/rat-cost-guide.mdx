# How Much Does a Pet Rat Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Rats are cheap to buy, but because they must be kept in pairs or small groups, the budget starts once you multiply that by two and add a large multi-level cage.

## The Rat(s): %%animal:rat%% Each, Budgeted as a Pair

A single pet rat typically runs %%animal:rat%% at a pet store, with rescue adoption often landing at the low end of that or less. The number that actually matters, though, is the multiple: rats are highly social and should never be kept alone: a pair or trio of bonded rats in a same-sex group is the standard. That means the realistic starting price for this species is $20 to $40 for two animals, not %%animal:rat%% for one, before a single piece of housing is bought.

Food and bedding make up most of the monthly total. Bedding is the bigger of the two recurring costs since a rat cage needs cleaning at least twice a week, not because rats are messier than other small pets, but because ammonia buildup from infrequent cleaning is one of the biggest drivers of chronic respiratory disease.

## Vet Costs

Budget $60 to $110 for a routine exotic-vet wellness exam, and expect that figure to roughly double once you're accounting for a full pair rather than a single rat. Emergency and surgical costs run higher: mammary tumor removal is one of the more common surgical costs in this species, and [most of those lumps turn out to be benign fibroadenomas that surgery can remove when they are caught early](/blog/rat-health-issues-guide/). Because rats are small, health problems can progress quickly, so prompt vet visits matter more than they might for a larger animal.

Two surgeries are worth pricing before they come up. Neutering a male typically costs $50 to $100, and it is the fee that makes a mixed-sex pair possible, since unneutered opposite-sex rats can produce a new litter every 3 to 5 weeks. Spaying a female is the tumor decision: mammary tumors show up far less often in spayed rats, and a spay done before about 7 months of age, before tumors start forming, lowers the chance of them later in life. Spaying at the same time as a tumor removal has not been shown to stop new ones. That matters for the budget, because fibroadenomas often come back in other mammary tissue, which runs from the chin to the base of the tail, and a rat may need several removals over its life. Tumor removal fees are set clinic by clinic, so ask for a written estimate that includes anesthesia, pain relief and the recheck.

## Lifespan

The average pet rat lives 18 to 36 months, 2 to 3 years in round numbers, with some individuals reaching 4.

<FunFact>
That lifespan window isn't just genetics, it's disease-driven in a very specific way. Chronic respiratory disease from the bacterium Mycoplasma pulmonis is so widespread in pet rats that one survey of pet ratteries in the northwestern US found virtually all of them, 95 percent, positive for it, and rats with the chronic form of the disease rarely live longer than 2 years. A clean, well-ventilated cage genuinely changes the odds here.
</FunFact>

A short lifespan cuts both ways financially: less time to spread that big cage purchase across, but also a real, bounded commitment rather than an open-ended one.

## The Honest Budgeting Takeaway

Here's the pattern worth remembering: the rats themselves are genuinely inexpensive, but this is a species where the housing cost dwarfs the animal cost more than with almost any other pet, because "properly housed" for a social, intelligent rodent means a large multi-level cage, not a small bin. Skipping that step to save money is the single most common way rat setups go wrong.
