# How Much Does a Pet Rat Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Rats are cheap to buy, but because they must be kept in pairs or small groups, the budget starts once you multiply that by two and add a large multi-level cage.

## The rats: $10 to $20 each, budgeted as a pair

A single pet rat typically runs $10 to $20 at a pet store, with rescue adoption often landing at the low end of that or less. The number that actually matters is the multiple. Rats are highly social and should never be kept alone. A pair or trio of bonded rats in a same-sex group is the starting point. That means $20 to $40 for two animals, not $10 to $20 for one, before a single piece of housing is bought.

Food, produce, bedding, and chews add to $165 to $285 a year, which is $14 to $24 a month. Add the wellness exam and the year is $225 to $395, or $19 to $33 a month. Bedding is the bigger recurring cost. A rat cage needs cleaning at least twice a week. Ammonia from infrequent cleaning is one of the biggest drivers of chronic respiratory disease.

The [rat health issues guide](/blog/rat-health-issues-guide/) is the page that explains why that cleaning schedule is not optional.

## Vet costs

Budget $60 to $110 for a routine exotic-vet wellness exam. For a pair, plan on that figure roughly doubling if both animals are seen. Emergency and surgical costs run higher. Mammary tumor removal is one of the more common surgeries in this species, and most of those lumps are benign fibroadenomas that surgery can remove when they are caught early. Because rats are small, health problems can progress quickly.

## Lifespan

Average pet rat lifespan is 18 to 36 months, commonly described as 2 to 3 years, with some individuals reaching 4.

<FunFact>
Chronic respiratory disease from the bacterium Mycoplasma pulmonis is so widespread in pet rats that one survey of pet ratteries in the northwestern US found 95 percent of them positive for it. Rats with the chronic form of the disease rarely live longer than 2 years. A clean, well-ventilated cage changes the odds.
</FunFact>

A short lifespan cuts both ways: less time to spread that cage purchase across, but a bounded commitment rather than an open-ended one. If you are weighing a rat against a smaller rodent, the [mouse encyclopedia](/encyclopedia/animal/mouse/) covers a species with a lower buy-in and a smaller footprint, with its own odor and handling tradeoffs.

## The honest budgeting takeaway

The rats themselves are inexpensive. Housing dwarfs the animal cost, because a properly housed pair means a large multi-level wire cage, not a small bin. Skipping that step to save money is the most common way rat setups go wrong.
