# Amano Shrimp Enrichment: The Algae Runs Out and Nobody Notices

Amano shrimp are the only animal on this site routinely bought as a maintenance tool. That framing is the source of most of what goes wrong. For everything else about them, see our complete [Amano Shrimp care guide](/guides/amano-shrimp/).

<KeyTakeaway>
An amano shrimp is bought to eat algae, does it well, and then lives in a tank with no algae in it. The single most common welfare failure in this species is slow starvation in a spotless aquarium, and it happens because the animal was classified as equipment rather than as something that eats.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Research Actually Says

Nothing on *Caridina multidentata*. The relevant evidence is the decapod sentience review, covered in full in our [Cherry Shrimp enrichment guide](/blog/cherry-shrimp-enrichment-guide/).

Birch and colleagues applied an **eight criterion framework** to **over 300 scientific publications** and concluded **decapod crustaceans are probably sentient**, after which UK law was extended to cover them. The report was explicit that the evidence was stronger for some groups than others, and that gaps remain for shrimp specifically.

Whatever weight you give that, it argues against treating a shrimp as a cleaning appliance, which is the practical point for this species.

<FunFact>
Amano shrimp are named after Takashi Amano, the aquascaper who popularised them for exactly this job. The name has stuck so firmly that the animal is now more widely known by its function than by anything else about it.
</FunFact>

## Feed Them, Deliberately

The item this species most needs and most often does not get. Once the algae is cleared, the shrimp are living on whatever they can scavenge, and in a well-maintained tank that is not much.

<AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00025JZAY?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Hikari Tropical Sinking Wafers for Catfish, Loaches and Bottom Feeders, 3.88 oz">Sinking wafers</AffiliateLink> are the practical answer, and other sizes are available. Blanched vegetables and shrimp foods work too. Drop food in more than one spot, because amanos are assertive and a single wafer becomes one shrimp's wafer.

Watch them eat. An amano that grabs food and runs off with it is a hungry amano, and that behaviour is so characteristic that it gets treated as charming rather than as information. Our [feeding guide](/blog/amano-shrimp-feeding-guide/) covers amounts.

## Surface Area to Work Over

The same principle as any grazing shrimp. <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eR0Z2l" product="Driftwood">Driftwood</AffiliateLink> grows biofilm and gives them structure to climb over, and dense planting, moss and leaf litter add grazeable surface.

Amanos are larger and more active than cherry shrimp and use more of the tank. A complex, planted layout gets far more visible behaviour out of them than an open one. Our [tank setup guide](/blog/amano-shrimp-tank-setup-guide/) covers dimensions and planting.

## Water Stability, and No Copper

Copper is lethal to shrimp and arrives in fish medications, some fertilisers and some tap water. Check everything before it goes in.

Beyond that, stability beats precision. Small regular water changes rather than large occasional ones, and no sudden swings in temperature or hardness. Our [health issues guide](/blog/amano-shrimp-health-issues-guide/) covers what a shrimp in trouble looks like.

## Moulting Cover and a Safe Filter

Freshly moulted shrimp are vulnerable and want somewhere to hide until the new shell hardens. Moss, wood crevices and leaf litter provide it.

Use a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3T8xyAd" product="Sponge filter">sponge filter</AffiliateLink> or guard the intake of whatever you run. Leave discarded shells in the tank, since shrimp eat them and recover the minerals.

## Expect No Population

Worth planning around. Amano larvae need brackish water to develop, so a female will carry eggs and the larvae will die in freshwater.

The population does not replace itself. Unlike cherry shrimp, whatever you buy is what you have, which makes each animal's individual survival worth more attention than it usually gets.

## Priority Order

1. Actual feeding, on a schedule, once the algae is gone
2. Food distributed across several spots
3. Grazing surface: wood, moss, planting and leaf litter
4. Stable water and nothing containing copper
5. Cover for freshly moulted shrimp, and old shells left in
6. A guarded filter intake
7. Accepting they will not breed

## What Not to Do

Do not treat an amano as a maintenance appliance that does not need feeding. Do not add copper in any form. Do not assume smaller shrimp are getting food when amanos are in the tank. Do not run an unguarded filter intake. Do not remove discarded shells. Do not make large sudden water changes. And see our [handling guide](/blog/amano-shrimp-handling-guide/) before moving them, since transfer shock is a common cause of loss.
