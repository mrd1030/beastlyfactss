# Corydoras Enrichment: Sand for the Sifting, Not for the Barbels

<AffiliateDisclosure />

The most repeated piece of corydoras advice is right about the conclusion and probably wrong about the reason. For everything else about them, see our complete [Corydoras Catfish care guide](/guides/corydoras-catfish/).

<KeyTakeaway>
Barbel erosion tracks **water quality and bacterial infection in the substrate** more closely than it tracks how sharp the gravel is. Sand is still the better substrate, because corydoras **sift it through their gills** and fine grains are what let them. The right answer, for a different reason than everyone gives.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Research Actually Says

There is no controlled corydoras enrichment study, and there is a fairly clear picture from aquarium literature and veterinary accounts of what barbel erosion actually is.

**It is largely bacterial.** Barbel erosion is associated with **poor water quality and bacterial infection in the substrate** rather than with mechanical wear. A dirty substrate erodes barbels whatever it is made of, and a clean one mostly does not.

**Sharp substrate is a secondary factor.** Not ideal, and not the primary cause. Long-term keepers have raised healthy corydoras on smooth gravel for decades, which is difficult to square with the abrasion story.

**Sand still wins on behaviour.** Corydoras feed by taking substrate into the mouth and passing it out through the gills, sifting food from it. Fine sand is the grain size that permits it. Gravel does not, and a corydoras on gravel is a corydoras that cannot do its characteristic behaviour.

<FunFact>
The abrasion explanation persisted because it is intuitive and because the recommendation it produces happens to be correct. Sand is the right answer. It is just worth knowing that a cory on spotless gravel is at less risk than a cory on filthy sand.
</FunFact>

## Fine Sand

The enrichment item, and the reason is the sifting. A bed of <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DQQZ69J6?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="CaribSea Super Naturals Aquascape Sunset Gold, 10 lb, natural substrate for freshwater aquariums">fine aquarium sand</AffiliateLink> lets the whole group forage the way they are built to, and other colours and sizes are available.

Watch what happens when you switch. A group of corydoras on sand buries its snouts and works the bottom continuously. The same group on gravel picks at the surface. That difference is the enrichment.

Keep it clean. Vacuum the surface layer regularly, keep nitrate low, and never let uneaten food rot into it. Our [tank setup guide](/blog/corydoras-catfish-tank-setup-guide/) covers substrate depth and maintenance.

## A Group of Six or More

Social bottom-dwellers that shoal, forage and rest in contact with each other. Two or three corydoras is a nervous, inactive version of the same fish.

Six minimum, more if the footprint allows, and one species of eight beats two species of four. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eSbKl2" product="20 gallon long tank">long tank</AffiliateLink> is the right shape, because for a bottom-dweller the floor area is the tank.

The same principle shows up in the group size study our [Neon Tetra enrichment guide](/blog/neon-tetra-enrichment-guide/) covers, where larger single-species groups meant lower aggression and faster feeding.

## Feed Them Deliberately

The myth that quietly starves them. Corydoras are not a cleanup crew and will not thrive on whatever falls past the other fish.

If faster tankmates take everything, feed after lights out. Add frozen bloodworm and similar, which they hunt through the sand for, and that is feeding and enrichment at once. Our [feeding guide](/blog/corydoras-catfish-feeding-guide/) covers it.

## Cover and Shade

Nocturnal-leaning fish that spend the day under things. Wood, broad-leaved plants and caves give somewhere to rest out of the light, and a group will often pile into one spot together.

Leave open sand as well. They need both foraging ground and somewhere to retreat to.

## Let Them Reach the Surface

Corydoras gulp air and absorb oxygen through the gut, so an unobstructed route to the surface is not optional. Floating plants should not cover the whole surface.

Occasional dashes up are normal. Constant ones mean the water is short of oxygen or fouled, and that is the point at which to test rather than to watch. Our [health issues guide](/blog/corydoras-catfish-health-issues-guide/) covers the distinction.

## Priority Order

1. Fine sand, kept clean
2. A group of six or more of one species
3. Floor area over height
4. Sinking food fed for them, after lights out if needed
5. Wood, plants and caves for daytime shade
6. Clear access to the surface
7. Frozen and live foods to hunt through the sand

## What Not to Do

Do not treat corydoras as a cleanup crew. Do not keep two or three. Do not let the substrate get dirty, which is the actual barbel risk. Do not cover the entire surface with floating plants. Do not keep them in a tall tank with a small footprint. And see our [handling guide](/blog/corydoras-catfish-handling-guide/) before netting one, since the pectoral spines lock and tangle in mesh.
