# Jumping Spider Enrichment: They Can Recognize Individuals

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Almost nothing kept as an invertebrate has cognition research behind it. The jumping spider has a whole literature, and it is stranger than anything a care sheet will tell you. For everything else about them, see our complete [Jumping Spider care guide](/guides/jumping-spider/).

<KeyTakeaway>
A 2024 paper in *eLife* documents individual recognition in *Phidippus regius*. The same species has published work on visual discrimination learning, and another salticid has demonstrated place avoidance learning and memory. Enrichment for these animals is a visual question, not a decorating one.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Research Actually Says

Salticids are, in the words of a 2020 review in *Learning and Behavior*, an exceptional group for comparative cognition studies. They provide excellent models for visually mediated behaviors including associative learning and navigation of complex environments, and produce what the literature describes as a wide spectrum of intelligent behavior with evidence of flexible learning and recognition memory.

On *Phidippus regius* specifically there is work on **visual discrimination learning**, a **replication study on shape discrimination** in a conditioning procedure, and the 2024 **individual recognition** paper. In another salticid, researchers used a conditioned passive place avoidance paradigm and found spiders learned to associate a stimulus with visual background cues, with the extent of learning depending on which visual stimulus was present and the interval between trials.

<FunFact>
Comparisons between lab-reared and field-collected <em>Phidippus audax</em> found differences in colour preferences, aggression, courtship and exploratory behavior. Captivity changes these animals measurably, which is a more honest thing to tell a keeper than pretending it does not.
</FunFact>

## Visual Complexity, Because That Is Their Channel

A jumping spider navigates by sight in a way almost no other invertebrate does. A bare acrylic cube gives those eyes nothing.

Vary the surfaces, depths and textures inside the enclosure. <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0H1SKZRT5?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Cork bark mini flats for jumping spiders">Cork bark mini flats</AffiliateLink>, small branches, and artificial or live foliage create the layered, three-dimensional space a salticid actually reads. Height matters, since they build silk retreats in elevated corners.

## Live Prey, Pursued

Jumping spiders stalk and pounce. They do not build capture webs, so the hunt is the behavior, and prey that has to be located and chased is the closest thing to a natural outlet available.

Vary the prey type and where it is released. Our [feeding guide](/blog/jumping-spider-feeding-guide/) covers prey sizing and frequency.

## Climbing and Retreat Space

The literature describes behavioral enrichment for salticids in terms of climbing and nesting structures such as natural wood branches. They need somewhere high and enclosed to build a silk retreat, and they will pick a top corner if you give them nothing better.

Vertical space with real structure in it is the whole enclosure design brief for this animal. Our [tank setup guide](/blog/jumping-spider-tank-setup-guide/) covers dimensions and ventilation.

## Interaction, With Care

Unusually for an invertebrate, interaction with human caretakers during handling and maintenance is mentioned in the salticid literature as a form of behavioral enrichment. These spiders track movement and will watch you.

Keep it brief, low to a soft surface, and never over a hard floor. A fall can kill a jumping spider outright, which our [handling guide](/blog/jumping-spider-handling-guide/) covers.

## Priority Order

1. Vertical space with layered climbing structure
2. Live prey that has to be located and pursued
3. Visual and textural complexity across the enclosure
4. A high, enclosed spot suitable for a silk retreat
5. Brief low interaction, over something soft
6. Rearrangement, occasionally

## What Not to Do

Do not keep a jumping spider in a bare cube. Do not handle over a hard surface or at height, since a fall is often fatal. Do not house two together, as they are not social and will treat each other as prey. Do not disturb a spider that has sealed itself into a retreat, because it is likely moulting and interrupting that is dangerous, as our [health issues guide](/blog/jumping-spider-health-issues-guide/) covers. And do not offer prey larger than the spider's body.
