# How Much Does a Quaker Parakeet Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Quaker parakeets sit in the middle of the pet-bird price range, and one detail matters more here than with almost any other bird on this site before you buy at all: whether it's even legal where you live.

For the full care picture beyond budgeting, see our complete [Quaker Parakeet care guide](/guides/quaker-parakeet/).

## The bird: $250 to $500

A well-socialized Quaker from a reputable breeder typically runs in this range, with color mutations, age, and sex affecting the exact price.

## Upfront setup: roughly $300 to $800

- **A quality <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4ymrwMJ" product="Prevue Hendryx Wrought Iron Flight Cage (5 ft, rolling)">flight cage</AffiliateLink>**, often $150 to $300 or more on its own.
- **An initial vet visit.**
- **Toys, perches, and dishes.**

The cage is the line to shop carefully. The Prevue flight cage that fits a Quaker, 31 by 20 by 53 inches with half inch bar spacing, lists at $286 and sells for $190 when it is on sale, and that gap is most of the difference between the two ends of the total. Perches are $10 to $25 each in manzanita, cups are $5.50 to $7.50, and a pound of Harrison's pellets is $16. Toys start at $7 and top out near $15, and this is the one row that never stops: whatever you buy on day one gets shredded. The first vet visit is on this list on purpose, because a new bird needs a baseline exam and a new client exam costs more than a repeat one.

## Ongoing costs: roughly $40 to $110 a month

<AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4wGLzDI" product="Harrison's High Potency Fine Bird Food, Organic (1 lb)">Pellets</AffiliateLink>, fresh produce, cage liners, and a steady rotation of <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4wMu5Wu" product="Hanging Sola Ball Chew/Forage Toys, 2 Pack (BBjinronjy)">toys</AffiliateLink> make up the monthly total, and that last item deserves emphasis, Quakers are natural nest-builders and shred and destroy toys quickly, budget for genuinely frequent replacement, not an occasional purchase.

## Vet costs

An annual avian exam runs $85 and up, plus periodic nail and wing grooming. Avian vets are specialized, and given how long this species lives, covered below, you're looking at decades of these costs, not a handful of years.

A routine year with one wellness exam and a gram stain is roughly $170 to $205, and bloodwork adds $158 in the years it is due. Grooming is $35 a service if you do not learn to do it yourself. The emergency line is the one to hold in reserve: an after-hours exam at the Denver practice is $200 plus a $120 emergency fee before any treatment starts.

## Lifespan: 20 to 30 years, sometimes longer

This is a long-lived bird, on par with the largest parrots already covered on this site despite being considerably smaller and more affordable to acquire. Treat this as the real headline number when deciding whether this species fits your life, not the purchase price.

<FunFact>
The Quaker is the only parrot species that builds its own nest rather than nesting in a tree cavity, and in the wild these twig-woven nests can become massive multi-chambered structures shared by many pairs. A pet Quaker keeps that instinct fully intact, which is exactly why its toy budget runs higher than a similarly-sized parrot that isn't compelled to shred and weave everything in reach.
</FunFact>

## Before any of this: check your state's laws

This is worth stating as its own line item, since it affects whether you can legally buy this species at all. Quaker parakeets are banned or restricted in a meaningful number of US states, tied to concerns about escaped birds establishing invasive wild colonies that damage crops and build large stick nests on power infrastructure. **Verify current rules with your state wildlife or agriculture agency before you shop**, not after you've found a breeder, this is covered in full in the [handling guide](/blog/quaker-parakeet-handling-guide/).
