# How Much Does a Quaker Parakeet Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Quaker parakeets sit in the middle of the pet-bird price range, and one detail has to be settled before you buy at all: whether it's even legal where you live.

## The bird: %%animal:quaker-parakeet%%

A well-socialized Quaker from a reputable breeder typically runs in this range, with color mutations, age, and sex affecting the exact price.

## Upfront setup: %%setup:quaker-parakeet%%

<CostTable guide="quaker-parakeet" section="necessities" />

The cage is the line to shop carefully. Read the depth before you buy: many flight cages sold for small and medium parrots are only 20 to 23 inches deep, shallower than the 24 inch minimum this species wants, and they buy the room back in width and height rather than floor depth. Toys are the one row that never stops: whatever you buy on day one gets shredded, and replacing them belongs in the monthly budget below. The first avian exam comes on top of the table, and a new-client exam costs more than a repeat one, as the vet table further down shows.

## Ongoing costs: roughly %%monthly:quaker-parakeet%% a month

Pellets, fresh produce, cage liners, and a steady rotation of <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4wMu5Wu" product="Hanging Sola Ball Chew/Forage Toys, 2 Pack (BBjinronjy)">toys</AffiliateLink> make up the monthly total, and that last item deserves emphasis, Quakers are natural nest-builders and shred and destroy toys quickly, budget for genuinely frequent replacement, not an occasional purchase.

## Vet costs

An annual avian exam runs $85 and up, plus periodic nail and wing grooming. Avian vets are specialized, and given how long this species lives, covered below, you're looking at decades of these costs, not a handful of years.

These come from published exotic and avian price lists, one in Denver, one a mobile service in North Carolina, and one in-home practice's lab menu, so your clinic will land somewhere near them and not on them. A routine year with one wellness exam and a gram stain is roughly $170 to $205, and bloodwork adds $158 in the years it is due. Grooming is $35 a service if you do not learn to do it yourself. The emergency line is the one to hold in reserve: an after-hours exam at the Denver practice is $200 plus a $120 emergency fee before any treatment starts.

## Lifespan: 20 to 30 years, sometimes longer

This is a long-lived bird, outliving most small species by decades, though not matching the largest parrots, despite being considerably smaller and more affordable to acquire. Treat this as the real headline number when deciding whether this species fits your life, not the purchase price.

<FunFact>
The Quaker is the only parrot species that builds its own nest rather than nesting in a tree cavity, and in the wild these twig-woven nests can become massive multi-chambered structures shared by many pairs. A pet Quaker keeps that instinct fully intact, which is exactly why its toy budget runs higher than a similarly-sized parrot that isn't compelled to shred and weave everything in reach.
</FunFact>

## Before any of this: check your state's laws

This is worth stating as its own line item, since it affects whether you can legally buy this species at all. Quaker parakeets are banned or restricted in a meaningful number of US states, tied to concerns about escaped birds establishing invasive wild colonies that damage crops and build large stick nests on power infrastructure. Those are invasive-species rules rather than dangerous-animal ones, and they run [state by state](/blog/quaker-parakeet-legal-guide/). **Verify current rules with your state wildlife or agriculture agency before you shop**, not after you've found a breeder.
