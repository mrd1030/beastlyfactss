# Guppy Tank Setup: Warm Water, Gentle Filtration, and a Plan for Fry

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Guppies are one of the more forgiving fish to house well, a warm, cycled tank with gentle filtration covers most of what this species needs.

<Figure src="/assets/images/guppy-tank-setup-guide-1.jpg" alt="Labeled diagram of a guppy tank showing gentle sponge filtration, dense live plants for fry, and a water test kit for cycling" caption="A warm, cycled tank with gentle filtration covers most of what this species needs." shareable />

## Tank Size

5 gallons works as a bare minimum for a small trio, but 10 gallons is the more practical beginner starting point for a group of 5 or 6. Given how readily guppies breed, a 20-gallon tank is a better long-term choice if you're not actively separating males and females. Go long and rectangular rather than tall, and use a lid, guppies are capable jumpers.

## Temperature

A genuinely tropical species, comfortable in the 72 to 82°F range, with 76 to 78°F as a solid target. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eQY1L5" product="Submersible aquarium heater">heater</AffiliateLink> is necessary unless your room stays consistently warm year-round, budget roughly 3 to 5 watts per gallon, so a 25 to 50 watt heater for a 10-gallon tank.

## Water Chemistry

Guppies do best with a pH of 7.0 or above and moderately hard water, good calcium and magnesium content. If your tap water runs soft, a mineral supplement (a wonder shell or similar product) can help buffer it into a more suitable range.

## Filtration

Keep the flow gentle. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3T8xyAd" product="Sponge filter">sponge filter</AffiliateLink> works well and is the safer choice if you're allowing breeding, since it won't pull fry into strong current. A hang-on-back filter with adjustable flow also works well for a non-breeding setup. Strong, powerful filtration built for larger or messier fish isn't necessary here and can actually stress guppies.

Guppies are very peaceful and suit a quiet community of other livebearers, rasboras, corydoras, small plecos and peaceful tetras, provided those tetras share the harder water above. Keep them away from fin-nippers such as tiger barbs and serpae tetras, and away from bettas, a fin-nipping risk to a guppy's tail, or goldfish, a cold-water species that grows large and needs 20-plus gallons and serious filtration, a mismatch for a small tropical fish like this one. The [Betta fish tank setup guide](/blog/betta-fish-tank-setup-guide/) and [Goldfish tank setup guide](/blog/goldfish-tank-setup-guide/) cover what those two species need instead.

## Substrate

Fine gravel or sand both work well, and darker substrate colors tend to make guppy coloring stand out more. Avoid sharp or coarse gravel, which can damage delicate fins.

## Lighting

A basic LED light on an 8 to 10 hour daily cycle is sufficient. This isn't strictly necessary for the fish's health, but it supports live plants if you're using them and makes for better viewing.

## The Two Non-Obvious Requirements

Cycle the tank before adding fish. Establishing the beneficial bacteria colony that processes waste takes [four to six weeks with a fishless cycle](/blog/aquarium-cycling-guide/), or can be shortened somewhat with a bottled bacteria product. Skipping this step is one of the most common beginner mistakes in the hobby generally, not just with guppies, and it's a big part of what drives ammonia stress in a new guppy tank.

Always use a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3Rg5gTT" product="Water conditioner">water conditioner</AffiliateLink> on tap water before it goes in the tank, dechlorinating and neutralizing chloramine and heavy metals that are otherwise harmful to fish.

<FunFact>
Dense live plants aren't just decoration in a guppy tank, they're a genuine population control lever. If you're allowing your guppies to breed, dense plants like <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4grZYPq" product="Live terrarium plants">Java fern or vallisneria</AffiliateLink> give fry somewhere to hide from adult fish, meaningfully increasing survival rates. If you'd rather your population stay naturally controlled, skipping dense cover lets normal predation keep numbers in check instead. Same tank, same species, two very different outcomes depending on how you plant it.
</FunFact>
