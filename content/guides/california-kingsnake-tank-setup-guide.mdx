# California Kingsnake Enclosure Setup

<AffiliateDisclosure />

California kingsnake husbandry is straightforward, a big part of why this species has such a strong beginner reputation. Get the basics right and there's not much complexity beyond that.

<Figure src="/assets/images/california-kingsnake-tank-setup-guide-1.jpg" alt="Labeled diagram of a California kingsnake enclosure showing a tightly clamped lid, temperature gradient, and two hides one per zone" caption="Two non-negotiable security requirements: a tightly clamped lid, and never housing this species with or near another snake." shareable />

## Enclosure Size

A hatchling starts small and moves up, and the adult enclosure is a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0D1B332MY?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="120 Gallon Wooden Reptile Terrarium, 48 x 24 x 24 in">48 inches long by 24 inches wide by 24 inches tall</AffiliateLink> front-opening setup. Give an adult an enclosure at least as long as the snake itself. The one preference test run on a near relative offered corn snakes enclosures either two thirds of their body length or longer than their body, and [they chose the longer one](/blog/california-kingsnake-enrichment-guide/). These are active snakes that use the space they are given.

Hatchlings are about 8 to 10 inches long and reach adult size, usually 3 to 4 feet, in around three years. The working rule is an enclosure at least as long as the snake, which gives three steps:

Move up when the snake's length passes the length of its enclosure, not on a birthday. A 4-foot snake is already past the 36-inch step.

## Temperature

Night temperatures can drop to 68 to 72°F. Hatchlings run on the same numbers as adults; what changes is that a 20-inch enclosure has little room for a gradient, so put the heat source at one end only, cover no more than a third of the floor, and confirm with a thermometer that the far end sits in the cool range.

An <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/44hAxJ1" product="Under-tank heat mat + thermostat kit">under-tank heat pad, overhead heat source, or both</AffiliateLink> work well, always run through a thermostat. An unregulated heat mat can reach [burn temperatures](/blog/reptile-heating-thermostats-guide/), and where the probe sits decides what the controller is actually reading.

## Humidity

Keep it relatively low, 40 to 60%. Read it with a digital probe hygrometer whose probe sits in the middle of the enclosure: humidity runs higher at the cool end and lower at the warm end, and higher at night, so a probe at either end reads the extreme instead of the average. A well-ventilated enclosure and a water bowl that is not overfilled both help hold the range.

## Substrate

<AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3TkxAVF" product="Aspen shavings substrate">Aspen shavings</AffiliateLink> are the classic, well-supported choice for this species, supporting natural burrowing behavior. Cypress mulch, sani-chips, or a commercial forest-floor product also work. Avoid pine and cedar entirely, both release aromatic oils that irritate the respiratory system, and skip sand or any chemically treated substrate.

## Lighting

No strict UVB requirement if your snake gets a natural light cycle from the room it is kept in, though low-level UVB offers some benefit. If you add it, run a T5 HO tube in the 5.0 or 6% range on a 12-hour day, about 9 to 12 inches above the basking area through mesh or 14 to 17 inches with nothing in between.

## Diet Basics

California kingsnakes are reliable feeders on frozen/thawed rodents, which is a large part of the beginner reputation. Hatchlings and juveniles eat every 5 to 7 days, and adults drop back to every 10 to 14 days. Size the prey to roughly the width of the snake at its thickest point. Keep fresh water in a dish the snake can sit in, and never offer live rodents, which can injure a snake that does not strike cleanly.

## Security, on Two Fronts

California kingsnakes are accomplished escape artists, so a tightly clamped, secure lid is essential. The other front is company: one kingsnake per enclosure, with no contact between its enclosure and any other snake's, since this species eats snakes.

## Keeping It Clean

Remove feces and urates daily, replace soiled aspen as you find it, and change the substrate completely every three to four months. Any patch soaked by a tipped water bowl comes out the same day, since damp substrate is where scale rot starts. The weekly wipe-down and disinfectant contact times follow the [reptile enclosure cleaning schedule](/blog/reptile-salmonella-hygiene-guide/).

Furnish the enclosure with two <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3SKmfOx" product="Cork bark round hide">cork bark hides</AffiliateLink> at minimum, one on each end of the temperature gradient, then keep adding cover across the floor between them. Two hides in an otherwise bare enclosure leaves the middle a place the snake will not cross.
