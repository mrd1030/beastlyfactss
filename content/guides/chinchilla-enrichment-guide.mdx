# Chinchilla Enrichment: Fur Chewing Dropped With the Right Floor

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Chinchillas have one signature welfare problem, and a study on cage complexity moved it with a change to the floor.

<KeyTakeaway>
Flooring type greatly influenced natural behaviors including scratching and dust bathing, and fur-chewing cases decreased in solid-floor cages with litter. Fur chewing is the stereotypy chinchilla keepers dread, and the intervention was not a toy.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Cage Complexity Study Found

A study published in *Scientific Reports* examined the effects of age, size and **cage complexity** on the behavior of farmed female chinchillas. The type of flooring greatly influenced natural behaviors such as **scratching and dust bathing** when the animal was in contact with bedding material, and **fur-chewing cases decreased in solid-floor cages with litter**.

Separately, older work on the **bathing behavior of the chinchilla and the effects of deprivation** is what establishes dust bathing as a motivated behavior rather than a habit. A deprived animal does not simply skip it, which is the standard way of showing a behavior has real motivational weight behind it.

<FunFact>
Fur chewing is usually discussed as a stress response with vague causes. Here it moved with a flooring change, which puts it in the same category as [hamster wire-gnawing](/blog/hamster-enrichment-guide/): a visible stereotypy that responds to housing rather than to attention.
</FunFact>

## Solid Floors With Litter

This is the finding, applied directly. A wire floor removes the surface that scratching and dust bathing act against, and it is separately implicated in foot problems in this heavy-coated, high-jumping species.

Solid shelves and a solid floor with bedding on it give the animal something to interact with.

## Vertical Space and Jumping

Chinchillas are built to leap. Multiple solid shelves at varied heights turn a cage into something they can move through in three dimensions, and a tall cage with two shelves in it is mostly empty air.

## Gnawing, Continuously

Chinchilla incisors grow throughout life, so gnawing is maintenance with a physical consequence when it is unmet. <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0F6V5ZZR6?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Chinchilla mineral lava chew blocks">Mineral and lava chews</AffiliateLink>, untreated wood, and plenty of hay all count.

## Priority Order

1. Solid flooring and shelves with litter, the part with a study behind it
2. Scheduled dust bathing, offered and removed
3. Vertical space with multiple solid shelves
4. Continuous gnawing material
5. A correctly sized solid wheel
6. A compatible companion where introductions allow
7. Tunnels, hides and foraging, rotated

## What Not to Do

Do not use wire flooring, since flooring is the variable the study moved fur chewing with. Do not leave the dust bath in the cage permanently, which irritates eyes and dries skin. Do not use a small or wire wheel. Do not bathe a chinchilla in water, which soaks a coat that cannot dry properly and leads to fungal problems. And do not keep a chinchilla somewhere warm, since heat stress outranks every enrichment question here.
