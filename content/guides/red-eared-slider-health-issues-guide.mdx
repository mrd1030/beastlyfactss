# Common Red-Eared Slider Health Issues and What Causes Them

<VetDisclaimer />
<AffiliateDisclosure />

Three failures: no UVB, dirty water, a bad diet.

## Metabolic bone disease and soft shell

Calcium and phosphorus out of balance because the diet is wrong, the UVB is missing, or both. Soft or misshapen shell. Bent leg bones. In a bad case the turtle never reaches adult size.

Watch for a soft shell, bent legs, lethargy, swollen limbs, trouble swimming or walking. Early cases can improve once lighting and diet are fixed, with a vet. Advanced cases stay or kill. ## Pyramiding is not MBD

MBD is a soft shell. Pyramiding is a hard shell with raised, peaked scutes.

<FunFact>
Stancel, Dierenfeld and Schoknecht fed 48 young sliders four diets for 150 days, including extra calcium and phosphorus. Supplementation slowed growth. It did not cause pyramiding. Every turtle finished with a normal shell.
</FunFact>

Pyramiding has more than one driver: too much protein, overfeeding, a calcium-phosphorus gap, low humidity, no night drop, weak UVB, no exercise, fast captive growth, other illness. Calcium is one item on that list. For a slider the practical lever is growth rate. Overfeeding pellets and meat grows the shell faster than it can form.

UVB is still required. Missing UVB causes MBD, which is the worse of the two.

## Shell rot

Bacteria, fungus, or parasites, often after a crack, burn, or bite. Soft spots, pits, discoloration, a foul smell, white fuzz. Ulcer to the bone is severe. Dirty water, no dry dock, an existing wound. A crack can infect in hours. Do not wait.

Mild cases: clean water, dry-dock time, topical care. Deep cases: vet, debridement, antibiotics. ## Respiratory infections

Mucus at the mouth and nose, discharge, lethargy, wheezing. Tilting sideways in the water is pneumonia. Cold water, drafts, dirty water, a vitamin A gap. Urgent. Reptile vet. Do not wait.

## Ear abscesses

A lump on the side of the head, just behind the eyes. Trouble eating or swimming. Vitamin A gap plus dirty water. Surgical drainage and antibiotics. Not a home fix.

## Vitamin A deficiency

All pellets, iceberg lettuce, or an all-meat bowl. That gap sits under the eye swelling, the respiratory infections, and the ear abscesses. ## Emergency versus home

Now: trouble breathing, cannot dive, deep shell lesions, a large abscess, no food for more than a few days. Home once the cause is fixed: early soft shell after UVB and calcium are right, mild surface shell issues, minor scrapes in clean water. Wellness exams catch the rest.

No UVB plus low calcium is MBD. A weak filter plus skipped water changes is shell rot and ear or lung infections. An all-protein bowl is vitamin A. A dock that never dries is shell trouble.
