# Common Red-Eared Slider Health Issues and What Causes Them

<VetDisclaimer />
<AffiliateDisclosure />

Most problems in captive sliders trace directly back to one of three husbandry failures: inadequate UVB, poor water quality, or an unbalanced diet. See our [Red-Eared Slider tank setup guide](/blog/red-eared-slider-tank-setup-guide/) for the husbandry side, and our complete [Red-Eared Slider care guide](/guides/red-eared-slider/) for everything else.

## Metabolic Bone Disease and Soft Shell

Caused by insufficient UVB lighting, a calcium or vitamin D3 imbalance, or both together. Watch for a soft or deformed shell, lethargy, swollen limbs, difficulty swimming or walking, and pyramiding, a bumpy, raised growth pattern on the shell scutes.

<FunFact>
Pyramiding isn't just a cosmetic quirk. That bumpy, raised growth pattern on a slider's shell scutes is essentially a visible record of long-term UVB and calcium deficiency, the shell keeps growing, but abnormally, instead of staying smooth the way a well-lit, well-fed turtle's shell does.
</FunFact>

This is the classic result of weak or absent UVB combined with a poor diet. Early cases can genuinely improve once lighting and diet are corrected, alongside veterinary care, but advanced cases can be permanent or fatal. ## Shell Rot

A bacterial or fungal infection showing up as soft spots, pits, discoloration, a foul odor, or white and fuzzy patches, with exposed bone signaling a severe case. Prolonged exposure to dirty water, an existing injury, poor basking access, or high humidity without a chance to fully dry are the usual causes.

Mild cases often respond to improved water quality, dry-docking time, and topical treatment under guidance, but deep infections need veterinary intervention, debridement and antibiotics. ## Respiratory Infections

Wheezing, open-mouth breathing, discharge from the nose or eyes, lethargy, floating lopsidedly, and appetite loss are the signs to watch for. Water or ambient temperatures running too cold, drafts, poor water quality, and vitamin A deficiency all contribute. This can progress quickly, treat it as urgent and see a reptile vet promptly rather than waiting to see if it resolves.

## Aural (Ear) Abscesses

Visible swelling on the side of the head, along with difficulty eating or swimming. Often linked to vitamin A deficiency combined with chronically poor water quality or a bacterial infection. This almost always requires surgical drainage and antibiotics from a vet, it isn't something that resolves at home.

## Vitamin A Deficiency

Usually tied to a poor diet, especially an all-pellet regimen without enough variety or fresh greens. This deficiency contributes to the eye swelling, respiratory problems, and ear abscesses covered above, it's a genuine root cause worth taking seriously rather than treating each symptom separately. ## Emergencies vs. Manageable at Home

Difficulty breathing, severe lethargy, an inability to dive or swim properly, deep shell lesions, large abscesses, or a complete refusal to eat for more than a few days are all emergencies. Early soft shell, once UVB and calcium are corrected, mild superficial shell issues, and minor scrapes, once water quality is fixed, are generally manageable at home. Annual or semi-annual wellness exams catch most of this early, our [Red-Eared Slider cost guide](/blog/red-eared-slider-cost-guide/) covers what those visits and any emergency treatment typically run.

## The Husbandry Pattern Worth Remembering

No or inadequate UVB combined with low dietary calcium leads to MBD and soft shell. Weak or undersized filtration with infrequent water changes leads to shell rot and ear or respiratory infections, the same water-quality-driven pattern that shows up in other aquatic pets like koi, see our [Koi health issues guide](/blog/koi-health-issues-guide/) for that parallel. An all-protein or low-variety diet leads to vitamin A deficiency and the secondary infections that follow. And an insufficient dry basking area, one that doesn't let the turtle fully dry off, directly promotes shell problems.
