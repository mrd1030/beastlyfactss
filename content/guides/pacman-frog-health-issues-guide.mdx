# Common Pacman Frog Health Issues and What Causes Them

<VetDisclaimer />
<AffiliateDisclosure />

Two problems cover most of this list: too much food, and substrate in the mouth.

## Obesity

They eat when food appears. They do not self-limit. Adults about once a week, not the juvenile schedule. Pinky mice as a staple is the fat diet.

## Impaction

The strike swallows whatever is on the ground with the prey. That can kill.

**Watch for:** a hard lump in the belly, bloating, refusal to move.

Mild cases sometimes clear with warm soaks. A blockage that stays needs a vet. Prevention: <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CY23K32S?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="VCEPJH Loose Coconut Fiber Substrate">fine coco fiber</AffiliateLink>, not gravel or coarse bark, and <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0FB3PRD2Y?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="4-Pack Reptile Feeding Tongs Set">tongs into a dish</AffiliateLink>, not the floor.

<FunFact>
In heat and drought wild Pacman frogs seal themselves in a mucus cocoon and sit for months. That is estivation, not illness. A frog under a year doing that in a tank is a vet call.
</FunFact>

## Metabolic bone disease

Missing calcium or UVB. Droopy jaw, bowed legs, twitching. Vet. Prevent with <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0D7JWFV5W?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Repashy Calcium Plus 6 oz JAR">calcium and D3</AffiliateLink> from the start.

## Red-leg and bacterial infections

Red spots on belly or thighs, cloudy eyes, lethargy. Emergency. Quarantine. Vet. Dirty tank, dirty water, stress. More common in new or young frogs.

## Bloat and edema

Severe swelling and cannot right itself. Emergency. Could be impaction, organ failure, or fluid. Not wait-and-see.

Adults once a week. Fine fiber. Dish and tongs. Those three.
