# How Much Does a Tiger Salamander Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Tiger salamanders are genuinely affordable, and the real budgeting story with this species is more about longevity than any single expensive line item. A 2025 federal rule also touches this species, though not in the way headlines suggested, see our [Tiger Salamander legal guide](/blog/tiger-salamander-legal-guide/) for what it actually restricts. For everything else about day to day care, see our full [Tiger Salamander care guide](/guides/tiger-salamander/).

## The Salamander Itself: $30 to $125

Barred and eastern tiger salamanders, the most common types in the trade, typically run $30 to $50 as juveniles. Rarer morphs and larger, more mature animals can reach $125 or more. One thing worth knowing before you buy, the California tiger salamander is a protected, endangered species and isn't legally available as a pet, so make sure you're buying a different, legally kept species instead.

A 20-gallon-long or similarly sized tank works well here, horizontal footprint matters more than height for this species. The substrate is genuinely important, more detail on why in our [Tiger Salamander tank setup guide](/blog/tiger-salamander-tank-setup-guide/), and it's not a place to cut corners.

## Ongoing Costs: Roughly $15 to $30 a Month

Live feeders, nightcrawlers, crickets, and roaches among the common choices, make up nearly the entire monthly cost, genuinely modest for this species. Feeding discipline matters more than the dollar amount here though, see our [Tiger Salamander health issues guide](/blog/tiger-salamander-health-issues-guide/) for why overfeeding is the most common problem keepers run into.

## Vet Costs

A routine exotic exam runs $50 to $135, sometimes cited higher, $150 to $300, for a thorough workup. Emergency visits start around $150 to $300 before diagnostics.

## Lifespan: Genuinely Long for an Amphibian

12 to 15 years is typical, with well-documented cases reaching considerably longer. A recognized longevity database records a captive maximum of 25 years for this species, and wildlife agencies corroborate a range of 10 to 16 years in the wild, up to 25 in captivity. This is a genuinely long-term commitment, easily rivaling many reptiles already covered on this site, despite the animal's modest price and low ongoing cost.

Larval tiger salamanders, interestingly, start out looking and living almost exactly like the axolotl already covered on this site, see our [axolotl cost guide](/blog/axolotl-cost-guide/) for that comparison, before metamorphosing into the terrestrial, burrowing adult described above. That transition is covered in detail in our [Tiger Salamander handling guide](/blog/tiger-salamander-handling-guide/).

## The Honest Budgeting Takeaway

This is one of the more affordable pets on this entire site to buy and maintain month to month, but the real number to sit with is the potential 15 to 25 year lifespan. A cheap animal with a long life ahead of it is still a real, serious commitment, just one that happens to be financially modest along the way.
