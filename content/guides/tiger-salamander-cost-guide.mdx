# How Much Does a Tiger Salamander Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Tiger salamanders are affordable, and the budgeting story with this species is more about longevity than any single expensive line item.

## The Salamander Itself: $30 to $125

Barred and eastern tiger salamanders, the most common types in the trade, typically run $30 to $50 as juveniles. Rarer morphs and larger, more mature animals can reach $125 or more. One thing worth knowing before you buy, the California tiger salamander is a protected, endangered species and isn't legally available as a pet, so make sure you're buying a different, legally kept species instead. California goes further than that one species and restricts the whole genus, which is one of eleven jurisdictions where the animal is off the table entirely, set out state by state in the [tiger salamander legal guide](/blog/tiger-salamander-legal-guide/).

A 20-gallon-long or similarly sized tank works well here, horizontal footprint matters more than height for this species. The substrate is genuinely important, and it's not a place to cut corners.

<FunFact>
A tiger salamander bought for as little as $30 can easily outlive plenty of pets people think of as long-term commitments. The captive record for the species is 25 years, solidly in reptile territory despite this being one of the more affordable amphibians on the market.
</FunFact>

## Ongoing Costs: Roughly $15 to $30 a Month

Live feeders, nightcrawlers, crickets, and roaches among the common choices, make up nearly the entire monthly cost, genuinely modest for this species. Feeding discipline matters more than the dollar amount here though, and overfeeding is the most common problem keepers run into.

## Vet Costs

A routine exotic exam runs $50 to $135, sometimes cited higher, $150 to $300, for a thorough workup. Emergency visits start around $150 to $300 before diagnostics.

## Lifespan: Long for an Amphibian

12 to 15 years is typical, with well-documented cases reaching considerably longer. The captive maximum on record is 25 years, and ordinary captive adults have reached 16. In the wild the documented figure is 10 years or more, with no firm upper bound established. This is a long-term commitment, easily rivaling many reptiles, despite the animal's modest price and low ongoing cost.

Larval tiger salamanders, interestingly, start out looking and living almost exactly like an [axolotl](/blog/axolotl-cost-guide/), before metamorphosing into the terrestrial, burrowing adult described above. The [Tiger Salamander vs. Axolotl guide](/blog/tiger-salamander-vs-axolotl-guide/) goes further into how the two actually compare once you get past that larval resemblance.

## The Honest Budgeting Takeaway

A tiger salamander is cheap to buy and cheap to run month to month, but the real number to sit with is the 12 to 15 years you should expect and the 25 on record. A cheap animal with a long life ahead of it is still a real, serious commitment, just one that happens to be financially modest along the way.
