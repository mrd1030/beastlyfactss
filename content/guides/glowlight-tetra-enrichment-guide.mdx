# Glowlight Tetra Enrichment: A School, Open Water and Stained-Stream Cover

<AffiliateDisclosure />

A glowlight tetra is a fish of about 1.6 inches that spends its days in the middle of the water, moving with a group, and its enrichment is built around that habit.

<KeyTakeaway>
Glowlight tetras come from dark, forested streams and live as a school in open mid-water. Enrichment means giving them that: **a school of 10**, **fine-leaved plants with open space in the center**, **floating plants and leaf litter for a stained-stream feel**, and **small live or frozen food as a treat**.
</KeyTakeaway>

## How They Live in the Wild

The glowlight tetra is known only from the Essequibo River in Guyana. It lives mainly in forested areas, in tributaries that branch off the main river channel. The water there is stained dark brown by tannins from decaying organic material, and it is often strongly acidic.

That brown, shaded water is the brief for the tank: acidic and dim in places, with plenty of plant cover close by and clear water to swim through.

## The School: Ten or More

Six is the floor and 10 or more is the target. A group of 10 fills the middle of a tank with movement and color, while a few fish scatter. Buy them as one species, together.

## Plants, Open Water, Leaf Litter

Plant the tank with a variety of fine-leaved plants at different heights, and keep a patch of open water in the center. The school uses that open middle as its swimming lane, and the plants around it give somewhere to retreat. Fine sand makes a gentle floor.

Floating plants shade the surface the way forest cover shades the home streams. Scattered leaf litter adds to the effect and releases tannins as it breaks down. Some keepers also tuck a small bag of aquarium-safe peat into the filter for the same reason. Any of these softens and acidifies water, so test first and keep the tank inside pH 5.5 to 7.5, and check the tap water's [GH, KH and pH](/blog/freshwater-ph-gh-kh-guide/) before adding any of them.

A school of 10 needs a tank with a base of 24 x 15 inches (60 x 38 cm), which leaves room for both the planting and the open middle. The [glowlight tetra tank setup guide](/blog/glowlight-tetra-tank-setup-guide/) explains the size.

## Small Live and Frozen Food

A glowlight is an easy eater, and in the wild it takes worms, small crustaceans and plant matter. Small live and frozen foods such as bloodworm, Daphnia and brine shrimp are the closest a tank gets to that, and they give the school something to hunt.

Offer them as a treat beside a staple of <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B004PBB5TK?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Hikari Tropical Semi-Floating Micro Pellets, 0.77 oz">micro-pellets</AffiliateLink> or small flakes. Portion size and how often to feed are covered in the [glowlight tetra feeding guide](/blog/glowlight-tetra-feeding-guide/).

<FunFact>
Glowlight tetra eggs are extremely sensitive to light, so breeders shield the spawning tank from bright light.
</FunFact>

## Priority Order

1. A school of 10 glowlight tetras, one species
2. Fine-leaved plants at several heights, with open water in the center
3. Floating plants
4. Water on the acidic side at 75 to 82°F
5. Small live or frozen food as a treat
6. Leaf litter

## What Not to Do

Do not buy three because the tank looks big. Do not fill the middle of the tank with decor and leave no room to swim. Do not run the heater above 82°F. Do not skip a [30-day quarantine](/blog/fish-quarantine-and-treatment-guide/) for new fish.

In the forest streams of the Essequibo, a glowlight's world was shaded water, plants within reach and a group moving around it. A tank that supplies those covers most of what the fish needs.
