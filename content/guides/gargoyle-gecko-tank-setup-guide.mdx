# Gargoyle Gecko Tank Setup: A Low Ceiling, Not No Heat at All

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Gargoyle geckos run cooler than most reptiles people keep, and that fact gets repeated until it turns into something else: that they need no heat at all. It is a misconception, and a common one.

<Figure src="/assets/images/gargoyle-gecko-tank-setup-guide-1.jpg" alt="Labeled diagram of a gargoyle gecko enclosure showing a low-wattage basking bulb over one side, a daily wet-dry humidity cycle, and heavy branch and cork bark clutter" caption="A gargoyle gecko still wants a gradient. What is different is the ceiling: 86°F is dangerous, where a desert species would be basking well past it." shareable />

## Enclosure Size

Minimum for a single adult is an <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4vlb6Bp" product="18x18x24 in glass terrarium (arboreal)">18x18x24 inch enclosure</AffiliateLink>, with 24x24x24 inches as the ideal. Vertical space matters more than floor space, this is an arboreal, climbing species. Hatchlings and juveniles under about 12 grams do better in a smaller grow-out enclosure temporarily. House one gecko per enclosure, they're territorial.

## Temperature: A Gradient With a Low Ceiling

Give the enclosure a gradient: a cool end around 70 to 75°F and a basking spot around 82 to 85°F, with ambient temperature sitting at or below about 82°F. Nights can drop into the low 70s or even the mid-60s without issue, and 65°F is the floor. The ceiling is the part worth memorizing: air above 86°F is dangerous, which is a lower limit than almost any other lizard in the hobby.

The "no heat needed" claim is worth addressing directly, because it is everywhere and it is wrong. A gecko is ectothermic and relies on its surroundings for warmth, and a room that suits people gives it no gradient to use. A low-wattage bulb or ceramic heat emitter on a thermostat, over one side of a screen top so the basking branches reach range, is the normal setup. What is genuinely different about this species is how little headroom you have above the basking spot.

## Humidity: The Daily Cycle That Prevents Most Health Problems

Target 50 to 70%. Mist heavily in the evening, and let it dry back down toward 50% before misting again. Constant, unbroken moisture causes respiratory infection, one of the most common preventable problems in this species. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3QHrM81" product="Digital thermometer/hygrometer combo">thermometer and hygrometer combo</AffiliateLink> makes it easy to confirm the cycle is actually happening rather than guessing.

Gargoyle geckos mostly drink water droplets off leaves and walls rather than from a dish, though a shallow dish is still worth providing. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4vPKMjX" product="Fine mist spray bottle">fine mist spray bottle</AffiliateLink> is all you need to run the daily cycle.

## Substrate

<AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4wqEOFU" product="Bioactive substrate mix">Coconut fiber</AffiliateLink> (like Zoo Med Eco Earth) works well for humidity retention, and paper towel is a solid budget option, especially for hatchlings or a sick gecko. A bioactive soil mix with a drainage layer and a cleanup crew is another good option. Avoid sand, wood chips, and gravel, which are the substrates that carry a real [impaction risk](/blog/gargoyle-gecko-health-issues-guide/). Soil and coconut fiber are a different case: this is an arboreal species that spends most of its time off the floor, and soil-based and bioactive substrates are the standard recommendation for it.

## Lighting

Gargoyle geckos can survive without UVB since a complete powdered diet supplies vitamin D3. Low-level <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00M9ONY1W?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Zoo Med 5.0 T5-HO 14&quot; Terrarium Hood with ReptiSun T5 HO 5.0 UVB Lamp">UVB</AffiliateLink>, a 12-inch low-output T5 bulb such as the Zoo Med ReptiSun T5 HO 5.0, positioned for a basking-area UVI of roughly 1.0 to 2.0, offers real digestive and immune benefits. Run a 12-hour light cycle, with no light overnight. UVB doesn't pass through glass or plastic, so the bulb needs to sit directly over open mesh.

## What Makes a Gargoyle Setup Different

Heavy clutter, not sparse decor, is the right instinct here. Branches, vines, and cork bark genuinely help prevent floppy tail syndrome by giving your gecko somewhere other than bare glass to rest. Gargoyle geckos climb glass poorly compared to crested geckos, so grippable branches matter more here than in a typical crested gecko setup.

Ventilation matters just as much as clutter: a screen top and real airflow let the enclosure dry out within about 24 hours after misting. Keep the enclosure away from sunny windows too, since ambient room heat is the thing most likely to push a gargoyle past its ceiling, and a summer heatwave is a real risk in a way a cold snap is not.

<FunFact>
Crested geckos stick to glass with ease. Gargoyle geckos, a close relative, are noticeably worse climbers on smooth surfaces and lean on branches and cork bark instead. It's a small anatomical difference with a real practical consequence: a sparsely decorated gargoyle enclosure just pushes the gecko toward sleeping flat against the glass, which is exactly what causes floppy tail syndrome.
</FunFact>
