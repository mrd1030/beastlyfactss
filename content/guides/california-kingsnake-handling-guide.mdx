# How to Handle a California Kingsnake (And Why It Must Live Alone)

California kingsnakes are docile, hardy, and one of the best choices for keepers at any experience level. There's one rule with this species that overrides everything else about its otherwise easygoing temperament, and it comes from what this snake eats in the wild.

## What to Expect Day to Day

Calm and tolerant of regular handling once settled, this is a classic beginner snake for good reason. Young kingsnakes can deliver quick, pin-prick defensive bites. That settles with consistent, gentle handling over time. Approach hand-over-hand rather than grabbing, and skip handling within 48 hours of feeding or during a shed cycle, when cloudy eyes make a snake more defensive and jumpier than usual.

For the full day-to-day picture, see our complete [California Kingsnake care guide](/guides/california-kingsnake/).

## The Reason for the Name: Kingsnakes Eat Other Snakes

This is genuinely central to understanding this species. In the wild, California kingsnakes eat other snakes, including venomous species like copperheads, cottonmouths, and rattlesnakes. They constrict and swallow prey headfirst, and documented cases show kingsnakes consuming snakes equal to or even longer than themselves.

<FunFact>
Research on serum from the kingsnake genus found meaningful resistance to rattlesnake venom specifically. That resistance isn't universal, though, kingsnakes have little to no protection against the neurotoxic venom of coral snakes, and the exact degree of resistance varies by population.
</FunFact>

## The Rule This Creates: Never House With or Near Another Snake

This is the single most important housing rule for this species, more important than temperature or humidity. Kingsnakes retain a genuine cannibalistic drive in captivity. This isn't a minor risk to manage carefully: it's an absolute rule. Never house a California kingsnake with another snake of any species, whether that's a milk snake, a boa constrictor, or anything else, and never allow contact between enclosures. This belongs as much in the handling conversation as the setup one, since it affects how you plan your entire reptile collection, not just this one animal's enclosure. For setup specifics, including how the lid factors into this same rule,, see our [California Kingsnake tank setup guide](/blog/california-kingsnake-tank-setup-guide/).

## First-Timer Mistakes

- Assuming a docile temperament means safe cohabitation with another snake. It doesn't, this species will eat a cage-mate.
- Handling too soon after feeding.
- Underestimating this species as an escape artist, a loose lid is a real risk.

## How This Compares to the Corn Snake

The [corn snake](/blog/corn-snake-handling-guide/), another classic beginner colubrid already covered on this site, is similarly hardy and easy to handle. The real difference isn't temperament. It's diet: corn snakes aren't dedicated snake-eaters, which is exactly why the kingsnake's absolute solo-housing rule doesn't apply in the same way to its corn snake cousin.
