# How Much Does a Cockatoo Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Cockatoos are a serious financial and emotional commitment, and the purchase price is just the smallest part of a decision that plays out over decades.

## The Bird: %%animal:cockatoo%%, More for Rare Species

Common species like Goffin's, Galah, and Umbrella cockatoos typically run in this range, with Umbrella cockatoos commonly $1,000 to $3,000, Moluccan cockatoos around $1,400 to $3,500, and rarer species like the palm cockatoo reaching $2,000 to $16,000 or more.

Adoption is worth strong consideration for this species. Rescue fees run $300 to $800, and cockatoos are, unfortunately, one of the most commonly surrendered large parrots.

## Upfront Setup: %%setup:cockatoo%%, Bird Included

<CostTable guide="cockatoo" section="necessities" />

The cage is most of the bill: $850 for a heavy-gauge powder-coated cage big enough for this bird, up to $2,200 for stainless steel. The play stand is bought at the same time, because a cockatoo needs hours out of the cage daily and somewhere to spend them. The pellet and palm rows are the first supply; after that they move to the ongoing table below. The rest of the first aid kit (gauze, saline, a large towel, blunt scissors) is household stock, and a washable mat under the cage is only needed on carpet, so neither is priced here.

Food is the small line. The bigger recurring cost is toy replacement: cockatoos destroy wooden toys extremely fast, and constant destructible enrichment isn't optional, it's a real welfare need. Cardboard, plain paper and untreated wood scraps can carry part of that line for free.

<FunFact>
Cockatoos destroy wooden toys so fast that constant replacement isn't an occasional expense, it's a baked-in monthly cost, and skipping it is a welfare problem, not just a budget shortcut.
</FunFact>

## PBFD Screening Before You Pay

Psittacine beak and feather disease was first described in Australian cockatoos, has no treatment, and is usually fatal. A young bird can carry it for a long time before any feather or beak change shows, so a healthy-looking cockatoo proves nothing. Every new bird should be screened with a blood test or an oral swab, and many birds are sold with a health warranty that lets the buyer return one that tests positive. Book the test inside that window. If a bird already lives in the house, test both before they meet.

The lab work is the cheap part. One US avian lab lists a PBFD PCR at $24.50, with results in one to two days, and a psittacosis PCR at the same price. The larger cost is the visit that draws the sample: $115 for a wellness exam or $135 for a medical exam at one Denver exotics hospital, and $128 plus a $55 travel fee for a new client of a mobile avian vet. A pre-purchase exam with a PBFD test lands at roughly $140 to $210, before any handling fee a practice adds for sending samples out.

## Vet Costs

After the first visit, a wellness exam for an established client runs $78 at the mobile practice and $115 at the Denver hospital. Routine beak and nail grooming adds up on top, and chronic conditions common in this species, feather-destructive behavior among them, can become expensive to manage. Some owners budget for pet insurance specifically for this reason, often $300 to $500 a year.

## Lifespan: The Number That Should Drive the Entire Decision

25 to 45 years is typical, and the larger species can reach 70 to 80 or more. This is a lifelong commitment that may need to be planned across more than one owner's lifetime. Before any other cost consideration, this is the number worth sitting with seriously.
