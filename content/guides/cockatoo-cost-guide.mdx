# How Much Does a Cockatoo Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Cockatoos are a genuinely serious financial and emotional commitment, and the purchase price is really just the smallest part of a decision that plays out over decades.

## The Bird: $700 to $3,000, More for Rare Species

Common species like Goffin's, Galah, and Umbrella cockatoos typically run in this range, with Umbrella cockatoos commonly $1,000 to $3,000, Moluccan cockatoos around $1,400 to $3,500, and rarer species like the palm cockatoo reaching $2,000 to $16,000 or more.

Adoption is genuinely worth strong consideration for this species. Rescue fees run $300 to $800, and cockatoos are, unfortunately, one of the most commonly surrendered large parrots. If you want to understand why that surrender pattern exists before you commit either way, our [cockatoo handling guide](/blog/cockatoo-handling-guide/) covers it directly.

A large, appropriately sized cage runs $200 to $1,000 depending on quality and size, and perches, bowls, and an initial set of toys add another $20 to $200. Once you factor in a play gym and possibly an air purifier, given how much feather dust this species produces, the total can push toward $1,500 to $3,500. For the full breakdown of what the cage itself actually needs to include, see our [cockatoo cage setup guide](/blog/cockatoo-tank-setup-guide/).

## Ongoing Costs: Genuinely Significant

Food runs $20 to $50 a month, but the bigger recurring cost is toy replacement. Cockatoos destroy wooden toys extremely fast, and constant destructible enrichment isn't optional, it's a real welfare need.

<FunFact>
Cockatoos destroy wooden toys so fast that constant replacement isn't an occasional expense, it's a baked-in monthly cost, and skipping it is a welfare problem, not just a budget shortcut.
</FunFact>

## Vet Costs

Annual avian exams plus routine beak and nail grooming add up, and chronic conditions common in this species, feather-destructive behavior or PBFD among them, can become genuinely expensive to manage. Some owners budget for pet insurance specifically for this reason, often $300 to $500 a year. Our [cockatoo health issues guide](/blog/cockatoo-health-issues-guide/) covers what those conditions actually look like and what drives them.

## Lifespan: The Number That Should Drive the Entire Decision

40 to 70-plus years, with Umbrella cockatoos commonly living 50 to 70 years. This is a lifelong commitment that may genuinely need to be planned across more than one owner's lifetime. Before any other cost consideration, this is the number worth sitting with seriously.
