# Hermit Crab Enrichment: Shells Are a Welfare Need, Not Decoration

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Almost nothing kept as an invertebrate pet has real welfare research behind it. The hermit crab is the exception, and the research is stranger and more useful than anything in a care sheet.

<KeyTakeaway>
Given small shocks inside their shells, hermit crabs will abandon the shell that keeps them alive in order to escape, and the shock strength required depends partly on how much they value the shell they are in versus the alternatives. Shell provision is not a decorating decision, it is the thing these animals demonstrably weigh their own safety against.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Research Says

Elwood and Appel's experiments are the anchor. By comparing how hermit crabs responded to electric shocks when in preferred versus unpreferred shells, they showed the animals **prioritizing shell retention while managing a painful stimulus**. A crab is extremely vulnerable outside its shell, and it will still leave one to escape a shock, with the threshold depending on what it is giving up.

That structure, weighing one cost against another rather than simply reacting, is the standard bar researchers use to argue for something beyond a reflex. Elwood's 2022 paper in *Animal Cognition* argues the sentience case; Briffa published a commentary disagreeing, and Elwood responded. The disagreement is worth showing instead of resolving, because the husbandry conclusion does not depend on which side wins.

<FunFact>
Shell fighting and shell assessment in hermit crabs are among the most-studied behaviors of any invertebrate. These animals inspect, compare and negotiate over shells, which is why a tank with one spare is not a tank with a choice in it.
</FunFact>

## Shells, in Quantity and Variety

Provide **several shells per crab**, across a range of sizes and opening shapes, and keep offering new ones as the crabs grow. Natural shells, never painted ones. Painted shells shed coating that gets ingested, and the paint is for the owner, not the animal.

## Substrate Deep Enough to Molt In

This is functional husbandry, not enrichment, and it outranks everything else regardless. Hermit crabs molt buried, and a crab that cannot dig down far enough is in genuine danger and not merely under-stimulated. ## Climbing and Surface Complexity

Land hermit crabs climb, and a bare tank floor wastes most of the enclosure. <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3QQNV3L" product="Climbing branch (Mopani wood)">Branches</AffiliateLink>, cork, and rough surfaces they can grip all add usable territory and produce the most visible activity these animals show in captivity.

Anchor everything. A structure that shifts while a crab is on it is a fall risk, and a falling crab can be seriously injured.

## Foraging and Variety

Scatter the food, never bowl it. Hermit crabs are opportunistic scavengers, and spreading a varied diet across the substrate and on climbing surfaces turns feeding into searching. Keep the variety genuine: the same pellet daily in the same dish is the opposite of what these animals do.

## Priority Order

1. Substrate deep enough to bury and molt in
2. Multiple shells per crab, in varied sizes, natural not painted
3. Correct humidity and both fresh and salt water dishes
4. Climbing structure, anchored
5. Scattered and varied food
6. Novel objects and rearrangement, last

## What Not to Do

Do not keep a single crab, since they are social and are normally kept in groups. Do not supply one spare shell for a group, because that creates competition over a resource the research shows they weigh heavily. Do not use painted shells. Do not let substrate depth slip, since substrate that is too shallow is one of the causes of a bad molt, and a bad molt is frequently fatal. And do not dig up a buried crab to check on it, which interrupts the molt it is buried for.
