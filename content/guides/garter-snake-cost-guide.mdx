# How Much Does a Common Garter Snake Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Common garter snakes are some of the most affordable snakes you can keep, cheap to acquire, cheap to feed, and in a lot of the country, not even something you have to buy at all. The one place the budget gets less predictable is the diet, which looks nothing like a standard mouse-eating snake's.

For the full care picture beyond budgeting, see our complete [Garter Snake care guide](/guides/garter-snake/).

## The Snake Itself: $20 to $50 Typical, More for Morphs

A real current listing from Backwater Reptiles prices a medium to large normal garter snake at $49.99, and other online retailers list smaller juveniles starting around $30. That puts a normal garter snake below a normal corn snake ($25-70) and well below a normal hognose ($50-100), covered in our [Corn Snake cost guide](/blog/corn-snake-cost-guide/) and [Hognose Snake cost guide](/blog/hognose-snake-cost-guide/). Color and pattern morphs (albino, checkered albino, anerythristic, and combinations like erythristic-het-anery or flame het albino) are a smaller, newer market than corn snake or hognose morphs, but they do exist and command real premiums on the exotic pet marketplace.

## The Free Option: Catching One in Your Backyard

This is unique among the snakes covered on this site. The common garter snake is the most widely distributed snake in North America, found in gardens, vacant lots, and wetlands from Florida to coastal Quebec and west to British Columbia, and plenty of keepers end up with one simply because it turned up in the yard rather than because they went shopping for it.

Two real caveats keep this from being a free lunch. First, regulations vary by state, and a few subspecies, including the federally endangered San Francisco garter snake, carry specific legal protections, so it's worth a quick check of local wildlife rules before you keep a wild one. Second, a wild-caught animal skips the purchase price but not the setup cost or the vet visit, a fecal exam for parasites is a genuinely good idea for any wild-caught snake, covered in more detail in our [Garter Snake health issues guide](/blog/garter-snake-health-issues-guide/).

<FunFact>
A wild garter snake costs nothing to acquire, but it still needs the exact same $200-plus setup as one bought online. Where the free-snake math actually pays off is over time: skip the $20-50 purchase price and you've saved maybe one month's worth of the ongoing cost, not the meaningful part of the budget.
</FunFact>

This is where the diet stops being a simple one-line cost. Frozen mice are the cheap, easy default for a corn snake or hognose, one supplier, one product, done. A garter snake's diet spans frozen and thawed fish (tilapia and silversides are common, thiaminase-free choices), earthworms, and sometimes mice, and the total dollar cost lands in a similar range to a standard rodent-only snake. What changes is the sourcing: you're keeping two or three food types on hand instead of one, and if fish make up a large share of the diet, a vitamin B1 supplement becomes a real recurring line item, not an optional extra. See our [Garter Snake feeding guide](/blog/garter-snake-feeding-guide/) for why that supplement matters and how often to use it.

## Vet Costs

A routine reptile wellness exam runs roughly $50 to $100, with a fecal test around $25 to $50, worth prioritizing for this species given how much of its natural diet (fish, earthworms, amphibians) can carry parasites. Thiamine deficiency from an unsupplemented, fish-heavy diet is treatable with injectable thiamine plus a diet correction, but it's a condition specific enough to piscivorous snakes that the standard veterinary reference for exotic animal medicine calls out garter and water snakes by name as the group most likely to develop it. Catching it early keeps the vet bill to a single visit; catching it late, once neurological symptoms appear, costs more in both money and risk to the snake. Our [Garter Snake health issues guide](/blog/garter-snake-health-issues-guide/) covers the full symptom list.

## Lifespan and the Long Game

Wild garter snakes rarely live long, most don't survive their first year or two, with 2 to 4 years being a commonly cited range for the ones that do. Captivity changes that picture substantially: 6 to 10 years is typical with good care, and individuals have been documented living past 20. That's a real span to budget for, even on a snake this inexpensive to run month to month.

## The Value Proposition

Among the beginner snakes covered on this site, a normal garter snake is one of the cheapest to acquire and among the cheapest to set up. The catch isn't cost so much as convenience: a keeper who wants to buy one food type and never think about it again will have an easier time with a corn snake, while someone willing to keep fish and earthworms on hand gets a genuinely inexpensive, active, semi-aquatic pet in return.
