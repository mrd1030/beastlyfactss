# How Much Does a Veiled Chameleon Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Veiled chameleons are one of the more affordable chameleons to buy, genuinely one of the cheapest entry points into the species. The setup and the vet costs are where this pet gets more expensive than the sticker price suggests. For the full care picture beyond budgeting, see our complete [Veiled Chameleon care guide](/guides/veiled-chameleon/).

## The Chameleon: $20 to $100

Captive-bred juveniles are the cheapest option. Unlike many reptiles, veiled chameleons don't have a large morph market, hypomelanistic and hypermelanistic color mutations exist and cost more, but the selection is genuinely limited compared to species like ball pythons or geckos. Females sometimes cost less than males, worth knowing given the lifespan difference covered below.

<FunFact>
A female veiled chameleon will produce egg clutches on a regular cycle even if she has never been near a male in her life. The eggs simply come out infertile. It's the reason her lifespan budgeting looks so different from a male's, laying clutches is a real physical cost whether or not there was ever a mate involved.
</FunFact>

## Vet Costs

A routine exotic exam runs roughly $50 to $100, with a fecal test adding $25 to $50. This is a species where emergency costs can escalate fast: egg-binding in females, covered in detail in our [health issues guide](/blog/veiled-chameleon-health-issues-guide/), can require medical management around $200 to $400, or surgery running $800 to $1,500 or more. Budget accordingly if you're considering a female specifically.

## Lifespan: Short, and Genuinely Different by Sex

This is the detail that changes how you should think about the whole cost picture. Males commonly live 6 to 8 years. Females typically live considerably shorter, often just 2 to 6 years, and many don't make it past 2 to 3, driven by the physical toll of producing egg clutches. Spaying a female can meaningfully extend her life, worth discussing with an exotic vet if you're set on keeping a female long term.
