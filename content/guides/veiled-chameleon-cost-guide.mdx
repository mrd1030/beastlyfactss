# How Much Does a Veiled Chameleon Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Veiled chameleons are one of the more affordable chameleons to buy, one of the cheapest entry points into the species. The setup and the vet costs are where this pet gets more expensive than the sticker price suggests.

## The Chameleon: %%animal:veiled-chameleon%%

Captive-bred juveniles are the cheapest option. Unlike many reptiles, veiled chameleons don't have a large morph market, hypomelanistic and hypermelanistic color mutations exist and cost more, but the selection is genuinely limited compared to species like ball pythons or geckos. Females sometimes cost less than males, worth knowing given the lifespan difference covered below.

Two places will not let you keep one at any price. Washington DC permits only non-venomous snakes, fish and turtles, so every lizard is out, and Hawaii bars anything not on an approved list, which this species is not. New Jersey wants a permit.

## Upfront Setup: %%setup:veiled-chameleon%%

<CostTable guide="veiled-chameleon" section="necessities" />

A tall screen or hybrid enclosure sized for an adult from the start, a linear T5 HO UVB fixture and bulb, and a basking heat bulb make up most of the bill. A misting system or dripper is genuinely essential, not optional. Round it out with live plants, branches, and drainage, plus a thermometer and hygrometer. Experienced keepers commonly report around $500 for a complete single-animal setup, with elaborate builds, multiple cages, an automated mister, extensive planting, reaching $1,200 or more.

Feeder insects such as crickets, dubia roaches, and silkworms make up the bulk of the monthly total, alongside calcium and multivitamin supplements and occasional plant or substrate replacement.

<FunFact>
A female veiled chameleon will produce egg clutches on a regular cycle even if she has never been near a male in her life. The eggs simply come out infertile. It's the reason her lifespan budgeting looks so different from a male's, laying clutches is a real physical cost whether or not there was ever a mate involved.
</FunFact>

## Vet Costs

A routine exotic exam runs roughly $50 to $100, with a fecal test adding $25 to $50. This is a species where emergency costs can escalate fast: egg-binding in females can require medical management around $200 to $400, or surgery running $800 to $1,500 or more. Budget accordingly if you're considering a female specifically.

## Lifespan: Short, and Different by Sex

This is the detail that changes how you should think about the whole cost picture. Males commonly live 6 to 8 years. Females typically live considerably shorter, often just 2 to 6 years, and many don't make it past 2 to 3, driven by the physical toll of producing egg clutches. Spaying removes the ovaries and ends the [egg-laying cycle](/blog/veiled-chameleon-health-issues-guide/) behind that difference, which takes follicular stasis and egg binding off the table. It is an elective surgery that exotic vets perform on chameleons, worth discussing if you're set on keeping a female long term.

Two very differently priced alternatives for anyone still deciding are the [ferret](/blog/ferret-cost-guide/) and the [hognose snake](/blog/hognose-snake-cost-guide/).
