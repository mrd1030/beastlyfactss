# Chinchilla Housing: Dust Baths, Not Water, and a Cool Room

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Chinchilla care has a few rules that surprise new owners, especially around bathing. Get the cage, the temperature, and the dust bath right, and the rest of this species' care is fairly manageable.

<Figure src="/assets/images/chinchilla-tank-setup-guide-1.jpg" alt="Labeled diagram of a chinchilla cage showing the dust bath house, solid exercise wheel, and constant hay access" caption="Dust baths, not water, and a cool room are the two things new chinchilla owners most often get wrong." shareable />

## Cage

Go tall and multi-level, with solid, non-wire floors and shelves. Wire flooring contributes to sore feet over time. Around 24 by 24 by 36 inches is the smallest cage most care sheets accept, but veterinary guidance sets a single chinchilla at 2 by 2 by 4 feet, and that taller cage is the one to buy, since height is what a jumper uses. A pair needs roughly 18 cubic feet more on top of that. Wire sides should be spaced no more than 1 inch apart. Use metal, not plastic, chinchillas chew constantly, and ingested plastic can cause a fatal blockage. <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B001NJ0DAY?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Critter Nation by MidWest Homes for Pets Double Unit Cage">Multi-level small-animal cages designed for ferrets</AffiliateLink> are popular among chinchilla keepers for exactly this combination of height, solid flooring, and metal construction.

Inside, give each chinchilla its own hide box, at least about 12 by 8 by 8 inches, and put them on different levels. The highest one usually becomes the favorite sleeping spot.

Place the cage in a room where people spend time, out of direct sun and drafts, and never near rabbits. Even a healthy rabbit can carry Pasteurella bacteria, which can kill a chinchilla.

## Dust Baths: Not Water, Ever

Chinchillas need a dust bath, not a water bath, and this is one of the most important things to get right. Provide <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4vgxhss" product="Chinchilla dust (Oxbow Poof)">chinchilla dust</AffiliateLink> (a fine volcanic ash) in a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4w2STJO" product="Chinchilla dust bath house">dust bath house</AffiliateLink> 2 to 3 times a week, some owners go daily, for up to 15 minutes at a time, then remove the container so it doesn't become a permanent fixture in the cage. Use roughly 1 to 2 inches of dust in a shallow container.

**Chinchillas must not get wet.** Their fur is extremely dense, among the densest of any mammal, and trapped moisture in a coat that thick promotes fungal growth rather than washing anything away. There's no safe way to bathe a chinchilla in water the way you might a dog.

## Diet

Grass hay (timothy or orchard) should make up 80 to 90% of the diet, available at all times, not rationed. <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4h9WW2u" product="Timothy hay (Oxbow Western Timothy Hay, 90 oz)">Timothy hay</AffiliateLink> is the standard choice. Add plain, hay-based pellets, roughly 16 to 20% protein and 15 to 35% fiber, at about 1 to 2 tablespoons a day. Keep treats minimal, and provide safe, untreated wood chews to support natural dental wear alongside the hay.

## Bedding and Cleaning

Unscented paper bedding or fleece both work well. Avoid cedar and pine shavings entirely, both carry respiratory and impaction risks.

- **Daily:** spot-clean droppings, wet bedding and leftover food, and wash the food and water dishes with soap and water.
- **Weekly:** replace all the bedding and wash the empty cage with soap and water.

The dust bath container gets the same attention: dust left in the cage collects droppings and has to be replaced, which is another reason it comes out after each session.

## Exercise

Chinchillas need daily activity. A solid metal wheel, 15 inches or larger, is the right choice. **Never use an exercise ball,** they're a genuine overheating and injury risk for this species specifically, even though they're marketed broadly for small pets. That injury risk matters more than usual given how fragile this species' skeleton is.
