# Three Reptiles Worth Starting With, Each for a Different Reason

Reptile keeping covers a huge range of commitment levels, and these three sit at very different points on that range despite all three counting as approachable for a first time keeper. One is a pet you will still have decades from now. One is a species most people have already met in their own backyard without realizing it. And one is small and calm enough to be one of the most forgiving snakes in the entire hobby.

## Red-Footed Tortoise: A Pet You'll Have for Decades

Named for the bright red-to-orange scales on its limbs and head, the red-footed tortoise is native to South America east of the Andes, from Colombia down to northern Argentina. Unlike most tortoises people picture as strict grazers, red-foots are genuine omnivores. Alongside fruit and foliage, wild individuals regularly eat carrion, insects, and land snails, a real source of animal protein most tortoises never touch. Their adult size, 11 to 16 inches, lands squarely between the smaller [Russian tortoise](/guides/russian-tortoise/) and the sulcata, which can top 100 pounds, manageable without being tiny. The real commitment here is time, not size or temperament: with good care, red-footed tortoises commonly live 50 to 70 years, with some individuals documented past 90.

## Garter Snake: The Snake Probably Already in Your Backyard

Garter snakes are one of the most widely distributed reptiles in North America, turning up in gardens, wetlands, and vacant lots from Florida to coastal Quebec and west to British Columbia. Common enough that plenty of keepers end up with one they found rather than one they bought. Two things set them apart from almost every other pet snake. They are live bearing rather than egg laying, and their diet runs to fish, earthworms, and amphibians instead of rodents, many individuals never accept a plain mouse. Like the [hognose snake](/guides/hognose-snake/), they carry a mild rear-fanged venom used to subdue small prey, harmless to people in normal handling. Small, hardy, and inexpensive, the main adjustment is sourcing food that is not just a bag of frozen mice.

## Rosy Boa: Small, Calm, and Easy to Underestimate

The rosy boa is a small desert boa native to the southwestern United States and Baja California, rarely exceeding 3 feet as an adult, a striking contrast to the [boa constrictor](/guides/boa-constrictor/), which can grow six to ten times longer. Famously docile and slow moving even by boa standards, rosy boas rarely bite and are frequently recommended as one of the calmest snakes kept in the entire hobby. Their desert origin also means simpler climate needs than the tropical boas and pythons most people picture, lower humidity and no rainforest style setup to maintain. With good care they are remarkably long lived too, averaging 18 to 22 years with individuals documented past 30.

<FunFact>
These three do not earn "beginner-friendly" the same way. The red-footed tortoise is easy to handle day to day but asks for a 50-plus year commitment most pets never require. The garter snake is cheap and hardy but wants fish and earthworms instead of a standard frozen-mouse diet. The rosy boa is about as low-maintenance as a snake gets, calm, small, and desert-simple to house. Match the right one to the kind of commitment you actually want, and any of the three makes a solid first reptile.
</FunFact>
