# Milk Snake Enrichment: A Shy Snake Needs More Cover, Not Less Space

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Milk snakes hide, constantly, and that behavior gets used to justify keeping them in small boxes. The research on their nearest studied relative suggests that reads the situation backwards.

<KeyTakeaway>
A secretive snake in a large bare enclosure is short of cover, not short of walls. Corn snakes given enclosures longer than their body stretched out fully and preferred them. The fix for a snake that never comes out is to make coming out feel safe, which means clutter in an enclosure of full size.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Research Says

Nothing on *Lampropeltis triangulum*. The [colubrid evidence](/blog/corn-snake-enrichment-guide/) comes down to three findings.

- **Space gets used.** Corn snakes housed in enclosures either two thirds of their body length or longer than their body stretched out fully when they could and preferred the larger enclosure.
- **Enrichment shows in behavior.** Added enrichment affected behavior and welfare measures in corn snakes, and in ratsnakes Almli and Burghardt found that enriched-housed snakes showed different behavioral profiles across problem solving, feeding and field tasks.
- **Enrichment reaches the senses.** Enrichment changed how well corn snakes performed at odor discrimination.

## What the Wild Snake Is Doing

The field record gives the cover argument its species-specific footing. Wild milk snakes usually take their heat from beneath rocks and wood instead of basking in the open, and in the northern Plains they live in grassland with rocky outcrops and rocky cover. They overwinter in mammal burrows and rock crevices. Their prey list runs from rodents to lizards and other snakes, animals that are themselves found under cover.

That is the enclosure brief in three parts: warm cover to sit under, tight gaps to push into, and a route between them. In practice it means a cork flat or slate piece laid over the basking spot so the snake can take heat while hidden, and a crevice or two, such as stacked flat bark with a snake-width gap, that the snake has to squeeze into.

## Clutter Is the Whole Guide

The practical test: **can the snake cross the entire enclosure without ever being fully exposed?** If not, it will use two hides and nothing else, and you will conclude it wanted a smaller enclosure.

## Space, Which the Clutter Makes Usable

The enclosure-size finding and the cover point are the same recommendation from two directions. Length gives the snake somewhere to go; cover gives it a reason to go there. Either alone produces a disappointing enclosure. For an adult milk snake the length starts at the [48 by 24 by 24 inch minimum](/blog/milk-snake-tank-setup-guide/), and the clutter goes in across all of it.

## Climbing

Climbing is an option, not a need. A firmly braced <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3QQNV3L" product="Climbing branch (Mopani wood)">branch</AffiliateLink> or stacked cork adds a level. Stability matters more than height, since a structure that shifts is one the snake stops using.

## Scent

Scent is the free option, and the one that best matches how a colubrid experiences the world. Try a shed from another enclosure, a scent-marked object, or prey dragged across the substrate before it is offered.

## Priority Order

1. Cover across the entire floor, which is this species' specific need
2. An enclosure longer than the snake
3. Substrate deep enough to push into
4. A stable climbing structure
5. Scent novelty
6. Rearrangement, last

## What Not to Do

Do not use "it hides all the time" as an argument for a smaller enclosure. Do not house two together, since *Lampropeltis* eat other snakes. Do not add floor space without adding clutter. Do not handle a hiding snake to check on it. And do not read corn snake findings as milk snake findings, even though the borrow is a short one.

## Walk the Route It Would Take

Trace the path from the warm hide to the cool one at the snake's own height. Wherever that path crosses open floor, a cork flat, a drift of leaf litter or a plant goes in. The enclosure is finished when the snake can make the whole crossing without once being exposed.
