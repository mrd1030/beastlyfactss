# Milk Snake Enrichment: A Shy Snake Needs More Cover, Not Less Space

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Milk snakes hide, constantly, and that behavior gets used to justify keeping them in small boxes. The research on their nearest studied relative suggests that reads the situation backwards. For everything else about them, see our complete [Milk Snake care guide](/guides/milk-snake/).

<KeyTakeaway>
A secretive snake in a large bare enclosure is short of cover, not short of walls. Corn snakes given enclosures longer than their body stretched out fully and preferred them. The fix for a snake that never comes out is to make coming out feel safe, which means clutter rather than a smaller tank.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Research Actually Says

Nothing on *Lampropeltis triangulum*. The colubrid evidence, covered in full in our [Corn Snake enrichment guide](/blog/corn-snake-enrichment-guide/), is that corn snakes housed in enclosures either two thirds of their body length or longer than their body **stretched out fully when they could and preferred the larger enclosure**, that added enrichment affected behavior and welfare measures, and that enrichment changed odour discrimination performance.

Almli and Burghardt separately found enriched-housed ratsnakes showed different behavioral profiles across problem solving, feeding and field tasks.

<FunFact>
Milk snakes and corn snakes are both North American colubrids of similar size, build and habit, which makes this one of the shorter evidence borrows in this series. It is still a borrow, and this guide will keep saying so.
</FunFact>

## Clutter Is the Whole Guide

The practical test: **can the snake cross the entire enclosure without ever being fully exposed?** If not, it will use two hides and nothing else, and you will conclude it wanted a smaller enclosure.

## Space, Which the Clutter Makes Usable

The enclosure-size finding and the cover point are the same recommendation from two directions. Length gives the snake somewhere to go; cover gives it a reason to go there. Either alone produces a disappointing enclosure.

## Climbing

Milk snakes climb more readily than the terrestrial label suggests. A firmly braced <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3QQNV3L" product="Climbing branch (Mopani wood)">branch</AffiliateLink> or stacked cork adds a level. Stability matters more than height, since a structure that shifts is one the snake stops using.

## Scent

The free option, and the one that best matches how a colubrid experiences the world. A shed from another enclosure, a scent-marked object, prey dragged across the substrate before it is offered. Our [feeding guide](/blog/milk-snake-feeding-guide/) covers the feeding itself.

## Priority Order

1. Cover across the entire floor, which is this species' specific need
2. An enclosure longer than the snake
3. Substrate deep enough to push into
4. A stable climbing structure
5. Scent novelty
6. Rearrangement, last

## What Not to Do

Do not use "it hides all the time" as an argument for a smaller enclosure, which is the reasoning this guide exists to challenge. Do not house two together, since Lampropeltis eat other snakes. Do not add floor space without adding clutter. Do not handle a hiding snake to check on it, which our [handling guide](/blog/milk-snake-handling-guide/) covers. And do not read corn snake findings as milk snake findings, even though the borrow is a short one.
