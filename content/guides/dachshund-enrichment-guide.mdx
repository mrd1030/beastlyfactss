# Dachshund Enrichment: Keep It at Floor Level

The dachshund is one of the few breeds where the safest activity and the most natural activity are the same thing. See our general [dog enrichment guide](/blog/dog-enrichment-guide/) for the fundamentals.

<KeyTakeaway>
A dachshund is a **scent hound built to go underground**, with a spine that is the breed's defining vulnerability. Everything that suits the breed, digging, tracking, searching, nose-down work, happens with **four feet on the ground**. The enrichment plan and the spinal protection plan turn out to be the same plan.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Research Actually Says

No enrichment study on the breed. The general finding, covered in full in our [dog enrichment guide](/blog/dog-enrichment-guide/), fits it neatly.

Duranton and Horowitz gave twenty pet dogs either **nosework** or **heelwork**, then ran a cognitive bias test. The nosework group's **latency to approach an ambiguous bowl declined significantly**, the standard optimism measure. The heelwork group **did not change**. The proposed mechanism is a dog expressing a natural behaviour **autonomously**.

The breed constraint comes from the veterinary literature on intervertebral disc disease and chondrodystrophy, covered in our [IVDD and chondrodystrophy guide](/blog/ivdd-and-chondrodystrophy-guide/). Dachshunds are the breed most strongly associated with it, and the association is with the same genetics that produce the shortened legs.

<FunFact>
The word dachshund means badger dog. Everything about the shape, the short legs, the long body, the loud voice, the willingness to go into a hole after something larger than itself, was engineered for underground work. The back problems arrived with the same package.
</FunFact>

## A Digging Pit

The best enrichment available for this breed and the one most owners never provide.

A sandpit, a raised bed of loose soil, or a large container of sand. Bury things in it, food, toys, scent articles, and let the dog work. It combines digging with searching, costs almost nothing, and it is entirely spine-safe.

It also redirects the behaviour. A dachshund with a pit digs the pit. A dachshund without one digs the lawn.

## Protect the Spine, Practically

Manage the environment rather than relying on the dog to be careful.

**Ramps or steps** onto sofas and beds, used consistently. **Stairs gated off** or the dog carried. **Lift with the whole body supported**, front and back, never under the front legs alone. **Non-slip flooring** in the places the dog moves fast.

None of this is over-cautious. Our [IVDD and chondrodystrophy guide](/blog/ivdd-and-chondrodystrophy-guide/) covers what a disc event looks like and how quickly it can go from a yelp to hind limb paralysis.

## Weight, as Spinal Care

One of the few IVDD risk factors an owner fully controls. Extra weight loads a spine that is already the breed's weak point.

Weigh the food, deliver it through the digging pit, <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4g102oU" product="Nina Ottosson Dog Tornado Puzzle Toy">puzzle feeders</AffiliateLink> and scatter feeding, and take it out of the ration. Learn to body condition score, covered in our guide to [pet obesity and body condition](/blog/pet-obesity-body-condition-guide/).

Dachshunds are efficient beggars and small enough that a few extra treats a day is a meaningful percentage.

## Controlled, Steady Exercise

Regular walks on the flat build the core and back musculature that supports the spine, so exercise is protective rather than risky when it is the right kind.

Steady walking, yes. Repetitive jumping, twisting chase games, and hard landings from height, no. Frequent short walks beat one long one.

Useful at transitions, before you leave, and after activity.

## The Voice

Dachshunds were bred to be heard from underground, so they are loud for their size and that is not fixable. Most of the excess is boredom and alert barking, and a dachshund with daily digging and scent work barks less than one with neither.

## Priority Order

1. A dedicated digging pit with things buried in it
2. Daily scent work, indoors and out
3. Ramps, gated stairs and correct lifting, used consistently
4. Weight kept down, as spinal care
5. Steady flat walking rather than jumping and twisting games
6. Food delivered through the pit and puzzles, out of the ration
7. Calm chewing and licking tasks at transitions

## What Not to Do

Do not let a dachshund jump on and off furniture. Do not lift one under the front legs. Do not build games on leaping or hard turns. Do not let the weight creep on a breed where it loads the spine directly. Do not try to stop the digging without offering somewhere to dig. And do not treat a sudden yelp or reluctance to move as a strain, since in this breed it warrants a vet the same day.
