# Should You Handle an Axolotl? Only When You Truly Have To

Axolotls are not a hands-on pet, and this isn't a preference, it's about their physical vulnerability. Handling should be minimal and reserved for genuine necessity, not casual interaction.

## Why Hands-Off Is the Right Default

Axolotls have sensitive, scaleless skin and delicate external gills, the feathery structures on either side of their head. Unlike fish, they don't have a robust protective slime layer to fall back on when touched. Handling risks physical damage to the skin and gills, real stress, and an increased chance of infection at any point of contact.

Their skeleton is also largely cartilage instead of solid bone, which is part of why keeping pressure and air exposure to a minimum matters so much. This is a genuinely fragile animal in ways that aren't obvious just from looking at one.

## When Handling Is Unavoidable

Sometimes a move is genuinely necessary: tank maintenance, a water change that requires temporarily relocating the axolotl, or a health check. When that happens:

- Guide the axolotl into a submerged container while it's still underwater, instead of lifting it out by hand or scooping it into a net.
- Avoid nets if you can. The fine gill filaments and toes can snag in mesh, causing real injury. If a net is the only way, use a soft, fine mesh one and have the axolotl in it for seconds, not minutes.
- If you have to touch the animal, wear clean, disposable nitrile gloves. The oils and residues on bare hands harm its skin.
- Keep the axolotl in water at all times during the process. Air exposure and physical pressure are both genuine risks given how much of their skeleton is cartilage.

<FunFact>
Those feathery gills aren't just decoration, they're doing the actual work of breathing. That's exactly why a snagged gill filament in a net is a bigger deal for an axolotl than a scrape would be for most animals, it can interfere with how the animal takes in oxygen, not just how it looks.
</FunFact>

## Getting to the Vet

A vet visit is the most common reason an axolotl has to leave its tank, and the container does most of the work. Move it in a small, clear, watertight container filled with its own tank water, kept cool on the way, so the vet can look it over through the side without taking it out at all. Much of an axolotl exam happens that way. When a vet needs to restrain one for a closer look, a sample or a procedure, the standard is sedation in the water, with drugs such as MS-222 or alfaxalone, instead of holding a struggling animal by hand.

## Telling Males From Females Without Picking One Up

Sexing an axolotl is done by looking through the glass, with no handling at all. A mature male develops a swollen cloaca, a visible bulge behind the back legs, and dark nails; a mature female stays slimmer there and has neither. Males mature at about 10 months and females at 12 to 18 months, so an axolotl younger than that cannot be sexed reliably by anyone.

## Bringing a New Axolotl Home

If another axolotl already lives in the tank, the newcomer does not go in yet. It spends 6 to 8 weeks in a separate quarantine tub first, and the acclimation below happens into that tub, the sequence behind [amphibian quarantine and acclimation](/blog/amphibian-quarantine-and-water-guide/).

Skip handling a new arrival to "settle it in." Acclimate through drip-matching instead, slowly introducing your tank's water to the bag or container the axolotl arrived in so temperature and water chemistry shift gradually. This avoids the stress of a sudden environmental change without requiring any direct handling at all. It goes smoother when the target numbers are known in advance: 60 to 68°F, and a pH of 7.4 to 7.6, the range the [cold, clean water an axolotl needs](/blog/why-axolotls-need-cold-clean-water/) comes down to.

## The Honest Takeaway

If part of what you want from a pet is physical interaction, an axolotl isn't going to offer that, and pushing for it isn't good for the animal. The relationship here is built through observation and careful tank maintenance, not touch, and that's worth knowing before you commit to keeping one.
