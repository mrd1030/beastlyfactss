# Guppy Feeding: Schedule, Diet, and Why They Stop Eating

Guppies take food readily, which makes too much the easier mistake at feeding time, and the water pays for it when the extra goes uneaten.

## Feeding Schedule by Life Stage

- **Adults:** At least once a day, in small portions that together come to about 3% of body weight. Feed what the fish finish quickly and clear away the rest.
- **Fry and juveniles:** Young fish can take up to about 5% of their body weight a day, split across several small feeds in food fine enough for their mouths. In a trial on juveniles 30 days old, fish fed four meals a day grew the heaviest and fish fed one meal a day used their food most efficiently, and all four groups survived.

## What Guppies Eat

Wild guppies feed on zooplankton, small insects and detritus, so the aquarium diet leans on animal protein with some plant matter alongside. The base should be a high-quality tropical flake or small pellet made for community fish, crushed if the pieces are too big for the mouths in the tank. Rotate in live, frozen, or freeze-dried foods such as brine shrimp, daphnia, bloodworms, and mosquito larvae, and add small amounts of blanched vegetables cut fine, plus algae wafers. A wide variety is how an aquarium diet covers the nutrients a single food misses.

## Leftovers and Foods to Skip

Bread, cereal, and other processed human foods do not belong in the tank. Whatever the fish leave behind rots and fouls the water, so remove uneaten food and cut the next portion back.

## Why Guppies Stop Eating

1. **Stress.** New-tank relocation, tank cleaning, and aggressive tankmates are all common triggers. Too many males for the females is another: females spend less time feeding when a male is near.
2. **Poor water quality.** [Ammonia or nitrite spikes](/blog/aquarium-cycling-guide/) and low oxygen are worth testing for rather than waiting out.
3. **Water outside the range.** Guppies are tropical fish kept at 72 to 82°F, and water outside that range is worth correcting before anything else.
4. **Illness.** [Ich, velvet disease, bacterial infections, and dropsy](/blog/guppy-health-issues-guide/) usually come with other visible signs, and appetite loss is one of the early ones.
5. **Pickiness.** Guppies may reject an unfamiliar food or one too large for their small mouths, easily fixed by switching food or crushing pellets smaller.

## When to Worry

Appetite loss combined with lethargy, hiding, or erratic swimming, gasping at the surface, clamped or discolored fins, cloudy eyes, visible injuries, white spots, or a swollen pinecone-like body points to illness or bad water. Test water parameters immediately, and consider a fish-health specialist rather than waiting, especially if the signs spread to other fish.
