# Guppy Feeding: Schedule, Diet, and Why They Stop Eating

Guppies beg for food no matter how full they already are. The risk is giving too much, not too little.

## Feeding schedule by life stage

- **Fry (newborn to roughly 2 to 4 weeks).** Highest frequency. Infusoria or liquid fry food for the first few days. Newly hatched brine shrimp and microworms from about one to two weeks. Every 2 to 3 hours in those earliest days, then 3 to 5 times a day as they grow.
- **Juveniles (roughly 1 to 3 or 4 months).** Around 3 to 4 meals a day, higher protein than the adult ration, tapering as they reach adult size.
- **Adults.** Once or twice a day. Up to three times is fine if each feeding is small. The portion rule does not change: only what is fully cleared in about 1 to 2 minutes.

## What guppies eat

Guppies are omnivores. The base is a tropical community flake or small pellet, not goldfish food. Rotate in protein: brine shrimp, daphnia, bloodworms, mosquito larvae, tubifex from a commercial source, and microworms for fry. Add vegetable matter, blanched and cut small: spinach, zucchini, cucumber, peas, lettuce, plus algae wafers. They will graze tank algae on their own. A small amount of boiled egg yolk is an occasional treat.

Thaw frozen food before feeding. Do not microwave it. Do not refeed leftovers. Flake or finely crushed pellet fits a small mouth better than a large pellet.

## Safe treats and foods to avoid

Safe: live, frozen, or freeze-dried brine shrimp and daphnia, bloodworms, mosquito larvae, commercial tubifex, microworms for fry, blanched peas, zucchini, cucumber, and spinach, algae wafers, small amounts of boiled egg yolk.

Avoid: goldfish food, bread, cereal, and other processed human food. Scoop the seedy center out of tomato or cucumber. Skip spoiled food. Overfeeding itself causes constipation, swim bladder problems, and water-quality crashes from rotting leftovers.

## Why guppies stop eating

1. **Impending birth.** Females often eat less the day before they deliver. Appetite usually returns quickly afterward.
2. **Stress.** A new tank, a cleaning, aggressive tankmates, or too many males for the females. Keep roughly 1 male per 2 to 3 females. The [guppy enrichment guide](/blog/guppy-enrichment-guide/) covers that ratio.
3. **Poor water quality.** [Ammonia or nitrite spikes](/blog/aquarium-cycling-guide/), bad pH, and low oxygen cut appetite. Test rather than wait.
4. **Cold water.** Guppies need roughly 72 to 82°F, with 76 to 78°F the steadiest range. Cold water slows metabolism. Sustained temperatures well below about 72°F for more than two days is a real risk.
5. **Illness.** [Ich, velvet, bacterial infections, and dropsy](/blog/guppy-health-issues-guide/) usually show other signs, not appetite loss alone.
6. **Pickiness.** An unfamiliar food or something too large. Crush the pellet.

## When to worry

A skipped meal in a fish that looks and swims normally is not an emergency, especially around birth or a tank change. A full day without food plus odd behavior is the line: test the water the same day.

Appetite loss plus lethargy, hiding, erratic swimming, gasping, clamped or discolored fins, cloudy eyes, white spots, or a pinecone body means test the water immediately. If it is spreading to other fish, treat it as a tank problem, not a picky eater.
