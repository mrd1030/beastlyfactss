# Guppy Feeding: Schedule, Diet, and Why They Stop Eating

Guppies take food readily and will beg no matter how full they already are, which makes too much the easier mistake at feeding time, and the water pays for it when the extra goes uneaten.

## Feeding Schedule by Life Stage

- **Fry (newborn to roughly 2 to 4 weeks):** The highest feeding frequency of any stage, tiny stomachs and rapid growth. Guppies are livebearers, so fry take newly hatched baby brine shrimp or finely crushed flake from birth. Feed small amounts 3 to 5 times a day, and as often as every 2 to 3 hours in the earliest days.
- **Juveniles (roughly 1 to 3 to 4 months):** Around 3 to 4 meals a day, more frequent and higher-protein than adult feeding, tapering as they approach adult size. In a trial on juveniles 30 days old, fish fed four meals a day grew the heaviest and fish fed one meal a day used their food most efficiently, and all four groups survived. Treat the exact number as approximate.
- **Adults:** At least once a day, in small portions that together come to about 3% of body weight. Feed what the fish finish quickly and clear away the rest. There is no single right number: anywhere from once a day to three times a day works. The portion rule holds at any frequency: feed only what's fully cleared within about 1 to 2 minutes.
- **Fry and juveniles:** Young fish can take up to about 5% of their body weight a day, split across several small feeds in food fine enough for their mouths.

## What Guppies Eat

Wild guppies feed on zooplankton, small insects and detritus, so the aquarium diet leans on animal protein with some plant matter alongside. Guppies are omnivores and will also naturally graze on tank algae. The base should be a high-quality tropical flake or small pellet made for community fish, crushed if the pieces are too big for the mouths in the tank, and not goldfish food, which is made for a different fish. Rotate in live, frozen, or freeze-dried foods such as brine shrimp, daphnia, bloodworms, and mosquito larvae, plus tubifex worms from a commercial source and microworms, especially for fry. Add small amounts of blanched vegetables cut fine and small (spinach, zucchini, cucumber, peas, and lettuce) and algae wafers. Small amounts of boiled egg yolk are an occasional protein treat some keepers use. A wide variety is how an aquarium diet covers the nutrients a single food misses.

Thaw frozen food before feeding, never microwave it, and never refeed something previously thawed and left over, that encourages bacterial growth. Because guppies have small mouths, flake or finely crushed pellet generally works better than large pellets.

## Leftovers and Foods to Skip

Bread, cereal, and other processed human foods do not belong in the tank, because none of them match a guppy's actual nutritional needs. Whatever the fish leave behind rots and fouls the water, so remove uneaten food and cut the next portion back. Scoop the seedy center out of cucumber pieces before feeding. Skip spoiled or expired food, guppies will typically nibble it and reject it anyway. More than any single food, overfeeding is the mistake to watch: it can lead to constipation and swim bladder problems, and uneaten food rotting in the tank crashes water quality.

## Why Guppies Stop Eating

1. **Impending birth.** Female guppies, livebearers, often eat less or refuse food entirely in the day or so before giving birth, from reduced stomach capacity and a shift toward finding a secluded spot to deliver. Appetite typically returns quickly afterward.
2. **Stress.** New-tank relocation, tank cleaning, and aggressive tankmates are all common triggers. Too many males for the females is another, since females spend less time feeding when a male is near, and roughly 1 male per 2 to 3 females reduces mating-chase stress.
3. **Poor water quality.** [Ammonia or nitrite spikes](/blog/aquarium-cycling-guide/) and low oxygen are worth testing for rather than waiting out.
4. **Water outside the range.** Guppies are tropical fish kept at 72 to 82°F, and water outside that range is worth correcting before anything else. Cold water slows metabolism directly, causing lethargy and appetite loss along with weakened immune function, and 76 to 78°F is the steadiest ideal range. Temperatures held well below about 72°F for more than two days are a real risk.
5. **Illness.** [Ich, velvet disease, bacterial infections, and dropsy](/blog/guppy-health-issues-guide/) usually come with other visible signs, and appetite loss is one of the early ones.
6. **Pickiness.** Guppies may reject an unfamiliar food or one too large for their small mouths, easily fixed by switching food or crushing pellets smaller.

## When to Worry

There's no single, universally agreed number for how long a guppy can safely go without food, and that's worth stating plainly rather than picking a side. Contacting a vet when decreased appetite lasts more than one day is the cautious course, notably stricter than usual hobbyist practice, which generally treats a skipped meal or even a full day as not inherently concerning if the fish otherwise looks and behaves normally, especially around a known trigger like pregnancy or a tank disturbance.

Appetite loss combined with lethargy, hiding, or erratic swimming, gasping at the surface, clamped or discolored fins, cloudy eyes, visible injuries, white spots, or a swollen pinecone-like body points to illness or bad water. Test water parameters immediately, and consider a fish-health specialist rather than waiting, especially if the signs spread to other fish.
