# How Much Does a Leopard Gecko Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Leopard geckos are one of the more budget-friendly reptiles to keep, but "budget-friendly" still means a real setup, not a shortcut version.

## The Gecko Itself

A standard or wild-type leopard gecko from a pet store runs $20 to $45. Reputable breeders often charge $50 to $150 even for basic morphs, which sounds backwards until you factor in health guarantees and proper care history. Popular morphs land around $75 to $300, and rare designer morphs can run $500 to $3,500 or more. If you're buying online, add $35 to $100 for live shipping.

Whatever the price, look the animal over before paying: clear, clean eyes, a good appetite, a bright and alert manner, and a thick tail, since the tail is where a leopard gecko stores its fat and a thin one is the first sign of wasting disease. Ask a breeder whether the animal has had a fecal test. Cryptosporidium, the parasite behind "stick tail," was found in 51% of geckos screened at one Thai breeding facility. It has no cure, and apparently healthy carriers spread it. That is why a new gecko is [kept alone for 3 to 6 months](/blog/reptile-quarantine-guide/), and why the first vet visit in the table below includes a fecal test.

## Vet Costs to Plan For

An annual wellness exam runs $85 to $105. That's the routine cost. What catches people off guard is that sick or emergency visits run $100 to $800 or more, and if diagnostics or surgery get involved, it climbs from there. A leopard gecko should see a vet once a year even when it looks healthy, so the exam is a fixed yearly line. Keeping a small emergency fund set aside is buys more peace of mind than it costs.

## The Lifetime Number

The monthly figures look small because the timeline is long. A leopard gecko lives 10 to 20 years with proper care, and some reach 25 to 30. At $360 to $1,020 a year in running costs, 10 to 20 years adds up to $3,600 to $20,400 before a single sick visit. Budget for the gecko reaching its twenties: a hatchling bought today may outlast the enclosure, the heat mat, and several UVB tubes.

## Spend on the Enclosure and the Thermostat First

The monthly bills are small, so the setup is where the money goes, and it is the one purchase that cannot be made cheaper safely. Buy the adult enclosure and a thermostat for the heat mat on day one, and the running cost settles into feeders, power and a yearly exam. Plan for a gecko that may still be here in its twenties, the same long commitment every [beginner reptile](/blog/five-beginner-reptiles-overview/) asks for.
