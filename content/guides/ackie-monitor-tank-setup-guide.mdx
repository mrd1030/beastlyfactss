# Ackie Monitor Enclosure Setup: Bigger, Hotter, and Deeper Than You'd Guess

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Ackie monitor husbandry runs hotter and bigger than most reptile setups, and getting comfortable with those numbers upfront is the key to keeping this species well.

<Figure src="/assets/images/ackie-monitor-tank-setup-guide-1.jpg" alt="Labeled diagram of an ackie monitor enclosure showing the UVB lighting, Retes stack, deep substrate, and cool side" caption="Bigger, hotter, and deeper than most reptile setups, an ackie monitor enclosure runs 130-170°F on the basking surface and needs 12-24 inches of diggable substrate." shareable />

## Enclosure Size

The minimum for a single adult is 5 feet long by 2.5 feet wide by 4 feet tall, larger than the older 4x2x2 foot standard often quoted for this species, and the reason is directly tied to how active ackies actually are. This isn't an animal that thrives in a modest footprint the way a more sedentary lizard might, our [Bearded Dragon tank setup guide](/blog/bearded-dragon-tank-setup-guide/) and [Blue-Tongued Skink tank setup guide](/blog/blue-tongue-skink-tank-setup-guide/) cover two considerably calmer lizards that get by with a noticeably smaller footprint by comparison.

## Temperature: 130 to 170°F on the Basking Surface

This is the detail that surprises a lot of first-time keepers. Ackies bask at surface temperatures roughly 130 to 170°F, measured directly on the basking stone or stack with an <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4flGgUg" product="Infrared temp gun">infrared thermometer</AffiliateLink>, not air temperature. A basking surface reading below about 130°F is too cool for this species to properly synthesize vitamin D. Cool side should sit around 75 to 80°F, giving a real thermal gradient across the enclosure.

<FunFact>
An ackie monitor's basking spot can run as hot as 170°F measured on the surface, a temperature that would be dangerously high for almost any other commonly kept pet lizard. Most keepers actually under-provide heat rather than over-provide it for this species, so erring toward the hotter end of that range is usually the safer mistake to make.
</FunFact>

## Humidity

Ackies need both dry and humid zones, but here's the key detail: most of the moisture should live underground rather than in the air. Ambient sits between 20 and 50%, which is drier than most reptile keepers expect, while the burrow itself should hold 80% or higher, since a semi-arid burrower goes underground to find moisture rather than getting it from the room. This is exactly why deep, moisture-holding substrate matters so much, covered next, it's doing real thermoregulatory and hydration work, not just providing digging material.

## Substrate: Deep Enough to Burrow In

This is a defining, non-negotiable requirement for this species. Provide 12 to 24 inches of a soil and sand mix that holds a proper burrow, a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07Z5CJ573?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="The Bio Dude Terra Sahara Bioactive Reptile Substrate, 36 qt">bioactive-style substrate</AffiliateLink> or a DIY topsoil and play sand combination both work, contained with a substrate dam tall enough to hold that depth. Avoid shallow bark-only setups or dry, pure sand, both collapse and fail to support the burrowing behavior this species relies on. This kind of substrate depth is a genuine gap in most standard reptile setups, budget for multiple bags rather than assuming a single one will cover it.

## Lighting

UVB is genuinely important here, not optional. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3TipZXE" product="UVB - Arcadia Desert 12% (ProT5 kit, 22in/24W)">T5 HO bulb in the 12% desert range</AffiliateLink>, spanning roughly half the enclosure over the basking area, replaced every 12 months. Target a basking-area UVI of 4.0 to 6.0, meaningfully higher than most other commonly kept lizards need. Bright daylight LED alongside the UVB supports this full-sun species well, paired with <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4vfa5L0" product="High-wattage basking bulb (250W)">high-wattage basking bulbs</AffiliateLink> to reach those triple-digit surface temperatures.

## The Non-Obvious Requirement: A Retes Stack

Stacked shelves or tiles positioned under the basking lamp let your ackie choose its exact preferred basking spot along a genuine temperature gradient, rather than being stuck with one uniform hot zone. Combined with the substrate dam holding deep, diggable material, this is what separates an adequate ackie setup from a good one, most keepers under-provide heat rather than over-provide it, so err toward the hotter end of the basking range.
