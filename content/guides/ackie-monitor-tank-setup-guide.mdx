# Ackie Monitor Enclosure Setup: Bigger, Hotter, and Deeper Than You'd Guess

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Ackie monitor husbandry runs hotter and bigger than most reptile setups, and getting comfortable with those numbers upfront is the key to keeping this species well.

<Figure src="/assets/images/ackie-monitor-tank-setup-guide-1.jpg" alt="Labeled diagram of an ackie monitor enclosure showing the UVB lighting, Retes stack, deep substrate, and cool side" caption="Bigger, hotter, and deeper than most reptile setups, an ackie monitor enclosure runs 130-170°F on the basking surface and needs 12-24 inches of diggable substrate." shareable />

## Enclosure Size

The minimum for a single adult is 5 feet long by 2.5 feet wide by 4 feet tall, larger than the older 4x2x2 foot standard often quoted for this species, and the reason is directly tied to how active ackies are. This isn't an animal that thrives in a modest footprint the way a more sedentary lizard might. A [bearded dragon](/blog/bearded-dragon-tank-setup-guide/) or a [blue-tongued skink](/blog/blue-tongue-skink-tank-setup-guide/) is a considerably calmer lizard and gets by with a noticeably smaller footprint.

A <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DC2292M8?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Reptile Habitats 48x24x48 (240 Gallon) PVC Enclosure">large custom or PVC enclosure</AffiliateLink> at this size is the biggest single line in the build.

## Temperature: 130 to 170°F on the Basking Surface

This is the detail that surprises a lot of first-time keepers. Ackies bask at surface temperatures roughly 130 to 170°F, measured directly on the basking stone or stack with an <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4flGgUg" product="Infrared temp gun">infrared thermometer</AffiliateLink>, not air temperature. A basking surface reading below about 130°F is too cool for this species to properly synthesize vitamin D. Cool side should sit around 75 to 80°F, giving a real thermal gradient across the enclosure.

<FunFact>
An ackie monitor's basking spot can run as hot as 170°F measured on the surface, a temperature that would be dangerously high for almost any other commonly kept pet lizard. Most keepers actually under-provide heat rather than over-provide it for this species, so erring toward the hotter end of that range is usually the safer mistake to make.
</FunFact>

## Humidity

Ackies need both dry and humid zones, but here's the key detail: most of the moisture should live underground rather than in the air. Ambient sits between 20 and 50%, which is drier than most reptile keepers expect, while the burrow itself should hold 80% or higher, since a semi-arid burrower goes underground to find moisture rather than getting it from the room. This is why deep, moisture-holding substrate matters so much: it does real thermoregulatory and hydration work beyond giving the lizard something to dig in.

Measure the two numbers separately. A digital hygrometer mid-enclosure reads the ambient air. A probe buried at burrow depth reads the substrate, and when it drops below 80%, pour water into the soil until the mix clumps and holds its shape again.

## The Humid Dig Zone: Mid-80s

The damp section of substrate needs warmth as well as moisture. Females looking for a nest site search for soil in the mid-80s°F, preferably 86 to 87°F, that is damp enough to hold a tunnel, and a female kept without that spot has nowhere suitable to lay. Place the deepest, dampest section where the ground warms toward that range, part-way toward the hot end instead of under the cool side, and check it with a probe thermometer pushed into the soil. Females need at least 18 inches of depth there; 12 inches is the floor for a male.

## Substrate: Deep Enough to Burrow In

This is a defining, non-negotiable requirement for this species. Provide 12 to 24 inches of a soil and sand mix that holds a proper burrow, a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07Z5CJ573?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="The Bio Dude Terra Sahara Bioactive Reptile Substrate, 36 qt">bioactive-style substrate</AffiliateLink> or a DIY topsoil and play sand combination both work, contained with a substrate dam tall enough to hold that depth. Avoid shallow bark-only setups or dry, pure sand, both collapse and fail to support the burrowing behavior this species relies on. This kind of substrate depth is a genuine gap in most standard reptile setups, budget for multiple bags rather than assuming a single one will cover it.

## Lighting

UVB is required here. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3TipZXE" product="UVB - Arcadia Desert 12% (ProT5 kit, 22in/24W)">T5 HO bulb in the 12% desert range</AffiliateLink>, spanning roughly half the enclosure over the basking area, replaced every 12 months. Target a basking-area UVI of 4.0 to 6.0, meaningfully higher than most other commonly kept lizards need. Bright daylight LED alongside the UVB supports this full-sun species well.

Heat comes from halogen floods, not a single spot bulb. A typical adult build runs two 100-watt PAR30 halogens side by side over the stack, which warms a patch wide enough for the whole body, on a dimming thermostat. In a cold room or a tall enclosure, step up to <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4vfa5L0" product="High-wattage basking bulb (250W)">higher-wattage basking bulbs</AffiliateLink>. The rule for sizing runs the other way too: if a bulb has to be dimmed by half or more to hold the target, it is the wrong wattage and a lower one should replace it. All of the heat goes off at night.

## The Non-Obvious Requirement: A Retes Stack

Stacked shelves or tiles positioned under the basking lamp let your ackie choose its exact preferred basking spot along a temperature gradient, rather than being stuck with one uniform hot zone. The top layer sits closest to the lamp and runs hottest; each gap below it is cooler, and the ackie slots itself in at the level it wants.

To build one:

1. Use flat, heat-safe layers: kiln-dried wood, flagstone, slate tile, or concrete pavers.
2. Separate them with rectangular wood spacers long enough to span two parallel sides of the stack.
3. Make each gap roughly the height of your ackie from belly to back, so it can squeeze in with its back touching the layer above.
4. Anchor the base to the enclosure floor, down through the substrate, so a digging lizard cannot undermine it and bring the stack down.
5. Read the top surface with the infrared thermometer once the lamps have run for an hour, then adjust lamp height or dimming.

Combined with the substrate dam holding deep, diggable material, this is what separates an adequate ackie setup from a good one. Most keepers under-provide heat rather than over-provide it, so err toward the hotter end of the basking range.

The stack stands in for the rock crevices wild ackies wedge themselves into across the rocky arid country of northwestern and central Australia, described in the [ackie monitor encyclopedia profile](/encyclopedia/animal/ackie-monitor/). The UVI target above comes from the Ferguson zone for full-sun baskers, and bulb distance is what moves a reading into it, as the [UVB lighting guide](/blog/uvb-lighting-complete-guide/) lays out.
