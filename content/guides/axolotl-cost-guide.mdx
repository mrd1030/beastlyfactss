# How Much Does an Axolotl Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Axolotls look deceptively simple to keep, a tank of water and a strange, smiling salamander. The cost driver isn't obvious until you get into the specifics: keeping the water cold enough is what determines your budget.

Worth checking before you buy though: this species is banned in a handful of states.

## The Axolotl Itself: $30 to $100

Standard morphs run $30 to $100, with rarer colors like mosaic or GFP pushing the price higher still. This is one of the smaller pieces of the overall cost picture, the tank and temperature control matter a lot more.

### What the Price Tags on Morphs Mean

Besides the dark wild type, the common colors, melanistic, leucistic and albino, come from selective breeding in captivity. White (leucistic) and albino are single-gene traits kept as breeding strains, an albino simply makes no melanin, and breeders can produce these colors reliably, which is why they sit at the low end of the price range.

**GFP** is a different kind of animal. It carries the gene for green fluorescent protein, first put into axolotls by transgenesis for laboratory research, and under ultraviolet light the whole body glows green. The glow is the only difference: the care, the water and the food are the same as for any other axolotl.

**Mosaic** and **chimera** axolotls are built from two genetically different cell lines, each laying down its own color, so it carries patches of two morphs on one body. Researchers have made them in the lab by fusing white and albino embryos, and the pigment in each patch follows the cell line it came from. Each egg or sperm carries only one line's genes, so a mosaic does not breed more mosaics. That rarity is the whole price premium: nothing about its care differs.

## Picking a Healthy Axolotl

The purchase price stops mattering the moment an axolotl comes home sick, and a few checks at the store prevent most of that.

- **Feel the water, or ask its temperature.** An axolotl kept above about 75°F turns sluggish, floats, and becomes open to bacterial and fungal infection, and one kept warm in a store tank carries that home.
- **Look at the gills.** Full, feathery gills held out to the sides are what you want. Gills curled forward are a stress sign, often from too much water current.
- **Check the skin.** White, cotton-like tufts are water mold, the most common fungal infection in the species. Red patches or open sores need a vet, not a purchase.
- **Watch it sit.** A healthy axolotl rests on the bottom. One floating at the surface, or with a swollen, fluid-filled belly, has a buoyancy or fluid problem.
- **Ask to see it eat.** Poor appetite is one of the first signs of illness, and a seller with a healthy animal can show it taking food.
- **Skip gravel-floored tanks.** An axolotl that has lived on pebbles may already have swallowed some.

A new axolotl still goes into its own tub for quarantine before it shares water with another one, however good it looked in the store.

<FunFact>
A chiller isn't an optional upgrade the way it might be for other tank setups, it's closer to a heater's opposite number. Most aquarium pets are kept warmer than room temperature, axolotls are one of the few common ones that need active cooling instead, which is exactly why this single item can double the price of an otherwise modest setup.

The chiller's electricity draw is the cost most first-time owners don't budget for, and it's the one that varies most by climate and season. If you live somewhere warm, running a chiller through summer months adds up in a way a simple filter-and-heater setup for most other aquatic pets wouldn't.

## Vet Costs

An axolotl can cost more to see than a reptile or bird, because some exotic hospitals book aquatic animals as a longer, two-part appointment. As a benchmark, one Colorado exotics hospital lists a standard wellness exam at $115, a medical exam at $135, and its aquatic animal exam, a 30 minute technician appointment followed by 30 minutes with the vet, at $235. After-hours care adds an emergency fee of $120 on top of a $200 emergency exam. Tests, medication and any surgery for a swallowed stone come after that, so keep a few hundred dollars set aside from the first week.

Not every exotic practice sees axolotls, and the time to find one that does is before the first emergency, since warm water or a swallowed stone rarely waits for business hours.

## The Budgeting Lesson

Axolotls need cool water, ideally 60 to 64°F, and most homes run warmer than that for a meaningful part of the year. If you can't reliably keep the water in range without a chiller, that's not an optional upgrade, it's part of the actual cost of keeping this species responsibly.
