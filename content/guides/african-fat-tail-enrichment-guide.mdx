# African Fat-Tailed Gecko Enrichment: The Closest Cousin Was Studied

<AffiliateDisclosure />

African fat-tailed geckos spend their days asleep under rocks, surface debris and abandoned burrows in the West African savanna, and their nights hunting insects on the ground. No enrichment study has been run on the species itself, so the evidence comes from two places: its closest studied relative, and one thermal-choice study that did use fat-tails.

<KeyTakeaway>
The leopard gecko study tested five enrichment types and ranked them: thermal and feeding at the top, mirrors and visual novelty at the bottom. Fat-tails are eublepharids, the same family, so that ranking is the best available steer. Where this species differs is humidity, which it needs more of, and heat choice, where its own females pick hotter spots than leopard geckos do. Keep one gecko per enclosure.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Leopard Gecko Research Showed

Bashaw and colleagues tested **five types of environmental enrichment** on [leopard geckos](/blog/leopard-gecko-enrichment-guide/) and found thermal and feeding enrichment produced the strongest responses, while **mirror-based visual enrichment was the weakest**.

A separate study of leopard geckos and crested geckos compared reactions to novel objects with fecal stress-hormone levels. Individuals that approached sooner and investigated longer had lower baseline levels, and no object feature (color, shape or smell) was preferred across animals. Novelty is worth offering, then, but which object a given gecko takes to is individual.

## The Humid Hide, Which Is Not Optional Here

Fat-tails need a persistently damp retreat as a standing feature, not a shedding aid set up now and then. Maintain it: a humid hide that has dried out is only a hide.

## Thermal Choice, the Top-Ranked Item

Three hides minimum: warm, cool and humid. The point is that the animal can be concealed **and** at its chosen temperature at the same time, without trading one for the other.

This is the one area with fat-tail data. In a 2000 study by Bragg, Fawcett, Bragg and Viets, gravid females given a temperature gradient chose nest sites averaging 32.4°C (about 90°F), while leopard geckos in the same study chose 28.7°C (about 84°F). A fat-tail uses the warm end of the range more deliberately than its cousin, so the warm hide has to reach the top of the gradient, not just sit near it.

## A Laying Box for Females

The same study is the case for a laying box. Female reptiles can form eggs with no male present, so every adult female needs somewhere to dig and lay, and fat-tail females are selective about temperature when they do it. A covered box of moist substrate deep enough to dig into, set over the warm end, gives her a site that matches what the gradient study says she looks for. Lack of a suitable nesting site is one of the recognized causes of egg-binding.

## Feeding as Hunting

Ranked second. Tong feeding with <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0FB3PRD2Y?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Short soft-tip feeding tongs, 4 pack">soft-tipped tongs</AffiliateLink>, short supervised insect hunts inside the enclosure, and varying when and where prey appears all beat a static bowl. A fat-tail stalking prey often raises its tail and waves it slowly, which is a good sign that the hunt is doing its job.

Feed in the evening. Fat-tails are nocturnal, and food offered in daylight gets a duller response from an animal that should be asleep.

## Floor Space Over Height

This is a ground species that shelters under rocks and in burrows. Cork flats, stacked slate and a deep patch of diggable substrate do more than any branch, and floor area plus a working hide setup is where the enclosure budget goes.

## One Gecko or Several

Keep one per enclosure. Both sexes are territorial, males fight over space and females, and two males must never be housed together. Female groups and one male with females are kept by breeders, but only as carefully monitored groups with room to separate, and a pair produces eggs the keeper then has to incubate or discard. A single gecko loses nothing: in the wild this species is solitary and rarely seen in groups, and none of the enrichment above depends on a tankmate.

## Priority Order

1. A properly maintained humid hide
2. Thermal choice with hides at both ends, and a warm end that reaches the top of the range
3. Feeding and hunting presentation
4. Floor space and diggable substrate
5. A laying box for any female
6. Novelty objects, last, since visual enrichment ranked bottom

## What Not to Do

Do not let the humid hide dry out. Do not raise humidity across the whole enclosure to compensate, since that risks respiratory infection. Do not bother with mirrors, which ranked last in the closest study available. Do not add a second gecko as enrichment, and never a second male.
