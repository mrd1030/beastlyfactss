# Common Argentine Tegu Health Issues

<VetDisclaimer />

Argentine tegus are hardy animals when housed properly, and the issues that do show up trace back to a fairly small set of preventable husbandry gaps, several of them tied directly to this species' large size.

## Metabolic Bone Disease (MBD)

Watch for weakness, decreased appetite, swollen joints or legs, tremors, and soft or fractured bones. Caused by inadequate UVB or insufficient calcium and vitamin D3. Always see a vet, and treat this as genuinely preventable, strong UVB and proper supplementation from the start make a real difference given how large and fast growing this species is. Our [tank setup guide](/blog/argentine-tegu-tank-setup-guide/) covers the UVB strength this species actually needs.

## Respiratory Infection

Look for lethargy, appetite loss, nasal discharge, and open mouth breathing. Early cases sometimes respond once temperature and humidity are corrected, but persistent symptoms need a vet for antibiotic treatment. Caused by an enclosure that's too cold, too damp, or poorly ventilated, harder to get right in a large enclosure than a small one, worth monitoring closely.

<FunFact>
Metabolic bone disease and respiratory infection are both driven by the same underlying problem, an enclosure that hasn't kept pace with how large and fast growing this species actually is. A setup sized correctly from the start prevents both rather than needing separate fixes later.
</FunFact>

## Obesity

Genuinely common in this species. Tegus are enthusiastic eaters, and continuing to feed them the fatty, rodent heavy diet appropriate for a fast growing juvenile well into adulthood is a common mistake. Manageable at home through proper diet correction and an appropriate feeding schedule for an adult's actual needs, and worth having a vet assess body condition periodically to confirm you're on track. This mirrors a pattern seen in other large lizards on this site, including the [savannah monitor](/blog/savannah-monitor-health-issues-guide/), where the same combination of husbandry gaps and overfeeding drives most of the health problems that show up.

## Parasites

Watch for weight loss, diarrhea, and skin issues, more common in imported or wild caught animals. See a vet for a fecal exam and treatment.

## Prolapse, Scale Rot, and Dehydration

Secondary issues that generally trace back to husbandry gaps elsewhere. Prolapse specifically is a genuine emergency requiring immediate veterinary attention, not something to wait out.

## The Husbandry Pattern Worth Remembering

Weak UVB and calcium cause MBD, especially consequential given how fast and large this species grows. Poor temperature or humidity control, harder to maintain consistently in a large enclosure, causes respiratory infection. And continuing a juvenile feeding schedule into adulthood causes the obesity that's genuinely common in this species. Getting the large scale setup right, covered in our [tank setup guide](/blog/argentine-tegu-tank-setup-guide/), and adjusting diet as your tegu matures both matter more here than with almost any smaller lizard on this site.
