# Sulcata Tortoise Enclosure Setup: Indoor Is Temporary, Outdoor Is the Goal

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Sulcata housing needs change dramatically as the animal grows, and the single most important thing to understand upfront is that indoor housing is a phase, not a permanent solution. Planning for that transition from day one makes everything else easier. For the full care picture, see our complete [Sulcata Tortoise care guide](/guides/sulcata-tortoise/).

<Figure src="/assets/images/sulcata-tortoise-tank-setup-guide-1.jpg" alt="Labeled diagram of an outdoor sulcata tortoise pen showing the heated shelter, buried escape-proof fencing, and real outdoor space" caption="Indoor housing is a phase, not a permanent solution, sulcatas grow into outdoor-only space within just a few years." shareable />

## Enclosure Size

A juvenile under 10 inches needs roughly 7 feet by 3.5 feet by 2.5 feet of indoor space, a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3Rkp3S6" product="Tortoise table">tortoise table</AffiliateLink> built to that scale works well at this stage. That sounds like a lot for a small tortoise, and it is, this species grows into its space fast. An adult housed indoors long-term would need an absolute minimum around 8 feet by 8 feet, and realistically, indoor-only housing simply doesn't work well for a full-grown sulcata. Outdoor space should provide at least 80 to 100 square feet per tortoise, ideally several hundred, our [Sulcata Tortoise cost guide](/blog/sulcata-tortoise-cost-guide/) breaks down what building that space actually runs.

A guppy, by contrast, thrives for its entire life in a properly cycled 10-gallon tank, see our [Guppy tank setup guide](/blog/guppy-tank-setup-guide/) for that far more modest ceiling. Almost nothing else in the pet trade scales its space requirement the way a sulcata does.

## Temperature

Basking area 95 to 105°F, ambient 80 to 90°F, cool side 75 to 85°F, never sustained below 70°F. Outdoor shelters need lightless heat, a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0002AQCQO?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Zoo Med Repticare Infrared Ceramic Heat Emitter (150 watt)">ceramic heat emitter or radiant panel</AffiliateLink>, if nighttime temperatures drop below 50°F, set to maintain 70 to 75°F. In regions with genuinely cold winters, the shelter needs to stay heated to at least 55 to 60°F to allow safe brumation, or winter housing needs to move indoors entirely for the season.

## Humidity

This is where indoor and outdoor housing diverge, and where age matters too. Adults do best in a dry, well-ventilated environment. Hatchlings and juveniles are a different story, they need humid hides, roughly 70% humidity, to support proper shell growth and reduce pyramiding risk. Wild sulcatas achieve this naturally by using humid burrow microclimates even in an otherwise arid habitat. Getting this wrong is the leading cause of the shell pyramiding covered in our [Sulcata Tortoise health issues guide](/blog/sulcata-tortoise-health-issues-guide/).

<FunFact>
Wild sulcata tortoises live in some of the hottest, driest habitat around, yet hatchlings and juveniles depend on humid underground burrows to grow a healthy shell. The dry, desert-dweller image most people picture is only half the story.
</FunFact>

## Substrate

A diggable mix works best: organic topsoil and play sand at roughly 60/40, coconut fiber, or cypress mulch. Depth matters, 6 inches for juveniles, up to 24 inches for adults who genuinely dig and burrow, expect the digging described in our [Sulcata Tortoise handling guide](/blog/sulcata-tortoise-handling-guide/) to be a daily fact of life. Keep it dry rather than wet, dampness is a direct path to shell rot. Quarantine any new tortoise before introducing it to an established, naturalistic substrate setup.

## Lighting

Essential for any indoor housing: a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0FF9GHCDC?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Zoo Med ReptiSun T5 HO Terrarium Hood with 10.0 Bulb 24in L">T5 HO UVB bulb</AffiliateLink> in the 10 to 12% range, or a mercury vapor bulb, run on a 10 to 14 hour photoperiod, paired with a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4bqzQAS" product="Basking bulb (standard, 75W)">basking bulb</AffiliateLink> to hit that 95 to 105°F basking target. Outdoors, natural sunlight provides free, genuinely superior UVB, one more reason outdoor access matters as soon as climate and size allow it.

## The Defining Requirement: Outdoor Space, on a Timeline

Sulcatas grow rapidly for their first 5 to 10 years, which means outdoor housing isn't a someday consideration, it becomes necessary within just a few years of bringing home a hatchling. <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4aCGvb1" product="Escape-proof outdoor fencing">Escape-proof fencing</AffiliateLink> buried at least 12 inches deep is non-negotiable, sulcatas are dedicated diggers. An opaque barrier 6 to 12 inches high around the enclosure perimeter helps stop pacing behavior along fence lines. If you can't provide meaningful outdoor space within that timeframe, this is genuinely the wrong species, not just a harder version of the right one.
