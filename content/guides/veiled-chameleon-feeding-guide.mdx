# Veiled Chameleon Feeding: Schedule, Diet, and the Supplement Schedule That Actually Matters

Veiled chameleon feeding is really a two-part job: what you feed matters, but how you supplement it matters just as much, this is genuinely one of the more supplement-dependent species on this site, and getting that schedule right is the difference between a healthy chameleon and one headed toward metabolic bone disease. For the rest of their care needs, see our complete [Veiled Chameleon care guide](/guides/veiled-chameleon/).

## The Staple Diet

This species is primarily insectivorous. Build the diet around a genuine variety, crickets, dubia roaches, black soldier fly larvae, superworms, silkworms, and locusts all work well as regular staples, rotate between several rather than relying on just one. Hornworms, waxworms, and butterworms are higher in fat or water content and belong in treat territory, no more than about twice a week. Unusually for a chameleon, veiled chameleons will also take some plant matter, leafy greens and small amounts of vegetables or fruit, though this stays a minor addition rather than a dietary requirement.

## Feeding Frequency and Portions

Size prey to the space between your chameleon's eyes, nothing bigger. Frequency drops as your chameleon matures:

- **Babies (1 to 3 months):** as much as they'll eat, roughly twice daily.
- **Juveniles (3 to 6 months):** around 10 to 12 small crickets daily.
- **Sub-adults (6 to 12 months):** 8 to 10 medium crickets daily or every other day.
- **Adults (12-plus months):** roughly 4 to 6 feeders every other day. Adult females specifically should be fed a somewhat restricted amount, overfeeding drives excessive egg production even without a male present.

## Foods to Avoid

Skip wild-caught insects entirely, pesticide and parasite risk are real. Fireflies are toxic and should never be fed. Avoid feeders too large for the eye-spacing rule above, and don't lean too heavily on any single feeder type, crickets alone or mealworms alone create real nutritional gaps over time, and mealworm exoskeletons are genuinely hard to digest in quantity. Skip iceberg lettuce and onions if offering plant matter, neither provides real nutritional value.

## Supplementation: The Part That Actually Matters Most

This is genuinely the core of keeping this species healthy.

## The Mistakes That Actually Matter

Inadequate calcium, D3, or UVB is the single biggest risk with this species, and it leads directly to metabolic bone disease, one of the most frequently observed pathological conditions in herpetoculture. The opposite mistake is real too: over-supplementing vitamin A or D3 can cause throat swelling (gular edema) and organ stress, more isn't automatically safer here.

<FunFact>
Overfeeding females specifically drives excessive, frequent egg-laying that depletes calcium reserves and raises the risk of egg-binding, a genuine emergency. ## A Hydration Note Worth Knowing

Veiled chameleons generally don't recognize still water sitting in a bowl as something to drink, they respond to movement. Hydration comes from a dripper system and regular misting of leaves and branches, not a water dish, covered in more detail in our [chameleon hydration guide](/blog/chameleon-hydration-drippers-misters-fogging/).
