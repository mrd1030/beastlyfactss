# Neon Tetra Enrichment: Someone Counted, and the Number Is Ten

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Fish keeping runs on received numbers, and this is one of the few that got tested properly. For everything else about them, see our complete [Neon Tetra care guide](/guides/neon-tetra/).

<KeyTakeaway>
Saxby and colleagues measured darting, aggression, shoaling and latency to feed across group sizes in four common aquarium species. For neon tetras, **groups of ten showed improved welfare compared with smaller groups**. The standard advice is six. Six is the floor.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Research Actually Says

Saxby, Adams, Snellgrove, Wilson and Sloman published **The effect of group size on the behaviour and welfare of four fish species commonly kept in home aquaria** in *Applied Animal Behaviour Science* in 2010. The four species were **neon tetras, white cloud mountain minnows, angelfish and tiger barbs**.

They tracked **darting, aggression, shoaling and latency to feed**, and combined the behavioural measures in a principal components analysis to identify group sizes associated with better welfare.

**Groups of ten neon tetras and white cloud mountain minnows showed improved welfare** compared with smaller groups. Aggression and darting were lowest in the larger groups, and **latency to feed decreased as group size increased**.

The effect was **species-specific**. The same study found **no evidence of a group size effect on angelfish welfare**, which our [Angelfish enrichment guide](/blog/angelfish-enrichment-guide/) covers, and which is a useful reminder that shoal-size advice does not generalise across every fish in the shop.

<FunFact>
Latency to feed is the measure that translates best to a living room. You do not need equipment to notice whether your fish come out to eat straight away or hang at the back for a minute first, and the study says that gap is telling you something.
</FunFact>

## Ten, Not Six

The headline. If the tank supports it, buy ten rather than six, and prefer one species of ten to two species of five.

That last point matters. Neon tetras shoal with neon tetras, and a mixed group of similar-looking fish does not give an individual the same thing. The study looked at single-species groups for a reason.

Our [tank setup guide](/blog/neon-tetra-tank-setup-guide/) covers the tank size that ten fish actually need, which is more than the ten gallon they are usually sold into.

## Planting and Cover

Neon tetras come from shaded blackwater streams thick with vegetation and leaf litter. Bright, open, bare tanks are the opposite of that, and the fish respond by staying pale and hugging the edges.

Dense <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4grZYPq" product="Live terrarium plants">live planting</AffiliateLink>, especially around the sides and back, plus <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eR0Z2l" product="Driftwood">driftwood</AffiliateLink> and leaf litter, gives cover and tints the water the way their habitat does.

Leave open swimming space through the middle. A shoal needs somewhere to move as a group as well as somewhere to hide, and the combination is what gets you fish that colour up and use the whole tank.

## Water Stability

The reason most neon tetras die, and it happens before any of the above matters. They are sold as beginner fish and are genuinely sensitive to poor water and sudden changes.

Cycle the tank fully before they go in. Small regular water changes rather than large occasional ones. Our [health issues guide](/blog/neon-tetra-health-issues-guide/) covers neon tetra disease, which is untreatable and spreads, so quarantine matters more with this species than most.

## Feeding a Shoal

Small mouths and a tendency to lose out to faster tankmates. <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B004PBB5TK?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Hikari Tropical Semi-Floating Micro Pellets, 0.77 oz">Micro pellets</AffiliateLink> sized for the fish, and other sizes are available.

Spread food across the surface rather than dropping it in one spot, so the whole shoal eats rather than the boldest three. Vary it with frozen and live foods, which gets a visibly stronger response and is the closest thing to hunting available here. Our [feeding guide](/blog/neon-tetra-feeding-guide/) covers the diet.

## Dim Light, or Shade It

Blackwater fish under a bright aquarium light spend their time hiding. Floating plants, a tinted tannin-stained water column from wood and leaves, or simply a less powerful light all help.

The visible result is colour. A neon tetra that feels covered is a brighter fish than one that feels exposed, which makes this the rare welfare improvement you can see immediately.

## Priority Order

1. Ten fish of one species, not six, and not two groups of five
2. A cycled, stable tank before they arrive
3. Dense planting and cover with open swimming space through the middle
4. Subdued lighting, or shade from floating plants
5. Food spread across the surface so the whole shoal eats
6. Varied diet including frozen or live foods
7. Wood and leaf litter for tannins

## What Not to Do

Do not stop at six because a care sheet said six. Do not split the budget across two small groups of different species. Do not add them to an uncycled tank. Do not run a bright light over an open bare tank and then wonder why the fish are pale. Do not skip quarantine, given neon tetra disease. And see our [handling guide](/blog/neon-tetra-handling-guide/) before netting them, since chasing a shoal around a tank undoes a lot of the work above.
