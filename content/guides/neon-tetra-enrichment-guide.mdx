# Neon Tetra Enrichment: Someone Counted, and the Number Is Ten

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Neon tetras shoal in groups, and the size of the group changes how each fish behaves.

<KeyTakeaway>
In a 2010 home aquarium study, groups of ten neon tetras showed improved welfare compared with smaller groups. Six is the floor, and **ten** is the number to aim for.
</KeyTakeaway>

## Group Size

Saxby, Adams, Snellgrove, Wilson and Sloman measured darting, aggression, shoaling and latency to feed in neon tetras, white cloud mountain minnows, angelfish and tiger barbs, and published the results in *Applied Animal Behaviour Science* in 2010. A principal components analysis of those measures picked out the group sizes linked to better welfare.

Groups of ten neon tetras and white cloud mountain minnows showed improved welfare compared with smaller groups. Aggression and darting were lowest in the larger groups, and latency to feed shortened as group size rose.

The effect was species-specific. The same study found no group size effect on [angelfish](/blog/angelfish-enrichment-guide/) welfare, so shoal-size advice does not carry across every fish in the shop.

<FunFact>
Latency to feed is the easiest of these measures to check at home. Fish that come out to eat straight away are doing better than fish that hang at the back for a minute first, and in the study that wait shortened as the groups grew.
</FunFact>

## Ten Fish of One Species

If the tank supports it, buy ten rather than six, and prefer one species of ten to two species of five.

Neon tetras shoal with neon tetras, and a mixed group of similar-looking fish does not give an individual the same thing. The study tested single-species groups.

## Planting and Cover

Neon tetras come from shaded blackwater streams thick with vegetation and leaf litter. Bright, open, bare tanks are the opposite of that, and the fish respond by staying pale and hugging the edges.

Dense <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B008HXF16K?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="SubstrateSource Anubias Nana Live Aquarium Plant, 1 Pot">live planting</AffiliateLink>, especially around the sides and back, plus <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eR0Z2l" product="Driftwood">driftwood</AffiliateLink> and leaf litter, gives cover and tints the water the way their habitat does.

Leave open swimming space through the middle. A shoal needs somewhere to move as a group as well as somewhere to hide, and the combination gets fish that color up and use the whole tank.

## Water Stability

An unstable tank kills more neon tetras than anything else, and it happens before any of the above matters. They are sold as beginner fish and are sensitive to poor water and sudden changes.

[Cycle the tank fully](/blog/aquarium-cycling-guide/) before they go in. Small regular water changes suit them; large occasional ones do not.

## Feeding a Shoal

Small mouths and a tendency to lose out to faster tankmates. <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B004PBB5TK?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Hikari Tropical Semi-Floating Micro Pellets, 0.77 oz">Micro pellets</AffiliateLink> sized for the fish.

Spread food across the surface instead of dropping it in one spot, so the whole shoal eats and the boldest three do not take it all. Vary it with frozen and live foods, which gets a visibly stronger response and is the closest thing to hunting available here.

## Dim Light, or Shade It

Blackwater fish under a bright aquarium light spend their time hiding. Floating plants, a tinted tannin-stained water column from wood and leaves, or simply a less powerful light all help.

The visible result is color. A neon tetra that feels covered is a brighter fish than one that feels exposed, which makes this the rare welfare improvement you can see immediately.

## Priority Order

1. Ten fish of one species, in a single group
2. A cycled, stable tank before they arrive
3. Dense planting and cover with open swimming space through the middle
4. Subdued lighting, or shade from floating plants
5. Food spread across the surface so the whole shoal eats
6. Varied diet including frozen or live foods
7. Wood and leaf litter for tannins

## What Not to Do

Do not stop at six. Do not split the budget across two small groups of different species. Do not add them to an uncycled tank. Do not run a bright light over an open bare tank and then wonder why the fish are pale. Do not skip quarantine, given neon tetra disease. And net them carefully, since chasing a shoal around a tank undoes a lot of the work above.
