# Labrador Enrichment: A Gene Deletion Explains the Appetite

<AffiliateDisclosure />

The joke about Labradors and food turns out to have a specific molecular basis, and the research also tells you what to do about it.

<KeyTakeaway>
A **14 base pair deletion in the POMC gene** is associated with greater food motivation, higher body weight and more body fat in Labradors, with each copy worth about **0.33 standard deviations of weight**. The useful part: the deletion is **more common in assistance dogs than in pets**, and those dogs stay lean, because they are active. Genetics set the appetite. Management sets the weight.
</KeyTakeaway>

For the fundamentals, see our general [dog enrichment guide](/blog/dog-enrichment-guide/).

## What the Research Says

Raffan and colleagues at the University of Cambridge published **A deletion in the canine POMC gene is associated with weight and appetite in obesity-prone Labrador retriever dogs** in *Cell Metabolism* in 2016.

**The finding.** A 14 base pair deletion in POMC, at an allele frequency of around **12 percent** in Labradors, disrupting the beta-MSH and beta-endorphin coding sequences, and associated with **body weight, adiposity and greater food motivation**.

**The size.** Each deletion allele confers an increase of about **0.33 standard deviations** in weight, an effect size the authors note is **over three times** the per-allele effect at the FTO locus in humans.

**The other breed.** Among other breeds tested, the deletion was found only in the closely related **flat-coated retriever**, where it has the same association.

**The part that matters most.** The mutation is **significantly more common in Labradors selected to become assistance dogs** than in pets. In those dogs the mutation increased food motivation, and they **maintained a healthy body weight** because of their regular physical activity.

<FunFact>
That assistance dog result is not an accident of sampling. A dog that will work relentlessly for food is easier to train, so selection for trainability quietly selected for the appetite gene. The same variant that makes a pet Labrador fat makes a working one exceptional.
</FunFact>

## Use the Food Motivation

The reframe. A dog that will do almost anything for a piece of kibble is the easiest animal on this site to enrich with food.

The rule that makes it work: **out of the ration, not on top of it**. Weigh the food. A Labrador with the deletion is not going to self-regulate, and expecting it to is the failure the study explains.

## Activity, Which Is the Other Half

The assistance dog finding is the argument. Those dogs carry the variant more often and stay lean because they are working regularly.

Daily, consistent, real activity. Not an escalating arms race the way it is for a border collie, just a genuine routine. Our guide to [why your dog needs daily exercise](/blog/why-your-dog-needs-daily-exercise/) covers the baseline, and our guide to [pet obesity and body condition](/blog/pet-obesity-body-condition-guide/) covers how to score whether it is working.

Body condition score beats the scales for this. Learn to feel the ribs.

## Retrieving, Combined With Scent

Labradors retrieve enthusiastically and it is a good outlet. It gets considerably better when the retrieve has to be **found** first.

Hide the item, send the dog, let it search. That combines the breed's retrieving drive with the nosework finding covered in our [dog enrichment guide](/blog/dog-enrichment-guide/), where scent work made dogs measurably more optimistic and heelwork did not.

Twenty minutes of that beats twenty ball throws, and it is easier on the joints.

## Water

Most Labradors swim readily and love it, and swimming is genuinely useful here: high effort, low joint load, and it suits an overweight or arthritic dog that cannot run.

Rinse afterwards and dry the ears, since ear infections are common in this breed and water makes them likelier.

## Joints, Alongside the Weight

Labradors are prone to hip and elbow dysplasia, covered in our guide to [hip and elbow dysplasia](/blog/hip-and-elbow-dysplasia-guide/). Excess weight makes both worse.

That is why the feeding section and the exercise section are the same problem. Enrichment that keeps a Labrador lean is joint management.

## Chewing

Strong chewers that need durable outlets, and chewing is calming. Match the chew to the dog and supervise, since Labradors swallow things, and foreign body surgery is one of the more common emergency presentations in the breed.

## Priority Order

1. Weighed food, delivered through puzzles, scatter feeding and scent work
2. Consistent daily activity, since that is what keeps the carriers lean
3. Retrieves that have to be found first
4. Swimming where available
5. Body condition scoring rather than trusting the eye
6. Durable, supervised chewing
7. Joint-aware exercise as the dog ages

## What Not to Do

Do not free-feed a Labrador. Do not add enrichment food on top of meals. Do not treat the appetite as a training failure, since the research says otherwise. Do not rely on the dog to stop eating. Do not build the whole exercise routine on repetitive ball throwing. And do not leave swallowable items out with a breed this prone to eating them.
