# How Much Does an African Fat-Tailed Gecko Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

African fat-tailed geckos cost more than most beginner geckos, and morphs push that number considerably higher.

## The Animal Itself: $75 to $600, Averaging $100 to $200 for Normals

Normal, wild-type patterned animals are the cheapest, commonly around $100. Morphs change the price substantially: mid-range combinations like Amelanistic or single-gene Oreo animals often run $200 to $400, while rare or high-end combos, Whiteout, Zulu, or stacked multi-gene animals, can reach $600 to $1,000 or more, occasionally higher for truly rare lines.

<FunFact>
The "fat tail" isn't just a name, it's a real energy reserve. Like a leopard gecko, this species stores fat in its tail and can draw on it during lean periods, which is part of why a healthy fat-tail's tail should always look plump rather than thin.
</FunFact>

## Vet Costs

A routine exam commonly runs $35 to $105, plus a fecal test around $65 to $110. Illness or emergency visits with diagnostics typically run $150 to $300 or higher, with surgical intervention, for example egg-binding, costing more still.

## Lifespan: A Long Commitment

15 to 20 years is the commonly cited range, and the best-documented figure, a maximum recorded captive lifespan in the AnAge longevity database, is just over 16 years. Either way, it's a long-term commitment, well beyond many other beginner reptiles, including the leopard gecko this species is constantly compared to.

If you're weighing the two, the [leopard gecko cost guide](/blog/leopard-gecko-cost-guide/) is the side-by-side: same heat mat, same feeders, a few dollars apart on the animal. Among the other easygoing geckos, the [gargoyle](/blog/gargoyle-gecko-cost-guide/) skips the heating bill and the [mourning gecko](/blog/mourning-gecko-cost-guide/) skips the feeder bill, which is where the real differences show up.
