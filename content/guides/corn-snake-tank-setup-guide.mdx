# Corn Snake Enclosure Setup: Floor Space, a Mandatory Thermostat, and a Humidity Debate

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Corn snakes are a forgiving species to set up correctly, with one non-negotiable exception: whatever heat source you choose, it needs a thermostat. Everything else here has some flexibility.

<Figure src="/assets/images/corn-snake-tank-setup-guide-1.jpg" alt="Labeled diagram of a corn snake enclosure showing the heat mat and thermostat, two hides, and a soakable water bowl" caption="Corn snakes are a forgiving species to set up correctly, with one non-negotiable exception: the heat source needs a thermostat." shareable />

## Enclosure Size and Orientation

Corn snakes are terrestrial, so floor space matters more than height, though they do appreciate some climbing opportunity. Adult minimum is a 40-gallon breeder (36x18x18 inches), with 4x2x2 feet (48x24x24 inches) as the preferred size for a fully grown adult. Front-opening enclosures are generally preferred over glass aquariums, since they lock securely and hold heat better, and security matters for this species. House corn snakes singly.

**Security is not optional.** Corn snakes are notorious escape artists, and a loose lid or a gap at the corner of a screen top is often all it takes. Confirm the enclosure locks or latches securely before your snake ever goes in.

**Every heat source must run through a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/44hAxJ1" product="Under-tank heat mat + thermostat kit">thermostat</AffiliateLink>.** This is the one rule on this list with zero exceptions. An unregulated heat mat can get hot enough to cause a serious burn, which is what [a reptile thermostat](/blog/reptile-heating-thermostats-guide/) exists to prevent. When you set up your thermostat, place the probe on the warm-side surface where your snake actually lies, so it reads what the snake feels.

## Humidity: A Genuine Disagreement Worth Knowing About

This is one area where sources don't fully agree. Some specialist husbandry guides recommend a higher baseline around 65 to 75%, arguing that wild corn snake habitats run more humid than conventional reptile-keeping wisdom assumes. Many other sources and vets cite a lower 40 to 60% baseline as sufficient, raised to around 70% during shedding.

In practice, either range can work well. What matters more is consistency and providing a dedicated humid hide with damp <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4bpnvNm" product="Sphagnum moss (for moist hide)">sphagnum moss</AffiliateLink> so your snake can access higher humidity during a shed regardless of what the rest of the enclosure reads. Measure with a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3QHrM81" product="Digital thermometer/hygrometer combo">digital hygrometer</AffiliateLink> with its probe in the middle of the enclosure, where it reads the average your snake lives in.

<FunFact>
This humidity disagreement is a good reminder that reptile husbandry advice keeps evolving as more field data comes in. Wild corn snake habitat surveys have pushed some specialist keepers toward higher humidity numbers than the classic care-sheet range, even though plenty of corn snakes have thrived for decades under the older, drier guidance too.
</FunFact>

## UVB

Not required for survival, corn snakes have been kept successfully for decades without it, but it's increasingly viewed as a beneficial addition for long-term health. If you add it, use a 5.0 or 6% T5 HO bulb over the warm side, targeting a maximum UVI of 2.0 to 3.0 in the basking area, and replace it yearly. Provide 12 hours of light a day either way.

## Substrate

<AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3TkxAVF" product="Aspen shavings substrate">Aspen shavings</AffiliateLink> are the classic choice, they dry quickly and suit a lower-humidity setup well. Coconut fiber, cypress mulch, or a soil-based mix work better if you're running toward the higher end of the humidity range. Whatever you choose, keep it at least 3 inches deep to allow natural burrowing behavior. Avoid pine or cedar entirely, both can damage a reptile's airways, and skip loose bark chips, which can cause impaction if swallowed, and gravel, which absorbs nothing. Remove waste as soon as you see it and do a full substrate change every 3 to 4 months.

## Furnishings

- At least two hides, one on the warm side, one on the cool side
- A sturdy <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eKyN0W" product="Large soak-able water dish">water bowl large enough to soak in</AffiliateLink>, changed daily
- Branches or cork bark for enrichment and light climbing
- Never a hot rock, they frequently cause burns and are simply not worth the danger

Corn snakes eat frozen-thawed rodents sized to roughly the width of the snake at its thickest point; the [feeding guide](/blog/corn-snake-feeding-guide/) has the exact schedule by age and weight.
