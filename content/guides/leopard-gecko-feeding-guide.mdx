# Leopard Gecko Feeding: Schedule, Diet, and Why They Stop Eating

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Easy eaters most of the time. A refusal stands out. Here is the routine.

## Feeding schedule by age

- **Hatchlings and young geckos:** daily. Insects around a quarter inch.
- **Juveniles:** daily or every other day. Slightly larger prey.
- **Adults (tail as thick as the neck):** every 3 days. If the tail looks thin, every other day. If it is fat and the gecko is slowing down, stretch toward every 5 days.

A few insects at a time, or what it clears in about 15 minutes. Nothing bigger than the space between the eyes. Feed in the evening.

## What leopard geckos eat

Insects only. No fruit. No vegetable puree. Commercially raised crickets as the staple, plus dubia, discoid roaches, mealworms, superworms, and black soldier fly larvae. Captive-bred hornworms are safe. Wild hornworms are not. Buy from a supplier, not the yard.

Gut-load 24 to 48 hours on vegetables or gut-load powder. Dust with plain calcium at most feedings for juveniles and breeding females, 2 to 3 times a week for adults. ## Safe treats and foods to avoid

Waxworms and superworms occasionally. Waxworms are fatty: a couple of times a week at most. Superworms are adults-only.

Never fireflies. Never wild-caught insects. Never fruit or vegetables in the bowl.

## Why leopard geckos stop eating

1. **Brumation.** Cooler days, shorter light. Less activity, less appetite, brief waking to drink.
2. **Shedding.** A day or two before and sometimes after.
3. **A warm side that is too cool.** They cannot digest without roughly [88 to 92F on the warm side](/blog/leopard-gecko-temperature-guide/) and 70 to 77F on the cool side.
4. **Stress.** A new tank, too much handling, a tankmate.
5. **Breeding season.** Both sexes may eat less. Gravid females often do near laying.
6. **Prey boredom.** Switch the insect.
7. **Illness or impaction.** Bloating, straining, loose stools, bad sheds, shrunken eyes. The [health issues guide](/blog/leopard-gecko-health-issues-guide/) is that page.

## When to worry

Watch the tail. A healthy adult with a plump tail can miss a couple of weeks. A tail that thins over those weeks is the line, not a day count.

See a reptile vet if refusal comes with a thinning tail, lethargy beyond a winter slowdown, bloating, straining, discolored skin, a stuck shed, sunken eyes, or wrinkled skin. Weigh every couple of weeks during a suspected brumation. They should not lose real mass while eating less.

Insects are only as good as what they ate. The [gut-loading guide](/blog/gut-loading-feeder-insects-guide/) is the other half of this diet.
