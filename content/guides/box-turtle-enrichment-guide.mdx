# Box Turtle Enrichment: Substrate and a Hide Box Changed Their Bloodwork

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Reptile enrichment evidence is usually behavioral, which leaves room to argue about interpretation. The box turtle study measured something in the blood, and that is harder to wave away.

<KeyTakeaway>
Thirty-eight eastern box turtles were randomized to barren enclosures (flat newspaper) or enriched ones (cypress mulch, shredded paper, a hide box) for a month. The enriched group finished with a significantly lower heterophil to lymphocyte ratio, a physiological stress measure, and spent significantly less time trying to escape.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Box Turtle Study Found

Case, Lewbart and Doerr examined the physiological and behavioral impact of, and preference for, an enriched environment in eastern box turtles (*Terrapene carolina carolina*).

Thirty-eight turtles were randomized for one month. The barren condition was **flat newspaper substrate**. The enriched condition was **cypress mulch substrate, shredded paper and a hide box**. At the end, turtles in enriched enclosures had a **significantly lower heterophil to lymphocyte ratio**, and enriched-housed turtles **spent significantly less time engaged in escape behavior**.

<FunFact>
Escape behavior is the measure worth carrying into your own keeping. A turtle pushing at the glass and climbing the corners reads as an active, curious animal. In this study it was a number that went down when the enclosure improved.
</FunFact>

The enrichment was not elaborate. Mulch instead of newspaper, some shredded paper, a box to get under. That is the entire intervention, and it moved both a behavioral measure and a physiological one.

## Substrate That Can Be Pushed Through

The single biggest difference between the two conditions was the floor. Box turtles burrow, wedge themselves under things and push through leaf litter, and newspaper removes all of it.

## Somewhere to Get Under

The enriched enclosures had a hide box, and it is worth taking literally. Multiple <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00SLM5GWQ?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Exo Terra Reptile Cave XL">large hides</AffiliateLink>, plus cork and dense planting, mean a turtle can be concealed anywhere in the enclosure rather than only at one end.

Cover and substrate work together here: a turtle that can wedge under a log and then bury into mulch has two ways to feel unexposed, which is what the study's enriched side offered and the newspaper side did not.

## Browse, Scattered

Box turtles are omnivorous foragers that cover ground looking for food. A single dish compresses a day's activity into a couple of minutes.

Scatter a varied diet across the substrate and under cover. Vary what appears and where. Food-based enrichment was what raised activity in this species' relatives, and it is the cheapest thing on this list.

## Space and Outdoor Time

Escape behavior dropping tells you something about how the animal experiences its boundaries. Floor area matters for a species that walks, and a secure outdoor enclosure in suitable weather is the highest-quality enrichment available for a box turtle: real substrate, real weather, real UV and genuinely varied terrain.

## Priority Order

1. Deep substrate that can be burrowed and pushed through
2. Multiple hides and cover throughout
3. Floor space, and outdoor time where climate and security allow
4. Scattered, varied browse
5. Humidity gradient with damp and dry zones
6. Target training and food puzzles
7. Novel objects, last

## What Not to Do

Do not keep a box turtle on newspaper or bare liner, since that is exactly the condition that produced the higher stress ratio. Do not read perimeter pacing as exploration. Do not use a shallow scattering of substrate and call it deep, because the behavior depends on being able to get under it.
