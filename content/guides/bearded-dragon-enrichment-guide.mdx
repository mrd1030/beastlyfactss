# Bearded Dragon Enrichment: Climbing, Digging, and Safe Free-Roam

Enrichment means giving a bearded dragon real opportunities to do what a bearded dragon actually does: climb, dig, thermoregulate by choice, hunt, and explore. It's a genuine part of good care, but it comes after the basics, not instead of them. For everything enrichment doesn't cover, see our complete [Bearded Dragon care guide](/guides/bearded-dragon/).

<KeyTakeaway>
Enrichment never replaces correct heat, UVB, diet, hydration, or a properly sized enclosure (4x2x2 ft / 120x60x60 cm is the adult minimum). If any of those are still wrong, fix them first, no amount of climbing structure or free-roam time makes up for the fundamentals.
</KeyTakeaway>

## Climbing and Structure

Sturdy branches, cork, ledges, and a basking platform at more than one height give a dragon something to survey from and somewhere to warm up properly. <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3SKmfOx" product="Cork bark round hide">Cork bark</AffiliateLink> and <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3QQNV3L" product="Climbing branch (Mopani wood)">sturdy climbing branches</AffiliateLink> both work well here, and rough textures help with grip and natural nail wear. Anchor everything so nothing can shift or collapse under an adult dragon's full weight, and keep hides on both the warm and cool ends so the dragon never has to choose between feeling secure and being at the right temperature.

If reflections or heavy foot traffic near the tank seem to be causing stress, covering two or three sides cuts that down. Rotating the layout occasionally is fine, constantly rearranging everything is not, a dragon still benefits from a space it recognizes.

## Dig Boxes

A dig box is genuinely useful enrichment for most dragons, and it's closer to a real husbandry requirement for gravid females who need somewhere to dig a lay site. Use a container large enough for the dragon to turn around in and actually push substrate, filled with a blend that holds its shape, organic topsoil mixed with washed play sand, often close to a 50/50 blend, kept slightly damp for laying so tunnels don't collapse.

<KeyTakeaway>
Depth depends on purpose. General enrichment digging works with around 4 to 6-plus inches of substrate. A gravid female preparing to lay wants considerably more, commonly 6 to 12-plus inches of cohesive, diggable material. Never use calcium sand for this, it's a real impaction risk dressed up as an enrichment product.
</KeyTakeaway>

If your dragon's main floor is paper towel or tile, which we recommend, a separate dig box still lets digging happen without giving up a safe primary substrate. Keepers who want digging available across the whole enclosure rather than in one box sometimes move to a [bioactive setup](/blog/bioactive-setups-bearded-dragons/) instead, which trades easy monitoring for a deep, diggable substrate that maintains itself.

## Foraging and Hunting

Tong-feeding and short, supervised live-insect hunts inside the enclosure both tap into real hunting behavior. Scattering greens instead of only presenting them in a bowl, or setting up a simple forage tray or hung greens the dragon has to work for, adds a little more of the same. Gut-load feeders and size prey correctly regardless of how it's presented, and don't leave a large number of uneaten insects in the tank overnight. Never use wild-caught insects, the pesticide and parasite risk isn't worth it. Our [Bearded Dragon feeding guide](/blog/bearded-dragon-feeding-guide/) covers the full diet and supplement schedule this connects to.

## Supervised Free-Roam

<FunFact>
Free-roam time outside the enclosure is enrichment, not a substitute for a proper tank. A dragon that gets regular house time still needs the correctly sized enclosure covered in our [tank setup guide](/blog/bearded-dragon-tank-setup-guide/) waiting for it afterward.
</FunFact>

Keep sessions short and supervised, often starting around 10 to 15 minutes and building toward 15 to 30 minutes for some adults, in a warm room with no drafts. Block gaps under furniture, remove other pets, cords, toxic plants, and anything small enough to be swallowed. End the session immediately if the dragon starts to cool down, panics, darkens its beard heavily, or turns lethargic. Never allow unsupervised house roaming.

Outdoor time is optional and more advanced: supervised only, ideally in a secure pen rather than an open yard, with shade and water always available. Never leave a glass enclosure sitting in direct sun, even briefly, it heats up far faster than the open air around it.

## Priority Order

1. Correct heat, UVB, and enclosure size, these come before any of the below
2. Climbing structure plus hides on both the warm and cool ends
3. A dig opportunity, general or gravid-depth depending on the dragon
4. Foraging and hunting presentation
5. Supervised free-roam time

## What Not to Do

Unstable, stacked rock piles are a real crush-injury risk if a dragon climbs on one that shifts. Calcium sand, walnut shell, and other impaction-risk substrates are sometimes marketed as enrichment, they aren't safe regardless of the label. Don't let climbing decor or clutter block the thermal gradient itself, the gradient is the actual foundation everything else sits on top of. Free-roam time doesn't excuse an undersized tank, and peeling stuck shed isn't a form of handling-as-enrichment, it's a health issue covered separately in our [health issues guide](/blog/bearded-dragon-health-issues-guide/). And skip cohabitation as "social enrichment" entirely, bearded dragons are solitary and do best housed alone.
