# How to Handle a Fire Skink (This Isn't the Blue-Tongued Skink)

Fire skinks and blue-tongued skinks share a family name and not much else temperamentally. If you've kept the docile, handleable blue-tongued skink, expect something genuinely different here.

## Shy, fast, and quick to hide, not aggressive

Fire skinks aren't defensive or bitey in any meaningful sense, but they are notably skittish, fast-moving, and prone to burrowing out of sight the moment they feel exposed. Wild-caught animals especially stay flighty, while captive-bred individuals tame more readily with patient, gradual handling. Even settled fire skinks tend to stay squirmy and quick rather than becoming the sit-still, seemingly-enjoys-it handling experience a [blue-tongued skink](/blog/blue-tongue-skink-handling-guide/) can offer.

## The handling risks are minor but real

An occasional hard bite if cornered, and like many lizards, [tail autotomy](/blog/fire-skink-health-issues-guide/) under grasping pressure. Never grab or restrain by the tail. Given how fast and squirmy this species is, handle low over a soft surface as a sensible precaution against an accidental drop or escape.

<FunFact>
Give a fire skink an inch or two of substrate and it vanishes into a corner under a hide. Give it the 4 to 6 inches of a soil-and-fiber mix it can actually tunnel through, and the same animal builds a burrow system, comes out to bask, and behaves like a completely different lizard, substrate depth changes this species' visible behavior more than almost any other single factor.
</FunFact>

## First-timer mistakes

- Buying a fire skink expecting the same docile, cuddly relationship the blue-tongued skink offers, this is genuinely a different kind of pet.
- Under-furnishing the enclosure. A bare tank leaves this naturally shy species perpetually hidden and stressed, exactly the wrong setup for a burrowing animal that needs to feel secure before it'll ever be visible or calm.
- Keeping substrate too dry, which compounds the skittishness and creates real health risk.
- Grabbing the tail.

## The honest comparison

Both are skinks, and that's about where the similarity ends. Blue-tongued skinks are larger, drier-climate, omnivorous, and personable, tolerant of and often seeming to enjoy interaction. Fire skinks are smaller, humidity-loving, insectivorous, and fossorial, closer to a beautiful display animal you occasionally get to interact with than a lizard built for regular handling. Go in expecting the latter, and this is a genuinely rewarding species to keep.
