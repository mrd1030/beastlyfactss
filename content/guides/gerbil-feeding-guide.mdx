# Gerbil Feeding: Schedule, Diet, and Why Seed Mixes Fail This Species

<AffiliateDisclosure />

A sunflower-seed-heavy mix looks like a complete gerbil diet on the label, but a gerbil will happily pick out the fatty seeds and leave the balanced parts behind, which is exactly how a seemingly complete diet quietly causes obesity.

## The Staple Diet

A nutritionally complete, uniform pelleted diet or lab block formulated for gerbils, or a low-fat rodent formula, is the best base because it prevents the selective feeding a loose mix invites. Aim for 18 to 20% protein and keep fat at or under about 4%: past that, gerbils develop raised blood cholesterol, more pronounced in males. The 14% minimum often quoted for gerbils is a floor, not a target, and many products labeled simply "Hamster and Gerbil Food" miss even that, since they're really formulated for hamsters. Check the guaranteed analysis panel on the bag, including on a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B06XY8RHVB?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Oxbow Garden Select Hamster & Gerbil Food">premium hamster and gerbil food</AffiliateLink>. Supplement with small amounts of fresh vegetables like carrot, broccoli, kale, or squash, and occasional animal protein such as a few mealworms a week or a bit of hard-boiled egg.

## Feeding Frequency and Portions

Feed once daily, roughly 5 to 8 grams, about a tablespoon, of pellets per adult gerbil. Offer fresh vegetables every other day or a few times weekly, and treat fruit as an occasional weekly indulgence given the sugar content. Treats and supplemental foods together should stay under about 10% of the diet.

## Where the Food Goes: On the Bedding, Not in a Bowl

A food bowl in a gerbil tank mostly ends up full of bedding. Scatter the daily ration straight onto the surface instead. Gerbils dig for food by instinct, so scattering turns a meal into foraging, and spreading it out also heads off squabbles over a single pile.

The catch is hoarding. Wild gerbils can stash up to 1.5 kilograms of grain in one store, and a pet pair will cache its share around the tank. Dry pellets keep, but fresh vegetables do not, so check the tank daily and lift out anything stale.

Water is constant, from a bottle with a metal sipper tube. Check it every day for leaks or a blocked nozzle, and wash the bottle and nozzle at each refill.

The same daily check doubles as a health check. Note roughly how much each gerbil eats and drinks. A change from its normal level, droppings that turn moist, or a soiled rear end all call for a vet, and diarrhea in a gerbil is a same-day visit.

<KeyTakeaway>
Young gerbils under 6 months and pregnant or nursing females need more calories and protein than a maintenance adult, some commercial diets are formulated to be fed without the usual portion cap during those life stages.
</KeyTakeaway>

## Foods to Avoid

Sunflower-seed-heavy and muesli-style mixes as a staple are the central risk, excessive amounts of fatty seeds like sunflower drive the obesity described above. Toxic foods to keep away entirely: grapes and raisins, rhubarb, chocolate, onion and garlic, avocado, raw potato, raw kidney beans, and apple seeds, plus salty, sugary, or processed human food and dairy. Citrus sits a step below that: it is acidic enough to be worth rationing, and oranges still count among the fruits that can supplement a pelleted diet. Watery lettuce can cause diarrhea, and any hoarded fresh food should be removed before it rots in a cache.

## Supplementation

None needed with a complete pelleted diet. Occasional insect protein and fresh produce provide variety rather than correct a deficiency. What matters more day to day is constant fresh water and safe wood to gnaw, since gerbil teeth grow continuously and [overgrowth is its own problem](/blog/gerbil-health-issues-guide/).

## The Mistakes That Matter

A seed or sunflower-heavy mix as the staple is the single biggest risk, gerbils pick their favorites and ignore the balanced components, leading to obesity and nutritional imbalance that shortens lifespan.

<FunFact>
Over-treating with fruit or fatty seeds adds weight gain on top of possible dental and digestive problems. And leaving hoarded fresh food in a cache invites spoilage and digestive upset, gerbils are natural hoarders, which makes this an easy habit to miss.
</FunFact>
