# How to Handle a Cockatoo (And Why They're So Often Surrendered)

Cockatoos are extremely social, intelligent, and deeply bonded to their people, genuinely affectionate as babies. That same intensity of bonding is also exactly what makes this species one of the most difficult parrots to keep long-term, and one of the most commonly surrendered.

## The Honest Reputation, and Why It's Earned

A parrot behavior consultant who specializes in this exact issue put it plainly: cockatoos are among the most consistently relinquished parrots, and clients with cockatoos make up over half of her entire consulting practice. Rescue organizations echo this directly, cockatoos are frequently described as their most often surrendered bird. This isn't a reputation to dismiss, it reflects a real, well-documented pattern.

## Why This Happens

Cockatoos form unusually intense bonds with their owners, and that intensity drives genuine separation anxiety when the bird is left alone, even briefly. Combined with being extremely loud, screaming and contact-calling are normal cockatoo behaviors, and a real tendency toward feather-destructive behavior, sometimes escalating to self-mutilation, when their emotional and social needs aren't fully met, the day-to-day demands of this species are genuinely higher than most people expect going in. ## Bite Risk

Large cockatoos bite more readily and with less restraint than many other parrots, and a bite from a bird this size can require stitches. Aggression often increases at sexual maturity, roughly 5 to 7 years old, sometimes catching owners of a previously sweet, cuddly bird off guard.

## First-Timer Mistakes

- Over-bonding early on, spending intense one-on-one time in the first weeks in a way that cements an unhealthy, anxious dependency rather than a healthy one.
- Inadvertently reinforcing screaming by responding to it with attention, even negative attention.
- Body-petting in ways that mimic mating cues, which can trigger hormonal behavior and aggression.
- Underestimating the daily time commitment. Real engagement with this species means 4 to 6 hours of interaction most days, not an occasional check-in.

## How This Compares to Other Large Parrots

Macaws are demanding too, and often have a stronger raw bite force, but cockatoos are generally considered needier, louder, and more prone specifically to separation anxiety and feather-destructive behavior.
