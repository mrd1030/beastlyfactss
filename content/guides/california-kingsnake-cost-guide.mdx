# How Much Does a California Kingsnake Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

California kingsnakes are one of the more affordable pet snakes, and the cost of keeping one month to month is genuinely low. For the complete day-to-day picture beyond budgeting, see our full [California Kingsnake care guide](/guides/california-kingsnake/).

## The Snake: $45 to $300

Wild-type banded babies start as low as $45. Morphs push the price up from there:

    ["Banana", "$150 - $250"],

Choosing a morph is purely an aesthetic decision here, it doesn't meaningfully affect care one way or another.

## Ongoing Costs: Roughly $15 to $30 a Month

One appropriately sized rodent a week, plus substrate and modest electricity, make this one of the cheapest reptiles on this site to feed on an ongoing basis.

## Vet Costs

A routine exotic exam runs $50 to $135. Emergency visits start around $150 and can reach $500 or more with treatment and diagnostics. Common issues that could trigger a vet visit are covered in our [California Kingsnake health issues guide](/blog/california-kingsnake-health-issues-guide/).

## Lifespan

20 years or more is achievable with good care, with some individuals surpassing that. This is a long-term commitment paired with a genuinely low day-to-day cost, making the total lifetime investment more modest than the years alone might suggest.

<FunFact>
California kingsnakes get their common name from being ophiophagous, meaning they eat other snakes, including venomous species. That instinct is exactly why this species carries a housing rule unlike almost anything else on this site.
</FunFact>

## The One Cost Consideration That Isn't About Money

This is worth flagging even in a cost guide, since it affects planning from the start: California kingsnakes are ophiophagous, meaning they eat other snakes, and that instinct doesn't turn off in captivity. If you're planning to keep multiple reptiles, this species needs to be housed completely separately, never even near another snake's enclosure in a way that allows contact. More detail on why lives in our [California Kingsnake handling guide](/blog/california-kingsnake-handling-guide/), but it's worth knowing before you buy a second snake and assume co-housing might work, whether that second snake is a milk snake, a boa constrictor, or anything else.
