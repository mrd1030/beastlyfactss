# How Much Does a California Kingsnake Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

California kingsnakes are one of the more affordable pet snakes, and the cost of keeping one month to month is low.

## The Snake: $45 to $300

Wild-type banded babies start as low as $45. Morphs push the price up from there:

Choosing a morph is an aesthetic decision here: it does not change care one way or another.

## Buying Well: Captive-Bred, and From Whom

Captive-bred kingsnakes make the best pets. Wild-caught animals are more likely to carry parasites and to refuse the frozen-thawed rodents a pet lives on, so a cheap wild-caught snake can cost more in its first year than a captive-bred one. Four checks separate a good seller:

- **Feeding records.** Ask what the snake has eaten, how often, and whether those meals were frozen-thawed. Young kingsnakes sometimes go off thawed prey and have to be coaxed back, so a log of steady thawed meals is worth more than a promise.
- **Both parents.** Jungle corn snakes are corn snake and California kingsnake hybrids, and they are fertile, so hybrid ancestry can be bred on and passed down. A breeder who can show both parents and name what they are is selling a kingsnake. One who is vague about the parents may be selling something else under the name.
- **Permits in California.** Anyone breeding and selling California kingsnakes in California needs a state propagation permit, and wild collection there has set limits. A California seller should be able to name the permit.
- **A clean start.** Budget an initial wellness exam and fecal test in the first weeks, and keep the new snake in [quarantine](/blog/reptile-quarantine-guide/) away from any other reptiles until it is clear.

## Ongoing Costs: Roughly $15 to $30 a Month

An adult eats one appropriately sized rodent every 10 to 14 days, and a growing juvenile every 5 to 7. That, plus substrate and modest electricity, makes this one of the cheapest snakes there is to feed on an ongoing basis.

## Vet Costs

A routine exotic exam runs $50 to $135. Emergency visits start around $150 and can reach $500 or more with treatment and diagnostics. The recommended schedule is an initial exam after purchase and an annual checkup after that to track weight and health, so a healthy kingsnake still carries one exam line a year.

## What It Adds Up To

At $15 to $30 a month, food, substrate and electricity come to $180 to $360 a year. Add the $50 to $135 annual exam and a healthy adult runs roughly $230 to $495 a year. Over the 20 years a well-kept kingsnake typically lives, that is about $4,600 to $9,900, before the snake, the first setup or any sick visit.

The setup line is paid more than once for a hatchling. A baby starts in a small enclosure, often moves to a mid-size one as a juvenile, and only reaches the 48x24x24 inch adult enclosure at around three years old, so a hatchling buyer is budgeting two or three enclosures, not one.

## Lifespan

20 years or more is typical with good care, and the oldest documented captive kingsnake reached 33.3 years. This is a long-term commitment paired with a genuinely low day-to-day cost, making the total lifetime investment more modest than the years alone might suggest.

## One Snake, One Enclosure

Kingsnakes eat other snakes, their own species included, so a second kingsnake is always a second full setup at $200 to $500, never a cheaper shared tank.
