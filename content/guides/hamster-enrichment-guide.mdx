# Hamster Enrichment: The Bedding Depth Study Everyone Should Know

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Most small pet enrichment advice is a list of things to buy. For hamsters there is a study with three numbers in it that reorders that list entirely. For everything else about the species, see our complete [Hamster care guide](/guides/hamster/).

<KeyTakeaway>
Forty-five golden hamsters were given 80 cm, 40 cm or 10 cm of bedding. The 10 cm group showed significantly more wire-gnawing and higher wheel activity. Every hamster with 40 cm or more built a burrow and used it. In the 80 cm group, wire-gnawing was not observed at all. Substrate depth is the enrichment, and everything else is secondary.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Research Actually Says

Hauzenberger, Gebhardt-Henrich and Steiger assigned 45 male golden hamsters to three groups with 80, 40 or 10 cm of wood shavings and measured behavior, including responses to acute stressors.

The results split cleanly. Hamsters on 10 cm showed **significantly more wire-gnawing** and higher running wheel activity than the other groups. All hamsters given **at least 40 cm** of material suitable for burrow construction built burrows and used them, and showed significantly less wire-gnawing. In the **80 cm** group, wire-gnawing was not observed during the study.

<FunFact>
Wire-gnawing is not a quirk, it is one of the standard behavioral measures used to assess housing quality in small rodents. That is why the study tracked it, and why "my hamster chews the bars" is a husbandry readout rather than a personality trait.
</FunFact>

The commercial default is nowhere near 40 cm. Most cages sold for hamsters cannot physically hold that depth, which makes cage choice and bedding depth the same decision.

## The Wheel, With One Caveat

A correctly sized solid-surface wheel is necessary. A <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08FC8JG1B?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Niteangel Super-Silent Hamster Exercise Wheel, 10 inch">10 inch silent wheel</AffiliateLink> lets a Syrian run with a flat back rather than a permanently arched one, and a solid running surface avoids the foot injuries that wire and mesh cause.

The caveat comes straight from the study: the hamsters on shallow bedding ran *more*. Heavy wheel use is not automatically a sign of a happy animal, and it can accompany the same setup that produces bar-chewing. Provide the wheel, but do not read the odometer as a welfare score.

## Tunnels, Cover and Foraging

Hamsters are prey animals and cross open ground reluctantly. Connected cover, <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B082FBW69F?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Niteangel Creative and Composable Hamster Tunnel">buried tunnel sections</AffiliateLink> and scattered hides change how much of the enclosure gets used at all, which is measurable and is the point: a large cage the animal will not cross is a small cage.

Scatter-feeding rather than bowl-feeding turns a two minute event into a foraging session, and it costs nothing. Our [feeding guide](/blog/hamster-feeding-guide/) covers what to scatter and how much.

## Priority Order

1. Bedding deep enough to hold a real burrow, 40 cm as the floor, more if the cage allows
2. A cage with the footprint and wall height to make that possible
3. A correctly sized solid wheel
4. Connected cover and tunnels
5. Scatter feeding and foraging toys
6. Novelty objects, last

## What Not to Do

Do not house Syrians together, since they are solitary and this is one species where companionship is the wrong answer. Do not use a wire or mesh wheel, or one small enough to arch the back. Do not read heavy wheel running as proof the setup is working, because the study found the opposite pattern. Do not use cotton-style fluffy nesting material, which wraps around limbs. And do not add accessories to a shallow setup and call it enriched, since the behavior that changed in the study changed with depth.
