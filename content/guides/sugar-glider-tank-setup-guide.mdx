# Sugar Glider Cage Setup: Tall, Not Wide

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Sugar gliders are arboreal, and their housing needs reflect that directly, height matters far more here than floor space does, the opposite priority from a lot of small mammal setups. This covers the full setup, for what all of it costs, see our [Sugar Glider cost guide](/blog/sugar-glider-cost-guide/), or the complete [Sugar Glider care guide](/guides/sugar-glider/) for everything else.

## Cage Size

A minimum of roughly 30 by 18 by 36 inches for a pair, though many current keepers recommend going larger still, 24 by 24 by 48 inches or more. Whatever footprint you choose, prioritize vertical space, this is a climbing, gliding animal, and a cage that's wide but short doesn't suit its natural movement the way a genuinely tall enclosure does. A <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B001NJ0DAY?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Critter Nation by MidWest Homes for Pets Double Unit Cage">24x24x48 in multi-level cage</AffiliateLink> is technically marketed for chinchillas, but it's a genuinely tall, multi-level wire cage that real sugar glider keepers commonly use for exactly this reason, plenty of height and levels for a climbing, gliding pair to move through.

## Bar Spacing

Keep it to no more than half an inch. Young gliders are small enough to squeeze through wider gaps, and escape-proofing matters more with this species than the cage's overall size might suggest.

## Temperature

75 to 88°F is the comfortable range. Keep the cage away from drafts and direct sun, and place it in a consistently warm part of the house rather than somewhere temperature fluctuates a lot through the day.

## Humidity and Lighting

No special humidity control is needed for this species. Ordinary household lighting is sufficient, there's no UVB requirement the way many reptiles have, a [bearded dragon](/blog/bearded-dragon-tank-setup-guide/) setup, for comparison, genuinely can't skip that step the way a glider's can.

## Substrate

A solid cage bottom with washable liners or trays underneath the main living area works best, rather than loose bedding that accumulates waste and becomes harder to keep clean.
