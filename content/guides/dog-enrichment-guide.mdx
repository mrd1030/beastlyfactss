## What the Research Actually Says

Duranton and Horowitz published **Let me sniff! Nosework induces positive judgment bias in pet dogs** in *Applied Animal Behaviour Science* in 2019.

**The design.** Twenty dogs of different breeds, at least a year old. Ten did **nosework**, where they were allowed to sniff and search. Ten did **heelwork**, where they were not allowed to search with their noses. Both groups got structured activity with their handler, which is what makes the comparison useful.

**The measure.** A cognitive bias test. Dogs learned that a bowl in one location always held food and a bowl in another never did, then were shown an empty bowl placed exactly between the two. **Latency to approach** the ambiguous bowl is the readout: a quicker approach is read as expecting good things.

**The result.** Latency **declined significantly** in the nosework group and **did not change** in the control group.

**The interpretation.** Nosework lets a dog express a natural behaviour and act **autonomously**. It is not that sniffing is magic, it is that following a scent means the dog decides where to go next.

<FunFact>
The control group is what makes this study worth citing. Both groups got twenty minutes of activity with a person. The difference was only whether the dog got to use its nose and make its own decisions, and that difference was enough to move an optimism measure.
</FunFact>

## Sniffing, Which Is Free

The item with the best evidence and no cost. On walks, let the dog stop and investigate rather than moving it along.

A **sniffari**, a walk where the dog chooses the route and the pace and you follow, is a different activity from a walk of the same distance at heel. Dogs that get one are visibly more settled afterwards, and the study suggests something is genuinely changing rather than the dog just being tired.

Add structured scent games indoors: hide treats around a room, hide them in a box of scrunched paper, teach a find-it cue. None of this needs equipment.

## Chewing

A self-soothing behaviour with a real calming effect, and one dogs need an outlet for regardless of how much exercise they get. Durable chews, appropriate to the dog's size and chewing style, available rather than rationed.

Match the chew to the chewer. A hard chew that suits one dog fractures another's teeth, and a soft toy that entertains one dog is swallowed whole by the next.

## Choice, as the Organising Idea

This is what the nosework finding is really about, and it applies well beyond scent.

Give the dog decisions: which way to go on a walk, whether to approach a stranger, where to lie, whether to opt out of an interaction. A dog that can leave a situation is a dog that mostly does not need to.

## Training as Enrichment

Short positive-reinforcement sessions are mental work, social attention and a predictable structure at once. Ten minutes twice a day beats a weekly hour.

Trick training, scent discrimination, and simple problem solving all count. The behaviour taught matters much less than the fact of the session, which is the same thing the cockatoo plucking research found, covered in our [Cockatoo enrichment guide](/blog/cockatoo-enrichment-guide/).

Skip the dominance framing, which does not describe dog behaviour and produces worse training. Our guide on [the alpha wolf myth](/blog/alpha-wolf-myth-what-the-research-actually-says/) covers where that idea came from and why its own author retracted it.

## Company and Rest

Dogs are social animals and being alone for a full working day is the single largest welfare problem in pet dogs that nobody sells a product for. Enrichment does not fix it.

At the other end, dogs need a great deal of sleep, and an over-scheduled dog with no downtime gets worse rather than better. A quiet, undisturbed place to rest is part of the picture.

## What Enrichment Does Not Replace

Worth stating plainly, because enrichment gets sold as a substitute for the harder things.

It does not replace **physical exercise**, **training**, **company**, or **veterinary care**. A dog that is under-exercised, alone all day and untrained will not be fixed by a puzzle feeder. Enrichment is the layer you add once the rest is in place.

## Priority Order

1. Sniffing time on walks, at the dog's pace
2. Physical exercise appropriate to the breed and age
3. Food delivered through scent and puzzle work rather than a bowl
4. Short daily positive-reinforcement training
5. Chewing outlets matched to the dog
6. Choice, including the choice to opt out
7. Enough undisturbed sleep

## What Not to Do

Do not treat a puzzle feeder as a substitute for exercise or company. Do not march a dog past everything interesting and call it a walk. Do not add enrichment food on top of the daily ration. Do not use dominance-based training. Do not over-schedule a dog that needs sleep. And do not assume every breed wants the same thing, which is where the breed-specific guides below come in.
