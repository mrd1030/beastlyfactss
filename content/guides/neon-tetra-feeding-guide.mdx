# Neon Tetra Feeding: Schedule, Diet, and the Rule That Prevents an Outbreak

Neon tetra feeding is genuinely simple, small food in small amounts, but the rule that actually protects the school has nothing to do with portions at all. For the rest of their care needs, see our complete [Neon Tetra care guide](/guides/neon-tetra/).

## The Staple Diet

Neon tetras are omnivores. Build the diet around a high-quality <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B004PBB5TK?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Hikari Tropical Semi-Floating Micro Pellets">tropical flake or micro-pellet</AffiliateLink> designed for small, nano fish, supplemented several times a week with small frozen or live foods: baby brine shrimp, daphnia, cyclops, and finely chopped bloodworms, plus some plant or algae content like spirulina flakes or the occasional blanched vegetable. In the wild their diet runs roughly 20 to 30% plant matter and algae alongside insect larvae and small invertebrates.

<KeyTakeaway>
Their mouths are only about 1 to 2mm across. Crush flakes into fine pieces, and stick to "micro" or "nano" sized pellets, food that's too large gets taken and spat back out repeatedly, which functionally starves the fish even while food is visibly going into the tank.
</KeyTakeaway>

## Feeding Frequency and Portions

Feed small amounts twice daily, morning and evening, only as much as the fish finish in about 2 to 3 minutes. Small, frequent feedings suit their tiny stomachs and fast metabolism, and protect water quality far better than one large feeding. Fry and juveniles need 3 to 4 small feedings a day, transitioning from infusoria or baby brine shrimp to crushed flake as they grow, adults from about 6 months do well on twice daily. Keep protein-rich frozen or live foods to 2 to 3 feedings a week to avoid digestive issues.

## Foods to Avoid

Oversized flakes or pellets, excessive protein-rich live or frozen food, and any overfeeding are the main risks. Avoid low-quality live foods like tubifex from questionable sources, which can transmit disease, and don't overfeed vegetables or fruit, they cause bloating in this small a fish.

<FunFact>
Critically, never leave a dead fish in the tank or let tankmates scavenge a visibly sick fish's body. Neon tetra disease is caused by the microsporidian parasite Pleistophora hyphessobryconis, and if a fish dies of it and its body is eaten by others, those fish contract it too. There is no cure and no effective treatment, prompt removal of dead or sick fish is the only control that actually exists.
</FunFact>

## Supplementation

None specific is required beyond a varied, vitamin-fortified diet. Dietary variety, color-enhancing and carotenoid foods, plant matter, maintains coloration and immune function on its own, no separate vitamin or mineral supplement is needed for a properly fed community tank.

## The Mistakes That Actually Matter

Food too large for their mouths means the fish can't actually eat it, it gets spat out, rots, and pollutes the water while the fish itself goes underfed. Overfeeding or a single large daily feeding lets uneaten food decay and spike ammonia and nitrite, and neons are very sensitive to both. And leaving a dead or sick fish in the tank is how an outbreak of an incurable disease spreads to the whole school.
