# How Much Does Zebra Danio Ownership Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Zebra danios are one of the cheapest fish in the hobby to buy and keep, and one of the only community species where you can honestly skip a line item most other tropical fish require: the heater.

## The Fish: $2 to $3 Each

Individual zebra danios sell for about $2.00 each, and group listings land in the same range: a 12-pack has run $24.99, a little over $2 per fish. Either way, since this is a schooling species, you're buying a group, not one fish. A starting school of 6 to 10, the range most care sources recommend for this species, runs roughly $12 to $30.

That puts zebra danio right in line with [cardinal tetra](/blog/cardinal-tetra-cost-guide/) on a per-fish basis, but without the wild-caught supply chain that pushes cardinal tetra toward the higher end of its own range. Zebra danio is bred commercially at enormous scale, which is a big part of why it stays cheap and consistently available.

<FunFact>
The fluorescent GloFish sold in most pet stores are this exact species, just with a jellyfish-derived fluorescent protein gene added to the genome. A set of GloFish danios runs about $8 a fish whether you buy a 3-pack or a dozen, roughly four times the price of a plain wild-type zebra danio, even though the two need identical care. The gene was originally developed by researchers at the National University of Singapore to build a fish that would fluoresce in the presence of water pollutants, and only later got licensed for the pet trade.
</FunFact>

## A Quarantine Tank: Roughly $52 to $90, Once

The line most danio budgets leave out is the second tank. Mycobacteriosis, the danio disease with no reliable cure once a fish shows it, is kept out mainly by quarantining new fish, and the [quarantine guide](/blog/fish-quarantine-and-treatment-guide/) sets the kit: a bare tank, a sponge filter run by an air pump, and a heater. For danios it is a small version of the main setup.

Against a school that costs $12 to $30, the quarantine tank can cost more than the fish, and it is still the cheaper line next to losing an established school to an infection a new fish brought in.

## Vet Costs

Not applicable in the traditional sense. Fish health management here is almost entirely about water quality and, when needed, over-the-counter treatments, not veterinary visits.

## Lifespan

Outbred zebra danios average around 3.5 years in captivity and can live up to 5.5 years with excellent care. That's a dramatically longer life than this fish gets in the wild, where most zebrafish live to be about one year old before predation, seasonal habitat loss, or the annual monsoon cycle catches up with them. A stable aquarium, with no dry season and no snakeheads, is a genuinely easier life than the one this fish evolved for.

## Why Zebra Danio Costs Less Than Almost Any Other Starter Fish

Cheap stock, no strict heater requirement, and low food and water-treatment costs add up to one of the least expensive ways into the fishkeeping hobby. The one place people overspend without meaning to is the fish count itself, buying two or three "to start" instead of a full school of 6 to 10 from day one. An understocked school is more prone to fin-nipping and stress, which costs more to correct later than it does to just stock correctly the first time.
