# Molly Feeding: Schedule, Diet, and Why Vegetables Matter

Mollies get a large part of their diet from plants and algae, so a menu with no plant food at all leaves out most of what they naturally eat.

## Feeding Schedule by Life Stage

- **Fry and juveniles:** Fry accept newly hatched baby brine shrimp or powdered flake from birth, and crushed flake works as they grow. Young fish take a larger ration for their body weight than adults, up to about 5% of body weight against about 3% for an adult, fed in small amounts.
- **Adults:** At least once a day. The portion rule matters more than the exact frequency: feed only what's fully cleared within a minute or two.

## What Mollies Eat

The sailfin molly is almost entirely a plant eater in the wild, grazing algae and other plant material and taking aquatic invertebrates such as mosquito larvae on the side. The short-finned molly is an omnivore that feeds on worms, small crustaceans and insects as well as plants, but it still needs a large share of plant food. That balance should carry over into the tank: a high-quality flake or pellet formulated for tropical community fish as the base, with plant food layered on top rather than treated as optional.

<FunFact>
Sailfin males that get too little greenstuff may not develop their full sail-like dorsal fin.
</FunFact>

Spirulina flake and pellets make good staples, and blanched lettuce and cooked spinach are popular additions, cut small and left in the tank only long enough to be grazed on. Protein-rich foods, bloodworms, brine shrimp, and similar live, frozen, or freeze-dried options, should stay a supplement rather than a staple, with bloodworms once or twice a week at most.

## Safe Treats and Foods to Avoid

Safe: quality flake or pellet with real plant content, spirulina flake, blanched lettuce and cooked spinach, and occasional bloodworms or brine shrimp as a protein supplement.

Avoid feeding an all-protein diet with no vegetable or algae component at all. Beyond that, the general aquarium-keeping rules apply: skip spoiled or expired food, and don't refeed frozen food that's already been thawed and left sitting out, that encourages bacterial growth in the tank.

## Why Mollies Stop Eating

1. **Stress.** New-tank relocation, aggressive tankmates, and an imbalanced male-to-female ratio are common triggers. A ratio of roughly one male to two or three females reduces the chasing and harassment that comes with too many males pursuing too few females.
2. **Poor or unstable water quality.** Ammonia or nitrite spikes and incorrect pH are common causes of both appetite loss and lethargy in freshwater aquarium fish generally, worth testing for directly rather than waiting out.
3. **Cold water.** Mollies are a tropical species, and cold water slows metabolism directly, which shows up as lethargy and reduced appetite along with a weakened immune response.
4. **Illness.** Ich, velvet, and similar conditions are usually accompanied by other visible signs, not appetite loss alone.

What's worth acting on regardless of exact duration: appetite loss combined with lethargy, hiding, clamped or discolored fins, visible spots, or unusual swimming, including the side-to-side "shimmy" this species is known for. Any of those together are a signal to check water parameters immediately rather than wait it out.

Overfeeding is the easiest risk to avoid: uneaten food breaking down in the tank is a common driver of the water-quality decline behind [most other molly health issues](/blog/molly-health-issues-guide/), the shimmy among them. Steady filtration and a regular water-change habit keep that from compounding.
