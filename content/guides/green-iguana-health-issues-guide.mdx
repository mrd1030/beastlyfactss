# Common Green Iguana Health Issues

<VetDisclaimer />

Green iguana health problems trace back overwhelmingly to husbandry, and one condition in particular is common enough in this species that understanding it should shape every other decision you make about care. See our [enclosure setup guide](/blog/green-iguana-tank-setup-guide/) for the husbandry side of prevention, and our full [Green Iguana care guide](/guides/green-iguana/) for everything else.

## Metabolic Bone Disease (MBD)

This is genuinely the defining health issue for pet iguanas. Veterinary sources describe MBD as probably the most common medical problem in captive iguanas, and among the most common diseases in captive lizards generally. Watch for a swollen or rubbery lower jaw, swollen or bowed hind limbs, soft bones, tremors, and an inability to lift the trunk while walking. Caused by low dietary calcium relative to phosphorus, insufficient vitamin D3, or inadequate UVB exposure. **Always see a vet**, advanced cases need immediate, long-term treatment, and prevention through correct UVB, calcium, and diet ratio from day one is genuinely the difference between a healthy iguana and a statistic.

<FunFact>
An estimated 70% of captive green iguanas die within their first year of life, and inadequate diet, lighting, and housing (the exact same three factors behind metabolic bone disease) are named as the leading cause. This single condition is both the most common health problem in the species and, when prevented correctly, one of the most avoidable.
</FunFact>

## Kidney Disease

Often the result of chronic dehydration from low humidity, combined with too much animal protein in a diet that should be strictly plant-based, no insects, no dog or cat food, no eggs, ever. This is a leading cause of death in adult iguanas. **Always see a vet.**

## Mouth Rot, Respiratory Infection, and Skin or Fungal Infections

All linked to poor husbandry, temperatures running too low, or humidity that's mismanaged. **See a vet** for any of these, they need professional treatment rather than home correction alone.

## Egg Binding (Ovostasis)

A genuine reproductive emergency in females, occurring even without a male present. **This is always an emergency**, get to a vet immediately if you suspect it.

## Thermal Burns and Substrate Impaction

Manageable and entirely preventable. Burns come from unguarded heat sources positioned where the iguana can contact them directly, impaction from ingesting substrate that's too fine while feeding. Both are solved through the setup choices covered in the [tank setup guide](/blog/green-iguana-tank-setup-guide/).

## The Husbandry Pattern Worth Remembering

Nearly everything on this list traces back to getting UVB, calcium, humidity, and diet right from the start. MBD specifically, the most common and most serious issue here, is described by veterinary sources as highly preventable. Given how routinely this species is under-cared-for, treating these basics as non-negotiable rather than optional is genuinely what separates a thriving iguana from the far too common alternative.
