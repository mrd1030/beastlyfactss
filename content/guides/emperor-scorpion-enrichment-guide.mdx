# Emperor Scorpion Enrichment: One of the Few You Can Keep in a Group

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Almost every arachnid on this site is a solitary animal that will eat its neighbor. This one is a partial exception.

<KeyTakeaway>
Emperor scorpions are **subsocial** in the wild, with mothers and their offspring or siblings sharing a burrow, and they can be kept communally in captivity. That is unusual and it is conditional: ample space, multiple hides and every animal properly fed. Get any of those wrong and communal housing becomes cannibalism.
</KeyTakeaway>

For everything else about them, see the [Emperor Scorpion care guide](/guides/emperor-scorpion/).

## What the Research Says

Nothing on *Pandinus imperator* enrichment. The borrow is from the tarantula housing study, covered in full in our [Tarantula enrichment guide](/blog/tarantula-enrichment-guide/).

Brazilian black tarantulas given an enclosure roughly six times larger with six times as much structure showed **77 percent larger home ranges**, **113 percent larger core areas**, and **150 percent greater odds of being active in a novel environment** afterwards. They were **no more active overall**, and offered both enclosures at once they showed **no clear preference**.

A scorpion is not a spider, so this is a genuine borrow and not a close one. What transfers is the honest shape of it: complexity looks like it helps on inferred measures, the animal will not tell you so directly, and none of it displaces getting the substrate right.

<FunFact>
Scorpion cuticle fluoresces bright green under ultraviolet light, and nobody is certain why. It is useful in practice: a UV torch is the fastest way to find an animal that spends most of its life underground.
</FunFact>

## Burrow Depth

The highest-value item, as it is for most of the invertebrates on this site. Emperor scorpions dig, and shallow substrate takes the behavior away and leaves a large arachnid sitting on a floor.

A deep bed of <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CY23K32S?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="VCEPJH Loose Coconut Fiber Substrate, 2 Pack Natural Coco Coir Reptile Bedding">coconut fiber substrate</AffiliateLink>, damp enough to hold a tunnel, is the standard. Other sizes and volumes are available and a communal enclosure needs several. Our [tank setup guide](/blog/emperor-scorpion-tank-setup-guide/) covers depth, humidity and heating.

## Hides, More Than One Per Animal

<AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00DDZ55D6?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Zoo Med Natural Cork Bark Round - Small (2)">Cork bark</AffiliateLink> laid on the surface gives a starting burrow and somewhere to be immediately concealed.

In a communal setup this is not decoration, it is the mechanism that makes grouping work. Every animal needs somewhere to be that is not somewhere another animal already is, and the standard failure is one good hide and three scorpions.

## Communal Housing, Done Deliberately

Space first. A long enclosure with real floor area rather than a tall one, since these are ground animals.

Then multiple hides, then reliable feeding. A hungry scorpion in a crowded enclosure is the cannibalism scenario, and all three factors have to hold at once. Start with animals raised together where you can, and be prepared to separate.

If this is your first scorpion, keep one. The communal option is a thing you graduate to.

## Humidity and Warmth

Rainforest species. Warm and humid, with damp substrate and genuine ventilation, never a sealed box.

Dry conditions cause failed molts, which is among the commonest causes of death in captive scorpions and is almost entirely a husbandry problem. Our [health issues guide](/blog/emperor-scorpion-health-issues-guide/) covers what a failed molt looks like coming, and our [moulting guide](/blog/invertebrate-molting-guide/) covers the process across invertebrates.

## Feeding as Ambush

Nocturnal sit-and-wait predators. Release live prey in the evening rather than presenting it with tongs, and let the animal hunt.

Feed each animal in a communal setup, which in practice means offering more prey than you think and checking it gets taken. Vary timing and location. Our [feeding guide](/blog/emperor-scorpion-feeding-guide/) covers prey sizing.

Remove uneaten live prey before a molt, since crickets will chew on a soft scorpion.

## The UV Torch, as a Tool

A <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07TQKY1GC?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="uvBeast V3 385-395nm Black Light UV Flashlight">UV torch</AffiliateLink> makes a hidden scorpion instantly findable, which is how you check on an animal without dismantling its burrow.

Brief use. It is a husbandry tool for your benefit, not enrichment for the animal's, and there is no reason to think a nocturnal arachnid enjoys being lit up.

## Priority Order

1. Substrate deep enough to burrow properly
2. Correct warmth and humidity with real ventilation
3. Multiple hides, more than one per animal
4. Floor area over height
5. Live prey released in the evening
6. Communal housing, only with the three conditions above met
7. Checking with a UV torch instead of digging them out

## What Not to Do

Do not attempt a communal setup in a small enclosure, with one hide, or with irregular feeding. Do not use shallow substrate. Do not let the enclosure dry out. Do not leave live prey in with a pre-molt animal. Do not house one as a first invertebrate if you plan to group them later.
