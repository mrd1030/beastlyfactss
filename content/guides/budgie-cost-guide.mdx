## The Vet Cost People Underestimate

An initial well-bird exam with an avian vet is the first cost after the cage, and it typically runs $45 to $65. That's just the baseline. Diagnostics like a fecal exam, gram stain, or bloodwork add to the bill, and an emergency exam alone is $50 to $75 before any treatment. Emergency treatment or surgery can run anywhere from $100 to $1,500 or more.

Avian vets are genuinely harder to find than general small-animal vets, and pet insurance is rarely available for budgies, so most owners end up self-insuring. It's worth setting aside a dedicated emergency fund for this specifically rather than assuming routine savings will cover it.

## What the Vet Line Covers Over a Year

The first exam falls within one to two weeks of bringing the bird home. The base fee buys the physical: weight, a look in the mouth, the heart and breathing listened to. The tests that make up the diagnostics line are chosen from there, typically blood work, a fecal check for parasites, and swabs for abnormal bacteria or yeast. Budgies have one extra worth paying for: some avian practices test every new budgie for trichomonas, the parasite behind canker, because it is a common cause of death.

After that first visit the floor is one checkup a year, and many avian vets recommend two. At the $45 to $65 exam range, a twice-yearly schedule is $90 to $130 a year before any tests, against the $45 to $65 a year the amortized line in the table below assumes.

<FunFact>
Budgies are prey animals that instinctively mask signs of illness, since looking sick makes them a target in the wild. That's part of why avian vets emphasize catching problems early through routine checkups rather than waiting for obvious symptoms.
</FunFact>

Chewing and shredding toys is normal, healthy budgie behavior, not something to discourage, so budget for regular replacement rather than treating it as wasteful. The standard advice is to rotate toys weekly and replace them monthly, which is where the toy line comes from. Without the exam the other rows come to $20 to $55 a month. A budgie kept indoors away from unfiltered sunlight also needs the full-spectrum UV light in the setup table, run 10 to 12 hours a day. A T5 tube's wattage is not its length: a 24-watt tube is about 22 inches long, a 39-watt tube about 34 inches and a 54-watt tube about 46 inches, so go by the length in inches when you match it to two-thirds of the enclosure.

A second budgie does not double everything. The cage, if it was sized for a pair from the start, stays the same. Food, toys and above all the vet line do double, since each bird needs its own exam and its own tests.

## The Budgeting Takeaway

The bird itself is the cheap part. What determines your long-term cost is the quality of the cage (worth spending more on upfront since a too-small cage becomes a welfare problem, not just a maintenance one) and how seriously you treat avian vet care, which is a smaller, pricier specialty than most first-time bird owners expect going in.
