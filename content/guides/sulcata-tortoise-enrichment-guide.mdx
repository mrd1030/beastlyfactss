# Sulcata Tortoise Enrichment: A Hundred Pound Grazer in a Back Garden

<AffiliateDisclosure />

The sulcata gets sold as a hatchling that fits in a hand and grows into the third largest tortoise in the world.

<KeyTakeaway>
Tortoise cognition research is a good deal more interesting than the animals' reputation suggests: touchscreen tasks, knowledge transfer between contexts, anticipation of scheduled food, and measurable cognitive bias. Then a sulcata adds a hundred pounds and a burrowing habit, and the enrichment question becomes a land question.
</KeyTakeaway>

For everything else about them, see the [Sulcata Tortoise care guide](/guides/sulcata-tortoise/).

## What the Research Says

Nothing on *Centrochelys sulcata*. The borrow is from red-footed tortoises, covered in full in our [Red-Footed Tortoise enrichment guide](/blog/red-footed-tortoise-enrichment-guide/).

Red-footed tortoises have **learned touchscreen tasks and transferred that knowledge to a different context**, **anticipated cyclical resource availability**, meaning they learned when food would appear and behaved accordingly, and shown **cognitive bias**, a measure used to infer something like mood in other animals.

There is also a controlled chelonian enrichment experiment: Case, Lewbart and Doerr tested an enriched environment in **eastern box turtles**, one of the few in the group.

A red-footed tortoise is a humid forest omnivore and a sulcata is an arid grassland grazer, so the husbandry does not transfer at all. The cognition does. There is no reason to think a sulcata is the less capable animal.

<FunFact>
The anticipation result is the one that should change how you feed. A tortoise that has worked out your schedule is already doing something cognitively interesting, and it is also a reason to vary the schedule rather than run the same slot every day.
</FunFact>

## Space, and Then More Space

Everything else is downstream. Adults reach around two and a half feet of shell and can exceed a hundred pounds, and in a suitable climate the honest answer is a secure outdoor enclosure measured in yards.

## Grazing, Not Feeding

This is the enrichment item that fits the species best and costs nothing. Sulcatas are grazers built to walk and eat continuously through the day, and a bowl of chopped food deletes both halves of that.

## Somewhere to Dig

Not optional, and not a behavior you can talk them out of. Sulcatas dig substantial burrows to escape heat, and a captive one will excavate under whatever is nearest if there is nowhere better.

Give a deliberate digging area with deep, diggable substrate, plus a shelter that provides the thermal refuge the burrow would. Then protect fence lines, shed bases and foundations, because a determined adult moves a lot of soil.

## Terrain Worth Crossing

Flat lawn is a boring enclosure for a walking animal. Gentle slopes, varied ground texture, rocks, logs and low obstacles turn the space into somewhere with routes through it and not a field.

Keep it navigable. A tortoise that flips onto its back in a hot enclosure can die there, so nothing should be steep enough or wedge-shaped enough to turn one over.

## Training, Which the Research Supports

Given the touchscreen and anticipation results, target training a sulcata is entirely reasonable and genuinely useful. A tortoise that will follow a target moves itself for weighing, health checks and enclosure transfers, which for a hundred pound animal is worth real effort.

Vary the feeding schedule while you are at it, so the anticipation has something to work on.

## Priority Order

1. Secure outdoor space sized for the adult, with fencing that goes down
2. Grazing on safe growing plants
3. A deliberate digging area, and a thermal shelter
4. Scattered, distributed high-fiber browse
5. Varied terrain with safe, navigable obstacles
6. Target training and a varied feeding schedule
7. Novel objects, last

## What Not to Do

Do not buy a hatchling without a realistic plan for a hundred pound adult, which is the failure this species is defined by. Do not feed fruit or a red-footed tortoise diet to an arid grazer. Do not assume fencing above ground is enough. Do not build terrain that can flip a tortoise. Do not skip the shelter and expect a burrow to substitute in a climate that gets cold, covered in our [health issues guide](/blog/sulcata-tortoise-health-issues-guide/). And check our [legal guide](/blog/sulcata-tortoise-legal-guide/) before rehoming one, since adults are notoriously hard to place.
