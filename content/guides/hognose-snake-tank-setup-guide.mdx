# Western Hognose Snake Enclosure Setup: Dry, Deep, and Built for Burrowing

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Western hognose care comes down to a fairly short list of priorities: keep it dry, keep the substrate deep enough to burrow in, and get the heat source right. Nail those and this is an easy species to house well.

<Figure src="/assets/images/hognose-snake-tank-setup-guide-1.jpg" alt="Labeled diagram of a Western hognose snake enclosure showing the halogen basking bulb, low-output UVB, deep dry substrate, and a dedicated humid hide" caption="Halogen heat at one end, a low-output UVB tube overhead, a deep dry bed, and one humid hide for shedding." shareable />

## Enclosure Size

A useful rule of thumb is roughly one square foot of floor space per foot of snake length. For adult males, a 20-gallon-equivalent enclosure (around 30x13x13 inches) works as a minimum. Females grow noticeably larger, and a 40-gallon-equivalent setup (36x18x16 inches) is a more appropriate minimum for them. ### Hatchlings and juveniles

A hatchling under 6 inches long can live in a 5 gallon enclosure, about 16 by 8 by 10 inches, and a juvenile can move up to a 10 gallon until it is about a year old. Starting a young hognose in the adult enclosure also works, as long as it has enough hides and cover to feel secure in the bigger space. Whatever the size, the lid on a baby's enclosure needs clamps or latches. Never use tape, which is notorious for injuring snakes.

Floor space matters more than height here. This is a ground-dwelling, burrowing species, and it does not climb. If you're using glass, cover three sides to reduce stress, and make sure the lid is secure. Hognoses will push at any gap they find.

## Temperature

Basking area around 90 to 95°F on one end and cool side 70 to 75°F. At night the heat goes off and the enclosure can fall to room temperature, as long as it stays no colder than about 60°F. A hognose needs no night bulb, and needs night heat, such as an <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/44hAxJ1" product="Under-tank heat mat + thermostat kit">under-tank heat mat and thermostat kit</AffiliateLink>, only if the room drops below that. A daytime heat source is required, and the commonly recommended choice is a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B000YS3H0I?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Exo Terra Sun Glo Halogen Basking Spot Lamp, 50W">low-wattage halogen basking bulb</AffiliateLink> positioned over the substrate, on a dimming thermostat, rather than relying on a heat mat or ceramic heat emitter as the primary source.

Some general care sheets recommend an under-tank heat mat as the main heat source instead. That is a point of disagreement in the hobby, but halogen basking bulbs are the more current, specialist-endorsed choice. Whichever heat source you choose, the thermostat itself is not optional.

## Humidity: Keep It Low

30 to 50% ambient humidity, on the dry side compared to many other pet snakes. Don't mist the whole enclosure to raise humidity. Provide one dedicated humid hide for shedding instead, and only add moisture there as needed. Running the whole enclosure damp is a direct path to respiratory infection and scale rot.

## Substrate: Deep Enough to Burrow In

This is a fossorial species that puts substrate depth to use, and where you put that depth matters as much as how much of it there is: the [cool end is where a hognose digs](/blog/hognose-snake-enrichment-guide/). Provide 3 to 6 inches of loose, dry, diggable substrate. A soil and sand mix works well, and roughly 70% soil-based substrate to 30% reptile-safe sand is a common ratio. <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3TkxAVF" product="Aspen shavings substrate">Aspen or coconut fiber substrate</AffiliateLink> also works well as the soil-based component. Keep it dry. Damp substrate combined with this species' preference for arid conditions is a fast route to skin and respiratory problems.

Feed your snake off the substrate, in a separate container, to prevent impaction.

Cleaning is simple on a dry setup. Pull droppings and urates as soon as you see them. Replace the whole substrate about every three months even if it looks clean, because urine soaks into it, and use that empty enclosure as the time to disinfect every surface and hide. That is a longer interval than the once or twice a month the general schedule sets for loose sand or soil, and it holds only for a bed kept dry with every dropping pulled the day it appears. A bed that has gone damp, smells, or hid a dropping for days gets the monthly change instead. A bioactive setup is the exception: once its cleanup crew is established it can run for years without a full substrate change. The water dish, the disinfectant and its contact time follow the general [reptile enclosure cleaning schedule](/blog/reptile-salmonella-hygiene-guide/).

<FunFact>
Hognoses are one of the few pet snake species that are diurnal, active by day instead of at night or dusk. That's part of why a low-output UVB bulb and a normal 12-hour light and dark cycle are increasingly recommended for this species, even though care sheets for nocturnal snakes like ball pythons rarely bother with lighting at all.
</FunFact>

## Lighting

Hognoses are active during the day, unlike many pet snakes, and benefit from a normal 12-hour light and dark cycle. UVB isn't strictly required by every source, but a low-output linear T5 bulb spanning about two-thirds of the enclosure length is increasingly recommended for this diurnal species. For a 5.0 or 6% T5 HO tube, the distance from the tube to the snake's back on the basking spot is 10 to 14 inches through a screen lid, or 14 to 18 inches with no mesh in the way, and a UV index meter is the way to confirm it.

## Deep, Dry, and Fed Elsewhere

Deep, dry, burrowable substrate sits close to the core of what this species needs to feel secure and behave normally, and feeding off it keeps that depth from turning into an impaction risk. The result is a drier, more active setup than a [corn snake](/blog/corn-snake-tank-setup-guide/) or a ball python needs, which is the adjustment to make when either of those is the snake you kept before.
