# Western Hognose Snake Tank Setup: Dry, Deep, and Built for Burrowing

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Western hognose care comes down to a fairly short list of priorities: keep it dry, keep the substrate deep enough to burrow in, and get the heat source right. Nail those and this is a genuinely easy species to house well. For the full care picture, see our complete [Hognose Snake care guide](/guides/hognose-snake/).

## Enclosure Size

A useful rule of thumb is roughly one square foot of floor space per foot of snake length. For adult males, a 20-gallon-equivalent enclosure (around 30x13x13 inches) works as a minimum. Females grow noticeably larger, and a 40-gallon-equivalent setup (36x18x16 inches) is a more appropriate minimum for them. A <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CJB82285?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="REPTI ZOO 50 Gallon PVC Reptile Enclosure, 36x18x18 inch">2x2x2 foot or similarly sized front-opening PVC enclosure</AffiliateLink> makes a solid adult setup for either sex.

Floor space matters more than height here, this is a ground-dwelling, burrowing species, not a climber. If you're using glass, cover three sides to reduce stress, and make sure the lid is genuinely secure, hognoses will push at any gap they find.

## Temperature

Basking area around 90 to 95°F on one end, cool side 70 to 75°F, with a night temperature around 75 to 78°F. A heat source is required, and the commonly recommended choice is a low-wattage halogen basking bulb positioned over the substrate, on a thermostat or dimmer, rather than relying on a heat mat or ceramic heat emitter as the primary source.

You may see some general care sheets recommend an <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/44hAxJ1" product="Under-tank heat mat + thermostat kit">under-tank heat mat and thermostat kit</AffiliateLink> instead, this is a genuine point of disagreement in the hobby, but halogen basking bulbs are the more current, specialist-endorsed choice. Whichever heat source you choose, the thermostat itself is not optional.

## Humidity: Keep It Low

30 to 50% ambient humidity, genuinely on the dry side compared to many other pet snakes. Don't mist the whole enclosure to raise humidity, instead provide one <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3SKmfOx" product="Cork bark round hide">dedicated humid hide</AffiliateLink> for shedding, and only add moisture there as needed. Running the whole enclosure damp is a direct path to the respiratory infection and scale rot covered in our [Hognose Snake health issues guide](/blog/hognose-snake-health-issues-guide/).

A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3QHrM81" product="Digital thermometer/hygrometer combo">digital thermometer and hygrometer</AffiliateLink> is worth treating as core equipment here, not an optional extra, since this species' whole husbandry pattern hinges on keeping humidity confirmed low rather than guessed at.

## Substrate: Deep Enough to Actually Burrow In

This is a fossorial species that genuinely uses substrate depth, not just decoration. Provide 3 to 6 inches of loose, dry, diggable substrate, a soil and sand mix works well, roughly 70% soil-based substrate to 30% reptile-safe sand is a common ratio. <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3TkxAVF" product="Aspen shavings substrate">Aspen or coconut fiber substrate</AffiliateLink> also works well as the soil-based component. Keep it dry, damp substrate combined with this species' preference for arid conditions is a fast route to skin and respiratory problems.

Feed your snake off the substrate, in a separate container, to prevent the impaction risk covered in our [Hognose Snake health issues guide](/blog/hognose-snake-health-issues-guide/).

<FunFact>
Hognoses are one of the few pet snake species that are genuinely diurnal, active during the day rather than at night or dusk. That's part of why a low-output UVB bulb and a normal 12-hour light and dark cycle are increasingly recommended for this species, even though care sheets for nocturnal snakes like ball pythons rarely bother with lighting at all.
</FunFact>

## Lighting

Hognoses are active during the day, unlike many pet snakes, and benefit from a normal 12-hour light and dark cycle. UVB isn't strictly required by every source, but a low-output linear T5 bulb spanning part of the enclosure is increasingly recommended for this diurnal species.

## The Non-Obvious Requirement

Deep, dry, burrowable substrate isn't a nice-to-have here, it's close to the core of what this species needs to feel secure and behave normally. Combined with feeding off-substrate to prevent impaction, this is really the whole game with hognose husbandry. It's a drier, more active setup than a [corn snake](/blog/corn-snake-tank-setup-guide/) or [ball python](/blog/ball-python-tank-setup-guide/) needs, worth knowing if you're coming to hognoses from either of those species.
