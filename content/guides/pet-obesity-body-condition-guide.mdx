# Body Condition Score: How to Tell if Your Pet Is Actually Overweight

<VetDisclaimer />

Two numbers from the 2022 Association for Pet Obesity Prevention survey explain why this article exists.

**Fifty-nine percent of dogs and 61% of cats were classified as overweight or obese.** And among owners whose pet scored 6 to 9 on the body condition scale, meaning overweight or worse, **32% described that pet as normal, ideal or thin.**

The problem is not that owners do not care. It is that most people have never been shown what to look for, and a slightly heavy dog has become so normal that it now reads as the baseline.

<KeyTakeaway>
Obesity has a clinical definition, and it is the same for both species. Ten to 20% above ideal body weight is overweight. More than 20% above ideal is obese. Ideal body weight is an individual judgment, not a breed average, which is why body condition scoring exists as the practical tool.
</KeyTakeaway>

## The 9-Point Scale

A **body condition score**, usually written BCS, grades body fat by touch and sight rather than by the scale, because two dogs of identical weight can carry very different amounts of fat. Ideal is 5 out of 9 for cats, and 4 to 5 out of 9 for dogs.

Each point matters more than it sounds. One point above ideal is roughly a 5% increase in body fat, and about 10 to 15% more body weight. **That means a pet at 7 out of 9 is already sitting at around the obesity threshold.**

Even-numbered scores are defined as sharing the characteristics of the scores either side of them.

## The Hand Test

This is the single most usable thing in this article, and it needs no equipment.

Run both hands flat along your pet's ribcage, then compare what you feel to your own hand:

- **Back of your hand:** about right
- **Your knuckles**, or ribs you can see: too thin
- **The fleshy pad below your thumb:** carrying too much weight

Then two more checks. From the side, look for an **abdominal tuck**, where the belly rises toward the hind legs rather than running straight. From above, look for a **waist** behind the ribs.

<FunFact>
Body condition scoring becomes unreliable at the top of the scale. It is described in the veterinary literature as imprecise in extremely obese cats, because once the ribs cannot be felt at all, there is nothing left to grade by touch. At that point the scale has run out of resolution, which is one reason weight tracking matters alongside it.
</FunFact>

## What It Costs Them

The strongest evidence here is not an observational survey but a controlled lifetime trial. Kealy and colleagues followed **48 Labrador Retrievers, 24 pairs of littermates**, feeding one of each pair 25% less food from 8 weeks of age until death.

**A 1.8 year difference in median lifespan**, in genetically similar dogs, from food alone.

A separate study of 50,787 dogs across roughly 900 North American veterinary hospitals found reduced median lifespan in overweight dogs of every one of the 12 breeds studied, ranging from 5 months in male German Shepherds to **2 years 6 months in male Yorkshire Terriers**.

Worth noting a subtlety that a large osteoarthritis cohort of 131,140 dogs turned up: body condition score itself was a weak predictor of arthritis (hazard ratio 1.02), but **weight gain** was strong. A 50% increase in body weight was associated with arthritis arriving 16 months earlier in larger dogs. The trajectory matters more than the snapshot.

## Cats Are Not Small Dogs

Cats are **obligate carnivores**, meaning their metabolism requires nutrients found only in animal tissue. Adult cats need at least 26% crude protein on a dry matter basis against 18% for dogs, and must get vitamin A, arachidonic acid and taurine ready-made from the diet.

One difference matters enormously for dieting: **cats remain in continuous gluconeogenesis and cannot downregulate it during fasting.** Restrict a cat too aggressively and fat mobilizes from the body faster than the liver can process it, producing **hepatic lipidosis**, the most common acquired and potentially lethal liver disease of cats. Affected cats typically present having lost more than 25% of body weight. Fewer than 10% of cases are idiopathic, meaning over 90% are secondary to some other illness or stressor, and forced weight loss with unacceptable food substitutions is a named trigger.

The practical rule follows directly: **if a cat on a diet stops eating or eats much less than usual, pause the plan and call your vet.** That is not a wait-and-see situation.

Other feline specifics worth knowing:

- Obese cats aged 8 to 12 show a **2.8-fold increase in mortality** against lean cats.
- Neutered cats are more than three times as likely to be overweight, and preventing post-neuter gain requires a **30 to 40% reduction in daily calories**.
- Indoor-only cats carry an odds ratio of 1.18 for obesity against indoor/outdoor cats.
- Strictly indoor cats scoring an *ideal* body condition still had body fat of 23.6 to 38.4%, so the score can flatter a sedentary indoor cat.

Cornell names **free feeding**, leaving dry food out all day, as a major factor in feline obesity, and recommends discrete meals instead.

## The Labrador Gene

Some dogs really are hungrier. A **14 base pair deletion in the POMC gene** disrupts two peptides involved in satiety signaling, beta-MSH and beta-endorphin, so affected dogs do not receive proper fullness signals.

Roughly 22 to 25% of Labradors carry at least one copy. Each copy is associated with about **1.90 kg more body weight** and a **9.9% higher food motivation score**. Among Labradors used as assistance-dog breeding stock the allele frequency was 45%, which makes sense: a food-motivated dog is easier to train with food rewards, so the trait was selected for.

The mutation appears only in Labradors and the closely related Flat-Coated Retriever, where the allele frequency reaches 60%. The [Labrador enrichment guide](/blog/labrador-enrichment-guide/) is built around it.

## Measure With a Scale, Not a Cup

Two studies say the same thing, and the error runs toward overfeeding.

A University of Guelph study had 100 dog owners measure kibble with three common devices. Error ranged **from a 48% underestimate to a 152% overestimate**. A review of 12 studies on cup portioning found accuracy from an 18% underestimate to an 80% overestimate, with inaccuracy greatest for smaller portions, and found that **in four of six cups with manufacturer-printed graduations, the graduations themselves were wrong**, giving portions up to 40% bigger than expected.

A digital kitchen scale set to grams solves this outright.

**Treats should stay under 10% of daily calories**, because most are not complete and balanced and displacing real food with them causes nutrient imbalances. During a diet, the better tactic is to take treats out of the measured daily ration rather than adding them on top.

## Safe Rates of Weight Loss

Real programs run slower than the theoretical ceiling, and that is fine. Slow loss preserves lean body mass at the expense of fat, keeping metabolically active tissue intact.

Monthly weigh-ins matter, to confirm the rate is neither too fast nor too slow. For cats, Cornell suggests buying a baby scale to track at home.

Weight is the central management issue for the Labrador above all, then the [small breeds](/guides/dog-small-breed/) that live on treats, and the [Ragdoll](/guides/cat-ragdoll/), whose calm nature makes indoor weight gain a particular risk.
