# Common Veiled Chameleon Health Issues and What Causes Them

<VetDisclaimer />
<AffiliateDisclosure />

Veiled chameleons are hardy by chameleon standards, but a handful of conditions come up often enough, and one is specific to females and genuinely urgent, that every owner should recognize the signs. See our [Veiled Chameleon care guide](/guides/veiled-chameleon/) for the full picture, and our [tank setup guide](/blog/veiled-chameleon-tank-setup-guide/) for the husbandry side that prevents most of this list in the first place.

## Metabolic Bone Disease (MBD)

The most common and most preventable serious issue. Watch for a soft or bendable "rubber jaw," curved or broken limbs, tremors, and an inability to grip branches or properly shoot the tongue to catch food. This comes from inadequate <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3TiqeC4" product="UVB - Arcadia Forest 6% (ProT5 kit, 36in/39W)">UVB</AffiliateLink> and/or a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0D82LT16W?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Repashy Calcium Plus LoD 3 Oz JAR">calcium and vitamin D3 imbalance</AffiliateLink>.

<FunFact>
Research on juvenile veiled chameleons found that proper calcium and vitamin A supplementation prevented MBD even without added UVB or D3, underscoring how much diet matters alongside lighting. It's not an either-or, but it's a reminder that lighting alone can't cover for a poor supplement routine.
</FunFact>

Always see a vet, damage caught early is only partly reversible, and advanced cases are permanent.

## Egg-Binding (Dystocia)

This is the health issue every owner of a female veiled chameleon needs to know cold. Females produce egg clutches whether or not a male is present, and a female that can't pass her eggs is in genuine danger.

**Watch for:** restlessness, digging behavior without producing eggs, straining, lethargy, and swelling. Sky-blue spots appearing on the flanks are a normal sign after a female has successfully laid, worth knowing so you don't mistake healthy post-laying color for illness.

This is an emergency. Egg-binding can kill within 24 hours, and severe cases often require surgery. A permanent, always-available egg-laying bin, covered in our [tank setup guide](/blog/veiled-chameleon-tank-setup-guide/), and proper calcium supplementation are the real prevention here, not something to address only once a female shows signs of trouble. Our [cost guide](/blog/veiled-chameleon-cost-guide/) breaks down what medical management or surgery for this typically runs.

## Eye Problems

The second most commonly reported issue in this species, ranging from minor discharge to more serious turret abscesses and infections, often linked to vitamin A deficiency. Always see a vet.

## Respiratory Infection

Wheezing, gaping, and visible mucus point here, usually from temperatures running too cool or humidity that's off. A properly positioned <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4bqzQAS" product="Basking bulb (standard, 75W)">basking bulb</AffiliateLink> that holds a real thermal gradient goes a long way toward preventing it. See a vet for antibiotics if it develops.

## Dehydration and Kidney Disease

A frequently overlooked but genuinely serious risk. Veiled chameleons only recognize moving water droplets as drinkable, they don't reliably use a standing water dish, so inadequate misting leads directly to chronic dehydration and, over time, kidney disease, a leading cause of death in this species.
