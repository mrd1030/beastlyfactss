# Should You Handle a Praying Mantis? Gently, and Rarely

Praying mantises are calm around people and rarely bite, some can even be tamed to walk across your hands. That said, this is a genuinely fragile animal, and minimal, careful handling is the right approach, not frequent interaction.

## The Correct Approach

Let the mantis walk onto your hand or a tool rather than grabbing it. Handle only in a closed room, mantises can jump or fly a surprising distance, roughly twice their own body length, and losing one in an open space is a real and common risk. Never restrain or squeeze a mantis, their bodies are delicate in ways that aren't always obvious just looking at them.

<FunFact>
A praying mantis can jump or fly roughly twice its own body length in a single motion, which is exactly why handling sessions belong in a closed room. An open door or window turns a brief slip of the grip into a genuinely hard to recover from search.
</FunFact>

## The One Time to Never Handle at All

Avoid handling in the days leading up to a molt. A pre-molt mantis is at its most physically vulnerable, and handling during this window raises real risk of a failed or fatal molt. If your mantis has stopped eating and seems unusually still, treat that as a signal to leave it alone entirely, not to check on it by hand.

## Why Mantises Are Always Housed One Per Enclosure

This is genuinely one of the most important facts about this species, and it belongs in the handling conversation because it directly affects how you manage multiple animals if you ever keep more than one. Praying mantises are solitary and highly cannibalistic. They're never housed together, with one narrow exception: a brief, closely supervised introduction for mating, and even then, there's a real chance the female eats the male during or after the encounter. Nymphs need to be reared in separate containers from the moment they hatch, siblings will eat each other without hesitation.

## First-Timer Mistakes

- Housing two mantises together thinking they'll simply coexist
- Handling too frequently just because a mantis tolerates it well
- Handling during the vulnerable pre-molt period
- Leaving live feeder insects in the enclosure right before a molt, an insect can injure a mantis that's unable to defend itself normally during this time

## How This Compares to Stick Insects

Mantises are sometimes grouped with stick insects as similarly delicate arboreal invertebrates, but the comparison only goes so far. Stick insects are herbivores that can often be kept together in groups without issue. Mantises are predatory and cannibalistic, and that single difference is really the whole story behind why they're always kept alone. It's a related but distinct kind of fragility from a [tarantula](/blog/tarantula-handling-guide/), where the concern is a fatal fall rather than cage-mate aggression, both end up as look-more-than-touch pets for different reasons.
