# Green Anole Enclosure Setup

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Green anoles need more setup investment than their small size and low price suggest, tropical humidity, real UVB, and careful heat placement all matter here.

<Figure src="/assets/images/green-anole-tank-setup-guide-1.jpg" alt="Labeled diagram of a green anole enclosure showing a basking bulb mounted above the mesh, UVB and LED lighting, and climbing material at every level" caption="Green anoles need more setup investment than their small size and low price suggest, tropical humidity, real UVB, and careful heat placement all matter here." shareable />

## Enclosure Size

24 inches long by 24 inches wide by 24 inches tall as a minimum for one adult, larger is better. This species is semi-arboreal, so a tall, front-opening enclosure with good ventilation suits it well. Don't house multiple anoles together, they don't coexist peacefully, and the [enrichment guide](/blog/green-anole-enrichment-guide/) has the behavioral reason: an anole that cannot get out of another's sight has no way to end an encounter.

## Temperature

Basking spot around 90F, cool side 70 to 77F, nighttime 65 to 75F. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4bqzQAS" product="Basking bulb (standard, 75W)">60-watt incandescent basking bulb</AffiliateLink> in a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/44eEEFH" product="Basking dome/fixture">ceramic-socket dome</AffiliateLink> works well. A genuinely important safety detail, mount the heat lamp above or outside the enclosure's mesh top, not resting directly on it. Green anoles climb and rest inverted on mesh, and a bulb positioned where they can reach it risks real burns.

## Humidity

60 to 70% during the day, higher overnight. Achieve this through morning and evening misting with a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4vPKMjX" product="Fine mist spray bottle">pump sprayer</AffiliateLink>, and a cool-mist humidifier or fogger overnight in dry climates, using distilled water. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3QHrM81" product="Digital thermometer/hygrometer combo">thermometer/hygrometer combo</AffiliateLink> is how you'll actually confirm you're hitting that range rather than guessing.

## Substrate

Coconut fiber or a tropical soil blend, roughly 60% organic topsoil to 40% coconut fiber, works well, layered about 2 inches deep with leaf litter on top. Avoid gravel, sand, and anything containing vermiculite or pesticide residue. A coconut fiber substrate covers the base of that mix.

## Live Plants

Live plants do double duty here: they are the perches and cover an arboreal anole moves through, and they hold humidity. Safe choices include pothos, philodendron, dracaena, ficus, hibiscus, spider plant, staghorn fern, bromeliads and air plants. A mix works best: pothos and philodendron trail across the back and sides, a dracaena or ficus gives upright stems to climb and perch on, and bromeliads collect droplets after misting, which suits a lizard that drinks from leaves. Rinse new plants and swap their potting soil for the enclosure substrate before planting: the same no-vermiculite, no-pesticide rule that governs the substrate applies to what comes in the pot.

## Cleaning

Remove droppings and uneaten insects daily, scooping out any soiled substrate and replacing it, and wash the water dish at the same time. Replace the substrate completely every 3 to 4 months. In a planted enclosure that full change is also the time to trim plants back and wipe the glass. Wash hands after every session in the enclosure, and clean equipment in a utility sink, since the [same Salmonella rules](/blog/reptile-salmonella-hygiene-guide/) apply to an anole as to any larger reptile.

## Lighting

UVB is genuinely necessary for this diurnal species. Target a UV index of 3.0 to 4.0 at the basking branch, using an Arcadia ShadeDweller kit or a T5 HO bulb in the 5 to 6% range, paired with a separate 6500K LED for general brightness and to support live plants. Run a 10 to 14 hour photoperiod.

<FunFact>
Since the warmest spot in a green anole's enclosure sits up near the basking lamp rather than down on the floor, this species spends much of its day moving vertically just to regulate its temperature, which is exactly why climbing structure has to run the full height of the enclosure, not just the bottom.
</FunFact>

## Diet Basics

Insectivores, and small ones. Crickets, dubia and discoid nymphs, red runner and red head roaches, flightless fruit flies, black soldier fly larvae and the occasional mealworm all work, with fruit flies carrying hatchlings and small juveniles.

The portion is the number most care sheets leave out. A juvenile eats daily, as much as it will take; an adult gets two to three feeders every other day, each slightly smaller than the anole's head, since this is a small lizard with a small mouth and an oversized insect is a real choking and stress risk rather than a theoretical one.

Gut-load and hydrate feeders for 24 to 48 hours before they go in, then dust them. The simplest approach is a light dusting of a 50/50 calcium and multivitamin mix at every feeding, or a single all-in-one calcium-plus-vitamin powder used the same way. A small animal develops a nutritional deficiency fast, so consistency here matters more than it would for a larger lizard.

## The Non-Obvious Requirement: Climbing Material at Every Level

Provide branches and foliage spanning the enclosure's full height, not concentrated near the bottom. Since the warmest area sits at the top near the basking lamp, your anole needs a genuine path to thermoregulate by moving vertically, exactly why the mesh-burn risk mentioned above matters so much, and why climbing structure throughout the enclosure isn't just decorative, it's functional.
