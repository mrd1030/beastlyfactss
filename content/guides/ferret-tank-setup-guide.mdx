# Ferret Cage Setup (And Why the Cage Is Only Half the Job)

<AffiliateDisclosure />

A ferret's cage matters, but it's only part of what this species needs. Ferret-proofing the room they play in is just as essential, and it's the part first-time owners most often underestimate.

<Figure src="/assets/images/ferret-tank-setup-guide-1.jpg" alt="Labeled diagram of a multi-level ferret cage showing the covered ramp, fabric hammock, litter box, and cool room temperature" caption="Ferrets regulate body temperature poorly and are genuinely heat-sensitive, the real danger here is overheating, not a chilly room." shareable />

## Cage Size

A large, multi-level wire cage is the standard. For a pair, aim for at least 30 inches long by 24 inches wide by 48 inches tall, adding roughly 4 additional square feet of floor space for each ferret beyond that. The double-unit style multi-level cage, like the <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B001NJ0DAY?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Critter Nation by MidWest Homes for Pets Double Unit Cage">Critter Nation</AffiliateLink> (sold under the Ferret Nation branding for this species, same cage line), with solid levels connected by a covered ramp is the most consistently recommended setup among experienced owners and exotic vets, it offers real climbing and exploring space, not just a place to sleep. Bar spacing needs to be one inch or less. Avoid bare wire flooring and any mesh a claw could catch in.

## Temperature

Ferrets regulate their body temperature poorly and are heat-sensitive, this matters more than cold sensitivity does. Keep the room in the 50 to 75°F range, and treat anything above about 80 to 85°F as a real heatstroke risk. No supplemental heat source is needed indoors, the priority is avoiding overheating, not adding warmth. That bias toward cool over warm runs deep in the family, our [wolverine facts guide](/blog/wolverine-facts-the-toughest-animal-pound-for-pound/) covers a relative whose coat is structured so frost and snow won't cling to it at all, and while a pet ferret is nowhere near that hardy, it inherited far more cold tolerance than heat tolerance.

<FunFact>
Most exotic pets need help staying warm enough. Ferrets flip that assumption entirely, the real danger is overheating, not a chilly room, which is exactly why this species needs no heat lamp, no heat mat, and no basking spot anywhere in its setup.
</FunFact>

## Lighting

No special lighting is required, but the day and night cycle genuinely matters for this species. Prolonged exposure to artificial light is one of the factors linked to adrenal disease risk, our [adrenal disease guide](/blog/ferret-adrenal-disease-guide/) covers the hair-loss pattern it causes, so provide a normal light cycle and a dark, quiet sleeping area rather than constant illumination.

## The Part That Matters as Much as the Cage: Ferret-Proofing

A cage alone doesn't meet this species' needs. Ferrets need several hours of supervised out-of-cage play daily, and the room they play in needs real preparation: block every gap larger than about one inch, secure cabinets and low openings, and remove any rubber or foam items they could chew and swallow, this is directly tied to the GI blockage risk, not a minor precaution. Swapping in chew-safe enrichment, like a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0F37K1Z91?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Small Pet Select Tiny Paws Seagrass Play Pack">seagrass play pack</AffiliateLink>, gives your ferret something to dig into that isn't a blockage risk. Assume your ferret will find the one gap you missed, because a talent for getting into, under, and out of things is a family trait, our [honey badger facts guide](/blog/honey-badgers-the-toughest-little-troublemakers-on-the-planet/) covers a relative that learned to unscrew bolts and stack debris into steps to climb out of a rehab enclosure.

## Social Needs

Ferrets are genuinely social animals and tend to do better in pairs or small groups than kept alone, worth factoring into your setup planning from the start rather than adding a second ferret later as an afterthought.
