# Four Pets, Four Things Nobody Mentions Before You Buy

Every pet has a detail that matters more than the sales pitch, something the low price tag, the cute reputation, or the simple appearance doesn't tell you. Three of these four hide a real complication. One hides a genuine perk.

## Savannah Monitor: Cheap Because It's Overfed, Not Because It's Easy

A low purchase price and a "beginner monitor" reputation hide the real story: this species is notoriously overfed in captivity, and fatty liver disease kills far more pet savannah monitors young than anything genetically wrong with them. What nobody mentions upfront: an adult is 3 to 4 feet and 8 to 15 pounds, genuinely more animal than the price suggests.

## Russian Tortoise: The Good Surprise

Here's the one piece of good news in this batch. Unlike most tortoises, this species can live its entire multi-decade life indoors, no eventual outdoor enclosure required. If you want the tortoise-keeping experience without ever needing a yard, this is the species nobody tells you about.

## Cockatoo: The Surrender Statistics the Pet Store Won't Bring Up

A cuddly baby cockatoo doesn't advertise what one behavior consultant reports directly: cockatoo owners make up over half her practice, and rescues consistently name this species their most surrendered bird. Separation anxiety, extreme screaming, and feather-destructive behavior are the norm here, not the exception, and nobody mentions that at the point of sale.

## Stick Insect: Cheap, Gentle, and Legally Complicated

The animal itself is about as low-stakes as pets get, a few dollars, minimal care, a genuinely social species you can keep in groups. What rarely gets mentioned: most commonly sold stick insects are technically regulated under a federal permit system that hobbyists can't realistically satisfy. Worth knowing before you buy, not after, and the full legal breakdown is covered in the cost guide below.

## The Common Thread

Three of these four hide a real cost, financial, behavioral, or legal, behind an easy first impression. The fourth hides a genuine advantage behind an assumption that all tortoises need a yard eventually. Either way, the detail that actually matters is never the one on the price tag.
