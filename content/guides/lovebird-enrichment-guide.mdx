# Lovebird Enrichment: Foraging, Toys, and What Cuts Boredom

<AffiliateDisclosure />

A wild lovebird spends most of the waking day foraging. Recreate that search in the cage or you get the boredom behaviors this species is known for.

<KeyTakeaway>
Foraging is the behavior a food bowl deletes. Parrots will often choose to work for food over a free dish. Hide the food.
</KeyTakeaway>

## Foraging comes first

Hide a portion of the daily diet inside a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4fFoJqn" product="Kyouki Natural Foraging Box Toy">foraging box or puzzle toy</AffiliateLink>. Paper cups with pellets inside also work. Rotate the method every few days so it stays a puzzle.

## Toy rotation

3 to 5 toys at a time, rotated weekly. Mix categories: something to forage in, something to shred, something to chew. A toy that has been out of the cage for a few weeks often reads as new again.

<FunFact>
Boredom in captive parrots is not just sitting still. It shows up as plucking, pacing, and screaming past normal vocalizing. Consistent enrichment cuts those behaviors. Occasional enrichment does not.
</FunFact>

## Shred and chew

Untreated paper, cardboard tubes, and bird-safe wood. Balsa disappears fast. Keep a rotating supply rather than one expensive toy that is gone in a day.

## Social and cognitive work

A bonded pair cuts loneliness. Both birds still need their own foraging and shredding. A single lovebird needs daily supervised out-of-cage time and direct contact. Short [positive reinforcement training](/blog/parrot-training-guide/) sessions count as work, not just entertainment.

## Warning signs

Plucking, pacing the same perch, screaming that is new or louder. Those can also be medical. Do the vet check alongside the cage change.
