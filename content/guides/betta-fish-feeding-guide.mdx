# Betta Fish Feeding: Schedule, Diet, and Why They Stop Eating

Bettas are easy to overfeed and dramatic about refusing food. Here is the routine on this site.

## Feeding schedule

Twice a day. Two to four pellets a meal, or whatever the fish clears in about a minute. Nothing left on the substrate. One fasting day a week with no food.

Portion matters more than adding a third meal. A betta's stomach is roughly the size of its own eyeball. That is the basis for 2 to 4 pellets. Larger pellets mean fewer of them.

Fry start on infusoria several times a day, then 4 to 5 small meals as they become free-swimming, then 3 to 4 times a day by weeks 3 to 4, then 2 to 3 times a day as juveniles. Once they have their own space, they move onto the adult routine.

## What bettas eat

Obligate carnivores. In the wild they snatch insects, larvae, and small crustaceans off the surface. The staple is a betta-specific high-protein pellet, not tropical flakes or goldfish pellets. Look for dried meats like brine shrimp, krill, or fish meal near the top of the list, and roughly 30% protein or higher. If the pellet expands in water, pre-soak it.

Treats 1 to 3 times a week: frozen, freeze-dried, or live bloodworms, brine shrimp, daphnia, tubifex, mysis, mosquito larvae. Bloodworms stay at 1 to 2 times a week, 1 to 2 worms per meal. Rehydrate freeze-dried food first.

## Safe treats and foods to avoid

Safe in moderation: bloodworms, brine shrimp, daphnia, tubifex, mysis, mosquito larvae, and a blanched shelled pea now and then for constipation, not as a regular food.

Never: bread, onion, garlic, salt, sugary foods, processed meats, citrus, dairy.

## Why bettas stop eating

1. **Environmental stress.** A new tank, a move, [handling](/blog/betta-fish-handling-guide/), or an aggressive tankmate.
2. **Poor water quality.** Ammonia, nitrite, or nitrate spikes. Check this first.
3. **Water that's too cold.** 76 to 82F. Cold water slows metabolism and appetite. Room temperature without a heater is usually too cold.
4. **Picky eating.** A new brand can get refused. Try a different food before assuming illness.
5. **Overfeeding, constipation, or swim bladder trouble.** A bloated belly, stringy feces, floating or sinking unnaturally.
6. **Illness.** White spots, clamped fins, or labored swimming alongside the refusal.

## When to worry

A healthy adult can last about two weeks without food. That is a ceiling. At 5 to 7 days of refused food, after a food change and a water check, look harder. Sooner if the belly is swollen, feces are stringy or absent, fins are clamped, there are white spots, the spine is curved, or the fish is floating, sinking, or laboring.

A weekend on an automatic feeder is low risk. The line is a week-plus with any of those signs.
