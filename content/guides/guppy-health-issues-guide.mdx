# Common Guppy Health Issues (Water Quality Is the Root of Almost Everything)

<VetDisclaimer />
<AffiliateDisclosure />

Nearly everything on this list connects back to one root cause: water quality. Guppies are hardy fish, but stressed, poorly maintained water opens the door to every condition below. Our [Guppy tank setup guide](/blog/guppy-tank-setup-guide/) covers the prevention side in detail, and our full [Guppy care guide](/guides/guppy/) covers everything else.

## Ich (White Spot Disease)

Small white spots resembling grains of salt across the body and fins, along with flashing (rubbing against tank decor), rapid breathing, and lethargy. Caused by stress, often from temperature swings or poor water quality. Treat by gradually raising the tank temperature to around 82°F alongside a commercial ich medication.

<FunFact>
One guppy-specific note worth remembering: this species doesn't tolerate aggressive salt treatment well, even though salting is a common home remedy for ich in other freshwater fish. Reach for a proper medication instead of heavy salting with guppies specifically.
</FunFact>

## Velvet

A fine, gold or rust-colored dusty coating on the body, along with clamped fins and scratching against decor. From poor water conditions or low temperature. Treat with a copper-based medication, raise the temperature slightly, and dim the tank lights during treatment. This is contagious between fish, quarantine affected individuals if possible.

## Fin and Tail Rot

Ragged, frayed, or discolored fin edges, bacterial in origin, usually from poor water quality, ammonia buildup, or an untreated injury. Improving water quality alongside an antibacterial treatment resolves most cases caught early. Left unaddressed, it can progress and become systemic.

## Dropsy

Bloating combined with scales that raise into a pinecone-like pattern. This is a visible sign of advanced internal or organ failure, usually from a systemic bacterial infection that's gone unaddressed for some time. This is the hardest condition on this list to treat successfully, and often carries a poor outlook by the time it's visibly obvious.

## Swim Bladder Disorder

Buoyancy problems, floating, sinking, or swimming at odd angles, usually from overfeeding or constipation. Often manageable at home with a short fast and a return to normal, appropriately sized feeding afterward.
