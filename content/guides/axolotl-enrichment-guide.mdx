# Axolotl Enrichment: Hides, Foraging, and Safe Novelty

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Enrichment for an axolotl isn't about toys, it's about choice: somewhere to rest out of open water, cover that feels secure, and food that takes a little searching instead of arriving in the same spot every time.

<KeyTakeaway>
Every single item in an axolotl tank has to be smooth and stable. No eyelids and delicate external gills mean the bar for "safe" is unusually high.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What Has Been Tested

Axolotl enrichment has at least one direct study. Billingham, writing in Animal Technology and Welfare in 2017, gave laboratory axolotls rocks, plants, plastic shelters, old compact discs and tunnels, and recorded what they did with them. The animals nudged and rubbed against the items and moved them around, in one case shifting a CD into a position that made a shelter. Of everything offered, tunnels came out as the item most worth adding to axolotl housing.

Research colonies that breed axolotls long term land on the same short list: artificial or natural plants and places to hide, in tanks with controlled temperature and a set day length. In one Mexican vivarium, natural breeding followed a cool spell of 12 to 14°C with about 11 hours of light, which is a reminder that the light cycle is part of the environment too. A timer on a dim light, on the same schedule every day, costs nothing.

## Hides and Structure

Provide at least 2 hides per adult, ideally 3 to 4 of different shapes and heights, and make at least one of them a tunnel, the tested favorite. Sideways terracotta pots with the drainage hole sealed, wide PVC pipe sections with the edges fully sanded, and smooth <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4dsuNBx" product="Resin Hide Cave Aquarium Decoration">resin or ceramic caves</AffiliateLink> all work well. Live or silk plants add cover too, java fern and anubias tied to decor rather than buried, with marimo moss balls kept larger than the axolotl's head. A gentle current on one side from a baffled filter, paired with a genuinely still-water refuge on the other, lets the axolotl choose its own water movement.

## Foraging

Static feeding, dropping food in the same spot and walking away, skips real hunting behavior entirely. Tong-feeding or scattering nightcrawler pieces so the axolotl has to locate them is a simple upgrade. A sand saucer, a shallow dish of very fine sand under 1mm with small food items hidden in it, gives a contained way to add searching behavior without loose sand across the whole tank. Rotate feeding locations every few days.

## Sensory Novelty

<FunFact>
Axolotls have no eyelids and generally prefer dim, low light. Floating plants or subdued lighting create the dappled shade they're actually drawn to, rather than a brightly lit tank.
</FunFact>

A mild monthly rearrange of non-critical decor, plants, stones, extra tunnels, adds spatial novelty without removing the hides an axolotl already relies on. Introducing one new safe item for 5 to 7 days at a time, then rotating it out, works the same way. Skip strong currents and bright lighting, neither is enrichment for this species, both are just stress.

## Safety Rules

Every edge sanded or factory-smooth, and nothing small enough to swallow. Axolotls are extremely sensitive to current and can stop eating under a strong one, so any new item that changes the flow gets watched for a day. Skip nets for routine handling, they snag on gills and toes. Temperature has to stay in the [low to mid 60s F](/blog/cooling-an-aquarium-without-a-chiller-guide/) for the animal to be healthy at all, well before enrichment is a relevant question.
