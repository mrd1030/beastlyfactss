# Axolotl Enrichment: Hides, Foraging, and Safe Novelty

Enrichment for an axolotl isn't about toys, it's about choice: somewhere to rest out of open water, cover that feels secure, and food that takes a little searching instead of arriving in the same spot every time. For everything this doesn't cover, see our complete [Axolotl care guide](/guides/axolotl/).

<KeyTakeaway>
Every single item in an axolotl tank has to be smooth and stable. No eyelids, delicate external gills, and a real impaction risk from loose or sharp material mean the bar for "safe" is higher here than for almost any other pet on this site.
</KeyTakeaway>

## Hides and Structure

Provide at least 2 hides per adult, ideally 3 to 4 of different shapes and heights. Sideways terracotta pots with the drainage hole sealed, wide PVC pipe sections with the edges fully sanded, and smooth <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4dsuNBx" product="Resin Hide Cave Aquarium Decoration">resin or ceramic caves</AffiliateLink> all work well. Live or silk plants add cover too, java fern and anubias tied to decor rather than buried, with marimo moss balls kept larger than the axolotl's head. A gentle current on one side from a baffled filter, paired with a genuinely still-water refuge on the other, lets the axolotl choose its own water movement.

## Foraging

Static feeding, dropping food in the same spot and walking away, skips real hunting behavior entirely. Tong-feeding or scattering nightcrawler pieces so the axolotl has to locate them is a simple upgrade. A sand saucer, a shallow dish of very fine sand under 1mm with small food items hidden in it, gives a contained way to add searching behavior without loose sand across the whole tank. Rotate feeding locations every few days, and never use gravel or coarse sand anywhere in the enclosure, impaction is a leading cause of preventable death in this species.

## Sensory Novelty

<FunFact>
Axolotls have no eyelids and generally prefer dim, low light. Floating plants or subdued lighting create the dappled shade they're actually drawn to, rather than a brightly lit tank.
</FunFact>

A mild monthly rearrange of non-critical decor, plants, stones, extra tunnels, adds spatial novelty without removing the hides an axolotl already relies on. Introducing one new safe item for 5 to 7 days at a time, then rotating it out, works the same way. Skip strong currents and bright lighting, neither is enrichment for this species, both are just stress.

## Safety Rules

No gravel, no coarse sand, no small loose stones anywhere in the tank. Every edge sanded or factory-smooth. Skip nets for routine handling, they snag on gills and toes. Temperature has to stay in the low to mid 60s F for the animal to be healthy at all, well before enrichment is a relevant question.
