# Chinchilla Feeding: Schedule, Diet, and Why They Stop Eating

<AffiliateDisclosure />

A chinchilla eating its own droppings overnight is how this species finishes a fibrous diet. Do not stop it.

## Feeding schedule

Unlimited grass hay, 24 hours, refreshed daily. Discard uneaten hay so it does not mold. On top of that, 1 to 2 tablespoons a day of a plain chinchilla pellet, once daily or split morning and evening.

Kits nurse and may nibble hay and pellets early. Wean around 8 weeks if the kit has also hit about 200 grams. Treats a few times a week, under about 10% of intake.

## What chinchillas eat

Unlimited dust-extracted, mold-free grass hay. <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4h9WW2u" product="Oxbow Western Timothy Hay, 90 oz">Timothy</AffiliateLink> is the hay on this site. Alfalfa is for pregnant, nursing, or still-growing animals, not an everyday adult staple. Higher calcium and protein in alfalfa track with bladder stones.

Then a small daily ration of <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B007SYZCOA?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Oxbow Essentials Chinchilla Food, 10 lb">chinchilla-specific pellets</AffiliateLink>. Not rabbit or guinea pig formulas.

Produce stays rare. Dark leafy greens such as romaine, plus bell pepper or celery, only if you introduce them over several days. A sudden swap of hay, pellets, or vegetables is a documented trigger for gut trouble.

## Safe treats and foods to avoid

Small amounts, a few times a week: plain rolled oats, a small wheat biscuit piece, dried dandelion, dried nettles, rosehips, a small piece of apple or pear once a week, a couple of blueberries. Chews: applewood, pearwood, elm, birch, cholla.

Never: nuts and seeds, dried fruit, muesli mixes, chocolate, onion, garlic, avocado, dairy, asparagus, rhubarb including leaves. Never cedar, plum, redwood, or cherry wood. Skip grapes, banana, and lettuce.

## Why chinchillas stop eating

1. **Dental disease.** Overgrown teeth or spurs. Dropped food and a wet chin.
2. **GI stasis.** Low hay, dehydration, dental pain, or a sudden diet change. Can run from mild to severe inside hours to a day.
3. **Heat stress.** They cannot sweat or pant well. The [housing guide](/blog/chinchilla-tank-setup-guide/) has the temperature targets. Appetite drops before open-mouth panting.
4. **Illness.** Wheezing or crackling with the refusal.
5. **Stress.** A new home, noise, a lost companion. Often settles in 3 to 5 days.
6. **A sudden diet change.** Transition over 5 to 7 days.
7. **Pain elsewhere.** Stones, a UTI, injury.
8. **Dehydration.** A clogged bottle shows up as less eating.

## When to worry

More than 12 to 24 hours off food is a same-day vet. No droppings for about 12 hours is the same call. Check both every day.

Red flags: a bloated or tense abdomen, teeth grinding, drooling, labored breathing, a hunched posture, lethargy, weight loss. GI stasis can go critical in under 24 hours. Fluids, motility medication, and syringe feeding work when they start soon.
