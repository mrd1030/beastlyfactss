# Gerbil Enclosure Setup: Depth Is Everything

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Gerbil care comes down to one detail more than any other: providing genuinely deep substrate for a species built to dig real tunnels, not just burrow superficially. For everything else about day-to-day care, see our complete [Gerbil care guide](/guides/gerbil/), or our companion guides on [cost](/blog/gerbil-cost-guide/), [handling](/blog/gerbil-handling-guide/), and [health issues](/blog/gerbil-health-issues-guide/).

## Enclosure Type and Size

A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eSbKl2" product="20 gallon long tank">glass aquarium</AffiliateLink> with a secure <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B000QFQ300?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Zilla Fresh Air Screen Cover, Metal Mesh Terrarium Lid, 30 x 12 inches">wire-mesh lid</AffiliateLink> is the right choice here, not a wire cage, wire construction simply can't hold the depth of bedding this species needs. A 20-gallon long tank works as a minimum for a pair, a 40-gallon breeder, roughly 36 by 18 by 16 inches, is genuinely the better target. Add more space for each additional gerbil beyond a pair.

## Temperature

Normal room temperature, 65 to 75°F, suits this species well without any special heating or cooling.

## Humidity

Keep it under about 50%, with good ventilation. Excess humidity causes the coat matting and skin issues covered in the [health issues guide](/blog/gerbil-health-issues-guide/), this is a species adapted to naturally arid conditions.

## Substrate: The Defining Requirement

This deserves real emphasis, since it's genuinely the most commonly under-delivered element of gerbil care. Provide 6 to 10 inches of digging depth at minimum, some welfare-focused sources recommend even more, so your gerbils can build real tunnel systems, not just surface-level burrows. A mix of <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3TkxAVF" product="Aspen shavings substrate">aspen shavings</AffiliateLink> and <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4voH9Ap" product="Small-pet paper bedding">paper-based bedding</AffiliateLink>, with some hay worked in, holds tunnel shape well.

<FunFact>
6 to 10 inches is the floor, not the ceiling, on how deep this substrate should run, and it's a genuinely different scale than what most starter kits ship with. A gerbil digging in 2 inches of shavings on a glass floor is going through the motions of a behavior it can't actually complete, real tunnel-building needs real depth to work with.
</FunFact>

**Avoid cedar entirely** (its natural oils are toxic to this species), skip aromatic non-kiln-dried pine, and avoid scented bedding and fluffy cotton-style nesting material, which can cause blockage or entanglement.

## Lighting

A normal day and night cycle is all this species needs, no UVB requirement.

## The Non-Obvious Requirements

**A solid-surface exercise wheel, 8 inches minimum, 10 to 12 inches is genuinely better**, a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08FC8JG1B?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Niteangel Super-Silent Hamster Exercise Wheel, 10 inch">solid wheel</AffiliateLink> sized this way works well, wire-rung wheels are a real injury risk. Keep some old, familiar-smelling nesting material aside during cage cleanouts and reintroduce it afterward, gerbils are strongly scent-driven, and a completely fresh-smelling environment after a full clean can cause temporary stress or even conflict between cage mates. Provide constant untreated wood or cardboard for gnawing alongside the depth this species genuinely needs to feel secure.
