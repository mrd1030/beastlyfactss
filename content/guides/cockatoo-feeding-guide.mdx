# Cockatoo Feeding: Schedule, Diet, and the Ratio Worth Memorizing

Cockatoo feeding comes down to a ratio worth memorizing: pellets should dominate the bowl, and seeds should stay firmly in treat territory, not the other way around. For the rest of their care needs, see our complete [Cockatoo care guide](/guides/cockatoo/).

## The Staple Diet

<AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4vEqngK" product="Lafeber's Premium Daily Diet Pellets for Parrots">Pelleted food</AffiliateLink> should make up 75 to 80% of your cockatoo's daily diet. The remaining 20 to 40% comes from fresh fruits and vegetables, dark leafy greens like kale and bok choy, plus carrots, bell peppers, broccoli, squash, and peas all work well. A couple of tree nuts, almonds, walnuts, or Brazil nuts, make a fine daily treat and double as genuine enrichment for a bird this intelligent.

## Feeding Frequency and Portions

Offer fresh food daily, and keep fresh water available at all times, cleaning both food and water dishes every day. A useful mental picture from veterinary guidance: a small handful of food for your cockatoo is roughly equivalent to a full dinner plate for a person, it's easy to over-portion without realizing it. Fruits and vegetables shouldn't sit in the cage more than a couple of hours before spoiling. If you're converting a seed-eating adult bird onto pellets, do it gradually and weigh your bird periodically during the transition to make sure intake stays adequate.

## Foods to Avoid

<KeyTakeaway>
Avocado, chocolate, caffeine, and alcohol are all toxic and can be fatal, keep these away entirely. Fruit pits and apple seeds release cyanide-related compounds, and onion and garlic can cause a serious blood disorder in birds.
</KeyTakeaway>

Beyond acute toxins, the bigger long-term risk is simply too much seed, sunflower seeds and peanuts especially are high in fat and should stay occasional treats, not a dietary foundation. Skip grit or gravel too, cockatoos already hull seeds before swallowing them, and grit can cause a genuinely dangerous digestive blockage.

## Supplementation

A bird eating a genuinely complete pelleted diet at 75 to 80% generally doesn't need additional vitamin or mineral supplements, that formulation is already fortified to cover it. Supplements matter more for a bird still transitioning off an all-seed diet, and any specific supplementation decision, egg-laying hens needing extra calcium, for instance, is worth making with your avian vet rather than adding something preventively.

## The Mistakes That Actually Matter

A seed-heavy diet is the central feeding mistake with this species, and it's directly linked to fatty liver disease, obesity, and vitamin A deficiency.

<FunFact>
High-fat diets specifically are also tied to lipomas, fatty skin tumors that show up notably often in overweight cockatoos and several other parrot species, while birds actually eating their pellets develop them far less. And even on a good base diet, regular calorie-dense treats or an overfed hand-raised baby can still tip a cockatoo toward obesity and the same fatty liver risk, portion awareness matters even when the food itself is right.
</FunFact>
