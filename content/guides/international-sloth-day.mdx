A three-toed sloth descends from the canopy about once a week to defecate at the base of a tree, which is the most dangerous thing it does. On the ground it cannot run and cannot climb, and a large share of sloth deaths happen during that trip. It does it anyway, and the reason has to do with the moths living in its fur.

The day named after the animal has a smaller problem, and one that is easier to fix. Most of the internet has it on the wrong date.

## The Date Is a Rule, Not a Number

International Sloth Day is the third Saturday in October. That is how AIUNAU, the Colombian wildlife foundation that created the day in 2010, states it on their own site, and they are the authority on the question because they invented it.

A floating date lands on a different number every year. 17 October in 2026. 16 October in 2027. 21 October in 2028.

What happened instead is that somebody wrote down 20 October, which was the third Saturday in 2013, and the number got copied. It is now printed as a fixed annual date by several large calendar sites, which means it is wrong in roughly six years out of every seven. In 2026 the gap is three days and a weekday: their 20 October is a Tuesday, and the day is a Saturday.

This is the ordinary failure mode of awareness-day calendars. They copy each other, a rule gets flattened into one year's answer, and the error then appears in enough places to look verified.

## What the Day Commemorates

AIUNAU set it against the first international meeting on sloth welfare and conservation, held in Medellín in November 2010. Sarita Kendall and Tinka Plese organised the observance out of it.

That origin explains the day's shape. It is a rehabilitation and welfare day before it is a conservation day, which suits the organisation that made it: AIUNAU's work is largely with confiscated and injured animals coming out of the illegal wildlife trade. Sloths are taken for the pet trade and, more commonly in tourist areas, passed around for photographs, which they tolerate quietly because tolerating things quietly is the only defense they have. It is the same trade that made [the pangolin the most trafficked wild mammal on Earth](/beastlypedia/pangolin/), running on a species nobody thinks of as a victim of it.

## The Weekly Descent, and Why It Happens

The trip down is the sloth fact that has an actual answer behind it, and the answer arrived in 2014.

Jonathan Pauli's team at Wisconsin published a study in Proceedings of the Royal Society B describing a three-way arrangement between the sloth, the moths that live in its coat, and the algae growing in its fur. When the sloth descends and defecates, moths lay eggs in the dung. Moths colonise the fur. Dead moths decomposing in the coat raise its nitrogen content, nitrogen feeds algal growth, and the sloth eats the algae, which is digestible and rich in lipids in a way that leaves are not.

The descent looks like a design fault until the fur is counted as a garden. Three-toed sloths carry more moths, more nitrogen and more algae than the two-toed species, which are less specialized and less committed to the trip. [The full mechanism, and what it costs the animal](/blog/why-a-sloth-climbs-down-to-defecate/), is worth the detour.

## The One That Needs the Day

Sloths as a group are in reasonable shape. Four of the six living species are Least Concern, which is unusual enough among tropical mammals to be worth stating plainly.

The exception is the pygmy three-toed sloth, and it is a severe one. It lives on Isla Escudo de Veraguas, a Panamanian island under five square kilometers, and nowhere else on Earth. It is a mangrove specialist, and surveys have put the mangrove habitat it depends on at under two hectares in total. Population estimates run from a few hundred to a few thousand with wide error bars, the IUCN lists it as Critically Endangered, and it appears on the list of the world's 100 most threatened species.

An animal restricted to one island with two hectares of the habitat it eats from does not need a bad year. It needs one storm.

That is the case for getting the date right, such as it is. A day that fires on a Tuesday when the events are on a Saturday is a day nobody turns up to, and the [sloth](/beastlypedia/sloth/) has a thin enough margin as it is.
