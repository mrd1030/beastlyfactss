# How Much Does a Lovebird Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Lovebirds are one of the more affordable parrots to buy, and the name suggests something gentler than what you actually get. This is a small bird with a real capacity for territorial behavior, which matters for cost planning as much as temperament. For the full care picture beyond budgeting, see our complete [Lovebird care guide](/guides/lovebird/).

## The Bird Itself: $50 to $400

Common peach-faced or Fischer's lovebirds run $50 to $150. Rare color mutations, lutino, blue, pied, and combinations, run $200 to $400 or more, with exceptional individuals reaching $1,000. Adoption typically runs $20 to $100.

Hand-raised birds cost more than parent-raised ones, and they're generally the better choice if you want a tame, interactive pet. A parent-raised bird can still make a fine companion, but expect more work getting there.

The cage is worth spending real money on. A quality stainless or powder-coated option runs $80 to $200. Avoid the $30 to $50 range entirely, cheap cages rust, and a rusting cage introduces a genuine zinc toxicity risk, a real veterinary emergency, not a minor concern. See our [Lovebird cage setup guide](/blog/lovebird-tank-setup-guide/) for the bar spacing and material details that matter most in choosing one.

That "sleep tent" line item deserves an honest note. Most products sold under that name are enclosed pouch or hut-shaped bird tents, and for a lovebird specifically, that shape is a real problem, not a style preference. A plain, breathable cage cover for nighttime darkness serves the same purpose without the risk. Skip tunnel-shaped, nest-like "happy hut" products marketed as bird tents, they're a documented trigger for hormonal, territorial behavior and chronic egg-laying in this species. Our [Lovebird handling guide](/blog/lovebird-handling-guide/) and [Lovebird cage setup guide](/blog/lovebird-tank-setup-guide/) cover exactly why in full.

## Vet Costs

A routine avian checkup runs $25 to $50, recommended once or twice a year, and annual care can add up to a couple hundred dollars total. Emergencies are the real budgeting risk here: egg-binding or an injury can run into the thousands. An emergency fund is genuinely worth having for this species, and our [Lovebird health issues guide](/blog/lovebird-health-issues-guide/) covers what tends to drive those emergency visits.

## Lifespan

10 to 15 years typically, up to 20 with excellent care. This is a multi-decade relationship for a bird often sold as a casual, low-commitment pet, worth sitting with before you buy.

## The Cost Decision That Actually Matters Most: One Bird or Two

This is where lovebird budgeting gets interesting, and it isn't really about money at all. A single, hand-raised lovebird bonds to you and becomes genuinely tame and interactive, but needs real daily time investment, roughly two hours of interaction a day. A bonded pair bonds to each other instead, largely ignoring you, entertaining to watch but rarely tame or reliable to handle.

<FunFact>
The decision to buy one lovebird or two isn't a minor preference, it changes the entire pet relationship. One hand-raised bird can end up riding on your shoulder and stepping up on command. A bonded pair will bond to each other so completely that you become little more than the source of food, closer to keeping a pair of finches than a tame parrot.
</FunFact>

If you can't commit meaningful daily time, two birds that keep each other company is honestly the more humane choice, even though it changes what kind of pet relationship you'll actually have.
