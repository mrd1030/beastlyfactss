# Emperor Scorpion Feeding: Schedule, Diet, and Why Fasting Is Normal

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Emperor scorpion feeding is low-effort once you internalize the one fact that changes everything about how often you should be doing it: this species has a slow metabolism and can comfortably go far longer between meals than most keepers assume.

## The Staple Diet

Emperor scorpions are obligate carnivores, and <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4uFr6hU" product="Dubia Roaches (feeder bag)">gut-loaded feeder insects</AffiliateLink> make up the entire diet. Crickets and dubia roaches are the most reliable staples, with locusts and the occasional mealworm or superworm added for variety. Adults use their powerful pincers to tear prey apart rather than relying on the stinger, which is really more of a last-resort defensive tool for this species.

## Feeding Frequency and Portions

Size prey to your scorpion's body, nothing larger than the abdomen. A practical portion is 3 to 4 crickets or the equivalent at a time. Frequency depends heavily on age and life stage:

- **Juveniles and nymphs:** every 2 to 4 days, since they are growing and eat more.
- **Adults:** once a week is standard, sometimes 2 to 3 insects offered weekly.

<KeyTakeaway>
Emperor scorpions can safely fast for a month or two, especially heading into a molt, and refusing food during that window is normal, not a sign of a problem. Always remove any uneaten prey within 24 hours. Live insects left in the enclosure can stress or injure a resting or molting scorpion. A good rule of thumb for adjusting frequency: an abdomen that looks narrower than the carapace means feed a bit more; a visibly distended abdomen means it's time to cut back.
</KeyTakeaway>

## Foods to Avoid

Never feed wild-caught insects, pesticide and parasite risk are both real concerns. Fireflies are genuinely toxic to scorpions and must never be offered. Skip human or table food entirely. Scorpions are built to digest liquefied insect prey. And avoid prey that's too large for your scorpion to subdue safely.

## Supplementation

None needed directly. Instead of supplementing the scorpion, supplement the food: <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08BTKWF15?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Fluker's High Calcium Dubia Roach Diet">gut-load feeder insects</AffiliateLink> with fresh produce or a commercial gut-load for at least 24 hours before offering them. That is how nutrition reaches a scorpion, and it is the same gut-loading step [every insect-eating pet](/blog/gut-loading-feeder-insects-guide/) depends on.

## Water

A scorpion is seldom seen drinking, yet it still needs more water than its prey supplies. Keep a shallow dish of clean, dechlorinated water in the enclosure at all times, shallow enough that the animal cannot drown in it, and do not count on misting as the only water source. Change it whenever feeder remains or substrate end up in it.

## Feeding Around a Molt

Appetite drops before a molt, often along with more hiding, a swollen look, less activity and a duller exoskeleton. Once those signs show, stop offering food and clear out every live and dead prey item, since crickets and roaches will chew on a soft, defenseless scorpion during or right after the molt.

After it molts, wait until the new exoskeleton has hardened before any prey goes back in. The animal stays open to insect bites for at least 3 days after the shed, and the hardening takes longer the bigger and older the animal, so an adult needs a longer wait than a juvenile.

## The Mistakes That Matter

Overfeeding is the risk here. Obesity is a documented, common problem in captive scorpions, and because this species needs so much less food than its constant hunting behavior might suggest, it's easy to offer more than it needs.

The second mistake is leaving live prey in the enclosure too long: an uneaten cricket can injure a molting or resting scorpion if it's not removed within a day. The third is feeding wild-caught insects, fireflies above all, which brings in a poisoning or parasite risk that bought feeders avoid entirely.
