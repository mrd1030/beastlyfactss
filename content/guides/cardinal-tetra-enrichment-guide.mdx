# Cardinal Tetra Enrichment: Most of Them Are Still Wild

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Nearly every fish in a modern aquarium shop was bred in a tank. This one usually was not.

<KeyTakeaway>
A large share of cardinal tetras are still caught in the Rio Negro rather than bred, which makes settling conditions matter more than for almost anything else in the shop. On top of that, the closest group size evidence says **ten, not six**, and cardinals want **soft, warm, tannin-stained water** that most tanks do not provide.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Neon Tetra Study Found

Nothing on cardinals specifically. The borrow is from the neon tetra work, covered in full in our [Neon Tetra enrichment guide](/blog/neon-tetra-enrichment-guide/).

Saxby, Adams, Snellgrove, Wilson and Sloman studied group size in four species commonly kept in home aquaria, tracking **darting, aggression, shoaling and latency to feed**. **Groups of ten neon tetras showed improved welfare** compared with smaller groups, with the lowest aggression and darting in the larger groups and **feeding latency falling as group size rose**.

The same study found **no group size effect for angelfish**, which our [Angelfish enrichment guide](/blog/angelfish-enrichment-guide/) covers. The effect was species-specific, so a neon result is a lead for a close relative rather than a proof. Cardinals are a closer match to neons than anything else in that study.

<FunFact>
The Rio Negro ornamental fishery is one of the few places where the aquarium trade is regularly argued to be a conservation positive, on the grounds that a living fishery gives river communities an economic reason not to clear the forest. It is a genuinely contested claim and it is not a marketing line.
</FunFact>

Water parameters, tank mates and the rest are in our complete [Cardinal Tetra care guide](/guides/cardinal-tetra/).

## Settle Them Properly

The part that is specific to a wild-caught fish. Cardinals often arrive after a long chain of transfers, already stressed, and losses in the first fortnight are common.

Acclimate slowly. Have the tank cycled and stable before they arrive, dim the lights, and leave them alone. Quarantine matters more with wild stock than with tank-bred fish because of what can come in with them. Our [health issues guide](/blog/cardinal-tetra-health-issues-guide/) covers what to watch for.

Buy from a shop that has held them a while, not one that got them yesterday.

## Blackwater Conditions

The thing that separates a cardinal tank from a neon tank. Soft, warm, acidic water, ideally stained with tannins.

<AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eR0Z2l" product="Driftwood">Driftwood</AffiliateLink> and leaf litter release tannins as they break down, tinting the water and lowering pH gently. The tint is not a cosmetic choice, it is the light environment the fish evolved in, and cardinals in tea-colored water look like different animals to cardinals under a bright light in clear water.

Dense <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4grZYPq" product="Live terrarium plants">live planting</AffiliateLink> around the sides and back, with open swimming space through the middle.

## Shade

Blackwater fish under a bright aquarium light spend their time near the bottom and stay pale. Floating plants, tannin staining, or a less powerful light all fix it.

Color is the immediate readout. A cardinal that feels covered is a brighter fish, which makes this a welfare improvement you can see the same evening.

## Feeding a Shoal

Small mouths that lose out to faster fish. Spread food across the surface, never into one spot.

Frozen and live foods get a much stronger response than flake. Our [feeding guide](/blog/cardinal-tetra-feeding-guide/) covers the diet.

## Priority Order

1. A cycled, stable, dimly lit tank waiting before they arrive
2. Slow acclimation and quarantine, given the wild-caught supply chain
3. Ten or more of one species
4. Soft, warm, tannin-stained water from wood and leaf litter
5. Dense planting at the sides with open swimming space
6. Subdued lighting or floating cover
7. Food spread across the surface, varied with frozen foods

## What Not to Do

Do not add wild-caught fish to a new or unstable tank. Do not skip quarantine. Do not stop at six. Do not keep them in hard alkaline water and expect them to thrive because neons managed. Do not run a bright light over a bare tank. And see our [handling guide](/blog/cardinal-tetra-handling-guide/) before netting a shoal that has only just settled.
