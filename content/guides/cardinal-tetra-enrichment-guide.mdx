# Cardinal Tetra Enrichment: Many Are Still Wild

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Nearly every fish in a modern aquarium shop was bred in a tank. This one often was not.

<KeyTakeaway>
A large share of cardinal tetras are still caught in the Rio Negro rather than bred, which makes settling conditions matter more than for almost anything else in the shop. Groups of **ten** are the target, and cardinals want soft, warm, tannin-stained water that most tanks do not provide. Of everything a stain does, **low light** has the clearest evidence: it keeps their stripes vivid.
</KeyTakeaway>

## Group Size: Ten or More

The group size work was done on neon tetras, the cardinal's closest relative, and [groups of ten showed improved welfare](/blog/neon-tetra-enrichment-guide/) compared with smaller groups.

Saxby, Adams, Snellgrove, Wilson and Sloman studied group size in four species commonly kept in home aquaria, tracking darting, aggression, shoaling and latency to feed. Aggression and darting were lowest in the larger groups, and feeding latency fell as group size rose.

The same study found no group size effect for angelfish, so the result is species-specific. Cardinals are a closer match to neons than anything else in that study, which is why ten is the working number here, and cardinals need at least 8 to 10 of their own kind.

In the middle Rio Negro, wild cardinals move about in groups of a few dozen. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eSbKl2" product="20 gallon long tank">20 gallon long tank</AffiliateLink> supports a proper group, and cardinals shoal more tightly and color more strongly in a large one.

One species of ten beats two species of five. A cardinal shoals with cardinals.

## Settle Them Properly

The part that is specific to a wild-caught fish. Cardinals often arrive after a long chain of transfers, and wild-caught fish cope worse with deteriorating water than commercially bred ones, so the first weeks decide a lot.

The chain starts within about 125 miles of Barcelos, on the middle Rio Negro, where fishers catch cardinals with dip nets and baited traps. The fish travel downriver to Manaus in tubs of water holding anything from a few to 1,000 fish, and fishers often add industrial-grade tetracycline to those tubs. Losses on that leg have averaged under 5% since 1995, against earlier claims of more than 50%, but it is only the first stage before export.

Acclimate slowly. Have the tank cycled and stable before they arrive, dim the lights, and leave them alone. Quarantine matters more with wild stock than with tank-bred fish because of what can come in with them, and [30 days is the minimum](/blog/fish-quarantine-and-treatment-guide/).

Buy from a shop that has held them a while.

<FunFact source="Chao 2001, Project Piaba review">
The Rio Negro ornamental fishery is one of the few places where the aquarium trade is regularly argued to be a conservation positive, on the grounds that a living fishery gives river communities an economic reason not to clear the forest. The fishery shows no sign of harming fish diversity, though the catch could fall if fishing pressure rises.
</FunFact>

## Planting and Cover

Wild cardinals live in forest streams and minor tributaries with slow-moving water, over litter-covered bottoms and, while the river is high, across the flooded forest floor.

Dense <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B008HXF16K?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="SubstrateSource Anubias Nana Live Aquarium Plant, 1 Pot">live planting</AffiliateLink> around the sides and back, with open swimming space through the middle. Dried leaf litter on the bottom brings back a piece of that habitat, and the microbe colonies that grow on the decaying leaves are a valuable secondary food for fry.

## Clear Water or Tannin-Stained?

Soft, warm, acidic water, ideally stained with tannins, is the thing that separates a cardinal tank from a neon tank. What the stain itself does for the fish is a separate question from the softness and acidity.

The wild fish live with and without it. Rio Negro streams are stained brownish by humic substances from decomposing organic matter, and every site where cardinals were collected along about 280 miles of the middle river measured between pH 3.35 and 5.82. In the Orinoco drainage, the species turns up more often in morichal habitats, which tend to run clear and transparent over sand. Wild specimens do best toward the acidic end of the species' range.

The best evidence for tannins comes from zebrafish. Dissolved organic carbon from the upper Rio Negro gave almost complete protection against the sodium and chloride loss that pH 4.0 causes in ion-poor water, and fish exposed to it beforehand stayed protected after it was gone. No cardinals were tested, and an earlier study found that a commercial organic carbon product made the same losses worse.

Light is where this fish has its own data. In a Brazilian lab test, cardinals at 0 to 250 lux grew more pigment cells in their black and blue stripes and showed more vibrant color, while intense light faded both stripes within 72 hours of exposure as the red stripe grew stronger. The tint cuts the light reaching the fish, and dim light suits a species whose stripes fade under bright light. Cardinals in tea-colored water look like different animals from cardinals under a bright light in clear water.

## Shade

Blackwater fish under a bright aquarium light spend their time near the bottom. Floating plants, tannin staining, or a less powerful light all cut the glare.

Color is the readout, which makes shade a welfare improvement you can see. The researchers behind the light study suggest the pale skin may be camouflage in the bright hours of the day, helping the fish confuse predators that hunt by sight. No predator was part of the test, so that stays a hypothesis.

## Feeding a Shoal

In the Rio Negro a cardinal is essentially a predator, picking tiny animals off submerged leaves, roots and water plants. Of 80 wild fish examined, 47.5% had eaten microcrustaceans and 43.8% midge larvae, and algae turned up only occasionally.

Wild-caught fish do take prepared food. In the light study, store-bought Rio Negro cardinals lived for two months on a commercial feed given twice a day.

Small mouths that lose out to faster fish. Spread food across the surface, never into one spot.

Frozen and live foods get a much stronger response than flake.

## Priority Order

1. A cycled, stable, dimly lit tank waiting before they arrive
2. Slow acclimation and quarantine, given the wild-caught supply chain
3. Ten or more of one species
4. Soft, warm, tannin-stained water from wood and leaf litter
5. Dense planting at the sides with open swimming space
6. Subdued lighting or floating cover
7. Food spread across the surface, varied with frozen foods

## What Not to Do

Do not add wild-caught fish to a new or unstable tank. Do not skip quarantine. Do not stop at eight. Do not keep them in hard alkaline water and expect them to thrive because neons managed, and [hardness is GH, which is not the same measurement as pH](/blog/freshwater-ph-gh-kh-guide/). Do not run a bright light over a bare tank.

A cardinal that has come all the way from a Barcelos stream needs a tank that is ready before it is, and dim enough that its stripes stay lit.
