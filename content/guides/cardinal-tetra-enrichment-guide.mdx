# Cardinal Tetra Enrichment: Many Are Still Wild

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Nearly every fish in a modern aquarium shop was bred in a tank. This one often was not.

<KeyTakeaway>
A large share of cardinal tetras are still caught in the Rio Negro rather than bred, which makes settling conditions matter more than for almost anything else in the shop. Groups of **ten** are the target, and cardinals want soft, warm, tannin-stained water that most tanks do not provide.
</KeyTakeaway>

## Group Size

The group size work was done on neon tetras, the cardinal's closest relative, and [groups of ten showed improved welfare](/blog/neon-tetra-enrichment-guide/) compared with smaller groups.

Saxby, Adams, Snellgrove, Wilson and Sloman studied group size in four species commonly kept in home aquaria, tracking darting, aggression, shoaling and latency to feed. Aggression and darting were lowest in the larger groups, and feeding latency fell as group size rose.

The same study found no group size effect for [angelfish](/blog/angelfish-enrichment-guide/), so the result is species-specific. Cardinals are a closer match to neons than anything else in that study, which is why ten is the working number here.

<FunFact>
The Rio Negro ornamental fishery is one of the few places where the aquarium trade is regularly argued to be a conservation positive, on the grounds that a living fishery gives river communities an economic reason not to clear the forest. The fishery shows no sign of harming fish diversity, though the catch could fall if fishing pressure rises.
</FunFact>

## Settle Them Properly

The part that is specific to a wild-caught fish. Cardinals often arrive after a long chain of transfers, and wild-caught fish cope worse with deteriorating water than commercially bred ones, so the first weeks decide a lot.

Acclimate slowly. Have the tank cycled and stable before they arrive, dim the lights, and leave them alone. Quarantine matters more with wild stock than with tank-bred fish because of what can come in with them, and 30 days is the minimum.

Buy from a shop that has held them a while.

## Ten or More

The working number comes from the neon tetra result, and cardinals need at least 8 to 10 of their own kind. One species of ten beats two species of five. A cardinal shoals with cardinals.

## Blackwater Conditions

The thing that separates a cardinal tank from a neon tank. Soft, warm, acidic water, ideally stained with tannins.

<AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eR0Z2l" product="Driftwood">Driftwood</AffiliateLink> and leaf litter release tannins as they break down, tinting the water and lowering pH gently. The tint cuts the light reaching the fish, and dim light suits a species whose stripes fade under bright light. Cardinals in tea-colored water look like different animals from cardinals under a bright light in clear water.

Dense <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B008HXF16K?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="SubstrateSource Anubias Nana Live Aquarium Plant, 1 Pot">live planting</AffiliateLink> around the sides and back, with open swimming space through the middle.

## Shade

In a Brazilian lab test, cardinal tetras exposed to intense light lost color from their blue and black stripes, while fish kept at 250 lux or less kept more vivid skin. Blackwater fish under a bright aquarium light spend their time near the bottom. Floating plants, tannin staining, or a less powerful light all cut the glare.

Color is the readout, which makes shade a welfare improvement you can see.

## Feeding a Shoal

Small mouths that lose out to faster fish. Spread food across the surface, never into one spot.

Frozen and live foods get a much stronger response than flake.

## Priority Order

1. A cycled, stable, dimly lit tank waiting before they arrive
2. Slow acclimation and quarantine, given the wild-caught supply chain
3. Ten or more of one species
4. Soft, warm, tannin-stained water from wood and leaf litter
5. Dense planting at the sides with open swimming space
6. Subdued lighting or floating cover
7. Food spread across the surface, varied with frozen foods

## What Not to Do

Do not add wild-caught fish to a new or unstable tank. Do not skip quarantine. Do not stop at eight. Do not keep them in hard alkaline water and expect them to thrive because neons managed, and [hardness is GH, which is not the same measurement as pH](/blog/freshwater-ph-gh-kh-guide/). Do not run a bright light over a bare tank.
