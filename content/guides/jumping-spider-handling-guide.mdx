# How to Handle a Jumping Spider (And Why Its Eyesight Matters)

Jumping spiders are genuinely different from most spiders kept as pets, and that difference starts with how they actually hunt.

## A Visual Hunter, Not a Web Builder

Unlike the web-building spiders most people picture, jumping spiders are active visual hunters. They have unusually good eyesight for a spider, built around large, forward-facing eyes that let them track and stalk prey before pouncing, sometimes leaping many times their own body length to catch it. They do produce silk, but only for draglines and a small retreat, not for trapping food.

## What Handling Actually Looks Like

Curious and bold, jumping spiders track movement and generally aren't aggressive toward people, bites are rare and medically trivial when they do happen. Even so, minimal handling is the right approach. If you do handle one, let it walk freely onto and off your hand rather than grabbing or restraining it, they're genuinely fragile, and a fall, even a short one, can rupture the abdomen and prove fatal. Always handle low, over a soft surface.

<FunFact>
Jumping spiders can leap many times their own body length to catch prey, a feat their large, forward-facing eyes make possible by letting them judge distance and track movement before they pounce.
</FunFact>

## First-Timer Mistakes

- Dropping or injuring a spider during handling, by far the most common way keepers accidentally harm this species.
- Using an unmonitored heat mat, a genuine and common preventable cause of death. Our [tank setup guide](/blog/jumping-spider-tank-setup-guide/) covers how to run supplemental heat safely if you need it at all.
- Letting the enclosure run too dry, which stresses an animal that relies on consistent humidity.

## How This Compares to Other Spiders

Web-building spiders wait for prey to come to them. Jumping spiders go get it, actively stalking and pouncing rather than trapping. The regal jumping spider specifically is the largest commonly kept species in the group.
