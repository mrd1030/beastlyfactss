# Hamster Feeding: Schedule, Diet, and Why They Stop Eating

A hamster's cheek pouches can hold roughly a fifth of its own body weight. That changes how you read a quiet day at the bowl.

## Feeding schedule

Pellets stay available. Hamsters hoard. They rarely overeat a complete pellet. Refill to about 2 tablespoons a day for a Syrian and about 1 tablespoon for a dwarf. Activity clusters around dusk and overnight. The [Gerbil vs. Hamster guide](/blog/gerbil-vs-hamster-guide/) is the page if you want a small pet whose hours match yours.

Fresh extras: about 1 teaspoon of vegetables a day, plus about a teaspoon of fruit around 3 times a week. Fresh food, vegetables and fruit together, stays under about 10% of the diet. Seeds, nuts, and popcorn 1 to 2 times a week, under 5 to 10% of the total.

Dwarf hamsters typically wean around 3 weeks. Syrians nurse a bit longer, about 26 to 28 days. All species are self-sufficient by 4 to 5 weeks.

## What hamsters eat

A complete hamster pellet or lab block. Not a loose seed mix on its own. They pick the sugary, fatty bits and leave the fiber.

Daily vegetables: broccoli, cucumber, bell pepper, kale, spinach, peas, zucchini, carrot. Occasional fruit: apple with seeds out, banana, melon, pear. Timothy hay is a chew, not the diet base the way it is for a rabbit or guinea pig.

They are omnivores. Mealworms or boiled unseasoned egg belong. Scatter or hide food. Fresh water in a valveless sipper, checked daily.

## Safe treats and foods to avoid

Safe in moderation: mealworms, boiled egg, small amounts of nuts and seeds, plain pasta, unsalted popcorn, the vegetables and fruit above, timothy hay.

Toxic: grapes and rhubarb. Raw onion and raw garlic. Chocolate, bitter almonds, raw kidney beans, raw potato, eggplant, avocado. Skip citrus for every hamster on this site, including Syrians.

## Why hamsters stop eating

1. **Dental problems.** The usual cause. Overgrown or injured incisors make chewing painful.
2. **Wet tail.** Bacterial intestinal infection, especially in young or recently weaned animals. Can be fatal inside about 48 hours. Appetite loss plus soiled, wet hindquarters.
3. **Cold-induced torpor.** [Below roughly 60F](/blog/hamster-tank-setup-guide/) they can look dead. Warm them gradually. Pet hamsters do not truly hibernate.
4. **Stress.** A new cage or handling.
5. **Respiratory infection or other illness.** Labored breathing, discharge, lethargy.
6. **Diabetes in Campbell's and Chinese dwarfs.** Appetite and energy change.
7. **Cheek pouch impaction.** Soft or moist food clumping. One-sided swelling, face-pawing, drooling. Vet.

## When to worry

A hamster that is otherwise active and has a stash can go a day without you seeing a meal. Watch 6 to 12 hours of no eating if there is no stash and the animal looks off. Call at 12 to 24 hours, or any refusal with lethargy or diarrhea. No food intake past 24 hours is critical. Past 48 hours is life-threatening.

A hoard that lasts 3 to 4 days is not the same as a hamster refusing fresh food while sick. See a vet now for labored breathing, wet hindquarters, discharge, a swollen abdomen, seizures, weight loss, or a hunched, lethargic posture.
