# How Much Does a Jumping Spider Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Jumping spiders, the regal jumping spider especially, are among the most affordable and space-efficient pets anyone can keep, accessible even on a tight budget.

## The Spider: $15 to $60

Captive-bred spiderlings run $15 to $25. Juvenile or sub-adult females, generally more prized than males since they live considerably longer, run $25 to $60. Named regional color forms cost more than standard animals.

## Telling a Female Before She Matures

The price gap is a lifespan gap, so the sex of the spider is most of what the higher price buys. The regal jumping spider is one of the few spiders that can be sexed as a youngster. Juvenile females from the more southern populations grow a cover of colored scales as early as the third instar. Males stay stark black and white for their entire lives.

At adulthood the differences sharpen:

- **Males** are black with a white band and white spots on the abdomen, carry alternating black and white fringes on the first pair of legs, and have enlarged, iridescent green-blue-violet chelicerae with a small bump on each.
- **Females** usually have the black areas covered in colored scales, less obvious chelicerae hidden behind white-haired palps, and four tufts of hair around the eyes on top of the head, which males lack.

The catch is that some females keep the male's black and white pattern. A juvenile with color is a safe female. A black and white juvenile is probably male but not certainly, so a buyer who wants a sure female pays for one with color or buys after she matures.

## Choosing a Spider

Look for a plump, smooth abdomen with no wrinkling. A shriveled or shrunken one points to a spider that is hungry or dehydrated. Keep a newcomer in its own enclosure well away from any other pet invertebrates at first, the same [invertebrate quarantine](/blog/invertebrate-quarantine-cleaning-and-escapes-guide/) practice used for tarantulas and scorpions.

## Ongoing Costs: Roughly $5 to $15 a Month

Feeder insects make up nearly the entire monthly cost. Young spiders need a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CVSH6863?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Josh's Frogs Drosophila Melanogaster Fruit Fly Culture Combo (2 Pack)">fruit fly culture</AffiliateLink>, priced $28 to $34 for a starter combo, since prey that size is small enough for spiderlings to safely take down, while adults move on to small crickets or fly spikes.

## Vet Costs

Usually none. Few exotic vets treat spiders, and most health issues in this species trace back to the enclosure environment, humidity above all. The exception is an injury, fluid leakage, or a stuck molt, where an exotic vet who sees invertebrates is still the call, so it is worth finding out before an emergency whether one near you will.

## Lifespan: Short, and Worth Knowing Upfront

Males typically live only a few months after reaching maturity, while females last considerably longer. Most live one to two years in captivity, and a well-kept female can go longer. Wild spiders live shorter lives, because predation, gaps between meals, and extreme weather all take their toll.

## The Honest Value Proposition

If low cost, low space, and a short commitment appeal to you, this is close to the ideal pet on paper. The tradeoff is accepting that short lifespan from the start. A [tarantula](/blog/tarantula-cost-guide/) costs a similar amount to set up and can outlive a jumping spider by more than a decade, which makes the two a choice about commitment more than money.
