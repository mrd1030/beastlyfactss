# How Much Does a Jumping Spider Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Jumping spiders, the regal jumping spider especially, are one of the most affordable and low space pets on this entire site, accessible even on a tight budget.

## The Spider: $15 to $60

Captive-bred spiderlings run $15 to $25. Juvenile or sub-adult females, generally more prized than males since they live considerably longer, run $25 to $60. Named regional color forms cost more than standard animals.

## Ongoing Costs: Roughly $5 to $15 a Month

Feeder insects make up nearly the entire monthly cost. Young spiders need a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CVSH6863?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Josh's Frogs Drosophila Melanogaster Fruit Fly Culture Combo (2 Pack)">fruit fly culture</AffiliateLink>, priced $28 to $34 for a starter combo, since prey that size is small enough for spiderlings to safely take down, while adults move on to small crickets or fly spikes.

## Vet Costs

Effectively none. Exotic vets rarely treat spiders, and the large majority of [health issues in this species](/blog/jumping-spider-health-issues-guide/) trace back to the enclosure environment rather than anything requiring professional treatment.

<FunFact>
The longest documented jumping spider on record, a female kept at the Bronx Zoo, lived just under 5 years, a notable outlier against a typical lifespan of just 1 to 3 years.
</FunFact>

## Lifespan: Short, and Worth Knowing Upfront

Males typically live 8 to 12 months after reaching maturity, while females live longer, 1.5 to 3 years. A realistic overall range to expect is 1 to 3 years.

## The Honest Value Proposition

If low cost, low space, and a genuinely short-term commitment appeal to you, this is close to the ideal pet on paper. The tradeoff is accepting that short lifespan from the start.
