# Common Lovebird Health Issues and What Causes Them

<VetDisclaimer />
<AffiliateDisclosure />

Lovebirds hide illness well, a survival instinct that means by the time symptoms are obvious, a problem is often more advanced than it would be in a more expressive pet. A handful of conditions come up often enough to know in advance. See our [Lovebird cage setup guide](/blog/lovebird-tank-setup-guide/) for the husbandry side, and our full [Lovebird care guide](/guides/lovebird/) for everything else.

## Psittacine Beak and Feather Disease (PBFD)

Lovebirds are particularly susceptible to this virus, which attacks feather and beak cells along with the immune system. Watch for feather deformities, feather loss, and abnormalities in the beak. This is often fatal, has no cure, and is highly contagious between birds. Always see a vet, and testing any new bird before introducing it to an existing one is genuinely important prevention, not excessive caution.

## Chronic Egg-Laying and Egg-Binding

A hen can lay eggs regularly even with no male present, and an all-seed diet, low in calcium and vitamin D, makes this worse and raises the risk of a genuinely dangerous complication. Watch for straining, lethargy, and visible abdominal swelling. Egg-binding can turn fatal quickly. This is an emergency, don't wait to see if it resolves.

<FunFact>
A female lovebird doesn't need a male present to lay eggs, she can do so regularly entirely on her own. It's part of why nest boxes, mirrors, and enclosed "happy hut" style hideaways are discouraged for pet lovebirds, they can trigger chronic egg-laying even in a single, unmated hen.
</FunFact>

## Respiratory and Bacterial Infections

Wheezing, breathing difficulty, and discharge point here. Psittacosis specifically is worth knowing about because it's zoonotic, transmissible to people. Always see a vet.

## Polyomavirus

Mainly affects young birds, causing feather abnormalities and other symptoms. A vaccine exists and is worth discussing with an avian vet, especially if you're acquiring a young bird or planning to keep multiple birds.

## Watch for Hidden Illness

Because lovebirds mask symptoms so effectively, any sudden change, lethargy, appetite loss, fluffed feathers held constantly, or a shift in normal behavior, is worth a prompt vet visit rather than a wait-and-see approach.
