# Corydoras Catfish Tank Setup: Substrate Is the Detail That Actually Matters

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Corydoras catfish are genuinely easy to house well once you get one specific decision right, what's on the bottom of the tank. Everything else about this species' care is fairly standard community-fish practice. This covers the full setup, for what it all costs, see our [Corydoras Catfish cost guide](/blog/corydoras-catfish-cost-guide/), or the full [Corydoras Catfish care guide](/guides/corydoras-catfish/) for everything else.

## Tank Size

A 20-gallon long tank is the practical minimum for a proper school of six standard-sized cories, dwarf or pygmy species can work in something smaller, around 10 gallons. A 29 to 30-gallon tank or larger is genuinely better, especially if you're keeping a bigger group of 8 to 15. Favor a wide footprint over a tall one, this is a bottom-dwelling species that uses floor space, not vertical space.

## Temperature

72 to 78°F suits most commonly kept species, though the exact preference varies a bit, some species tolerate warmer water, others prefer it cooler. A reliable <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eQY1L5" product="Submersible aquarium heater">heater</AffiliateLink> is needed unless your room stays consistently warm, and stability matters more than hitting one precise number.

## Water Parameters

pH around 7.0 to 7.8 for captive-bred cories, which make up most of what's available in the trade. Ammonia and nitrite should read zero, and nitrate should stay low through regular water changes, this ties directly into preventing the barbel erosion covered in our [Corydoras Catfish health issues guide](/blog/corydoras-catfish-health-issues-guide/). A reliable <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4p1T7yd" product="Water test kit">water test kit</AffiliateLink> is worth keeping on hand so you catch a rising nitrate reading before it becomes a barbel problem.

## Substrate: The Decision That Matters Most

Soft sand or smooth, fine, rounded gravel is genuinely essential for this species. Avoid sharp or coarse gravel entirely, and be cautious with certain specialty planted-tank sands, some sharp-grained options are specifically flagged by manufacturers as unsuitable for bottom-dwelling fish like cories. About 2 inches of depth works well.

## Feeding Setup: Food That Actually Reaches the Bottom

Since cories forage along the substrate, floating flakes rarely reach them before other fish clean them up, so this is worth planning for at the setup stage rather than figuring out later. <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00025JZAY?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Hikari Tropical Sinking Wafers for Catfish, Loaches and Bottom Feeders, 3.88 oz">Hikari Tropical Sinking Wafers</AffiliateLink> are formulated specifically for catfish, loaches, and other bottom feeders, and sink straight down to where a cory school actually feeds.

## Lighting

Standard community tank lighting is fine. Cories are most active around dawn and dusk and appreciate some shaded cover and hides where they can retreat during brighter parts of the day.

## The Non-Obvious Requirement: Gentle Flow With Rest Spots

Provide gentle water movement with calmer areas where cories can rest, rather than uniform strong current throughout the tank. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3T8xyAd" product="Sponge filter">sponge filter</AffiliateLink> is a common way to get gentle, even flow without the strong current a hang-on-back filter can produce.

<FunFact>
Avoid over-cleaning or disturbing the substrate too aggressively during maintenance, and make sure your fish have easy access to the surface, they periodically gulp air as part of normal breathing, and that behavior depends on being able to reach the top of the tank without obstruction.
</FunFact>
