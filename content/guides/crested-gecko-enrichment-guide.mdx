# Crested Gecko Enrichment: Vertical Space, Cover, and Foraging

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Crested geckos live vertically, so enrichment here means real climbing complexity, cover at height, and feeding that asks for a little effort.

<KeyTakeaway>
Vertical climbing structure is the single highest-impact enrichment upgrade for this species. A tank with plenty of floor space but a bare, empty vertical dimension is missing the part of the enclosure a crested gecko actually uses most.
</KeyTakeaway>

## How a Wild Crested Gecko Uses a Forest

Crested geckos are endemic to the far south of New Caledonia, in three separate populations: one on the Isle of Pines and its islets and two on the main island, Grande Terre. They live in the lower canopy and understory of rainforest, sleep through the day in secure spots high in the branches, and come out at night to move, often by jumping long distances from branch to branch. Every enrichment choice for this species copies one piece of that: height for sleeping, open gaps for jumping, fine perches for moving, and wet leaves for drinking.

## Climbing Structure: Go Thin

Wild crested geckos spend much of their time among thin branches, and there is a reason for it. The larger, heavier New Caledonian giant gecko, *Rhacodactylus leachianus*, preys on them, and keeping to thin branches helps a crested gecko evade it. A crested gecko also climbs with a semi-prehensile tail whose tip carries the same adhesive setae as its toes, so it grips fine stems well. Build the network mostly from thin <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3QQNV3L" product="Climbing branch (Mopani wood)">branches</AffiliateLink>, bamboo, and vines, with only a few thick limbs, and run them diagonally across the enclosure so the gecko can travel from floor to ceiling without touching glass. Leave real gaps between perches on purpose: open space is where the jumping happens.

## Cover Where It Sleeps

Because wild crested geckos sleep high, daytime cover belongs in the top third of the enclosure. <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3SKmfOx" product="Cork bark round hide">Cork tubes</AffiliateLink>, birdhouse-style hides, and magnetic wall hides belong near the ceiling, and dense leaves around them let the gecko choose between shade and a little light. Give it at least two options at different heights so it can pick a warmer or cooler spot.

## Plants That Hold Water

Plants are the one piece of decor that does three jobs here: cover, climbing, and drinking water. Live leaves collect droplets after misting, and a crested gecko drinks by licking those droplets, just as it would from wet foliage in the forest. Bromeliads such as *Guzmania* and *Vriesea*, pothos, snake plant, bird's nest fern, and *Ficus* are gecko-safe choices. Live plants need 6500K full-spectrum light to survive; high-quality artificial plants give the same cover and still hold mist.

## Foraging and Feeding

Live insects offered once or twice a week bring out active hunting and stalking that a dish of crested gecko diet alone doesn't, and [what those insects ate first](/blog/gut-loading-feeder-insects-guide/) decides what the gecko gets out of them. Release a few into the upper branches after dark instead of dropping them on the floor, and keep the diet dish on an elevated magnetic ledge, matching where the gecko naturally feeds.

<FunFact>
Most adult crested geckos in the wild have already lost their tails, and the tail never grows back. A wild population of tailless adults is still a population of climbers and jumpers.
</FunFact>

## Bioactive Setups

A well-established bioactive enclosure, springtails and isopods working through a drainage layer, soil mix, and leaf litter, adds ongoing low-level stimulation on top of everything above. It's a long-term option worth considering once the basics are dialed in, not a requirement to get started.
