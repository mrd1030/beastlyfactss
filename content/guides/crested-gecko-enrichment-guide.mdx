# Crested Gecko Enrichment: Vertical Space, Cover, and Foraging

Crested geckos live vertically, so enrichment here means real climbing complexity, cover at height, and feeding that asks for a little effort. For everything this doesn't cover, see our complete [Crested Gecko care guide](/guides/crested-gecko/).

<KeyTakeaway>
Vertical climbing structure is the single highest-impact enrichment upgrade for this species. A tank with plenty of floor space but a bare, empty vertical dimension is missing the part of the enclosure a crested gecko actually uses most.
</KeyTakeaway>

## Climbing Structure

Build a multi-level network with <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3SKmfOx" product="Cork bark round hide">cork bark and cork tubes</AffiliateLink>, which double as daytime sleeping hollows, plus <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3QQNV3L" product="Climbing branch (Mopani wood)">sturdy branches</AffiliateLink> set at varied diameters and angles. Weave in real or artificial vines to create crossing routes, and add elevated ledges or a magnetic feeding platform so the gecko never has to come down to the floor to eat. Leave real gaps between perches on purpose, crested geckos are excellent jumpers and will use the open space.

## Cover and Daytime Security

Dense foliage lets a crested gecko control its own light exposure during the day. Pothos, philodendron, sansevieria, and bromeliads are common safe choices, live or high-quality artificial. Add several elevated hides, cork tubes, half coconut shells, or magnetic wall hides, placed at different heights so the gecko has a real choice of where to settle, not just one option.

## Foraging and Feeding

Live insects offered once or twice a week bring out active hunting and stalking that a dish of crested gecko diet alone doesn't. Scatter or tuck small amounts of food among foliage and ledges instead of always using the same spot, and keep feeding stations elevated on a magnetic ledge, matching where the gecko naturally feeds in the wild.

<FunFact>
Large gaps between branches aren't a design flaw, they're intentional. Crested geckos are confident jumpers, and open space between perches is part of what makes a climbing network genuinely enriching rather than just decorative.
</FunFact>

## Bioactive Setups

A well-established bioactive enclosure, springtails and isopods working through a drainage layer, soil mix, and leaf litter, adds ongoing low-level stimulation on top of everything above. It's a long-term option worth considering once the basics are dialed in, not a requirement to get started.
