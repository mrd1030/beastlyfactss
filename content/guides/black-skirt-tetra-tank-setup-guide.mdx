# Black Skirt Tetra Tank Setup: A 20-Gallon Long, Cooler Water, and Room to Swim

<AffiliateDisclosure />

The black skirt tetra (Gymnocorymbus ternetzi), also sold as the black widow tetra, is a rounded South American fish from the Rio Paraguay and Guaporé basins. A home-tank adult measures 2 to 2.5 inches, occasionally 3, and spends its time in the middle and upper layers of the water. Every one in the trade is captive bred, so the species handles a far wider spread of water than the soft-water tetras it often shares a store shelf with. White skirt and long-fin black skirts are the same fish and go in the same tank.

## Tank Size

<AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eSbKl2" product="20 gallon long tank">A 20-gallon long</AffiliateLink> is the tank for a school of 12. Its 30 x 12 inch (75 x 30 cm) base is the footprint to plan around: this is an active swimmer, and length gives a group of 2-inch fish room to move as one. A standard 10-gallon is too short for that school.

Twelve also keeps this tetra's habit of nipping turned inward on its own group, which is why slow fish with long, trailing fins make [poor tankmates for it](/blog/black-skirt-tetra-handling-guide/).

## Open Water in the Middle, Plants Around It

Leave the front and center of the tank clear. Black skirts cruise the middle and upper water and want a long open lane there, so the planting belongs along the back and both ends. Those thickets give the school somewhere quieter to drift into when something in the room startles it.

Add floating plants on top. Dense stands below and floating cover dimming the light from above bring the fish to its best color. Past that, decor is a free choice: this species settles into most layouts.

## Temperature

68 to 79°F (20 to 26°C) is the range, and it is a cool one for a tetra. Its floor sits 7°F under the 75°F where an [ember tetra tank](/blog/ember-tetra-tank-setup-guide/) starts, and its ceiling falls short of the 82°F that both an ember tank and a [cardinal tetra's everyday target](/blog/cardinal-tetra-tank-setup-guide/) reach. Set the <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eQY1L5" product="Submersible aquarium heater">submersible heater</AffiliateLink> inside the band and never above 79°F.

A heater still belongs in the tank, even with a floor that low. Any room that cools below 68°F at night takes the water out of range with it.

### What Cold Does to a Black Skirt

White skirt tetras cooled by 1°C (about 2°F) a day from 68°F showed how much margin the species has. They ate less and slowed down at about 63°F (17°C), quit feeding as the water neared 54°F (12°C), stopped moving just above 50°F (10°C), and lost their balance at about 50°F.

That margin buys time when a heater fails or [the power goes out](/blog/aquarium-power-outage-and-transport-guide/). It does not make a cool tank a good one. At 63°F the school is already off its food, five degrees under the bottom of its range.

### GloFish Skirt Tetras

GloFish Skirt Tetras are this species and use the same setup, with one adjustment. In a comparison with plain white skirts, adult green GloFish tetras tolerated cold far worse, and GloFish lists 76 to 80°F for its fish. A GloFish school therefore sits at 76 to 79°F, the part of both ranges that agree, with the heater running all year. The GloFish Pristella tetra is a different fish entirely.

## Water Chemistry

Aim for pH 6.0 to 7.5 and a general hardness of 5 to 19 dGH. Black skirts have been bred for the aquarium trade since at least 1950, and generations of captive stock have made them unfussy about water. That window is far broader than the soft water an ember or cardinal tank has to hold.

Test the tap before the tank is filled. Water that reads inside both bands needs nothing more than a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3Rg5gTT" product="Water conditioner">water conditioner</AffiliateLink> to remove chlorine and chloramine. Water outside them comes into range by [shifting GH and KH](/blog/freshwater-ph-gh-kh-guide/), which carries the pH along and keeps it there.

## Filtration

This fish comes from small, slow creeks, so the filter should turn the water over without pushing a current across the tank. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3T8xyAd" product="Sponge filter">sponge filter</AffiliateLink> sized for 20 gallons fits, and so does a hang-on-back with its output turned down. Either way, the bacteria growing on the media are [the part of the filter that keeps fish alive](/blog/aquarium-filtration-guide/).

<FunFact>
Black skirt tetras released from aquariums have established themselves in Colombia and, for a while, in a Colorado hot spring. In more than 70 years in the trade, no population has been reported taking hold anywhere with a temperate climate.
</FunFact>

## Cycle the Tank, Then Hold the Water Steady

The school goes into [a tank that has finished cycling](/blog/aquarium-cycling-guide/): ammonia and nitrite at zero, with nitrate starting to register. Any new fish first spend 30 days in [a separate quarantine tank](/blog/fish-quarantine-and-treatment-guide/) before joining a display that already has fish in it.

From then on, [partial water changes on a weekly routine](/blog/aquarium-water-changes-guide/) keep the chemistry where the school settled. Every refill is dechlorinated and brought to tank temperature first, so a fish that tolerates a wide band of water never meets a sudden jump within it.

The [black skirt tetra encyclopedia profile](/encyclopedia/animal/black-skirt-tetra/) has more on the species itself.
