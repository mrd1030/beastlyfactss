# Common Russian Tortoise Health Issues and What Causes Them

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Russian tortoises are hardy, and most health problems trace back to a handful of husbandry basics, temperature, humidity, and diet, that are genuinely within an owner's control. ## Metabolic Bone Disease (MBD)

VCA Animal Hospitals lists this among the most common conditions in pet tortoises. Watch for a soft or deformed shell and weak limbs. Caused by inadequate UVB or insufficient dietary calcium. ## Respiratory Infection

Wheezing, nasal discharge, open-mouth breathing, and lethargy. Caused by temperatures running chronically low, excess humidity, or drafts. Always see a vet immediately, this genuinely does not resolve on its own and needs antibiotics. Raise temperature and ensure hydration while arranging care.

<FunFact>
Shell pyramiding in Russian tortoises is entirely preventable through correct diet and hydration, but once those raised, cone-shaped scutes appear, they're permanent. Prevention really is the only fix.
</FunFact>

## Shell Rot

Soft, discolored, or foul-smelling patches on the shell, a bacterial or fungal infection that typically enters through a cut or scrape, often worsened by an overly damp enclosure. See a vet, this can progress to septicemia, a genuine life-threatening emergency, if left untreated.

## Pyramiding

Abnormal, raised growth of the shell scutes. Caused by excess dietary protein, overfeeding, insufficient hydration or humidity, or a calcium deficiency. Manageable and preventable through correct diet and hydration, but once it's happened, it isn't reversible.

## Parasites

Roundworms and protozoa are fairly common, particularly in wild-caught animals. A fecal test is worth doing for any newly acquired tortoise, whether or not symptoms are present.

## The Husbandry Pattern Worth Remembering

Weak UVB and calcium cause MBD. Cold, damp, or drafty conditions cause respiratory infection, the more urgent condition on this list. Overfeeding and poor hydration cause pyramiding. And an unscreened new tortoise brings parasite risk into your collection.
