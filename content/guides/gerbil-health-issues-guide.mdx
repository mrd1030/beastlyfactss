# Common Gerbil Health Issues

<VetDisclaimer />
<AffiliateDisclosure />

Gerbils are hardy, and the conditions that do show up in this species range from routine to genuinely urgent, worth knowing the difference before something comes up. See our [Gerbil enclosure setup guide](/blog/gerbil-tank-setup-guide/) for the husbandry side of prevention, and our complete [Gerbil care guide](/guides/gerbil/) for everything else.

## Scent-Gland Tumors

The most common tumor type in gerbils, arising from the ventral scent gland on the belly, more frequent in older males. Appears as a lump, sore, or ulcer along the midline of the abdomen. **See a vet**, caught early, these are usually operable with a good recovery. Checking the gland area periodically as part of routine handling is a reasonable habit.

## Tyzzer's Disease

The most common infectious disease in this species, a bacterial gut infection that's genuinely stress-related and highly contagious between gerbils.

**Watch for:** ruffled fur, lethargy, a hunched posture, poor appetite, and diarrhea.

**Diarrhea in a gerbil is an emergency, see a vet immediately**, this disease can progress to death quickly if untreated.

<FunFact>
Tyzzer's disease being described as "stress-related" isn't a vague catch-all, transport, overcrowding, and a sudden environment change are specific, documented triggers. A calm, properly settled gerbil is genuinely less likely to develop this disease than one that's just been rehomed or crowded into too small a tank, which is part of why the deep, spacious setup covered in the tank guide isn't just about comfort.
</FunFact>

## Tail Slip (Sloughing)

From improper handling, specifically grabbing or restraining by the tail, or occasionally from a fight between cage mates. The stump usually heals on its own; a full tail loss is worth a vet check to confirm there's no ongoing complication. Entirely preventable by never handling this species by the tail, covered in the [handling guide](/blog/gerbil-handling-guide/).

## Coat and Skin Issues (Matting, "Red Nose," Mites)

From humidity running too high, poor ventilation, overcrowding, or general stress. Nasal porphyrin discharge, sometimes mistaken for blood, can worsen into self-trauma and infection if the underlying cause isn't corrected. **Correct the environment first** (keep humidity under about 50%, and use clean, dry <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4voH9Ap" product="Small-pet paper bedding">bedding</AffiliateLink>), and see a vet for any infected sore or suspected mites.

## The Husbandry Pattern Worth Remembering

Stress, whether from isolation, overcrowding, or poor environment, is genuinely the thread connecting Tyzzer's disease, coat problems, and general immune resilience in this species. Rough handling causes tail slip. And insufficient gnawing material causes dental overgrowth. A calm, properly social, well-ventilated setup, covered in the [tank guide](/blog/gerbil-tank-setup-guide/), does most of the preventive work here.

## When to See a Vet

See a vet immediately for diarrhea, don't wait on this one. Book a routine visit promptly for a lump along the belly midline, dropped food or drooling, or a nose that looks persistently red or bloody.
