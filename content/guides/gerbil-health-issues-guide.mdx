# Common Gerbil Health Issues

<VetDisclaimer />
<AffiliateDisclosure />

Gerbils are hardy, and the conditions that do show up in this species range from routine to urgent, worth knowing the difference before something comes up.

## Scent-Gland Tumors

A tumor [common in aged male gerbils](https://cvm.missouri.edu/diseases-of-research-animals-dora/gerbils/squamous-cell-carcinoma), arising from the ventral scent gland on the belly. Tumors in general run at a 25 to 40% incidence past two or three years in this species, and scent gland carcinomas in males plus ovarian tumors in females account for roughly 80% of them. The gland sits as a bare orange-tan oval on the midline, so a lump, sore, or ulcer there is what to look for. **See a vet**, caught early, these are usually operable with a good recovery. Checking the gland area periodically as part of routine handling is a reasonable habit.

## Tyzzer's Disease

The most common infectious disease in this species, a bacterial gut infection that's stress-related and highly contagious, spreading through contact with infected animals or bedding via the fecal-oral route, per the [University of Missouri College of Veterinary Medicine](https://cvm.missouri.edu/diseases-of-research-animals-dora/gerbils/tyzzers-disease/).

**Watch for:** ruffled fur, lethargy, a hunched posture, poor appetite, and diarrhea.

**Diarrhea in a gerbil is an emergency, see a vet immediately**, this disease can progress to death quickly if untreated.

<FunFact>
Tyzzer's disease being described as "stress-related" isn't a vague catch-all, transport, overcrowding, and a sudden environment change are specific, documented triggers. A calm, properly settled gerbil is genuinely less likely to develop this disease than one that's just been rehomed or crowded into too small a tank, which is part of why a deep, spacious setup isn't just about comfort.
</FunFact>

## Aural Cholesteatoma

The one nobody warns you about. Merck records it in 50% of gerbils over two years old: a growth in the ear canal that pushes the eardrum inward, eventually causing bone damage and inner ear destruction. **Head tilt is the visible sign**, so a gerbil holding its head to one side needs a vet rather than watching.

## Seizures

Seizures occur in some lines and can look alarming. They typically pass in a few minutes and leave no lasting effects, and anticonvulsants are not usually needed. Merck notes that frequency and severity often decrease with age, and adds that a subset of adults instead get progressively worse, so a gerbil whose seizures are becoming more frequent is not simply growing out of it. Handling gently and often in the first weeks of life suppresses them in predisposed animals.

## Tail Slip (Sloughing)

From improper handling, specifically grabbing or restraining by the tail, or occasionally from a fight between cage mates. The skin slips away and the tissue underneath, now exposed, dies: Merck recommends amputating the bare portion of the tail as soon as possible to prevent infection setting in, so this is a vet visit rather than something to watch. Entirely preventable by never handling this species by the tail.

## Overgrown Teeth (Malocclusion)

Gerbil incisors grow continuously, and overgrowth typically follows when a tooth breaks, for instance from gnawing on metal cage bars, letting the opposing incisor grow unchecked, per the [University of Missouri College of Veterinary Medicine](https://cvm.missouri.edu/diseases-of-research-animals-dora/gerbils/malocclusion/). **Manageable at home** by providing genuine <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0G41RJPZJ?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Rabbit Chew Toys, Toys for Guinea Pigs & Hamsters - Natural Chew Sticks & Balls Set for Small Animals, Dental Care Treats for Chinchillas, Bunny - 20+ Toys for Teeth Health (32 Pieces)">chew material</AffiliateLink> instead of bars; visible overgrowth affecting eating needs a vet.

## Coat and Skin Issues (Matting, "Red Nose," Mites)

From humidity running too high, poor ventilation, overcrowding, or general stress. Nasal porphyrin discharge, sometimes mistaken for blood, can worsen into self-trauma and infection if the underlying cause isn't corrected. **Correct the environment first** ([get humidity below 40%](/blog/gerbil-tank-setup-guide/), and use clean, dry <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4voH9Ap" product="Small-pet paper bedding">bedding</AffiliateLink>), and see a vet for any infected sore or suspected mites. 50% is where the condition starts appearing, and 40% is the number that prevents it.

## The Husbandry Pattern Worth Remembering

Stress, whether from isolation, overcrowding, or poor environment, is genuinely the thread connecting Tyzzer's disease, coat problems, and general immune resilience in this species. Rough handling causes tail slip. And insufficient gnawing material causes dental overgrowth. A calm, properly social, well-ventilated setup does most of the preventive work here.

## When to See a Vet

See a vet immediately for diarrhea, don't wait on this one. Book a routine visit promptly for a lump along the belly midline, dropped food or drooling, or a nose that looks persistently red or bloody.
