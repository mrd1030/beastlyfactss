# Angelfish Enrichment: The Study Found Group Size Did Not Matter

<AffiliateDisclosure />

A rare thing in the literature: a study that included two species and reached opposite conclusions about them.

<KeyTakeaway>
Saxby and colleagues tested group size in four home aquarium species. Neon tetras did measurably better in groups of ten. For **angelfish, there was no evidence of a group size effect at all**. They are cichlids rather than shoaling fish, and the shoal-size rules that govern a tetra tank simply do not describe them.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Group Size Study Found

The study is **The effect of group size on the behaviour and welfare of four fish species commonly kept in home aquaria**, Saxby, Adams, Snellgrove, Wilson and Sloman, *Applied Animal Behaviour Science*, 2010. The four species: neon tetras, white cloud mountain minnows, angelfish and tiger barbs.

The authors tracked **darting, aggression, shoaling and latency to feed**, and found these varied with group size **in a species-specific manner**. Groups of ten neon tetras and white cloud mountain minnows showed improved welfare, covered in full in our [Neon Tetra enrichment guide](/blog/neon-tetra-enrichment-guide/).

For angelfish, **no evidence of a group size effect on welfare was found**.

That null result is genuinely useful. It means the number that matters most for a tetra is not the number that matters for an angelfish, and it points the enrichment question somewhere else entirely.

<FunFact>
The reason angelfish get treated as shoaling fish is that juveniles are sold in groups and behave like one. Adults do not. A tank stocked on the basis of how six young angelfish behave is a tank that will have a problem in about a year.
</FunFact>

## Height, Not Length

The defining physical requirement. Angelfish are laterally compressed and very tall, and a standard-profile tank cramps them vertically long before it cramps them horizontally.

A deep tank such as a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08BJB7FX4?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Tetra Glass Aquarium, 55 Gallons">55 gallon</AffiliateLink> gives adults room to turn and hold position without their fins touching anything. Height is the dimension to buy.

## Vertical Structure

They evolved among flooded root systems and vertical stems, and they use the water column in a way flat-bodied swimmers do not.

Tall <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4grZYPq" product="Live terrarium plants">live planting</AffiliateLink>, upright stems, and <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eR0Z2l" product="Driftwood">driftwood</AffiliateLink> arranged with vertical elements give the tank a structure they can orient to. It also breaks sight lines, which matters for the section below.

Leave open water to swim through. Angelfish want cover and cruising space, not a thicket.

## Territory Is the Social Variable Here

Since group size is not the lever, territory is. Adults pair off and defend space, and a bonded pair in a community tank will push other fish away from their chosen corner, more so around spawning.

Break the tank into visual zones with planting and wood, so a subordinate fish can get out of sight of a dominant one. Sight lines are what turn ordinary cichlid territoriality into one fish being hounded into a corner.

Either keep a bonded pair, or keep enough fish in a large enough tank that aggression spreads across the group. Two or three adults in a modest tank is the setup that produces a bullied fish.

## Tank Mates, Chosen Carefully

Adult angelfish eat what fits in their mouths, which is why the classic angelfish and neon tetra community so often ends with fewer neons. [Cardinal tetras](/blog/cardinal-tetra-enrichment-guide/) are slightly larger and the usual substitute.

Avoid fin nippers in the other direction. Long trailing fins on a slow fish are an invitation to barbs and some tetras, and [shredded fins are a route into infection](/blog/angelfish-health-issues-guide/).

## Feeding

Intelligent, food-motivated cichlids that learn a routine quickly and will beg. Vary the diet, include frozen and live foods, and spread feeding across the tank so slower fish get a share.

Do not feed to the begging. Angelfish are convincing and overfeeding a deep-bodied fish is easy.

## Priority Order

1. A tall tank, since height is the constrained dimension
2. Vertical structure and broken sight lines
3. Tank mates too large to be eaten and unlikely to nip
4. A bonded pair, or a group big enough to spread aggression
5. Varied diet fed across the tank
6. Stable water, changed little and often
7. Group size, which the study says is not the thing to optimize here

## What Not to Do

Do not apply shoaling advice to a cichlid, since the study found it does not apply. Do not stock on the basis of how juveniles behave. Do not buy a long shallow tank for a tall fish. Do not keep two or three adults in a modest tank and expect the aggression to spread. Do not keep them with fin nippers or with fish small enough to eat.

Height is the dimension that decides most of the above. A tank that is long and shallow fails this fish no matter what goes in it.
