# Common Stick Insect Health Issues (Molting Is the Biggest Risk)

<VetDisclaimer />
<AffiliateDisclosure />

Stick insects are genuinely hardy, and nearly everything that goes wrong traces back to one of two opposite mistakes: an enclosure too dry, or one too wet. Getting that balance right is most of what this species' health actually depends on. See our [tank setup guide](/blog/stick-insect-tank-setup-guide/) for the husbandry side of prevention.

## Molting Failure (Dysecdysis)

This is the single biggest risk for a pet stick insect. Watch for a shed that doesn't complete properly, resulting in deformity or death. It's caused by insufficient vertical space, this species needs enough height to hang freely while shedding, or humidity that's too low leading up to the molt. This isn't something to treat after the fact, prevention through correct enclosure height and misting, tracked with a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3QHrM81" product="Digital thermometer/hygrometer combo">digital hygrometer</AffiliateLink> rather than guesswork, is really the only effective approach.

<FunFact>
A stick insect molts by hanging from the top of its enclosure, and if the space beneath it is too short or the humidity too low in the days before, the result can be a permanently deformed or fatal molt.
</FunFact>

## Dehydration and Drowning Risk

Two opposite problems worth knowing about together. Dehydration comes from insufficient misting, stick insects drink water droplets rather than from a dish, so a reliable <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4vPKMjX" product="Fine mist spray bottle">spray bottle</AffiliateLink> and a consistent schedule prevents it. Drowning, meanwhile, is a real risk for small nymphs if you're using an open water reservoir to keep cut host-plant foliage fresh, cover or otherwise secure any standing water.

## Mold and Fungal or Bacterial Infection

From an enclosure kept too damp combined with poor ventilation or infrequent cleaning. Prevented with good airflow and a regular substrate change, roughly weekly is a reasonable habit.

## Leg Loss

Not a disease, but worth including here since it's a genuine and common outcome of rough handling. A stick insect that loses a leg through autotomy may partially regrow it across subsequent molts if it's a nymph, but an adult's lost leg is permanent. This connects directly to the gentle, body-only handling technique covered in our [handling guide](/blog/stick-insect-handling-guide/).

## The Husbandry Pattern Worth Remembering

Low humidity combined with insufficient enclosure height causes the molting failures that are this species' biggest real risk. Excess dampness with poor airflow invites mold instead. And rough handling causes preventable leg loss. Balance and gentleness, more than any complex intervention, are really the whole story with keeping stick insects healthy.
