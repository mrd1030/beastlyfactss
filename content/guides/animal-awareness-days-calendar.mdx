An animal awareness day is not an official thing. There is no registry and no authority handing out dates. Some of these were adopted by United Nations General Assembly resolution. Others were invented by one conservation charity, a hobbyist forum, or a single person who made a web page and waited to see if it stuck.

That matters because it decides who you believe about the date. When the UN sets an observance, the date is in a resolution and it does not move. When a Colombian sloth foundation sets one, the foundation's own site is the record, and if it says the third Saturday in October then that is the date, no matter how many calendars have copied 20 October off each other.

Three of the days below move every year. Most published calendars print all three as fixed numbers.

## January to March

**5 January, National Bird Day.** A US observance, run alongside the Avian Welfare Coalition's campaigning on the captive bird trade.

**Third Saturday in February, World Pangolin Day.** 21 February in 2026, 20 February in 2027. Pangolins are widely described as the most trafficked wild mammal in the world, and [all eight species are threatened](/beastlypedia/pangolin/), four in Africa and four in Asia.

**3 March, World Wildlife Day.** The strongest credential on this list. The UN General Assembly adopted it in 2013, and the date is the anniversary of the signing of CITES in 1973.

**20 March, World Frog Day.** Amphibians are the most threatened vertebrate class on Earth, which gives the day more weight than its cheerful branding suggests.

## April to June

**25 April, World Penguin Day.** Timed to the annual northward migration of Adélie penguins from Antarctica. Of the eighteen species, [the emperor is the one that breeds through the Antarctic winter](/beastlypedia/emperor-penguin/) instead of escaping it.

**20 May, World Bee Day.** A UN observance, adopted in 2017 on Slovenia's proposal. The date is the birthday of Anton Janša, the eighteenth century Slovenian beekeeper.

**23 May, World Turtle Day.** Created by American Tortoise Rescue in 2000.

**31 May, World Parrot Day.** Started by the World Parrot Trust in 2004, originally to push for an end to wild-caught bird imports into the EU.

**8 June, World Oceans Day.** Proposed at the 1992 Earth Summit in Rio and formally recognised by the UN in 2008.

**16 June, World Sea Turtle Day.** The birthday of Archie Carr, the biologist whose work founded modern sea turtle conservation.

**17 June, World Croc Day.**

**21 June, World Giraffe Day.** Run by the Giraffe Conservation Foundation and set on the solstice, on the reasoning that the longest day suits the tallest animal.

## July to September

**14 July, Shark Awareness Day.**

**16 July, World Snake Day.**

**29 July, International Tiger Day.** Agreed at the 2010 Saint Petersburg Tiger Summit, where the thirteen tiger range countries committed to doubling wild tiger numbers by 2022.

**8 August, International Cat Day.**

**10 August, World Lion Day.**

**12 August, World Elephant Day.** Launched in 2012.

**13 August, International Wolf Day.** The [dominance hierarchy that most people picture when they hear "alpha wolf"](/blog/alpha-wolf-myth-what-the-research-actually-says/) came from captive studies and does not describe how wild packs are actually organised.

**14 August, World Lizard Day.**

**15 August, National Honey Bee Day.** A US observance, distinct from the UN's World Bee Day in May.

**26 August, International Dog Day.**

**12 September, World Dolphin Day.**

**22 September, World Rhino Day.** Started by WWF South Africa in 2010. All five surviving species are covered by it, and [the horn the poaching is built on is keratin](/blog/a-rhino-horn-is-made-of-keratin/), the same protein as fingernails, with no bony core at all.

**Fourth Saturday in September, International Rabbit Day.** 26 September in 2026, 25 September in 2027.

## October

October is the densest month on the calendar.

**4 October, World Animal Day.** The oldest of them, founded in 1925, and [the reason it sits on 4 October is a booking problem](/blog/world-animal-day/) rather than anything to do with animals.

**8 October, World Octopus Day.** Started in 2007 by members of TONMO, an octopus enthusiast forum, as the opening day of Cephalopod Awareness Days. The date was chosen for the eight arms. The following days cover nautiluses, squid and cuttlefish, and then cephalopods in myth. [An octopus carries about two thirds of its neurons in its arms](/beastlypedia/octopus/), leaving only a third in its brain.

**Third Saturday in October, International Sloth Day.** 17 October in 2026, 16 October in 2027. Founded in 2010 by AIUNAU, a Colombian wildlife foundation, after the first international meeting on sloth welfare and conservation in Medellín. This is the date most calendars get wrong, including several large ones that print 20 October as a fixed annual date.

**21 October, National Reptile Awareness Day.** A US observance with genuinely unclear origins. No founding organization claims it, and the earliest solid traces are promotion by reptile publications and conservation groups. There is no founding announcement to point at.

**22 October, International Wombat Day.** Also unattributed. The date appears to date from 2005 and the usual explanation, that it falls near Australian spring planting, is folklore with no sourced account behind it. [A wombat's cubic droppings](/beastlypedia/wombat/) are the fact that carries the day every year.

## November and December

**12 November, National Axolotl Day.** Wild axolotls survive in a single lake system near Mexico City, and the population there is critically endangered while the animal is simultaneously one of the most common amphibians in captivity.

## The three that move

If you are scheduling anything against these, only three need re-checking each year:

| Day | Rule | 2026 | 2027 |
|---|---|---|---|
| World Pangolin Day | Third Saturday in February | 21 February | 20 February |
| International Sloth Day | Third Saturday in October | 17 October | 16 October |
| International Rabbit Day | Fourth Saturday in September | 26 September | 25 September |

The rest of the dates on this page are fixed and have been for years, in several cases for decades.

## How to check a date yourself

The rule that resolves almost every disagreement is to go to whoever created the day. A UN observance has a resolution number and a UN page. A charity-founded day has the charity. Where neither exists, as with reptile awareness day and wombat day, no source is authoritative and the consensus date is the best available answer, which is worth saying plainly rather than inventing a founder.

Aggregator calendars are where the errors breed. They copy each other, a floating date gets flattened into whatever number it fell on the year someone transcribed it, and the wrong figure then appears in fifty places and looks corroborated. The sloth day error is exactly this: 20 October was the third Saturday in 2013, and it has been reprinted ever since.
