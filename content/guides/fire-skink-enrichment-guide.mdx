# Fire Skink Enrichment: The Study Where Skinks Stopped Hiding

<AffiliateDisclosure />

The most common complaint about this species is that you never see it. There is a study on another skink that makes that worth thinking about.

<KeyTakeaway>
Blue-tongue skinks given live mealworms spent more time feeding, gained more weight, and spent **less time hiding**. Fire skinks are semi-fossorial and hiding is normal for them, so this is not a reason to worry about a buried skink. It is a reason to notice that hiding and having nothing to do can look identical from outside the glass.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Research Says

Nothing on *Lepidothyris fernandi*. The borrow is from eastern blue-tongue lizards, covered in full in our [Blue-Tongue Skink enrichment guide](/blog/blue-tongue-skink-enrichment-guide/).

Nine animals were kept individually with a brick for basking and a pipe for hiding. Adding live mealworms **increased time spent feeding**, **increased liveweight gain**, and **reduced time spent hiding**.

Different genus, very different animal, and it is still the closest thing to a controlled skink enrichment experiment that exists. The two share the thing that matters here: a ground-dwelling lizard whose main visible behavior is the choice between being out and being under something.

<FunFact>
Fire skinks get called expensive substrate by keepers who bought one and then never saw it again. The joke is fair and it is also a description of an enclosure where nothing has been made worth coming out for.
</FunFact>

## Substrate, Deep and Slightly Moist

The highest-value item and the one people skimp on because it makes the enclosure harder to inspect. Fire skinks are semi-fossorial forest floor animals that spend most of their time under things.

A deep bed of <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CY23K32S?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="VCEPJH Loose Coconut Fiber Substrate, 2 Pack Natural Coco Coir Reptile Bedding">coconut fiber substrate</AffiliateLink> holds a tunnel shape when slightly damp and lets a skink genuinely disappear instead of scratching at a floor. Other sizes and volumes are available, and a decent-sized enclosure takes more than one pack.

Damp underneath with good ventilation above, not saturated throughout.

## Leaf Litter and Ground Cover

The layer above the substrate, and it is what turns a bare surface into forest floor. Leaf litter, cork flats laid down and not propped up, and low planting give a skink cover it can move **through** rather than only hide **in**.

## Live Prey, Which Is the Tested Item

The one thing the borrowed study directly supports. Release live insects into the leaf litter rather than dropping them in a dish, so finding them is the activity.

<AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0FB3PRD2Y?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="4-Pack Reptile Feeding Tongs Set">Feeding tongs</AffiliateLink> are useful for the other half, offering an item directly and making the skink come and take it, which is how some keepers get a reclusive animal used to appearing at feeding time. Vary when and where prey turns up.

## Thermal Choice Under Cover

Warm end, cool end, and cover at both. A gradient a skink can only use by sitting exposed is a gradient it will mostly refuse to use.

Put hides, litter depth and structure across the full range, so temperature and security are independent choices.

## Digging Room, Horizontally

Floor area matters more than height for this species. A long enclosure with deep substrate gives a burrowing animal somewhere to make more than one tunnel, and more than one place to be.

Height is close to wasted here. Spend it on footprint instead.

## Priority Order

1. Deep, slightly moist, diggable substrate
2. Floor area over height
3. Leaf litter and low ground cover across the whole floor
4. A thermal gradient with cover at both ends
5. Live prey released into the litter
6. Varying when and where food appears
7. Rearranging the layout, occasionally and gently

## What Not to Do

Do not give shallow substrate to a semi-fossorial animal, which is the most common version of getting this species wrong. Do not read permanent invisibility as contentment without checking what feeding looks like. Do not keep the enclosure saturated, since damp with no ventilation is how respiratory and skin problems start, covered in our health issues guide. Do not house males together. And see our handling guide before treating handling as enrichment for a fast, secretive animal that would rather be underground.
