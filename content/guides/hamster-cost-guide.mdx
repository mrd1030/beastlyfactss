# How Much Does a Hamster Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Hamsters are one of the cheapest pets to buy and one of the easiest to underfund properly. The animal costs almost nothing. Doing right by it costs considerably more.

## The Hamster Itself: %%animal:hamster%%

Hawaii is the one state where none of these numbers matter, because hamsters cannot be kept there at all, and the same rule catches gerbils. Oregon's rule is unclear, so check before you buy there. Everywhere else you can walk out with one the same day.

Syrian (golden or "teddy bear") hamsters run about $25 at a pet store chain, whatever the coat. Dwarf species run about %%animal:hamster%%, with the Russian dwarf cheapest and the Chinese, Winter White and Roborovski at the top. Rescues rehome hamsters as well. Color and coat variation (short-haired, long-haired, satin, curly "rex") add some variety but rarely a meaningful price jump. This isn't a species with the kind of morph-driven pricing you'd see in reptiles.

Enclosure size is the biggest line item, and it's also the one most starter kits get wrong: go bigger than a basic kit offers. A Syrian's enclosure runs $30 for a large bin up to $250 for a ready-made 47-inch cage; a dwarf fits a 40-gallon breeder tank or a bin with the same floor, $30 to $150. The rows add up to %%setup:hamster%% with a dwarf and $185 to $550 with a Syrian, animal included, so the range for the whole setup is $165 to $550. The bedding, pellet, chew and hay rows are the first supply; after that they move to the yearly table below. A humane setup costs meaningfully more than the animal itself, which is exactly backwards from what the cheap sticker price suggests.

## Vet Costs

A small-mammal wellness exam runs about $80 to $115 at exotic practices that publish their fees, and a sick-animal exam a little more. An emergency exam, most urgently for [wet tail](/blog/hamster-health-issues-guide/), runs about $200 before any tests or treatment, and some hospitals add an after-hours fee on top. Wet tail specifically is a same-day emergency, not a wait-and-see situation.

## Lifespan: Short, Which Changes the Whole Calculation

This is where hamsters differ most from nearly every other pet. A large veterinary study (O'Neill et al., Journal of Small Animal Practice, 2022) tracking hamster deaths found a median age at death of just 1.75 years, with a range extending to about 3.65 years.

<FunFact>
A large-scale veterinary study tracking hamster deaths found a median age at death of just 1.75 years. Syrians and Roborovskis tend toward the higher end of the hamster lifespan range, roughly 2 to 3 years, Chinese hamsters 1.5 to 3, while Winter Whites often live only about a year. A hamster of 1.5 years or more already counts as elderly. It's a brief commitment compared to almost any other pet, which matters both emotionally and financially, less time to spread the setup cost across, but also less long-term expense overall.
</FunFact>

## The Cheapest Animal, Not the Cheapest Pet

Here's the pattern worth remembering: a hamster is the cheapest animal to buy in this roundup, but it demands a setup that's disproportionately expensive relative to that sticker price, a 700-plus-square-inch enclosure and six-plus inches of bedding cost real money no matter how little the hamster itself did. It's worth keeping that in mind if you're also weighing the cost of a blue-tongue skink, a White's tree frog, or a Pacman frog side by side, and [Four Pets, Four Different Lessons](/blog/hamster-skink-whitestreefrog-pacmanfrog-overview/) walks through all four together. None of these animals cost much on their own. What you spend on housing them properly is the real budget line.
