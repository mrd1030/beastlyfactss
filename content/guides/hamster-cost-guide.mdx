# How Much Does a Hamster Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Hamsters are one of the cheapest pets to buy and one of the easiest to underfund properly. The animal costs almost nothing. Doing right by it costs considerably more. For the full care picture beyond budgeting, see our complete [Hamster care guide](/guides/hamster/), or our companion guides on [handling](/blog/hamster-handling-guide/), [health issues](/blog/hamster-health-issues-guide/), and [enclosure setup](/blog/hamster-tank-setup-guide/).

## The Hamster Itself: $15 to $30

Syrian (golden or "teddy bear") hamsters typically run $15 to $25 at pet stores. Dwarf species, Winter White, Campbell's, Roborovski, and Chinese, run roughly $8 to $30, with Roborovski and Chinese often at the higher end. Rescue or adoption is usually $5 to $15. Color and coat variation (short-haired, long-haired, satin, curly "rex") add some variety but rarely a meaningful price jump. This isn't a species with the kind of morph-driven pricing you'd see in reptiles.

Enclosure size is the biggest line item, and it's also the one most starter kits get wrong: budget $30 to $90 and go bigger than a basic kit offers, more on why in our [Hamster enclosure setup guide](/blog/hamster-tank-setup-guide/). Add a water bottle, sand bath, and chew items, and most first-time setups land around $150 to $350 total. A humane setup costs meaningfully more than the animal itself, which is exactly backwards from what the cheap sticker price suggests.

## Ongoing Costs: Roughly $15 to $50 a Month

Food runs $5 to $12 a month. Bedding and sand bath refills are the bigger recurring cost, $15 to $30 a month, since deep bedding needs regular replacing to stay clean and dust free. ## Vet Costs

A routine exam runs $40 to $80. Emergency care, most urgently for wet tail, can run $100 to $800 depending on severity. Wet tail specifically is a same-day emergency, not a wait-and-see situation, covered in more detail in our [Hamster health issues guide](/blog/hamster-health-issues-guide/).

## Lifespan: Short, Which Changes the Whole Calculation

This is where hamsters differ most from nearly every other pet on this site. A large veterinary study (O'Neill et al., Journal of Small Animal Practice, 2022) tracking hamster deaths found a median age at death of just 1.75 years, with a range extending to about 3.65 years.

<FunFact>
A large-scale veterinary study tracking hamster deaths found a median age at death of just 1.75 years. Syrians tend toward the higher end of the hamster lifespan range, roughly 2 to 3 years, while dwarf species often run shorter, 1.5 to 2.5 years. It's a genuinely brief commitment compared to almost any other pet on this site, which matters both emotionally and financially, less time to spread the setup cost across, but also less long-term expense overall.
</FunFact>

## The Cheapest Animal, Not the Cheapest Pet

Here's the pattern worth remembering: a hamster is the cheapest animal to buy in this roundup, but it demands a setup that's disproportionately expensive relative to that sticker price, a 700-plus-square-inch enclosure and six-plus inches of bedding cost real money no matter how little the hamster itself did. It's worth keeping that in mind if you're also weighing the cost of a [blue-tongue skink](/blog/blue-tongue-skink-cost-guide/), a [White's tree frog](/blog/whites-tree-frog-cost-guide/), or a [Pacman frog](/blog/pacman-frog-cost-guide/) side by side. None of these animals cost much on their own. What you spend on housing them properly is the real budget line.
