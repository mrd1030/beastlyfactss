Hamsters are the most legal exotic-adjacent pet in the United States. Fifty states and the District of Columbia, and exactly one of them says no.

That makes this a short guide with two things worth your attention: the state that bans them does so absolutely, with no permit and no exception, and several states that allow them name a specific species in the rule. If the hamster you want is not that species, the sentence clearing it may not reach your animal.

## Hawaii Is the Only Ban

Hawaii bans hamsters outright. *Mesocricetus auratus*, the golden or Syrian hamster, appears on the state's List of Restricted Animals under Part A, which is headed "FOR RESEARCH AND EXHIBITION." That heading is the whole answer. Part A animals may be brought in by a university or a licensed exhibitor under permit. They may not be kept as pets, and there is no separate pet permit to apply for.

It is worth understanding why, because the reason is not about hamsters specifically. Hawaii runs an inverted rule: under HAR § 4-71-6, introduction of live animals "is prohibited except for those animals on the lists" of approved species. Everything is barred unless something clears it, and the only list that clears the golden hamster puts it in a research category. Hawaii's concern is escape and establishment, the same reason it bans every snake and the mongoose it already has.

The gerbil is on the same restricted list, under *Meriones unguiculatus*. So is the hedgehog's whole order. If you are moving to Hawaii with a small pet, assume no until you find it on a list.

Alaska and Wyoming are the ones to notice. Both work from closed lists, both name only the golden hamster, and neither mentions *Phodopus*. Read strictly, a Roborovski or a winter white is not the animal the rule cleared. In practice nobody is checking a dwarf hamster at the state line, but if you are the sort of person who wants the paperwork to actually match the animal, this is the gap.

## California Allows Hamsters and Bans Gerbils

California restricts more species than any other state, so it is a reasonable guess that hamsters are on the list. They are the opposite. Title 14 § 671 restricts the order Rodentia as detrimental, then writes five exceptions into it, and one of them says:

> Domesticated races of golden hamsters of the species Mesocricetus auratus and domesticated races of dwarf hamsters of the Genus Phodopus are not restricted

Both groups, by name, with the word domesticated doing the work of excluding wild-caught stock. It is the most precise pet-friendly sentence in California's entire restricted species regulation.

Then look at the cage next to it. Gerbils are not among the five exceptions, so the restricted classification stands and there is no permit type that covers pet keeping. California has banned the gerbil since the 1960s over concern that escaped animals would establish in the state's dry country, and the ban is still enforced. Two animals sold side by side in every other state, one perfectly legal in California and the other not.

<FunFact>
Washington DC gets hamsters right for a reason that has nothing to do with hamsters. D.C. Code § 8-1808(j) bans keeping "any living member of the animal kingdom" as a household pet except seven categories, one of which is "Domesticated rodents and rabbits." A hamster is a rodent, so it clears. A hedgehog is not a rodent, it is in the order Eulipotyphla with moles and shrews, and no other category fits, so the same sentence that permits your hamster prohibits a hedgehog on a taxonomic technicality.
</FunFact>

## Nebraska, and Why Absence Is Not Always a Ban

Nebraska is the one place where the answer takes a second look. Its wildlife rules run a closed importation list at 166 NAC 8, § 001.08A, and the list includes the chinchilla, the degu, the gerbil, the guinea pig and the greater jerboa. There is no hamster on it.

That looks alarming until you read what the list is a list of. The chapter governs wild birds and wild mammals as defined in Neb. Rev. Stat. 37-245 and 37-246. The golden hamster is a domesticated animal, not Nebraska wildlife, so the importation list is not the rule that applies to it. The guinea pig appearing on that list anyway is a fair sign the drafting is not perfectly consistent, but the structure is clear enough.

This is the general shape of hamster law across the country. States that regulate rodents are worried about wild ones, prairie dogs and pouched rats and anything that might establish outdoors. The hamster keeps clearing those rules because it is domesticated, small, and would not survive a winter loose in most of the United States.

## The Real Takeaway

Everywhere except Hawaii, you can keep a hamster and nobody needs to know. Hawaii is an absolute no with no permit route, and the same is true there for gerbils.

The one thing worth checking, if you keep dwarf hamsters rather than a Syrian, is whether your state's rule names *Phodopus* or only *Mesocricetus auratus*. California and Rhode Island name both. Alaska and Wyoming name one.
