# Russian Tortoise Enclosure Setup: Compact Housing With Real Requirements

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Russian tortoise care is genuinely manageable indoors for this species' entire life, and getting the enclosure type, temperature, and humidity right is most of what it takes to keep one thriving. For what all of it costs, see our [cost guide](/blog/russian-tortoise-cost-guide/).

## Enclosure Size

An absolute indoor minimum of 4 feet by 2 feet by 2 feet, though a more comfortable functional size is closer to 7 feet long by 3.5 feet wide. An <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3Rkp3S6" product="Tortoise table">open-topped tortoise table</AffiliateLink> is strongly preferred over a closed glass aquarium, better ventilation and considerably less stress for the tortoise. Floor space matters most for this terrestrial species. House alone, or with caution, two females together, but not multiple males, our [handling guide](/blog/russian-tortoise-handling-guide/) covers why male territoriality makes that a bad idea.

## Temperature

Basking spot around 95 to 100°F, measured at the surface with a temperature gun, cool side in the 70s°F. This is a genuinely cold-hardy species, tolerating night drops to around 60°F without needing supplemental night heat in most homes. A basking heat source, a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B000YS3H0I?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Exo Terra Sun Glo Halogen Basking Spot Lamp, 50W">halogen bulb</AffiliateLink> works well, is needed indoors during the day; tortoises kept outdoors in mild climates often need no additional heat at all.

## Humidity

Keep ambient humidity relatively low, 30 to 50%, appropriate for this arid-adapted species, but provide a moist hide lined with peat moss to create a humid microclimate for hydration and shedding. High ambient humidity across the whole enclosure is a real cause of respiratory infection, covered in our [health issues guide](/blog/russian-tortoise-health-issues-guide/), so resist the instinct to keep everything damp.

## Substrate

A <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CY23K32S?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="VCEPJH Loose Coconut Fiber Substrate, 2 Pack">50/50 mix of topsoil and coconut coir</AffiliateLink>, or a similar sand and loam blend, deep enough to support burrowing and kept slightly moist rather than bone dry. Avoid pure sand, which can cause mobility and impaction issues, and skip rabbit pellets or newspaper-only setups, neither supports natural behavior well.

## Lighting

Daily UVB is essential. A T5 HO tube, Arcadia 12% or <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0FF9GHCDC?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product='Zoo Med ReptiSun T5 HO Terrarium Hood with 10.0 Bulb 24" L'>Zoo Med ReptiSun 10.0</AffiliateLink> are both well-regarded choices, in a reflective fixture, sized to cover roughly a third to half the enclosure length. Target a basking-area UVI around 3.0 to 4.0, a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B076GXJFJG?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Solarmeter Model 6.5R Reptile UV Index Meter">UV index meter</AffiliateLink> takes the guesswork out of confirming that number. Avoid coil or compact UVB bulbs, they're not considered adequate for this species. Pair with a bright 6500K light.

## The Non-Obvious Requirement: Brumation

Russian tortoises are physiologically programmed to brumate, entering a period of dormancy for roughly 2 to 4 months over winter at cooler temperatures, around 40 to 55°F. Wild-caught adults especially may refuse food or show real stress if prevented from doing this entirely. Here's the genuine advantage of indoor keeping though: by maintaining full lighting, heat, and feeding year-round, indoor keepers can choose to keep their tortoise active through winter rather than brumating it, a real option this species offers that many others don't. If you do choose to brumate your tortoise, a pre-brumation vet health check is worth doing first.
