# Is It Legal to Own an Axolotl? State-by-State Guide (Virginia Myth Corrected)

<LegalDisclaimer />

Axolotls have a real, if narrow, ban problem in the US: a handful of states ban them outright, and about a dozen jurisdictions restrict them in some way. They also have a persistent myth problem: Virginia gets listed as banned across a surprising number of hobbyist sites and forum posts, and it simply hasn't been true since 2021.

## The Virginia Correction

This is worth stating plainly since the wrong version circulates so widely: as of August 1, 2021, it is legal to import, export, sell, and possess axolotls in Virginia, no permit required. This followed an amendment to Virginia's administrative code that specifically carved axolotls out of the state's general salamander-sale prohibition. If you've seen Virginia listed as a ban state, that information predates 2021 and hasn't been updated.

<FunFact>
Axolotls belong to genus Ambystoma, the same genus as North American mole and tiger salamanders, including California's own native tiger salamander, which is federally threatened. That taxonomic relationship, not anything about [axolotls being rare or endangered](/encyclopedia/animal/axolotl/) themselves, is the actual reason behind every restriction on this list.
</FunFact>

## Why These Bans Exist: Ecology, Not Conservation

The restrictions that do exist share a common logic, and it isn't about protecting axolotls. Regulators worry about hybridization between axolotls and native Ambystoma salamanders, the risk of an invasive population establishing if animals are released, and disease transmission, particularly the chytrid fungus. California's "detrimental species" label makes this reasoning explicit rather than leaving it implied.

## The 2025 Federal Rule: A Real Change, But Not an Ownership Restriction

A federal rule effective January 10, 2025 added 16 more salamander genera to the list of injurious wildlife under the Lacey Act, bringing the total to 36 genera, all tied to concerns about the Batrachochytrium salamandrivorans (Bsal) fungus. Genus Ambystoma, which includes both axolotls and tiger salamanders, is among the newly added genera.

Here's the part that matters for pet owners: this listing restricts importing axolotls into the US and shipping them to or from Hawaii, DC, Puerto Rico, or US territories. It does not restrict ownership, breeding, or sale within the 49 continental states, a distinction confirmed directly by USFWS's own FAQ on the rule. Our [Tiger Salamander legal guide](/blog/tiger-salamander-legal-guide/) covers this same federal rule in more depth, since it applies identically to both species in this genus.

## The Takeaway

If you live in California, New Jersey, Maine, Wyoming, Alabama, or DC, this isn't a species worth testing the boundaries on, the restrictions are real and specifically named. Everywhere else, including Virginia since 2021, axolotls are a legal pet, and the federal rule that made headlines in early 2025 is about importation and cross-territory shipment, not about whether you can keep the one you already have.
