# How Much Does a Russian Tortoise Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Russian tortoises are genuinely one of the more approachable tortoises to own, both in price and, unlike the sulcata already covered on this site, in the fact that this species can live its entire life indoors.

## The Tortoise: $50 to $400

Hatchlings and young captive-bred animals commonly run $50 to $150, with older captive-bred individuals reaching $225 to $400. Wild-caught adults occasionally show up cheaper, sometimes under $50 at reptile expos, though captive-bred is genuinely the better choice for this species, as for most.

One reason the smallest animals are not the ones in shops: a federal rule bars selling any turtle with a shell under four inches commercially, which pushes hatchlings out of retail and into private hands. No state bans this species, and New Jersey is the only place that wants a permit. Our [Russian tortoise legal guide](/blog/russian-tortoise-legal-guide/) covers what that four-inch rule actually prohibits, which is less than most people assume.

## Vet Costs

A routine exotic-vet checkup runs around $75, meaningfully more reliable than a general vet visit for this species. Illness requiring tests or medication commonly runs $150 to $300 or more.

## Lifespan: The Number That Matters Most for Planning

40 years or more is typical, with some individuals reaching 50-plus. This is genuinely a multi-decade, potentially generational commitment, the kind of pet you might realistically be planning a succession of care for, not just your own ownership.

## Why This Species Is a Genuinely Good Fit for Apartment Living

This is the detail that separates the Russian tortoise from the sulcata covered elsewhere on this site. Sulcatas grow enormous and need real outdoor space within a few years, see our [Sulcata Tortoise cost guide](/blog/sulcata-tortoise-cost-guide/) for what that scale of commitment actually costs. Russian tortoises stay small, 5 to 10 inches, and can be kept entirely indoors for their full, decades-long lifespan without ever needing outdoor housing. If you're drawn to tortoises but don't have, or won't eventually have, serious outdoor space, this is the more realistic choice.
