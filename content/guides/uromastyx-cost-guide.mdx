# How Much Does a Uromastyx Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Uromastyx sit in a similar price range to many popular pet lizards, but the setup cost tends to run higher than average, driven by just how hot and specialized this species' enclosure needs to be.

## The Lizard: $150 to $400

Common captive-bred species, Mali and ornate uromastyx among them, typically run in this range, with hatchlings toward the lower end and adults higher. Rarer or imported species can reach $300 to $800.

## Color and Price

More vividly colored species and individuals, bright reds, oranges, greens, and blues, command higher prices than the more common drab brown or gray animals. As a general rule, the more colorful the uromastyx, the more it costs.

The enclosure is the biggest single expense on this list, and it needs to be 4x2x2 feet at minimum from day one, since a smaller box just becomes a second purchase. The <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/43AcIvA" product="Arcadia T5 UVB Reptile Light Fixture Kit (36&quot; / 14% UVB Dragon)">T5 HO UVB kit</AffiliateLink> is the second biggest, and the one that cannot be downgraded: this species basks in Ferguson Zone 4, the most intense UVB zone. Halogens run on a [dimming thermostat](/blog/reptile-heating-thermostats-guide/); an on/off unit flicks a bulb on and off all day, which is the wrong controller for them.

What the table leaves out is the part that varies most. A 4x2 foot floor packed 4 inches deep takes at least 2.5 cubic feet of <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0002DIZKC?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Zoo Med ReptiSand, 10 Pounds, Desert White">sand</AffiliateLink> or a sand, topsoil and clay mix, which is many bags of packaged reptile sand, and the whole bed is replaced every 3 to 4 months. Add a bright daylight LED bar, a couple of hides, and flat slate or flagstone with spacers for the basking stack. Altogether, that's how a first-time setup lands at roughly $500 to $900.

Be cautious with pre-made "complete" starter kits, some are sized for a 20-gallon tank, which is genuinely too small for this species as an adult.

Fresh greens and produce, seeds, supplements, and the cost of running high-wattage bulbs make up the monthly total. ## Vet Costs

A routine exotic exam runs $50 to $100, with a fecal test adding $20 to $50. Emergencies involving metabolic bone disease, impaction, or egg-binding can run several hundred dollars. The first visit comes within the settling period, and a uromastyx that has not eaten by the end of its first two weeks goes straight to the vet.

## Lifespan

15 to 20 years is the number most commonly cited for this species. Truly high-quality care can push a uromastyx's lifespan past 25 years.
