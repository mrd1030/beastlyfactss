# How Much Does a Bearded Dragon Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

The dragon itself is the cheap part.

A standard morph runs $40 to $100 at most pet stores, sometimes less from a breeder. The equipment around it is where the budget goes, and it isn't optional. Bearded dragons need a specific setup to stay healthy, and cutting corners here is how expensive vet bills happen later.

## Where the Dragon Comes From

Most bearded dragons come from breeders or pet stores, since the species breeds well in captivity and is easy to find. A young, captive-raised dragon makes the better pet. An older, imported animal is harder to tame, more likely to carry intestinal parasites, and often stressed by captivity, so the cheapest adult on offer can end up the most expensive one.

Wherever it comes from, check the animal before paying:

- **Alert and active**, head up and watching you.
- **A round, plump tail base** just behind the back legs. A thin tail base, loose skin or a skinny body is a warning sign.
- **Bright, open eyes**, not sunken ones.
- **A bright pink mouth with clear saliva.** Cloudy mucus, cottage-cheese material, or red or bruised gums point to infection.

Ask the seller about a health guarantee, and book the reptile vet for the first 48 hours: that first exam is the $120 to $245 line below. If another reptile already lives in the house, the new dragon also needs a separate room and its own tools, and the [reptile quarantine guide](/blog/reptile-quarantine-guide/) sets out that setup and the three to six month clock.

Here's what a proper first-time setup actually requires:

Highly recommended, not counted in the total: a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B076GXJFJG?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Solarmeter Model 6.5R Reptile UV Index Meter">UV index meter</AffiliateLink>, about $240 to $265. It is the only way to check that the UVB tube sits at the right height and to know when the tube needs replacing; UV cards do not measure UVB.

The enclosure is the adult minimum, not a "starter size" you'll outgrow, buying it from day one saves you from buying twice. The UVB fixture isn't a nice-to-have either: bearded dragons need strong, specific UVB or they develop metabolic bone disease, which is both common and preventable. A T5 tube's wattage is not its length: a 24-watt tube is about 22 inches long, a 39-watt tube about 34 inches and a 54-watt tube about 46 inches, so go by the length in inches when you match it to two-thirds of the enclosure.

The thermostat is one of the bigger lines here, and it earns it. It regulates your heat source so it can't overheat and burn your dragon or start a fire, so don't skip it to save money. For thermometers, a digital probe is the minimum, but an infrared temperature gun is worth the extra cost since it reads actual basking surface temperature rather than air temperature.

Substrate costs next to nothing: paper towels work fine and eliminate impaction risk entirely, which matters more than it sounds like it should.

### Going Bioactive

Experienced keepers sometimes trade the paper towel for a living floor: a deep arid soil mix, plants, and a cleanup crew of isopods and springtails that breaks down waste. A [bioactive build](/blog/bioactive-setups-bearded-dragons/) keeps the same lighting and heat, so the extra cost is the floor itself, priced here with a DIY soil mix (bagged arid mixes cost several times more). It is a bigger commitment than paper towel, not a requirement.

The UVB bulb needs replacing every 6 to 12 months, since bulbs stop producing usable UVB well before they burn out, so replacing on a schedule matters even if the bulb still lights up.

<FunFact>
A UVB bulb can look completely normal, still glowing, still lighting up the tank, for months after it's stopped producing usable UV output. The visible light and the UV output degrade on two different timelines, which is exactly why "replace every 6 to 12 months" is a calendar rule, not a "replace when it looks dim" rule.
</FunFact>

Most owners land around $900 to $1,740 a year in routine costs, or roughly %%monthly:bearded-dragon%% a month once you average it out.

## The Cost People Don't Budget For

A single [impaction surgery](/blog/bearded-dragon-health-issues-guide/) runs $800 to $2,500. Metabolic bone disease treatment runs $200 to $500 or more. These are exactly the conditions that correct husbandry prevents, which is why spending properly on the thermostat and UVB upfront is cheaper in the long run than spending less now and gambling on a vet bill later.

If you're budgeting for a bearded dragon, plan for %%setup:bearded-dragon%% to get set up right, then %%monthly:bearded-dragon%% a month after that, plus a mental note that an emergency fund of a few hundred dollars is worth having rather than needing.
