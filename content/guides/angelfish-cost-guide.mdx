# How Much Does Angelfish Ownership Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Angelfish are cheap to buy and expensive to house properly, the tank, not the fish, is where the budget goes with this species.

## The Fish: %%animal:angelfish%%

Common freshwater angelfish run $3 to $20 for standard varieties. Fancy morphs, koi, platinum, marble, zebra, and veil-finned varieties among them, cost more, and rare or extra-large specimens can push past $100 on the secondary market.

<FunFact>
Typing "angelfish" into a shopping search can turn up two completely different animals. Freshwater angelfish run %%animal:angelfish%% for most varieties. Marine angelfish are a different, far pricier group entirely, commonly $150 to $1,500, and need a saltwater reef setup rather than a freshwater community tank. Worth double-checking which one is actually in the listing before you buy.
</FunFact>

## Upfront Setup: %%setup:angelfish%% for a Proper Tank

A 55-gallon tank and stand works well for a small group. A 29-gallon tall is the smallest tank worth starting with, because this species needs height more than length.

## Vet Costs

Most keepers manage illness themselves with water quality correction and over-the-counter medications like <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01M5LEDRA?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Seachem MetroPlex Metronidazole">metronidazole (sold as Fish Zole or MetroPlex)</AffiliateLink> or standard ich treatments, and a water test kit that catches a water problem early avoids a lot of medication in the first place.

A fish vet costs less than most people assume. Fees run about $50 to $100 for a visit in the clinic and about $200 to $300 for an appointment at home, where the vet can see the tank the fish lives in. Set that against the usual alternative: a string of shop trips for conditioner, a bacteria product, an antibiotic powder, an ulcer treatment, new food and filter media adds up to roughly $135 in one board certified fish vet's worked example, often without a diagnosis at the end of it. For a fish that can live 8 to 12 years, and for problems like the gut parasites angelfish carry that are only told apart under a microscope, one exam can be the cheaper route. Most small-animal clinics do not see fish, and the directories for finding one that does are in [the guide to spotting a sick fish](/blog/spotting-a-sick-fish-guide/).

## Lifespan

8 to 12 years is typical with good care, up to around 15 years under genuinely ideal conditions. This is a longer-lived fish than many community species, worth factoring into your tank planning since you'll likely be maintaining this setup for the better part of a decade or more. Compare that to a [guppy](/blog/guppy-cost-guide/), which typically lives only 2 to 3 years, and it's clear these two popular community fish sit at very different points on the commitment scale.

## The Cost Lesson With This Species

The fish itself is the cheapest part of the equation. What determines your total cost is [tank height and quality filtration](/blog/angelfish-tank-setup-guide/), both genuinely non-negotiable for a fish with this body shape and this waste output.
