# Blue-Tongued Skink Feeding: Schedule, Diet, and Why They Stop Eating

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Babies eat daily. Adults start every two days. Adjust from there on body condition.

## Feeding schedule by age

- **Babies (hatching to 3 to 5 months).** Daily. As much as the baby finishes in one sitting. One fasting day a week is fine.
- **Juveniles (roughly 3 to 10 months).** 3 to 4 times a week.
- **Subadults and adults (from about 12 months).** Start every two days. If fat rolls appear at the legs or neck, drop toward twice a week. If the tail base looks thin, stay nearer every other day. Weigh weekly on a gram scale.

One meal about the size of the skink's own skull, or about 1 to 2 tablespoons for an adult.

## What blue-tongued skinks eat

True omnivores. The ratio flips with age. Under about 12 months: protein-heavy, roughly 70 to 80 percent animal matter. Mature adults: plant-heavy, roughly half protein and half greens and vegetables, with about 5 to 10 percent fruit as treats.

**Protein:** gut-loaded crickets, dubia roaches, black soldier fly larvae, silkworms, hornworms, earthworms, captive-bred snails, occasional pinky mice, cooked chicken or turkey, cooked eggs, and high-quality canned dog or cat food. Skip fish-based formulas. Mealworms work as enrichment prey. They are fatty, so they are not the everyday protein. A separate multivitamin about once a week.

**Vegetables and greens:** collard, dandelion, mustard, kale, escarole, arugula, plus peas, green beans, squash, carrots, sweet potato, cucumber, and bell peppers. **Fruit treats:** mango, papaya, berries, melon, banana, and pitted cherries.

## Safe treats and foods to avoid

Safe: berries, mango, papaya, melon, banana, pitted cherries, cooked egg, snails, small amounts of canned cat or dog food.

Toxic: avocado, onion and garlic, rhubarb, fireflies, monarch butterflies, citrus. Eggplant and tomato leaves too. Skip wild-caught insects, centipedes, spiders, and chocolate. Spinach, beet greens, and Swiss chard bind calcium. Occasional, not a staple.

## Why blue-tongued skinks stop eating

1. **Brumation.** Typically November through March, about 3 to 4 months. They hide, sleep more, and largely stop eating and drinking. Little weight loss if temperatures are managed.
2. **Shedding.** Reduced appetite shortly before, during, and briefly after.
3. **Acclimation.** Newly acquired skinks hide and refuse. Captive-bred animals typically settle in about 2 weeks. Wild-caught can take up to 2 months.
4. **Dietary boredom.** The same two foods every time. Vary protein and greens, or mix vegetables into canned food.
5. **Inadequate basking temperature or UVB.** A cool dock or a dead bulb looks like illness. The [tank setup guide](/blog/blue-tongue-skink-tank-setup-guide/) has the surface targets, and they differ between Northern and Indonesian animals.
6. **Illness.** Respiratory infection, parasites, mouth rot, and metabolic bone disease often start as appetite loss.
7. **Impaction.** Substrate or an oversized feeder. Bloating and constipation with poor appetite.
8. **Dehydration.** Wrinkled skin and thick or stringy saliva. Fresh water and water-rich vegetables.

## When to worry

Days to 1 to 3 weeks in an otherwise active, normal-weight adult is unremarkable around a shed or a new enclosure. During brumation, up to about 3 months without food is expected if the skink is not losing significant weight.

Outside brumation, refusal lasting more than a few weeks with no explanation, or weight loss of 7 to 10 percent, is the vet line. Weigh weekly. Babies and juveniles cannot fast like adults. Take prolonged refusal in a young skink seriously sooner.

For the insect half of the diet, [gut loading](/blog/gut-loading-feeder-insects-guide/) is a separate job from dusting.
