# Common California Kingsnake Health Issues

<VetDisclaimer />

California kingsnakes are hardy, and the overwhelming majority of health problems in this species trace back to husbandry errors rather than any inherent fragility. Correct temperature, humidity, and hygiene prevent most of what's on this list.

## The Numbers That Prevent Most of It

A snake kept below this range is set up for respiratory infection, and damp, dirty substrate is what feeds scale rot. Check the thermometers and hygrometer before blaming the snake: most of the problems below start with one of these numbers drifting.

## Respiratory Infection

Wheezing, open-mouth breathing, nasal mucus, and lethargy. Caused by temperatures running too low, unsanitary conditions or excess humidity, and made more likely by parasites, poor nutrition and other illness. Early cases sometimes resolve once parameters are corrected, advanced cases need a vet for antibiotics.

## Scale Rot

Discolored, uneven, or damaged scales, sometimes with swelling and a foul odor. Moist, contaminated bedding lets bacteria and fungi multiply, and combined with contact with feces they cause skin sores. Blisters are the early stage and can heal without ulcerating if treated then. Manageable at home if caught early through improved cleaning and substrate management, see a vet if it progresses.

## Snake Mites

Tiny black or red specks moving around the eyes and vent. A useful early clue: excessive soaking in the water bowl is often a sign of mites before you spot them directly. Treat immediately, mites can become genuinely life-threatening if left unaddressed, and treatment needs to cover both the snake and a full enclosure cleaning, not one or the other.

<FunFact>
Excessive soaking is one of the easiest early mite signs to miss since it looks like ordinary behavior at first glance. A kingsnake spending unusual amounts of time sitting in its water bowl is often trying to drown mites before you've spotted a single one on its scales.
</FunFact>

## Mouth Rot (Infectious Stomatitis)

The earliest sign is tiny purplish-red spots inside the mouth. Later come cheesy or yellowish material along the rows of teeth and swelling of the gums, and a snake with a sore mouth often stops eating. Always see a vet, this can become life-threatening if untreated.

## Shedding Problems (Dysecdysis)

Retained skin from humidity running too low. Manageable at home with a humid hide lined with damp sphagnum moss. The separation itself is done by [fluid and enzymes released under the old skin](/blog/snake-shedding-humidity-myth-guide/), which is why a damp enclosure all month is the wrong fix and a humid hide at the right moment is the right one.

## When a Kingsnake Stops Eating

A skipped meal is not an emergency. Snakes normally go off food around a shed, in the breeding season and when gravid, and California kingsnakes often slow down or stop in the fall, then resume on their own. A pet kept at steady temperatures [does not need brumating](/blog/snake-brumation-guide/), but it may still eat less through the short days.

The threshold is **3 to 4 weeks of refusal**, or any weight loss along the way: past that point, book a reptile vet. Weigh the snake on a gram scale every few weeks and log each meal, since a record of temperatures and feedings is the first thing a vet will ask for. A refusal that comes with open-mouth breathing, mouth swelling or bloating does not wait the 3 weeks.

Every new kingsnake gets a wellness exam after purchase and an annual checkup after that to track weight.

## The Husbandry Pattern Worth Remembering

The vast majority of health problems in captive kingsnakes trace directly to incorrect temperatures, inadequate humidity, dirty conditions, or improper feeding, not genetic susceptibility; the [feeding guide](/blog/california-kingsnake-feeding-guide/) has the prey sizing and the adult interval that prevent the obesity half of that. Getting the basics right prevents nearly everything on this list before it starts.
