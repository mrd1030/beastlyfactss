# Common California Kingsnake Health Issues

<VetDisclaimer />

California kingsnakes are hardy, and the overwhelming majority of health problems in this species trace back to husbandry errors rather than any inherent fragility. Correct temperature, humidity, and hygiene prevent most of what's on this list.

## Respiratory Infection

Wheezing, open-mouth breathing, nasal mucus, and lethargy. Caused by temperatures running too low, excess humidity, or unsanitary conditions. Early cases sometimes resolve once parameters are corrected, advanced cases need a vet for antibiotics.

## Scale Rot

Discolored, uneven, or damaged scales, sometimes with swelling and a foul odor. Caused by dirty or damp substrate and general poor hygiene. Manageable at home if caught early through improved cleaning and substrate management, see a vet if it progresses.

## Snake Mites

Tiny black or red specks moving around the eyes and vent. A useful early clue: excessive soaking in the water bowl is often a sign of mites before you spot them directly. Treat immediately, mites can become genuinely life-threatening if left unaddressed, and treatment needs to cover both the snake and a full enclosure cleaning, not one or the other.

<FunFact>
Excessive soaking is one of the easiest early mite signs to miss since it looks like ordinary behavior at first glance. A kingsnake spending unusual amounts of time sitting in its water bowl is often trying to drown mites before you've spotted a single one on its scales.
</FunFact>

## Mouth Rot (Infectious Stomatitis)

Cheesy or yellowish material around the gums, along with swelling. Always see a vet, this can become life-threatening if untreated.

## Shedding Problems (Dysecdysis)

Retained skin from humidity running too low. Manageable at home with a humid hide lined with damp sphagnum moss, covered in the tank setup guide.

## The Husbandry Pattern Worth Remembering

The vast majority of health problems in captive kingsnakes trace directly to incorrect temperatures, inadequate humidity, dirty conditions, or improper feeding, not genetic susceptibility. Getting the basics right, covered fully in the tank setup guide, prevents nearly everything on this list before it starts. This husbandry-first pattern holds for most hardy beginner colubrids, including the [corn snake](/blog/corn-snake-health-issues-guide/), which shares a nearly identical list of preventable issues.
