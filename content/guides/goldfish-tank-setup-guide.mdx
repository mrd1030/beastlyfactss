# Goldfish Tank Setup: Bigger Than You Think, Filtered More Than You'd Guess

<AffiliateDisclosure />

If you take away one thing from goldfish care, make it this: the bowl is wrong, and the tank you need is bigger than most people expect for what looks like a small, simple fish.

<Figure src="/assets/images/goldfish-tank-setup-guide-1.jpg" alt="Labeled diagram of a goldfish tank showing heavy filtration, open swimming space, and a water test kit" caption="Bigger than you think, filtered more than you'd guess, the bowl was always wrong for this fish." shareable />

## Tank Size

Plan for roughly a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4pjiyeT" product="10 gallon tank">20-gallon-plus tank</AffiliateLink> for a single fancy goldfish, with at least 10 additional gallons for each fish after that. Single-tail varieties, comets and commons, need considerably more room than fancies and are genuinely better suited to a pond than an indoor tank long term, a minimum of about 55 gallons for one, considerably more for each additional fish you keep. Long and wide beats tall, goldfish are horizontal swimmers. Keep the layout simple, with open swimming space down the middle. [Goldfish Tank Size and the Bowl Myth](/blog/goldfish-tank-size-bowl-myth/) takes the bowl argument to its end, with the ammonia math behind it.

## Filtration: Go Bigger Than Feels Necessary

Goldfish are unusually heavy waste producers for their size. Filtration should process your total tank volume at least 4 times an hour, and ideally 5 to 10 times. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eQAk5K" product="Canister filter (Fluval 107, up to 30 gal)">canister filter rated for smaller tanks</AffiliateLink>, or a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4vNGY2v" product="Canister filter (Fluval 407, up to 100 gal)">heavier-duty canister rated for larger setups</AffiliateLink>, or a hang-on-back unit with mechanical, biological, and chemical media all work, and running two filters is common practice among experienced keepers rather than overkill. When you clean filter media, rinse it in removed tank water only, never tap water, chlorine and temperature shock kill off the beneficial bacteria living in it.

<FunFact>
A single goldfish can produce a bio-load comparable to several tropical fish combined, which is exactly why experienced keepers often run two separate filters on a goldfish tank as standard practice, not as backup equipment for when something breaks.
</FunFact>

## Water

Goldfish are a coldwater species, comfortable in the 65 to 75°F range, no heater strictly required. Keep ammonia and nitrite at 0 and nitrate under roughly 20 ppm. Do weekly water changes of 25 to 50%, and always treat new water with a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3Rg5gTT" product="Water conditioner">dechlorinator</AffiliateLink> before it goes in the tank. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4p1T7yd" product="Water test kit">test kit</AffiliateLink> is the only reliable way to confirm those numbers rather than guess at them.

## Substrate: Bare, Sand, or Big Gravel

Goldfish forage by sucking sand and gravel into their mouths and spitting out what isn't food. Usually that works. Occasionally a piece is the wrong size and shape and lodges in the mouth, and a lodged stone can need sedation and forceps at a fish vet to remove without tearing the lining of the mouth. Swallowed gravel is worse: a goldfish cannot digest it, and it can block the gut.

That leaves three safe floors. A bare bottom removes the risk entirely and is the easiest to keep clean. Fine sand lets the fish sift the way it wants to without swallowing harmful amounts. Gravel works only when every piece is too big to fit in the mouth, at least 1/2 inch across. Small pea gravel, the default in many starter kits, is the size that causes trouble.

## Plants and Decor

Goldfish eat and uproot most soft aquarium plants, so a planted goldfish tank is built from the tough ones. Anubias, especially the larger *Anubias barteri*, has thick leaves and rhizomes that survive being bumped and chewed. Java fern and bolbitis fern are equally hardy. All three grow attached to rock or driftwood instead of rooted in the substrate, which is also what keeps a digging goldfish from pulling them out: tie or wedge them onto hardscape, and never bury the rhizome. Crinum calamistratum, a tough bulb plant that prefers cooler water, and marimo moss balls round out the list.

Keep decor smooth. Fancy goldfish have delicate fins and, in some varieties, prominent eyes, so sharp ornaments and rough rock are injury risks in a tank where the fish spend all day nosing along the bottom.

## Cycling

[Cycle the tank before adding any fish](/blog/aquarium-cycling-guide/). This applies to goldfish as much as any other species, an established beneficial bacteria colony is what actually processes the waste this species produces in such quantity. The fishless method doses pure ammonia to about 3 ppm and redoses as it drops, and the cycle is done when a full dose reads zero within 24 hours, nitrite reads zero, and nitrate has started to build, usually four to six weeks in.

## Diet

Goldfish are omnivores, and a balanced diet includes quality pellets as the base, vegetables, and occasional protein. [Avoid overfeeding](/blog/goldfish-feeding-guide/), it's one of the most common mistakes and a direct contributor to the waste load your filtration has to handle.

## The Bottom Line

None of this is complicated, but it does require accepting that a goldfish needs meaningfully more tank and filtration than its size or price tag suggests. Set it up right from the start and this is a genuinely low-maintenance, long-lived fish, some goldfish live well over a decade in a properly maintained tank.
