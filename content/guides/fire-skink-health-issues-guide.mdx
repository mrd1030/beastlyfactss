# Common Fire Skink Health Issues

<VetDisclaimer />

Fire skinks are hardy, and nearly every health issue in this species connects back to one thing: getting humidity and substrate moisture right for a fossorial animal that depends on both.

## Respiratory Infection

Open-mouth breathing, mucus, and wheezing. Caused by substrate or overall enclosure conditions running too dry, or occasionally too cold. **See a vet**, this needs professional treatment. Prevention is straightforward: hold 60 to 70% humidity consistently, with misting morning and evening, damp substrate, and a digital probe hygrometer to check the numbers. The enclosure should dry out a little between mistings, since stagnant, saturated air causes its own problems.

## Poor or Incomplete Shed (Dysecdysis)

From low humidity. Retained shed on the toes or tail can constrict circulation if left unaddressed. **Manageable at home** with corrected humidity and a humid hide, though retained eye caps or a severe case should prompt a vet visit rather than repeated home attempts.

<FunFact>
A fire skink's entire visible behavior tracks substrate depth and moisture almost exactly. Too shallow and dry, and it stays wedged under a hide out of sight; deep and appropriately damp, and the same animal builds a burrow system, forages through leaf litter, and comes out to bask, most of what looks like "temperament" in this species is actually a humidity and depth response.
</FunFact>

## Substrate Impaction

From ingesting dry, loose substrate accidentally while feeding. **See a vet if suspected.** Prevented by proper moist substrate. Scatter feeding in the leaf litter stays; a dish is only for an animal with a past impaction.

## Metabolic Bone Disease

Less common in this species than in some other lizards, but still possible if UVB and calcium supplementation are inadequate. **See a vet.**

## Tail Loss: First Aid for a Dropped Tail

Not a disease, but a genuine, preventable outcome of rough handling, a grab at the tail, or a scuffle with a cagemate. The stump needs little help to close, and the tail regrows, though the new one never matches the original. What a keeper does in the first days:

1. **Leave the stump alone.** No bandage. A dab of povidone-iodine (Betadine) or a triple antibiotic ointment on the stump is enough, and many dropped tails heal with no treatment at all.
2. **Swap the substrate.** A fire skink lives in damp soil, which is exactly what should not pack into an open wound. Move it to damp paper towels, with a hide and a humid hide, until the stump has sealed over.
3. **Feed it up.** Regrowing a tail takes resources, so offer more food than usual for the next 2 to 4 weeks.
4. **Separate it.** If the tail went in a fight or a nip from a cagemate, the two stay apart from now on.
5. **Watch the stump.** One that looks infected instead of drying and closing goes to a vet.

## Parasites and New Arrivals

Much of the fire skink trade is still wild-caught, and wild-caught animals arrive more stressed and more likely to carry parasites than captive-bred ones. A fecal exam belongs in the first vet visit. Until it comes back clean, a new skink stays apart from any other reptile in the house, on the setup and timeline in the [reptile quarantine guide](/blog/reptile-quarantine-guide/).

## The Husbandry Pattern Worth Remembering

Low humidity is directly responsible for the respiratory infection and poor shedding that make up most of this species' real health risk. Dry substrate causes impaction, and scatter feeding on damp litter is safe where a dish on dry substrate is not. And rough handling causes preventable tail loss. This is a species where getting the moisture and substrate depth right, more than any other single factor, determines how healthy and how visible your fire skink actually is.

Most of what shows up on this list starts with substrate that has been allowed to dry out. Stuck shed from low humidity is also the most common complaint in the [African fat-tailed gecko](/blog/african-fat-tail-health-issues-guide/), another West African ground dweller that needs a damp retreat.
