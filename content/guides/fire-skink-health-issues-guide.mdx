# Common Fire Skink Health Issues

<VetDisclaimer />

Fire skinks are hardy, and nearly every health issue in this species connects back to one thing: getting humidity and substrate moisture right for a fossorial animal that genuinely depends on both. See our [enclosure setup guide](/blog/fire-skink-tank-setup-guide/) for the husbandry side of prevention, and our full [Fire Skink care guide](/guides/fire-skink/) for everything else.

## Respiratory Infection

Open-mouth breathing, mucus, and wheezing. Caused by substrate or overall enclosure conditions running too dry, or occasionally too cold. **See a vet**, this needs professional treatment. Prevention is straightforward, maintain 60 to 70% substrate humidity consistently, covered in the [tank setup guide](/blog/fire-skink-tank-setup-guide/).

## Poor or Incomplete Shed (Dysecdysis)

From low humidity. Retained shed on the toes or tail can constrict circulation if left unaddressed. **Manageable at home** with corrected humidity and a genuinely humid hide, though retained eye caps or a severe case should prompt a vet visit rather than repeated home attempts.

<FunFact>
A fire skink's entire visible behavior tracks substrate depth and moisture almost exactly. Too shallow and dry, and it stays wedged under a hide out of sight; deep and appropriately damp, and the same animal builds a burrow system, forages through leaf litter, and comes out to bask, most of what looks like "temperament" in this species is actually a humidity and depth response.
</FunFact>

## Substrate Impaction

From ingesting dry, loose substrate accidentally while feeding. **See a vet if suspected.** Prevented by using proper moist substrate, covered in the [tank setup guide](/blog/fire-skink-tank-setup-guide/), and considering feeding from a dish to reduce accidental ingestion.

## Metabolic Bone Disease

Less common in this species than in some other lizards, but still possible if UVB and calcium supplementation are inadequate. **See a vet.**

## Tail Loss or Injury

Not a disease, but worth including since it's a genuine, preventable outcome of rough handling, covered further in the [handling guide](/blog/fire-skink-handling-guide/). A dropped tail heals but regrows imperfectly. Minor cases are manageable on their own, an open or infected wound needs a vet.

## The Husbandry Pattern Worth Remembering

Low humidity is directly responsible for the respiratory infection and poor shedding that make up most of this species' real health risk. Dry substrate causes impaction. And rough handling causes preventable tail loss. This is a species where getting the moisture and substrate depth right, more than any other single factor, determines how healthy and how visible your fire skink actually is.
