# Degu Enclosure Setup: Tall, Solid, and Genuinely Cool

<AffiliateDisclosure />

A degu enclosure needs to support an active, social, day-active pair, tall, solid-floored, and cool enough to keep this heat-sensitive species comfortable.

<Figure src="/assets/images/degu-tank-setup-guide-1.jpg" alt="Labeled diagram of a degu enclosure showing the tall multi-level cage, solid exercise wheel, and dust bath house" caption="A degu enclosure needs to support an active, social, day-active pair, tall, solid-floored, and cool enough to keep this heat-sensitive species comfortable." shareable />

## Enclosure Size

About 28 by 18 by 28 inches is the minimum for a pair, and larger is always better. A tall, <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B001NJ0DAY?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Critter Nation by MidWest Homes for Pets Double Unit Cage">multi-level cage</AffiliateLink> with ramps and platforms suits this species' active, climbing nature well. Use metal construction rather than plastic or wood, degus chew through both readily. Bar spacing should be no more than half an inch.

Two details most cage listings never mention. Fit a solid roof: degus are a prey species whose main wild predators are birds of prey, and movement above them frightens them. And give them branches of safe wood, pear, apple or beech, with platforms at different levels to climb or jump between.

Skip the fish tank and the glass vivarium. Degus housed in them can develop respiratory problems, because glass sides don't let enough air circulate in and out.

## Temperature

Genuinely cool by small-pet standards, 65 to 70°F (18 to 21°C). This species tolerates heat poorly, keep the cage away from windows, radiators, and any spot that gets direct sun, and never let ambient temperature climb toward 77°F.

<FunFact>
A degu's comfortable range tops out well below what most small pets tolerate. 77°F sounds like an ordinary warm room, and for a degu, it's the edge of a genuine heatstroke risk, worth remembering before placing a cage near a sunny window "just for the winter."
</FunFact>

## Humidity

No special humidity requirement. Good ventilation and low dust in the bedding matter more here than any specific humidity target.

## Substrate

Dust-free <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3TkxAVF" product="Aspen shavings substrate">wood shavings like aspen</AffiliateLink>, or a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4voH9Ap" product="Small-pet paper bedding">paper-based bedding</AffiliateLink>, combined with plenty of hay and a genuine deep-digging area. That means a solid enclosure bottom under a deep layer of material, hay, shredded paper and paper tissue among them, so a degu can dig and burrow rather than scratch at a tray. Avoid cedar entirely, and skip anything dusty or sugary. Use solid flooring rather than wire, this directly prevents bumblefoot.

## Lighting

A normal bright-day, dark-night cycle suits this diurnal species well. No UVB requirement.

## The Non-Obvious Requirements

**A solid exercise wheel, at least 12 inches (30 cm) across**, is important, wire or mesh wheels catch toes and tails and cause real injury. **A dust bath two to three times a week** keeps the coat in condition: a degu, like its chinchilla relatives, cleans itself in fine volcanic dust, which absorbs oil and dirt, and never in water. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4w2STJO" product="Chinchilla dust bath house">bath house</AffiliateLink> filled with <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4vgxhss" product="Chinchilla dust (Oxbow Poof)">chinchilla bathing dust</AffiliateLink> does the job, offered in sessions of about twenty minutes and then removed. A permanent tray becomes a toilet, overuse of dust baths causes health problems of its own, and the used dust is carrying information the degus are reading. And provide a proper nest box alongside constant gnawing material, replaced as it gets worked through, since degu incisors are open-rooted and this species chews structurally rather than idly: plastic shelves, hides and wheels do not last. Both matter more for a social pair that's genuinely active during your waking hours than they might for a more sedentary pet.

## Rounding Out the Enclosure

None of this needs to be elaborate, but a degu with nowhere to hide or dig is a chronically stressed one.
