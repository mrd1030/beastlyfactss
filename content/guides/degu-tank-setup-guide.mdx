# Degu Enclosure Setup: Tall, Solid, and Genuinely Cool

<AffiliateDisclosure />

A degu enclosure needs to support an active, social, day-active pair, tall, solid-floored, and cool enough to keep this heat-sensitive species comfortable.

<Figure src="/assets/images/degu-tank-setup-guide-1.jpg" alt="Labeled diagram of a degu enclosure showing the tall multi-level cage, solid exercise wheel, and sand bath house" caption="A degu enclosure needs to support an active, social, day-active pair, tall, solid-floored, and cool enough to keep this heat-sensitive species comfortable." shareable />

## Enclosure Size

At minimum, 24 by 18 by 24 inches for a pair, though larger is always better. A tall, <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B001NJ0DAY?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Critter Nation by MidWest Homes for Pets Double Unit Cage">multi-level cage</AffiliateLink> with ramps and platforms suits this species' active, climbing nature well. Use metal construction rather than plastic or wood, degus chew through both readily. Bar spacing should be an inch or less for adults, half an inch for young degus.

## Temperature

Genuinely cool by small-pet standards, 65 to 70°F (18 to 21°C). This species tolerates heat poorly, keep the cage away from windows, radiators, and any spot that gets direct sun, and never let ambient temperature climb toward 77°F.

<FunFact>
A degu's comfortable range tops out well below what most small pets tolerate. 77°F sounds like an ordinary warm room, and for a degu, it's the edge of a genuine heatstroke risk, worth remembering before placing a cage near a sunny window "just for the winter."
</FunFact>

## Humidity

No special humidity requirement. Good ventilation and low dust in the bedding matter more here than any specific humidity target.

## Substrate

Dust-free <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3TkxAVF" product="Aspen shavings substrate">wood shavings like aspen</AffiliateLink>, or a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4voH9Ap" product="Small-pet paper bedding">paper-based bedding</AffiliateLink>, combined with plenty of hay and a genuine deep-digging area. Avoid cedar entirely, and skip anything dusty or sugary. Use solid flooring rather than wire, this directly prevents bumblefoot.

## Lighting

A normal bright-day, dark-night cycle suits this diurnal species well. No UVB requirement.

## The Non-Obvious Requirements

**A solid exercise wheel, 11 to 12 inches**, is important, wire or mesh wheels catch toes and tails and cause real injury. **A sand or dust bath two to three times a week** supports healthy coat condition, this isn't optional grooming, it's a real physiological need for this species. Bathing sand is generally the better-suited fill for a degu specifically, finer chinchilla dust was formulated for chinchilla fur and is a reasonable substitute rather than the ideal. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4w2STJO" product="Chinchilla dust bath house">bath house</AffiliateLink> filled with <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B09PZ1772S?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Supreme Petfoods Science Selective Bathing Sand for Hamsters, Gerbils, Chinchillas & Degus">bathing sand</AffiliateLink> covers both. And provide a proper nest box alongside constant gnawing material, both matter more for a social pair that's genuinely active during your waking hours than they might for a more sedentary pet.

## Rounding Out the Enclosure

None of this needs to be elaborate, but a degu with nowhere to hide or dig is a chronically stressed one.
