# How Much Does a Fire Skink Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Fire skinks are genuinely affordable, and the price gap between captive-bred and wild-caught animals is worth paying attention to for reasons beyond just cost. For the full care picture beyond budgeting, see our complete [Fire Skink care guide](/guides/fire-skink/).

## The skink: $25 to $40, more for captive-bred

Common wild-caught or imported fire skinks run $25 to $40. There's no real color-morph market here, this species is prized for its natural red, black, and gold banding rather than selectively bred variants. Captive-bred individuals cost more but make genuinely better pets, healthier on arrival and more receptive to taming, worth the premium if you can find one.

## Upfront setup: a moderate reptile cost

A 36x18x18 inch enclosure, <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3TiqeC4" product="UVB - Arcadia Forest 6% (ProT5 kit, 36in/39W)">UVB kit</AffiliateLink>, heat lamp and thermostat, deep moisture-retentive substrate, hides, and a hygrometer make up the setup, genuinely more affordable than the larger lizards on this site and comparable to other mid-size terrestrial reptile setups. An <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4fibBY2" product="Automatic misting system/fogger">automatic misting system</AffiliateLink> is a worthwhile addition given how central moisture is to this species, covered in the [enclosure setup guide](/blog/fire-skink-tank-setup-guide/).

## Ongoing costs: modest

Live insects, crickets, roaches, and worms among the common choices, plus a calcium and D3 supplement and periodic substrate replacement, keep this a low-maintenance species to feed month to month.

## Vet costs

Fire skinks are hardy with relatively few reported health issues. An annual exotic vet checkup is still worth doing, and parasite screening is genuinely important for wild-caught animals specifically, given how common wild-caught stock is in this species' trade.

## Lifespan

15 to 20 years, a genuinely long commitment for a lizard this small and this affordable to acquire. The low purchase price shouldn't distract from the fact that you're taking on a two-decade relationship, on par with the [blue-tongued skink](/blog/blue-tongue-skink-cost-guide/) already covered on this site despite the two being genuinely different pets in every other respect.

## The real cost consideration: captive-bred vs. wild-caught

Given how much of this species' trade is still wild-caught, the more meaningful cost decision isn't really about morphs or price tiers, it's about sourcing. A wild-caught animal costs about the same as a captive-bred one but tends to arrive more stressed, more parasite-prone, and slower to settle, covered in more detail in the [handling guide](/blog/fire-skink-handling-guide/). Paying a bit more for verified captive-bred stock is genuinely the better value here.
