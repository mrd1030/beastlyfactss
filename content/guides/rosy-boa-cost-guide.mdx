# How Much Does a Rosy Boa Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

A rosy boa is one of the most affordable snakes to buy and set up on this entire site, a genuine relief if you've been pricing out the site's other boa, the boa constrictor, which can grow six to ten times longer. Everything about this snake's budget follows from its small size.

For the full care picture beyond cost, see our complete [Rosy Boa care guide](/guides/rosy-boa/).

## The Snake: $150 to $400, Depending on Locality and Morph

A normal, standard-colored rosy boa typically runs $150 to $200. Checking current listings from established reptile retailers, adults commonly sell for right around $189 to $199 for standard coloration, with an albino running about $199 at the same retailer. Specific wild localities, like Yorba Linda or Baja stock, and color mutations push the price up from there, commonly into the $200 to $400 range, occasionally more for rare or exceptional combinations. That's a real range, but it's a much narrower one than a boa constrictor's designer morphs, which can run into the thousands.

That lands at roughly $150 to $250 total, well under half of what a boa constrictor's setup runs. Our [Rosy Boa tank setup guide](/blog/rosy-boa-tank-setup-guide/) covers exactly why each of these items looks different from what a larger boa needs.

## Ongoing Costs: Low

Small frozen-thawed mice, fed roughly every 10 to 14 days for an adult, are the main recurring expense, and they're inexpensive even by feeder-rodent standards. Substrate replacement is infrequent since the enclosure stays dry rather than needing the regular refresh a humid setup demands, and a single small under-tank heater draws far less power than the heat sources a larger snake needs. Our [Rosy Boa feeding guide](/blog/rosy-boa-feeding-guide/) covers prey sizing and exactly why this species eats even less often than most pet snakes its size.

An adult rosy boa eats every 10 to 14 days, so two or three mice a month, and a bag of 100 small mice from RodentPro is $74, or $0.74 each. That is about $2 a month for food. Aspen is $9.98 for 8 quarts. ReptiFiles calls for a full change every three to six months and about 1.25 to 1.5 cubic feet of material in a 36x18x18, so a 20 to 30 gallon enclosure takes roughly two bags a change, or $3 to $7 a month. A Zoo Med heat mat draws 8 watts in the 10 to 20 gallon size and 16 watts in the 30 to 40 gallon size, and around the clock that is 5.8 to 11.5 kWh a month, $1 to $2 at the June 2026 US residential average of 18.34 cents per kWh. Consumables land at $5 to $8 a month. The vet set-aside from the section below, $100 to $200 a year, is bigger than food, substrate, and power combined.

## Vet Costs

Exotic vet pricing varies by clinic and region, but a currently published mobile exotic veterinary price list lists an established-client wellness exam at $78 and a new-client exam, which includes an ultraviolet light check for parasites and skin issues, at $128. An illness exam at the same clinic runs $118. Budgeting $100 to $200 a year for routine care, with more set aside if something needs treating, is a reasonable planning figure. Our [Rosy Boa health issues guide](/blog/rosy-boa-health-issues-guide/) covers the conditions most likely to trigger one of those visits, most of which trace back to humidity running too high rather than too low.

<FunFact>
A rosy boa can live just as long as a boa constrictor, 20 to 30 years, with documented individuals reaching past 30, while spending its entire life in an enclosure a fraction of the size. The lifespan commitment doesn't shrink with the snake, only the space and setup cost do.
</FunFact>

## Lifespan: A Long Commitment in a Small Package

20 to 30 years is typical in captivity, and this is genuinely one of the more long-lived snakes kept as pets relative to its size. That multi-decade window is worth weighing seriously even though the day-to-day cost is low, a rosy boa acquired today is realistically a commitment into the 2040s or 2050s.

## The Cost Picture

A rosy boa is about as affordable as pet snakes get, cheap to buy, cheap to house, and cheap to feed, all because it simply never grows very large.
