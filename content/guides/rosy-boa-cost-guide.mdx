# How Much Does a Rosy Boa Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

A rosy boa is one of the most affordable snakes to buy and set up. Everything about this snake's budget follows from its small size.

## The Snake: $150 to $400, Depending on Locality and Morph

A normal, standard-colored rosy boa typically runs $150 to $200. Checking current listings from established reptile retailers, adults commonly sell for right around $189 to $199 for standard coloration, with an albino running about $199 at the same retailer. Specific wild localities, like Yorba Linda or Baja stock, and color mutations push the price up from there, commonly into the $200 to $400 range, occasionally more for rare or exceptional combinations. That's a real range, but it's a much narrower one than a boa constrictor's designer morphs, which can run into the thousands.

That lands at roughly $150 to $250 total.

## Ongoing Costs: Low

Small frozen-thawed mice, fed roughly every 10 to 14 days for an adult, are the main recurring expense, and they're inexpensive even by feeder-rodent standards. Substrate replacement is infrequent since the enclosure stays dry rather than needing the regular refresh a humid setup demands, and a single small under-tank heater draws far less power than the heat sources a larger snake needs.

Prices last checked September 2026 at Backwater Reptiles, CountryMax, ReptMart and RodentPro. Retail prices move; treat the table as a snapshot, not a quote.

An adult rosy boa [eats every 10 to 14 days](/blog/rosy-boa-feeding-guide/), so two or three mice a month, and a bag of 100 small mice from RodentPro is $74, or $0.74 each. That is about $2 a month for food. Aspen is $9.98 for 8 quarts. A full change runs every three to six months and takes about 1.25 to 1.5 cubic feet of material in a 36x18x18, so a 20 to 30 gallon enclosure takes roughly two bags a change, or $3 to $7 a month. A Zoo Med heat mat draws 8 watts in the 10 to 20 gallon size and 16 watts in the 30 to 40 gallon size, and around the clock that is 5.8 to 11.5 kWh a month, $1 to $2 at the June 2026 US residential average of 18.34 cents per kWh. Consumables land at $6 to $11 a month. The vet set-aside from the section below, $100 to $200 a year, is bigger than food, substrate, and power combined.

## Vet Costs

Exotic vet pricing varies by clinic and region, but a currently published mobile exotic veterinary price list lists an established-client wellness exam at $78 and a new-client exam, which includes an ultraviolet light check for parasites and skin issues, at $128. An illness exam at the same clinic runs $118. Budgeting $100 to $200 a year for routine care, with more set aside if something needs treating, is a reasonable planning figure.

<FunFact>
A rosy boa lives 18 to 22 years on average and past 30 for documented individuals, while spending its entire life in an enclosure a fraction of the size. The lifespan commitment doesn't shrink with the snake, only the space and setup cost do.
</FunFact>

## Lifespan: A Long Commitment in a Small Package

18 to 22 years is the captive average and past 30 is documented, which makes this one of the more long-lived snakes kept as pets relative to its size. That multi-decade window is worth weighing seriously even though the day-to-day cost is low, a rosy boa acquired today is realistically a commitment into the 2040s or 2050s.

## The Cost Picture

A rosy boa is about as affordable as pet snakes get, cheap to buy, cheap to house, and cheap to feed, all because it simply never grows very large. The [boa constrictor](/blog/boa-constrictor-cost-guide/) is the other boa kept as a beginner-ish pet, and the size difference between the two drives the whole cost gap.
