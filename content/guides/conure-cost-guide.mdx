# How Much Does a Green Cheek Conure Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Green cheek conures sit at a moderate price point for a parrot, but color mutations and the wrong sales channel can both push that number up considerably.

## The Bird Itself: %%animal:conure%%+, More at Pet Stores

Normal (green) conures typically run $250 to $500 from breeders for hand raised, weaned babies, with common listings around $275 to $400. Common morphs, Pineapple, Yellow-Sided, Cinnamon, and Turquoise, often run $350 to $600, and high-red or combination morphs can reach $450 to $700 or more.

Pet store or big-box pricing frequently lands at $600 to $1,300, noticeably higher than buying direct from a breeder, and many experienced owners consider it inflated for what you get. Adoption or rescue is often lower or donation based, though availability varies and a bird's history may be unknown.

Color mutations are selectively bred and genuinely command a premium over normals, especially rarer or multi-mutation birds. Hand raised, DNA sexed, well socialized individuals also cost more than parent raised or unweaned birds.

<FunFact>
A "Pineapple" green cheek conure isn't a separate species or a marketing label, it's a real genetic combination of the Yellow-Sided and Cinnamon mutations, producing a mostly yellow-olive body with an orange face instead of the wild type green body and red belly patch. Every color mutation in the pet trade traces back to selective breeding of the same species.
</FunFact>

Quality pellets plus fresh vegetables and fruit run about $160 to $260 a year. Toy and enrichment replacement adds another $10 to $30 a month, conures destroy toys quickly, and regular rotation is a real part of keeping this bird mentally healthy, not an optional extra. What that rotation should actually consist of, and the priority order to spend it in, is set out in our [conure enrichment guide](/blog/conure-enrichment-guide/). Electricity is minimal unless you're running UV lighting or air filtration continuously.

## Vet Costs: Finding One May Be the Bigger Challenge

Avian and exotic vets are specialized and not available everywhere. A routine wellness exam commonly runs %%vet:conure%%, with diagnostics like fecal tests, bloodwork, or a Gram stain adding $50 to $200 or more. An emergency or urgent visit often starts at $150 to $300 for the exam alone, and treatment, hospitalization, or advanced testing can reach several hundred to over $1,000 depending on severity.

Many owners report finding a qualified avian vet is a bigger challenge than the cost itself in some regions, worth researching before you actually need one.

## Lifespan: The Biggest Cost Driver

20 to 25 years or more is the commonly cited range, with some individuals reaching 30 years under excellent care. The average pet lives nearer 10 years, and the shortfall is put down to poor diet and neglect, so plan around the long figure: it is what a well-kept bird reaches. This long commitment, more than any single line item, is what makes a green cheek conure a genuinely significant financial and time investment, not a casual purchase.
