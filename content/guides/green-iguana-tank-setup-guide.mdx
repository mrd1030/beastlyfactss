# Green Iguana Enclosure Setup: Plan for a Room, Not a Tank

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Green iguana husbandry needs to be planned around the adult this species becomes, not the baby you're bringing home. Building toward that end state from the start is genuinely the better approach than upgrading repeatedly. For the full care picture, see our complete [Green Iguana care guide](/guides/green-iguana/), and for what all of it costs, our [cost guide](/blog/green-iguana-cost-guide/).

## Enclosure Size

A single adult needs a minimum of 10 feet long by 5 feet wide by 6 feet tall, some vet and husbandry sources recommend 12 by 6 by 6. No commercial enclosure is large enough, this has to be a custom, walk-in build. A juvenile can start temporarily in something like a 4x2x4 foot enclosure or a 40 to 55 gallon tank, but outgrows it within the first year, plan for the move into adult housing rather than being surprised by it. Our [Savannah Monitor tank setup guide](/blog/savannah-monitor-tank-setup-guide/) covers a similarly large-format enclosure for a comparably demanding species.

## Temperature

Basking area 100 to 120°F, cool end around 80°F, nighttime not dropping below the low 70s°F. Heat via a cluster of <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DJRLWX1H?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Arcadia GoldenSun Basking Halogen Lamp 100W">halogen bulbs</AffiliateLink>, roughly six bulbs to adequately cover an adult's basking branch, positioned over a sturdy climbing perch. A heat source is genuinely required, not optional.

## Humidity

60 to 80%, reflecting this species' tropical origin. Achieve this through misting twice daily with a pressure sprayer or an <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4fibBY2" product="Automatic misting system/fogger">automated misting system</AffiliateLink>, plus a large soaking tub the iguana can fully access. For an enclosure this size housing an animal for 15 to 20 years, a higher-end multi-nozzle system like the <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B086V3Q5BP?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="MistKing Starter Misting System, 5th Gen">MistKing Starter System</AffiliateLink> covers the space far more evenly than a single fogger. Chronically low humidity is directly linked to the dehydration and kidney disease covered in the [health issues guide](/blog/green-iguana-health-issues-guide/).

## Substrate

2 to 4 inches of coconut husk or large-particle cypress mulch, sized specifically to reduce impaction risk. Avoid sand, gravel, corncob bedding, kitty litter, and wood shavings entirely. Some keepers prefer plain newspaper or butcher paper for juveniles, genuinely fine and easy to keep clean.

## Lighting

High-output <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/43AcIvA" product='Arcadia T5 UVB Reptile Light Fixture Kit (36" / 14% UVB Dragon)'>T5 HO UVB</AffiliateLink> is required for survival in this species, not a nice-to-have, replaced every 12 months regardless of whether the bulb still produces visible light. Glass blocks UVB entirely, so a sunny window is never an adequate substitute.

<FunFact>
An iguana's basking surface target of 100 to 120°F is far hotter than most reptiles on this site ever need, and it's heating a large-bodied animal evenly rather than warming a single spot, which is exactly why the setup calls for a cluster of bulbs rather than one.
</FunFact>

## The Non-Obvious Requirement: This Is an Arboreal Species

Roughly two-thirds of an iguana's total length is tail, and this is fundamentally a climbing animal that needs genuine vertical space and sturdy branches positioned near the basking zone, not just floor area. A wide, short enclosure undersells this species just as much as a small one does.
