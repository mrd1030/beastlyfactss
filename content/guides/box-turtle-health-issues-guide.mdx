# Common Box Turtle Health Issues

<VetDisclaimer />

Box turtles are genuinely good at hiding illness, which makes knowing the warning signs, and understanding how a few of these conditions connect to each other, especially worthwhile with this species.

## Metabolic Bone Disease (MBD)

A misshapen shell, deformed legs, and slow or stunted growth. Caused by a calcium and phosphorus imbalance, inadequate UVB, or poor overall diet. Always see a vet, and treat correct UVB and calcium as essential from the very start, our [enclosure setup guide](/blog/box-turtle-tank-setup-guide/) covers what that lighting setup actually needs to look like.

## Vitamin A Deficiency

Swollen, puffy eyes, sometimes sealed shut entirely, along with eye or nasal discharge and occasionally ear abscesses. Caused by a poor diet, too much iceberg lettuce, an all-meat diet, or low-quality commercial food, none of which provide adequate vitamin A. See a vet, and here's a connection worth knowing: this deficiency often sets the stage for the respiratory infection covered next, treating one without addressing the other doesn't fully solve the problem.

<FunFact>
Vitamin A deficiency and respiratory infection in box turtles are so closely linked that vets often treat them as a package deal. Fixing the diet problem without also treating the infection, or the other way around, tends to leave the turtle only partly better.
</FunFact>

## Respiratory Infection

Nasal discharge, mucus bubbles, wheezing, open-mouth breathing, and lethargy. Frequently secondary to the vitamin A deficiency above. Caused by an enclosure that's too cold, too dry, or poorly ventilated. Always see a vet, this will not resolve without antibiotic treatment. Our [cost guide](/blog/box-turtle-cost-guide/) covers what that vet visit and the standard antibiotic treatment typically run.

## Shell Rot and Shell Infections

Discolored patches on the shell with a foul odor. Caused by unsanitary, overly damp conditions or physical trauma. See a vet.

## Internal Parasites

Often found incidentally on a routine fecal exam even without obvious symptoms, heavier loads cause diarrhea and weight loss. A vet is required for diagnosis and treatment.

## Why Annual Checkups Matter More Than They Might Seem To

Because box turtles hide illness so effectively, an annual wellness exam with a reptile-experienced vet is genuinely worth doing even when your turtle seems completely healthy, catching a developing problem early, especially the vitamin A and respiratory infection combination, makes a real difference in outcome.

## The Husbandry Pattern Worth Remembering

Weak UVB and calcium cause MBD. Poor diet causes vitamin A deficiency, which frequently opens the door to respiratory infection. And unsanitary, overly damp conditions cause shell rot.
