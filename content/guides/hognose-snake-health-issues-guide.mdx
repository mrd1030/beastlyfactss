# Common Western Hognose Snake Health Issues

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Western hognoses are hardy snakes, and most of what shows up on this list traces back to substrate, humidity, or feeding habits, all fully within an owner's control. ## Respiratory Infection

One of the most common issues in this species. Watch for open-mouth breathing, wheezing, drooling or visible mucus, and lethargy. ## Impaction

Hognoses are enthusiastic, somewhat messy eaters, and they're prone to swallowing substrate along with their food. Watch for a failure to pass waste, bloating, and lethargy. Feeding in a separate container away from the loose substrate, using a set of <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CBKD64LP?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Lasnten Extra Long Stainless Steel Reptile Feeding Tongs with Rubber Tip">feeding tongs</AffiliateLink> to keep prey scent off your hands in the process, is the single most effective prevention, worth doing as standard practice rather than only after a problem shows up. Mild cases sometimes resolve on their own; anything that doesn't pass within a reasonable window needs a vet, and severe cases can require surgery.

## Obesity and Fatty Liver

A quieter but genuinely serious risk in captivity, where snakes get less activity than they would in the wild and are sometimes fed too much or too often for their enclosure size. This is a real contributor to shortened lifespans in this species. It's manageable at home by matching feeding frequency and portion size to your snake's actual activity level and adult size, rather than defaulting to a schedule that suited it as a fast-growing juvenile.

## Scale Rot (Blister Disease)

Caused by damp, dirty substrate, discolored or blistered belly scales are the sign to watch for. Prevention is straightforward: keep the <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3TkxAVF" product="Aspen shavings substrate">substrate dry and clean</AffiliateLink>, which lines up directly with what this species needs anyway. See a vet if it progresses beyond mild discoloration.

## Mites and Internal Parasites

Both are treatable. Periodic fecal checks, especially for a newly acquired snake, catch parasites before they become a bigger issue.

<FunFact>
Two of the most common problems on this list, respiratory infection and scale rot, both trace back to the exact same root cause: humidity or dampness running too high. That's really the throughline of hognose care. This species wants dry, not damp, and getting that one variable right prevents most of what shows up on a vet's exam table.
</FunFact>

## The Husbandry Pattern Worth Remembering

Humidity or dampness that runs too high causes both respiratory infection and scale rot, two of the more common problems on this list. Feeding on loose substrate causes impaction, easily avoided by feeding in a separate container. And overfeeding relative to activity level contributes to obesity over time. Get the dry setup and feeding habits right, and this is a genuinely low-maintenance snake to keep healthy.
