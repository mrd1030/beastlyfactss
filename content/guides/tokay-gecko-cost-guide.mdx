# How Much Does a Tokay Gecko Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Tokay geckos have a wide price range that reflects a real quality divide in this species: wild-caught animals are cheap and come with real downsides, while captive-bred animals cost more and are the meaningfully better choice.

## The Gecko: $15 to $400+

Wild-caught or unspecified "normal" tokays run as low as $15 to $50, and these are genuinely not recommended given the parasite load and poor temperament typical of wild-caught animals. Captive-bred normals run $100 to $150. Captive-bred color morphs, patternless, granite, blue-headed green, and rarer options like leucistic or albino, run $150 to $400 or more.

Altogether, this species runs roughly $300 to $500 before the gecko itself.

## Ongoing Costs: Roughly $15 to $30 a Month

Crickets or roaches, supplements, and regular misting make up the monthly total.

<FunFact>
A well-cared-for captive tokay gecko can live 20 years or more, well beyond the 10 to 15 years typical in captivity, which is a lot of years for a single upfront setup to be worth getting right the first time.
</FunFact>

## Vet Costs

A routine exam runs $50 to $100. Wild-caught animals frequently need a fecal test plus antiparasitic treatment on top of that. Emergencies, prolapse or respiratory infection among them, can reach $200 or more. See our [health issues guide](/blog/tokay-gecko-health-issues-guide/) for what these actually look like.

## Lifespan

10 to 15 years is typical in captivity, with well-documented cases reaching 20 years or more under excellent care.

## The Real Cost Decision: Captive-Bred vs. Wild-Caught

A cheaper wild-caught tokay isn't really the budget option once you factor in likely parasite treatment and a considerably more defensive, harder-to-work-with animal, something covered in more detail in our [handling guide](/blog/tokay-gecko-handling-guide/). Paying more for a captive-bred gecko is genuinely the better value for nearly every keeper.
