# How Much Does a Milk Snake Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Milk snakes span a wide price range depending on subspecies and morph, but even the higher end stays affordable compared to many other pet reptiles.

## The Snake: $70 to $500

Common Pueblan milk snakes run $70 to $100, though [a handful of states settle the question before price does](/blog/milk-snake-legal-guide/). Sinaloan and Honduran subspecies, both larger and more striking, run $150 to $200. Premium morphs, albino, tangerine, and snow among them, run $150 to $500. Across the board, this is a species where even the rare end of the market stays well within reach for most keepers.

## Choosing the Snake Behind the Price

Milk snakes are still collected from the wild, so the first question to a seller is whether the animal was captive bred. Wild-caught specimens are, with fussy hatchlings, the milk snakes most likely to refuse food. The second question is its feeding record. A hatchling that has already taken several frozen and thawed pinkies in a row is worth more than a slightly cheaper one that has not eaten yet.

At pickup, look for clear eyes, skin with no ulcers or swellings, a clean vent, a mouth with no discharge, and a snake that flicks its tongue and holds its color. A seller who will not show the snake before payment, or who ships a hatchling that has never eaten, is the one to walk away from. Even a healthy-looking snake then spends its first months in [quarantine, with a reptile vet's exam and a fecal screen](/blog/reptile-quarantine-guide/), away from any other reptile.

## Ongoing Costs: Roughly $15 to $30 a Month

Frozen and thawed rodents, along with occasional substrate replacement and modest electricity, make up nearly the entire monthly cost, genuinely low for a snake this size.

## Vet Costs

A routine reptile exam runs $50 to $100, with a fecal test adding $25 to $50. Emergency visits start around $150 to $300 for the exam alone, before any treatment.

<FunFact>
The milk snake's red, black, and yellow banding isn't just decorative, it's widely explained as Batesian mimicry, a resemblance to the venomous eastern coral snake close enough to deter predators, even though milk snakes are completely harmless. Researchers have even documented real regional variation in how closely the mimicry matches its model across the snakes' range, variation that measurably affects how well the disguise works in the wild.
</FunFact>

## The Long Game

With good care, milk snakes commonly live 20 years or more. This is a substantial commitment for an animal that stays affordable and low-maintenance to keep on a monthly basis, the investment here is time, not money.
