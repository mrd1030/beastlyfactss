# How Much Does a Milk Snake Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Milk snakes span a wide price range depending on subspecies and morph, but even the higher end stays affordable compared to many other pet reptiles.

## The Snake: $70 to $500

Common Pueblan milk snakes run $70 to $100, though [a handful of states settle the question before price does](/blog/milk-snake-legal-guide/). Sinaloan and Honduran subspecies, both larger and more striking, run $150 to $200. Premium morphs, albino, tangerine, and snow among them, run $150 to $500.

## Choosing the Snake Behind the Price

Milk snakes are still collected from the wild, so the first question to a seller is whether the animal was captive bred. Wild-caught snakes and unproven hatchlings are the ones most likely to refuse food. The second question is its feeding record. A hatchling that has already taken several frozen and thawed pinkies in a row is worth more than a slightly cheaper one that has not eaten yet.

At pickup, look for clear eyes, skin with no ulcers or swellings, a clean vent, a mouth with no discharge, and a snake that flicks its tongue and holds its color. A seller who will not show the snake before payment, or who ships a hatchling that has never eaten, is the one to walk away from. Even a healthy-looking snake then spends its first 3 to 6 months in [quarantine, with a reptile vet's exam and a fecal screen](/blog/reptile-quarantine-guide/), away from any other reptile.

## Ongoing Costs: Roughly $15 to $30 a Month

Frozen and thawed rodents, along with occasional substrate replacement and modest electricity, make up nearly the entire monthly cost, which is low for a snake this size.

## Vet Costs

A routine reptile exam runs $50 to $100, with a fecal test adding $25 to $50. Emergency visits start around $150 to $300 for the exam alone, before any treatment.

## Buy the Vivarium Once

The vivarium is the one large bill in keeping a milk snake, so it pays to buy it at adult size from the start and never buy it twice. After that, the snake costs little more than its mice and its electricity, and with good care a milk snake commonly lives 20 years or more. What is left to decide is whether those years are free to give.
