# Corydoras Catfish Aren't Handled, and They're Not Solo Fish Either

Like every fish covered on this site, corydoras catfish are an observation animal, not one you handle. What genuinely matters with this species is understanding its role in a tank, and it's a very different role from the [oscar](/blog/oscar-fish-handling-guide/) already covered here. For the full care picture, see our full [Corydoras Catfish care guide](/guides/corydoras-catfish/).

## A Supporting Cast Member, Not a Centerpiece

The [oscar](/blog/oscar-fish-handling-guide/) is a large, intelligent, assertive centerpiece fish that dominates a tank and needs to be planned around. Corydoras catfish are the opposite: small, peaceful, bottom-dwelling fish that genuinely belong in a community setup as a supporting presence rather than the main attraction. They're social, active foragers, and known for a synchronized group "dance" that's genuinely enjoyable to watch once you have a proper school.

## One Detail Worth Correcting: They're Not Algae Eaters

Despite a common "cleanup crew" reputation, corydoras catfish don't eat algae. What they actually do is forage leftover food off the substrate, which is useful but different, and they need their own dedicated sinking food, they won't survive on scraps and algae alone the way the reputation might suggest. Our [Corydoras Catfish feeding guide](/blog/corydoras-catfish-feeding-guide/) covers this myth in full along with real portions and timing, and our [cost guide](/blog/corydoras-catfish-cost-guide/) covers what that dedicated food actually costs to keep stocked.

## The Schooling Requirement, Genuinely Non-Negotiable

Corydoras must be kept in groups of at least six of the same species. A lone or paired cory shows real stress: hiding, erratic darting, and appetite loss are all common signs of an inadequate group size. This isn't a minor behavioral preference, it's close to a core welfare requirement, similar to the [neon tetra's schooling needs](/blog/neon-tetra-cost-guide/) already covered on this site, though the two species occupy completely different zones of the tank, cories at the bottom, tetras in open water.

<FunFact>
Corydoras dart to the surface periodically to gulp air, a genuine adaptation that supplements their gill breathing. This is completely normal in moderation and not a sign of oxygen problems in the water, worth knowing so you don't misread it as distress the first time you see it.
</FunFact>

## A Normal Behavior That Looks Alarming the First Time

That surface-gulping habit is worth calling out on its own, since it's the single most common thing that alarms new cory keepers. It's a normal, healthy behavior, not a sign that something is wrong with your water.

## First-Timer Mistakes

- Buying too few, three or four rather than a proper school of six or more.
- Choosing sharp substrate, which damages this species' sensitive barbels, covered in our [Corydoras Catfish health issues guide](/blog/corydoras-catfish-health-issues-guide/).
- Housing with aggressive or fin-nipping tankmates, or fish large enough to see a small cory as food.
