# Veiled Chameleon Tank Setup: The Dripper Isn't Optional

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Veiled chameleon husbandry has a reputation for being demanding, and it earns that reputation honestly. Get the enclosure type, misting, and lighting right, and this is a genuinely rewarding species to keep. Skip any one of them, and problems follow fast. This covers the full setup, for the full care picture see our complete [Veiled Chameleon care guide](/guides/veiled-chameleon/).

<Figure src="/assets/images/veiled-chameleon-tank-setup-guide-1.jpg" alt="Labeled diagram of a veiled chameleon tank showing UVB lighting, a dripper system, live plants, and a permanent egg-laying bin" caption="Veiled chameleons never recognize standing water as something to drink, a dripper or misting system is essential, not optional." shareable />

## Enclosure Size and Type

2 feet by 2 feet by 4 feet (24x24x48 inches) is the practical minimum for an adult, bigger, 4x2x4 feet, is better if you have the space. Juveniles can start smaller, around 18x18x36 inches, but veiled chameleons reach adult size in just 6 to 8 months, so plan and budget for the adult enclosure from the start rather than treating it as a future upgrade. A <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B001PHABI8?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Zoo Med Reptibreeze Open Air Screen Cage 24x24x48">screen enclosure</AffiliateLink> is the traditional choice and provides excellent airflow. Hybrid enclosures, with partially solid sides, hold humidity better and are increasingly preferred. Glass terrariums can work but need serious attention to ventilation, most aren't large enough for an adult anyway.

## Temperature

Basking spot around 85°F for females and juveniles, up to 90 to 95°F for adult males. Ambient temperature 72 to 80°F, with a beneficial night drop to 55 to 65°F. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4bqzQAS" product="Basking bulb (standard, 75W)">basking heat bulb</AffiliateLink> is required, this species needs a genuine thermal gradient to regulate itself properly.

## Humidity and the Dripper: The Non-Negotiable Part

Moderate humidity, raised through misting sessions morning and evening, plus a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4fibBY2" product="Automatic misting system/fogger">dripper or an overnight cool-mist humidifier</AffiliateLink> using distilled water. <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4grZYPq" product="Live terrarium plants">Live plants</AffiliateLink>, pothos, hibiscus, ficus, and parlor palm are all good choices, help maintain humidity naturally and give your chameleon cover.

Here's the detail that actually determines success with this species: veiled chameleons do not recognize standing water as something to drink. They only respond to moving droplets on leaves and branches. A water dish alone will not keep your chameleon hydrated, no matter how often you refill it. A dripper or misting system is a genuine essential, not an accessory, and skipping it leads directly to the dehydration and kidney problems covered in our [health issues guide](/blog/veiled-chameleon-health-issues-guide/). Our dedicated [chameleon hydration guide](/blog/chameleon-hydration-drippers-misters-fogging/) breaks down the differences between drippers, misting systems, and foggers in more depth if you're deciding between them. Drainage matters too, daily misting produces real runoff, and the enclosure needs somewhere for that water to go.

<FunFact>
Veiled chameleons are drought-adapted enough that they can also drink water condensing on the casque, the helmet-like structure on top of the head. It channels droplets down toward the mouth as an extra natural adaptation, on top of drinking directly from misted leaves and branches.
</FunFact>

## Substrate

Bare bottom or paper towel is the safest, easiest option. Coco fiber or a soil-based mix works for planted, bioactive setups. Avoid sand, gravel, and wood chips, all carry a real impaction risk if ingested during a feeding strike.

## Lighting

A linear T5 HO UVB tube (<AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3TiqeC4" product="UVB - Arcadia Forest 6% (ProT5 kit, 36in/39W)">ReptiSun 5.0 or Arcadia 6% are the commonly recommended options</AffiliateLink>) spanning the enclosure, with the basking branch positioned roughly 6 to 9 inches below it. Replace the bulb every 6 to 12 months regardless of whether it still visibly lights up. Skip compact or coil-style UVB bulbs, they're not considered adequate for this species.

## The Other Non-Obvious Requirement: A Permanent Egg-Laying Bin for Females

Even a female with no male present will produce eggs, and without somewhere appropriate to lay them, she's at real risk of the egg-binding covered in our [health issues guide](/blog/veiled-chameleon-health-issues-guide/). Keep a moist sand or soil bin, roughly 5 to 10 inches deep, permanently available, not something you set up only once she shows signs of needing it.
