# Cardinal Tetras Aren't Handled Either, But the School Size Rule Is Even Stricter

Like most fish, cardinal tetras are an observation pet, not a hands-on one. The real welfare consideration with this species isn't handling technique at all, it's group size, and getting that number right changes everything about how these fish actually behave. For the full care picture, see our complete [Cardinal tetra care guide](/guides/cardinal-tetra/).

## The Number That Matters, and It's a Bit Higher Than You'd Guess

Cardinal tetras are obligate schooling fish, meaning they don't just tolerate being in a group, they need one to feel secure and behave normally. [Fishlore](https://www.fishlore.com/profiles_cardinal_tetra.htm) names six as the workable minimum, but [Seriously Fish](https://www.seriouslyfish.com/species/paracheirodon-axelrodi/) recommends starting with a group of 8 to 10 or more for genuinely natural schooling behavior, a somewhat firmer bar than the six-fish floor usually cited for [neon tetra](/blog/neon-tetra-handling-guide/). A group that's too small produces fish that are pale, stressed, constantly hiding, and more vulnerable to illness. This isn't a minor behavioral preference, it's close to a core welfare requirement for the species.

## What a Properly Schooled Group Actually Looks Like

Confident, brightly colored fish swimming together in a loose group, exploring the mid-water areas of the tank actively rather than hiding in corners. It's genuinely one of the more rewarding things to watch in a community aquarium, and it only happens reliably once the school size is adequate. Our [Cardinal tetra tank setup guide](/blog/cardinal-tetra-tank-setup-guide/) covers how tank size and shape play into giving a school that room.

## First-Timer Mistakes

- Buying just a handful of cardinal tetras, thinking that's "enough," this is the single most common mistake with this species, and the recommended minimum here runs a little higher than it does for some other tetras.
- Adding them to a tank that hasn't finished cycling. A 2008 water-quality study on this exact species, published in [Acta Amazonica](https://acta.inpa.gov.br/fasciculos/38-4/BODY/v38n4a23.html), found cardinal tetra is notably sensitive to nitrite specifically, which is exactly what an incompletely cycled tank produces.
- Mixing them with fin-nipping tankmates, which disrupts normal schooling behavior even in an otherwise adequate group.

That second mistake in particular is a big part of what drives the disease risk covered in our [Cardinal tetra health issues guide](/blog/cardinal-tetra-health-issues-guide/).

## How to Actually Tell Cardinal Tetra and Neon Tetra Apart

The two fish get confused constantly, and the reliable tell is the red stripe. On a cardinal tetra it runs the full length of the body, from the gills all the way to the tail. On a [neon tetra](/blog/neon-tetra-handling-guide/), the red only covers roughly the back half, with the front half showing just the blue stripe. Cardinals also run slightly larger and tolerate noticeably warmer water, useful to know if you're stocking a warmer community tank where straight neon tetra would struggle.

<FunFact>
That vivid red-and-blue stripe isn't just decorative. [Practical Fishkeeping](https://www.practicalfishkeeping.co.uk/features/cardinal-tetra-paracheirodon-axelrodi/) explains that in the dim, tannin-stained water this species evolved in, the metallic stripe actually functions as a kind of camouflage, reflecting available light in a way that can create false or confusing targets for predators. What looks like the showiest feature in a home aquarium is doing real defensive work back in a blackwater stream.
</FunFact>
