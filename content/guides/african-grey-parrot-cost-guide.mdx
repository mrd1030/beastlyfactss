# How Much Does an African Grey Parrot Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

An African grey parrot is an expensive bird to buy, and the purchase price is just the entry fee for a decades-long commitment.

One thing worth understanding before you buy: this species carries the strictest CITES protection level there is, and that shapes what documentation a legitimately captive-bred bird should come with.

## The Bird: %%animal:african-grey-parrot%%

Price varies by subspecies, the larger Congo grey versus the smaller Timneh, along with age, whether the bird is hand-raised, and the individual breeder. Most African greys fall in the $1,500 to $3,500 range. If you're importing a bird, permit fees add another $111 to $565 on top of the purchase price.

## The Paperwork a Captive-Bred Grey Should Come With

A grey bred and sold inside the US does not come with a CITES permit, and it does not need one. The Appendix I listing governs birds crossing international borders, and CITES imposes no controls on moving a bird between US states. A seller who offers a CITES certificate as the proof for a domestic bird is offering the wrong document.

What a captive-bred grey should come with is a **closed leg band**: a solid steel or aluminum ring the breeder slips over the foot within the first week after hatching, while the foot is still small enough to pass through. It cannot come off later without being cut, which is what makes it evidence. An open or pinned band stamped with three letters and three numbers, or with "USDA", is an import quarantine band. Legal import of wild-caught parrots ended with the Wild Bird Conservation Act of 1992, so a bird wearing one entered the country before 1993 and is not the young bird a listing may claim.

Leave the band on unless it is injuring the bird. It is often a grey's only identification, and proof of captive birth may be asked for if the bird moves, travels or is sold. A microchip in the breast muscle, registered to the owner, is the backup.

Taking a grey out of the country, or bringing one back, is where CITES paperwork starts: permits from the US Fish and Wildlife Service and the other country, with applications averaging 60 days and no way to expedite them. Budget for that time and the fees before booking a move abroad.

## Vet Costs

An annual avian wellness exam runs %%vet:african-grey-parrot%%, and baseline bloodwork plus testing for conditions like PBFD belongs in the budget on top of that. Emergencies, hypocalcemic seizures or aspergillosis among them, can run several hundred to over a thousand dollars.

## Lifespan: The Number That Changes Everything

Research tracking captive lifespans puts the mean at 45 years, with some individuals reaching 60, compared to an average of about 23 years in the wild. Some exceptional individuals live 70 to 80 years. A grey acquired as a young adult can genuinely outlive its owner, which is worth sitting with before committing to this species.
