# Rosy Boa Enclosure Setup: Small, Dry, and Simple by Design

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Rosy boa housing is refreshingly modest. A small, dry, secure enclosure covers everything this desert species needs.

## Enclosure Size: Modest

A rosy boa's small adult size, typically 24 to 36 inches and only rarely approaching 4 feet, means it never needs the multi-foot enclosure a larger boa requires. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eSbKl2" product="20 gallon long tank">20 gallon long tank</AffiliateLink> is the floor for a standard adult, and the 36x18x18 inch setup is the better target, since [corn snakes](/blog/corn-snake-enrichment-guide/) given enclosures longer than their bodies stretched out and used the full length. This is a ground-dwelling, secretive species that spends much of its time tucked into rock crevices, so snug hides matter as much here as the floor space around them. House rosy boas individually, never together.

## Temperature

A basking surface temperature of 90°F, with a cool side around 65 to 75°F, is the standard target. Heating should generally be turned off at night, and temperatures can safely fall into the 60s without issue. A brief winter cooldown toward the mid-50s is fine too, and actually mirrors this species' natural seasonal slowdown in the wild rather than working against it. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/44hAxJ1" product="Under-tank heat mat + thermostat kit">thermostat-controlled under-tank heater</AffiliateLink> is the appropriately gentle heat source for a snake this size, there's no need for the ceramic heat emitter a larger boa requires.

Run the lights 10 to 12 hours a day on a timer, with full darkness at night. The room's own daylight can stand in for a lamp if the enclosure gets it, but a timer keeps the cycle steady. Rosy boas are mostly nocturnal in summer, active at dusk at times, and out by day in late winter and early spring, which is why a low-output tube in the 2 to 5% range is worth adding: [UVB is now advised at a low level](/blog/uvb-lighting-complete-guide/) even for night-active species, and snakes sit in the two lowest zones.

## Humidity: The Reverse of Most Boas

This is the detail that matters most, and it runs opposite to what almost every other pet snake wants. Keep ambient humidity well under 60 percent, with a baseline closer to 30 to 40 percent for most of the year, roughly half of what a [boa constrictor](/blog/boa-constrictor-tank-setup-guide/) needs as a much larger, tropical species. Rosy boas are sensitive to persistent dampness, and chronically high humidity is the leading cause of respiratory infection and scale rot.

That said, don't overcorrect into bone-dry. This species still needs consistent access to water and tolerates brief humidity spikes, a splash from the water dish, an occasional damp patch, as long as the enclosure is allowed to dry back out afterward. During the shed cycle specifically, raise humidity to roughly 40 to 50 percent using a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4bpnvNm" product="Sphagnum moss (for moist hide)">damp sphagnum moss hide</AffiliateLink>, then let conditions return to the dry baseline once the shed completes. A real <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3QHrM81" product="Digital thermometer/hygrometer combo">digital hygrometer</AffiliateLink> is worth having, this is a species where guessing in either direction causes a real problem.

<FunFact>
In the wild, rosy boas spend much of their time tucked into rock crevices and granite outcroppings rather than moving around in the open desert. That's exactly why snug hides carry so much weight in this species' enclosure, and why a flat rock over a burrow does more for it than any branch.
</FunFact>

## Substrate

Dry aspen shavings are the standard choice and hold up well at the low humidity this species needs. A sand and soil mix works too, and suits a snake that burrows.

Shavings work at 1 to 2 inches, which is enough to hide under and easy to spot-clean. For burrowing, go to 3 to 4 inches, the better target for an adult. The fill for that works out to:

## Furnishings: Minimal But Real

Provide two <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B092HDK2S4?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Zoomed Medium Habba Hut Reptile Hide">snug hides</AffiliateLink>, one on the warm side and one on the cool side, sized so your rosy boa's body touches the sides once inside. Add a small, shallow water dish, this species doesn't need the large soaking dish a bigger boa does. A flat piece of rock or cork bark gives it something to rub against during shedding and reflects its natural rocky habitat, but keep the overall setup simple, this isn't a species that needs heavy clutter to feel secure.

## The Non-Obvious Requirement: A Secure, Smooth Lid

Rosy boas are persistent, patient escape artists that will test any gap in an enclosure over time. A tight-fitting, securely fastened screen lid is essential on a glass or open-top enclosure. Smoothness matters too and is easy to overlook: a rough or coarse screen can cause rostral abrasion, real snout irritation and injury from a snake repeatedly pushing against it.
