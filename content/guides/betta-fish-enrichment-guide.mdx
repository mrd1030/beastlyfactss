# Betta Enrichment: Three Studies, and a Mirror Is Not a Toy

<AffiliateDisclosure />

The betta is the most-studied fish in the freshwater hobby, and the results are inconvenient for how the species is usually sold.

<KeyTakeaway>
Restricted space increased hiding and inactivity. Planted tanks produced the longest active swimming times and fewer negative behaviors. And the mirror, marketed as exercise, is what researchers use to provoke a stress response so they can measure cortisol afterward.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Three Studies Found

Three separate studies matter here. One, published in *Animal Welfare*, found that **space and environmental enrichment both affect behaviour** in *Betta splendens*, with restricted space raising signs of stress including hiding and inactivity. A second examined **tank size and furnishings** and their effect on behavior and welfare. A third looked at planting specifically and found fish in aquaria planted with *Sagittaria subulata* displayed the **longest average active swimming time and fewer negative behaviors**.

On aggression, the picture is more interesting than the hobby usually allows. Research indicates bettas of either sex can be group-housed with lower aggression **if they have been housed in groups since hatching under enriched conditions**, and a separate study found the timing of isolation from an enriched environment determines later aggressive behavior and sexual maturity.

<FunFact>
Aggression in this species is partly a rearing outcome rather than a fixed trait. That does not mean you can put two adult males together, it means the aggression a betta shows you was shaped before you bought it.
</FunFact>

## Volume First

Everything else in this guide sits inside a decision you make before buying a single plant. Larger tanks promoted natural, active behavior in the space work, and the restricted conditions produced the hiding and inactivity pattern that gets misread as a calm fish. ## Planting Increases Activity

The counterintuitive part. Adding dense cover to a betta tank does not produce a fish you never see, it produces one that swims more. A fish with somewhere to retreat to is more willing to be in open water, because being exposed is only a problem when there is no alternative.

If you use artificial plants instead, choose soft ones: stiff plastic tears betta fins, which is a real and common injury. ## Resting Places and Broken Sightlines

Bettas are labyrinth fish and surface breathers, so they benefit from resting spots near the top as well as cover below. ## Feeding, Varied

Move the feeding point around rather than dropping food in the same corner every day. Vary the food itself. This is the cheapest thing on the list and the one most consistent with the general fish enrichment literature, where structural and feeding variation both show effects.

## Priority Order

1. Tank volume, decided before anything goes in it
2. Dense planting and cover
3. Surface resting spots and broken sightlines
4. Varied feeding location and food type
5. Tannins and softened light
6. Mirrors, which belong at the bottom or off the list entirely

## What Not to Do

Do not use a mirror as a daily enrichment routine, since what you are provoking is a threat display and researchers use it to generate a measurable stress response. Do not use stiff plastic plants, which damage fins. Do not read a still, hidden betta in a bare tank as a relaxed one, because that is the pattern the restricted-space study produced. Do not combine adult males on the strength of the group-housing research, which is about fish raised together from hatching. And do not treat a heater and filter as optional extras, since no amount of enrichment compensates for unstable water.
