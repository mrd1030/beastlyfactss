# Oscar Enrichment: The Fish That Rearranges the Tank Itself

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Most fish enrichment is about arranging an environment for an animal that will accept it. Oscars have opinions.

<KeyTakeaway>
Oscars dig, uproot, reposition decor, learn feeding routines and respond differently to the person who feeds them. That gives you more to work with than almost any other aquarium fish, and it starts with a tank big enough for a foot-long cichlid, which is not the tank they are sold into.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Research Says

Nothing on oscars. The relevant group size work, done on [neon tetra](/blog/neon-tetra-enrichment-guide/), found effects were **species-specific** and found **no group size effect for angelfish**, the [cichlid in that study](/blog/angelfish-enrichment-guide/).

The honest position is that oscar enrichment is argued from behavior rather than from controlled studies. What is not in dispute is the behavioral repertoire: an oscar interacts with its environment, its routine and its keeper in ways a tetra does not, and an environment that offers none of that leaves a large intelligent animal with a glass box.

<FunFact>
The recognition behavior is the part keepers find hardest to overstate and hardest to prove. Oscars very clearly behave differently toward the person who feeds them than toward a stranger at the glass, which is the sort of everyday observation that formal research on aquarium fish rarely gets around to testing.
</FunFact>

## Space, First and Loudest

Everything else is downstream. Oscars reach a foot or more and get sold at two inches to people buying a tank for the two inch version.

Around 75 gallons is a floor for a single adult, and a pair or any tank mates needs considerably more. There is no enrichment that compensates for an adult oscar in a 40 gallon.

## Filtration to Match

Oscars are among the messiest fish in the hobby, and a large messy fish in a big tank needs filtration rated well above the volume.

## Things They Are Allowed to Move

The enrichment that is specific to this fish. Oscars dig substrate, shift decor and uproot plants deliberately, and an aquascape they cannot alter takes that away.

Give them **movable furniture**: smooth stones, sizeable <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eR0Z2l" product="Driftwood">driftwood</AffiliateLink> pieces, and a deep sand or fine gravel bed to push around. Accept that the layout will not stay where you put it, because the rearranging is the point.

Anchor anything that could fall on the fish or crack the glass. An oscar can shift more than people expect.

## Training and Routine

Oscars learn readily. Target training with a stick, hand feeding, and coming to a particular corner at a particular time all work, and they give a large intelligent animal a predictable task.

Vary feeding times and locations sometimes, so the routine is a structure and not a rut. This is the same logic as the anticipation findings in [red-footed tortoises](/blog/red-footed-tortoise-enrichment-guide/): an animal that has learned your schedule benefits from that schedule having some give in it.

## Feeding, Without Feeder Fish

Varied diet: quality large cichlid pellets as a base, plus frozen and prepared foods, occasional whole items that take work to eat.

Skip the feeder fish. They are a common route for parasites and disease into a tank and are nutritionally poor, and the hunting appeal is not worth what comes with them.

Feeding an oscar something it has to work at, rather than pellets that vanish in seconds, is the cheapest enrichment available here.

## Tank Mates, Cautiously

Large, robust species in a very large tank, and even then it is individual. Anything small enough to swallow eventually will be, and oscars vary enormously in temperament.

A species tank with one or two oscars is the simplest arrangement and the one least likely to go wrong.

## Priority Order

1. A tank sized for a foot-long adult
2. Filtration rated well beyond the volume, plus real water changes
3. Movable substrate, stones and wood they can rearrange
4. Target training and hand feeding
5. Varied diet with items that take work, and no feeder fish
6. A varied feeding schedule, never a fixed one
7. Tank mates chosen carefully, or none

## What Not to Do

Do not buy a tank for the two inch fish. Do not under-filter. Do not build an aquascape you need to stay put. Do not feed feeder fish. Do not keep them with anything they can swallow. Do not let water quality drift, given [the link to hole-in-the-head](/blog/oscar-fish-health-issues-guide/). And take real care moving a large cichlid, since they are strong and they do bite.
