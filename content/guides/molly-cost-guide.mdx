# How Much Does Molly Fish Ownership Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Mollies are inexpensive fish that ask for a moderately-sized setup, a step up from a guppy's basic 10-gallon starting point, but still an affordable species overall.

## The Fish: %%animal:molly%%, More for Specialty Strains

Common short-fin varieties, black, silver, and dalmatian mollies, run about $2 to $8 each, and a six-pack lands nearer $4 a fish. Balloon and lyretail mollies run a bit higher, about $5 to $10 each, or about $6 a fish in a six-pack. Rarer specialty color strains can reach $15 to $20 or more from specialty sellers.

<FunFact>
A $5 black molly and a $20 neon lyretail molly are the same basic care requirements underneath the color and fin differences. The price gap is almost entirely about selective breeding for looks, sailfin varieties grow larger and need more room, but day-to-day care doesn't change much across the price range.
</FunFact>

## Upfront Setup: %%setup:molly%%

<CostTable guide="molly" section="necessities" />

Mollies need more room than a guppy: 20 gallons is the real minimum, not the 10-gallon starting point that works for a small [guppy](/blog/guppy-cost-guide/) group, and 30 gallons is the cautious choice for sailfin varieties. That bigger water volume also means a bigger bioload, so the filter needs real turnover capacity rather than the gentle sponge filter that suffices for guppies, and the [aquarium filtration guide](/blog/aquarium-filtration-guide/) works through the turnover math and how to size a filter to a bioload. A basic setup, tank, filter, heater, and starter supplies, commonly runs %%setup:molly%% depending on the brands you choose. A sponge filter is the alternative if you'd rather prioritize a breeding-friendly tank over raw filtration capacity.

Whether aquarium salt belongs in a healthy molly tank at all is disputed, and it is a one-off line on this budget either way. A 65-ounce container of aquarium salt runs about $12 and treats up to 434 gallons, so it is effectively a multi-year supply for one tank rather than a recurring cost. Whether you need it is a more nuanced question than the price tag.

Food, water conditioner, occasional filter media replacement, and electricity for the heater and filter make up the bulk of the monthly cost. Mollies need a more plant-heavy diet than many fish, not just protein, so a flake or pellet with real plant content is the line worth paying for, and skimping on the plant side is a common molly-specific mistake. Taken together, a 20-gallon setup's full monthly upkeep commonly lands around $20 to $30.

## Vet Costs

Most molly health problems, things like ich, fin rot, and the species' well-known "shimmy," start with stress and poor water quality, and the first moves are water changes and a test kit. Over-the-counter products that contain formalin or malachite green are the usual parasite treatments, so the medicine shelf is the line to budget for. As with most aquarium fish, mollies rarely see a vet in any traditional sense: these issues get managed at home with water quality correction and over-the-counter medication rather than a clinic visit. Dedicated aquatic vets exist but are uncommon and rarely necessary.

## Lifespan

About 2 to 5 years. Wild sailfin mollies probably live 2 to 3 years, and wild males may live less than a year after reaching maturity, so a pet molly that reaches 5 years has had steady water and a good diet. Mollies get there on a bigger, somewhat pricier setup than a guppy.

## The Cost to Plan For: More Mollies Than You Started With

This is the detail that catches new owners off guard, and it's the same one that trips up new guppy owners. Mollies are livebearers, females give birth to live, free-swimming fry rather than laying eggs, and they breed readily in a mixed-sex tank. A female can drop a brood of roughly 20 to 35 fry in lab conditions, with ranges reaching 100 or more, and the gap between broods averages about 5 weeks, from 4 to 9 weeks in studies. She doesn't need a fresh mating for every litter, though broods from stored sperm tend to run smaller. Broods range from 10 to over 100 fry depending on the female's age.

Put males and females in the same tank with no plan, and you'll have meaningfully more mollies within a couple of months than you started with, which means more tank space, more filtration, and more of every ongoing cost listed above.
