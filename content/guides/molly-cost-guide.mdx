# How Much Does Molly Fish Ownership Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Mollies are inexpensive fish that ask for a moderately-sized setup, a step up from a guppy's basic 10-gallon starting point, but still a genuinely affordable species overall. For the full care picture beyond budgeting, see our complete [Molly care guide](/guides/molly/), or our companion guides on [feeding](/blog/molly-feeding-guide/), [breeding and handling](/blog/molly-handling-guide/), [health issues](/blog/molly-health-issues-guide/), and [tank setup](/blog/molly-tank-setup-guide/).

## The Fish: $2 to $20, More for Specialty Strains

Common short-fin varieties, black, silver, and dalmatian mollies, run about $2 to $8 each. [LiveAquaria sells black mollies in packs of six for $24.99](https://www.liveaquaria.com/products/black-molly), which works out to roughly $4 per fish, right in line with typical retail pricing. Balloon and lyretail mollies run a bit higher: [Splashy Fish Store lists balloon and lyretail color varieties at $6.99 each](https://splashyfishstore.com/collections/molly-fish), and [LiveAquaria's balloon molly six-pack runs $35.99](https://www.liveaquaria.com/products/balloon-molly), about $6 per fish. Rarer specialty color strains can reach $15 to $20 or more from specialty sellers.

<FunFact>
A $5 black molly and a $20 neon lyretail molly are the same basic care requirements underneath the color and fin differences. The price gap is almost entirely about selective breeding for looks, sailfin varieties and fancier fin shapes tend to need a bit more swimming room, but day-to-day care doesn't change much across the price range.
</FunFact>

Mollies genuinely need more room than a guppy: [Chewy puts the real minimum at a 20-gallon tank](https://www.chewy.com/education/fish/general/molly-fish-care-sheet), and [Aquarium Co-Op agrees, recommending at least 20 gallons](https://www.aquariumcoop.com/blogs/aquarium/molly-fish-care), rather than the 10-gallon starting point that works for a small guppy group, with sailfin varieties needing 30 gallons or more. That bigger water volume also means a bigger bioload, so the filter needs real turnover capacity rather than the gentle sponge filter that suffices for guppies. A basic setup, tank, filter, heater, and starter supplies, commonly runs $130 to $260 depending on the brands you choose; our [Molly tank setup guide](/blog/molly-tank-setup-guide/) covers exactly what each piece needs to do, including the sponge-filter option if you'd rather prioritize a breeding-friendly tank over raw filtration capacity.

Aquarium salt is a genuine, if optional, part of standard molly care since the species naturally tolerates brackish water. [A 65-ounce container of API Aquarium Salt runs about $21.72 at Walmart and treats up to 434 gallons](https://www.walmart.com/ip/API-Aquarium-Salt-Freshwater-Aquarium-Salt-65-oz/25270297), so a single container is effectively a multi-year supply for one tank rather than a recurring cost. Whether you actually need it is a more nuanced question than the price tag, covered in full in our [tank setup](/blog/molly-tank-setup-guide/) and [health issues](/blog/molly-health-issues-guide/) guides.

Food, water conditioner, occasional filter media replacement, and electricity for the heater and filter make up the bulk of the monthly cost. Taken together, a 20-gallon setup's full monthly upkeep commonly lands around $20 to $30. Mollies need real vegetable content in their diet, not just protein, which our [Molly feeding guide](/blog/molly-feeding-guide/) covers in detail, since skimping on that side of the diet is one of the most common molly-specific mistakes.

## Vet Costs

As with most aquarium fish, mollies rarely see a vet in any traditional sense. Most of the health issues covered in our [Molly health issues guide](/blog/molly-health-issues-guide/), things like ich, fin rot, and the species' well-known "shimmy," get managed at home with water quality correction and over-the-counter medication rather than a clinic visit. Dedicated aquatic vets exist but are uncommon and rarely necessary for a species this hardy.

## Lifespan

3 to 5 years on average, up to 5 to 7 years with excellent, stable water quality. That's a similar range to guppies, our [Guppy cost guide](/blog/guppy-cost-guide/) covers the closest comparison on this site, though mollies get there on a bigger, somewhat pricier setup. It's still nowhere near the multi-decade commitment of a species like the sulcata tortoise; our [Sulcata tortoise cost guide](/blog/sulcata-tortoise-cost-guide/) covers what a pet that can outlive its owner actually costs over a lifetime.

## The Real Cost to Plan For: More Mollies Than You Started With

This is the detail that catches new owners off guard, and it's the same one that trips up new guppy owners. Mollies are livebearers, females give birth to live, free-swimming fry rather than laying eggs, and they breed readily in a mixed-sex tank. A single female can produce roughly 20 to 60 fry per brood, with a documented range as wide as 10 to over 100 depending on the female's age and the source, about every 4 to 6 weeks, and she doesn't need a fresh mating for every litter.

Put males and females in the same tank with no plan, and you'll have meaningfully more mollies within a couple of months than you started with, which means more tank space, more filtration, and more of every ongoing cost listed above. Our [Molly handling guide](/blog/molly-handling-guide/) covers the practical side of managing that, from sexing your fish to deciding whether you want a breeding population at all.
