# Argentine Tegu Enclosure Setup

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Argentine tegu husbandry operates at a different scale than most pet lizards, this is a big bodied, high energy animal, and the setup needs to reflect that from the start rather than growing into it gradually.

<Figure src="/assets/images/argentine-tegu-tank-setup-guide-1.jpg" alt="Labeled diagram of an Argentine tegu enclosure showing the powerful heat source, strong UVB, automatic misting, and a brumation burrow" caption="Argentine tegu husbandry operates at a different scale than most pet lizards." shareable />

## Enclosure Size

8 feet by 4 feet by 4 feet is the minimum for an adult male, with females needing somewhat less but still substantial space. LafeberVet's floor is 6 by 3 by 3 feet; this page holds the larger figure because a tegu uses every inch of a 4-foot depth to burrow and turn, and the extra footprint is cheap next to the animal. Many experienced keepers aim for 10 feet by 5 feet by 5 feet or, where possible, a dedicated reptile room. Hatchlings can start in something smaller, around 40 gallons, but grow fast enough that rehousing within the first year is standard, not exceptional. Front opening with a mesh top for ventilation.

True adult sized housing for this species is typically a DIY build or ordered direct from a manufacturer.

## Temperature

Basking area 100 to 110F, which sits inside both LafeberVet's 95 to 100F and the hobby's 100 to 115F, warm ambient in the 90s, cool side around 80F, with nighttime allowed to drop to 65F without triggering brumation. This species needs a powerful heat source, a cluster of basking bulbs in ceramic-topped fixtures, a radiant heat panel, or both, run through a real <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0BX418XDF?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="MakerHawk TC816 Digital Temperature Controller Thermostat Timer, 1800W 120V 15A">heavy duty thermostat</AffiliateLink> rated for the wattage a setup this size actually draws. Sizing one to the load, and where the probe goes, is worked through in the [reptile heating and thermostats guide](/blog/reptile-heating-thermostats-guide/).

## Humidity

70 to 80%, maintained through deep, moisture retentive substrate, live plants in a bioactive style setup, a large water tub, and often an <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4fibBY2" product="Automatic misting system/fogger">automatic misting system</AffiliateLink> given how large this enclosure is, manually misting an 8 foot enclosure consistently isn't realistic for most keepers.

## Substrate

Deep, 12 to 18 inches or more, of an absorbent, burrow supporting mix: cypress mulch, coconut fiber, organic topsoil, or a bioactive blend. A <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CY23K32S?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="VCEPJH Loose Coconut Fiber Substrate, 2 Pack Natural Coco Coir Reptile Bedding Tarantula Substrate Soil Habitat Accessories for Tortoise Snake Bearded Dragon Spiders Hermit Crabs">coconut fiber substrate</AffiliateLink> works well as part of that mix, though reaching 12 to 18 inches of depth across an 8 by 4 foot floor takes considerably more than a pack or two. Tegus are dedicated diggers and burrowers, and this depth isn't excessive for the species, it's what they actually use. Avoid dry, dusty substrate that doesn't hold a burrow shape.

Keeping that much substrate clean is a routine, not a single job. Spot clean feces, urates and uneaten food as they appear, and pull any patch that has gone soggy, since wet bedding is the usual start of scale rot in this species. Keep the mix damp enough to hold a burrow without letting it sit waterlogged. Replace the whole bed at least every three months, which on an 8 by 4 foot floor at 12 to 18 inches deep is a large volume to buy and haul, so budget for it. The water tub, the decor and the disinfectant with its contact time run on the same [reptile enclosure cleaning schedule](/blog/reptile-salmonella-hygiene-guide/) as any other lizard, only at a tegu's scale.

## Lighting

Strong UVB is required. For an enclosure this large, a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0C7MTKC5S?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="LUCKY HERP T5 Reptile Light Fixture Combo Kit 54W, UVB Reptile Light Fixture with Desert UVB 10.0 46&quot; Fluorescent Tube">46 inch T5 HO bulb</AffiliateLink> in the 12 to 14% range, spanning a quarter to half the enclosure's length on the warm side, targeting a basking area UVI of 3.0 to 4.0. Add a 6500K LED for brightness and to support live plants. Run a 12 to 14 hour photoperiod.

## Diet Basics

Tegus are omnivores and generalists, and what a tegu eats shifts with its age in two directions at once: away from insects toward whole prey, and toward more plant matter.

Young tegus are primarily insectivorous. Gut-loaded crickets, phoenix worms, dubia roaches and other cockroaches, superworms, mealworms and waxworms all work, and interest in feeder insects drops off as the animal grows. From there the protein moves to appropriately sized pre-killed or frozen-thawed rodents. Live rodents are not worth the injury risk to the tegu. Lean ground meat, low-fat ground turkey specifically, fish and raw eggs are occasional treats rather than staples. Offer a variety of vegetables alongside smaller amounts of fruit, strawberries, melon, tomato and banana among them, and expect fruit to matter more as the animal ages.

Frequency is set by age rather than by size. Feed a young tegu every day. From one to three years old, every other day. Past three years, every third day. Adult tegus are prone to obesity, so weigh yours regularly and give it the room and the time to move, which is the other half of the same problem.

A calcium supplement without D3 goes on or in every meal, and a multivitamin once a week. That does not change with age.

Never feed from your hand. A hungry tegu is a voracious feeder and will not distinguish a finger from the food, so use a bowl, tongs, a puzzle feeder, or a separate paper-lined feeding container, which has the added effect of teaching the animal that its usual enclosure is not where food appears.

## The Non-Obvious Requirement: Plan for Brumation

Argentine tegus naturally slow down, eat less, and burrow for 2 to 4 months during cooler seasons, even as pets kept indoors. Build your setup with this in mind, deep substrate that supports extended burrowing, rather than treating it as an unusual behavior to prevent. This is normal for the species, and fighting against it isn't necessary or particularly effective.
