## Enclosure Size

8 feet by 4 feet by 4 feet is the minimum for an adult male, with females needing somewhat less but still substantial space. Many experienced keepers aim for 10 feet by 5 feet by 5 feet or, where possible, a dedicated reptile room. Hatchlings can start in something smaller, around 40 gallons, but grow fast enough that rehousing within the first year is standard, not exceptional. Front opening with a mesh top for ventilation.

True adult sized housing for this species is typically a DIY build or ordered direct from a manufacturer.

## Temperature

Basking area 100 to 115F, warm ambient in the 90s, cool side around 80F, with nighttime allowed to drop to 65F without triggering the brumation covered in our [handling guide](/blog/argentine-tegu-handling-guide/). This species genuinely needs a powerful heat source, a cluster of basking bulbs, a radiant heat panel, or both, run through a real <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0BX418XDF?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="MakerHawk TC816 Digital Temperature Controller Thermostat Timer, 1800W 120V 15A">heavy duty thermostat</AffiliateLink> rated for the wattage a setup this size actually draws.

## Humidity

70 to 80%, maintained through deep, moisture retentive substrate, live plants in a bioactive style setup, a large water tub, and often an <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4fibBY2" product="Automatic misting system/fogger">automatic misting system</AffiliateLink> given how large this enclosure is, manually misting an 8 foot enclosure consistently isn't realistic for most keepers.

Manually misting an enclosure this large, 8 feet by 4 feet by 4 feet, isn't realistic for most keepers on a consistent basis, which is exactly why an automatic misting system tends to be worth the cost for this species specifically, rather than an optional extra.

## Substrate

Deep, genuinely deep, 12 to 18 inches or more, of an absorbent, burrow supporting mix: cypress mulch, coconut fiber, organic topsoil, or a bioactive blend. A <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CY23K32S?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="VCEPJH Loose Coconut Fiber Substrate, 2 Pack Natural Coco Coir Reptile Bedding Tarantula Substrate Soil Habitat Accessories for Tortoise Snake Bearded Dragon Spiders Hermit Crabs">coconut fiber substrate</AffiliateLink> works well as part of that mix, though reaching 12 to 18 inches of depth across an 8 by 4 foot floor takes considerably more than a pack or two. Tegus are dedicated diggers and burrowers, and this depth isn't excessive for the species, it's what they actually use. Avoid dry, dusty substrate that doesn't hold a burrow shape.

## Lighting

Strong UVB is required. For an enclosure this large, a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0C7MTKC5S?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="LUCKY HERP T5 Reptile Light Fixture Combo Kit 54W, UVB Reptile Light Fixture with Desert UVB 10.0 46&quot; Fluorescent Tube">46 inch T5 HO bulb</AffiliateLink> in the 12 to 14% range, spanning a quarter to half the enclosure's length on the warm side, targeting a basking area UVI of 3.0 to 4.0. Add a 6500K LED for brightness and to support live plants. Run a 12 to 14 hour photoperiod.

## The Non-Obvious Requirement: Plan for Brumation

Argentine tegus naturally slow down, eat less, and burrow for 2 to 4 months during cooler seasons, even as pets kept indoors. Build your setup with this in mind, deep substrate that genuinely supports extended burrowing, rather than treating it as an unusual behavior to prevent. This is normal for the species, and fighting against it isn't necessary or particularly effective.
