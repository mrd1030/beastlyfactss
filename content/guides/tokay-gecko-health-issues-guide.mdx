# Common Tokay Gecko Health Issues and What Causes Them

<VetDisclaimer />
<AffiliateDisclosure />

Tokay geckos are generally hardy, and most issues trace back to temperature, humidity, or, for wild-caught animals specifically, parasites picked up before the gecko ever reached you.

## Metabolic Bone Disease (MBD)

Watch for a soft or rubbery jaw, tremors, difficulty gripping or climbing, and visible deformities, which tend to be worse in juveniles. Caused by low calcium or vitamin D3 and insufficient UVB. Always see a vet.

What a keeper notices first is usually appetite and movement, not bone shape. The Merck Veterinary Manual lists poor appetite, weakness, and an inability to walk normally ahead of the swollen jaw and leg bones, with fractures, muscle spasms, and cloacal prolapse later. Fix the UVB and calcium the same day, then get a vet to stage it.

## Respiratory Infection

Watch for wheezing, open-mouth gaping, mucus around the nostrils or mouth, and lethargy. Caused by temperatures running too low, humidity mismanagement, or both together. Always see a vet, this needs antibiotics.

The early version is subtle: a gecko that sits with its mouth slightly open, or a bubble at a nostril that comes and goes. PetMD's tokay care sheet puts the enclosure at 90 to 105F in the basking zone, 80 to 85F by day, and no lower than 75F at night, with humidity at 60 to 80%. A tokay kept cooler or damper than that, or in stagnant air, is the one that gets this. Merck's list of causes also includes unsanitary conditions, malnutrition, and vitamin A deficiency, which is why a clean enclosure and a supplemented diet are part of the treatment.

## Retained Shed (Dysecdysis)

Skin that doesn't come off cleanly can constrict around the toes or tail, cutting off circulation. Caused by humidity running too low for this genuinely humid-loving species. Manageable at home with a humidity boost, achieved with a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4vPKMjX" product="Fine mist spray bottle">fine mist spray bottle</AffiliateLink>, and, if needed, gently assisting the shed once it's soaked and loosened.

The first place to look is the toes and the tail tip, where a ring of old skin tightens as the new skin grows under it. A humid hide and a heavier evening mist handle a fresh case at home. A band that will not soak loose, or a toe that has gone dark or swollen past it, is a vet visit, because the constriction cuts circulation. Merck also lists skin parasites and nutritional deficiency among the causes of bad sheds, so a gecko that keeps retaining shed at correct humidity needs a closer look than another misting.

## Parasites

Watch for weight loss, lethargy, and abnormal stool. Genuinely common in wild-caught animals specifically, one of several reasons captive-bred is worth the higher purchase price. A vet is required for diagnosis and treatment.

Even farmed stock carries them. A 2025 study in the journal *Animals* screened 21 commercially farmed tokay geckos in China and found intestinal parasites in 42.9% of them, with *Strongyloides* in 19.1%, *Spauligodon* pinworms in 14.3%, and *Spironucleus* in 9.5%. The signs Merck gives for reptile parasitism are appetite loss, weight loss, vomiting, and diarrhea, and a gecko can carry a load for months before any of them show. That is why PetMD's tokay sheet calls for an annual exam with fecal testing even in a gecko that looks fine.

## Prolapse

Tissue protruding from the vent. This is always an emergency, get to a vet immediately.

The tissue can be cloaca, colon, oviduct, or a hemipenis, and Merck's list of what pushes it out runs from egg binding and breeding trauma to infection, metabolic disease, bladder stones, kidney disease, and masses in the abdomen. The prolapse is the symptom; the vet's job is to identify the organ, put it back, and find the cause underneath. Do not try to replace it yourself.

## Quarantine for a Wild-Caught Tokay

If your tokay is wild-caught, the first job is finding out what it arrived with. The Merck Veterinary Manual recommends a quarantine of 3 to 6 months for new reptiles, and its parasitology chapter is blunt that reptiles should be screened and treated in quarantine before they enter a collection. Dial A Vet's shorter figure is 30 days; the longer window is the safer one for a wild-caught animal. The rest is [practice rather than protocol](/blog/reptile-quarantine-guide/): its own enclosure in a separate room from any other reptile, a fecal exam from a reptile vet early and a repeat before quarantine ends, and a weekly weight on a gram scale, since the slow weight loss Merck names is the sign a keeper can catch before the gecko looks sick.

## When to Call a Vet

PetMD's tokay care sheet gives a list worth keeping near the enclosure. Any of these means a call, not a wait:

- Loss of appetite, or no interest in food at all
- Lethargy or unusual inactivity
- Nasal discharge or any change in breathing
- Swelling, damage, or changes in skin color
- Weight loss
- Limping or difficulty moving
- Loose or abnormal droppings
- Eye discharge or swelling
- Retained shed
- Any injury or trauma
- Ulcers or excessive discharge in the mouth
- Tissue protruding from the vent

## The Husbandry Pattern Worth Remembering

Low humidity, the opposite failure mode from many desert species, causes the retained shed that's especially common in this humidity-loving gecko. Weak UVB and calcium cause MBD, and the [feeding guide](/blog/tokay-gecko-feeding-guide/) has the calcium schedule for a gecko kept with and without UVB. And buying wild-caught rather than captive-bred is the single biggest predictor of a parasite problem.

The [leopard gecko](/blog/leopard-gecko-health-issues-guide/) and [crested gecko](/blog/crested-gecko-health-issues-guide/) fail in the same three places, temperature, supplementation, and whatever the animal arrived carrying, just at different numbers.
