# Tarantula Enrichment: The Spider Sat Still in Both Enclosures

<AffiliateDisclosure />

There is exactly one controlled tarantula housing study, and it is unusually honest about what it did and did not find. For everything else about them, see our complete [Tarantula care guide](/guides/tarantula/).

<KeyTakeaway>
Given an enclosure six times larger with six times as much structure, tarantulas were **no more active**, used **77 percent larger home ranges**, were **150 percent more likely to be active in a novel environment afterwards**, and showed **no preference** when offered both. All four of those results are from the same study, and a guide that quotes only the flattering ones is not telling you the truth.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Research Actually Says

Stalter, Dorleus, Milone, Sincage, Skurski and Leeds published a preliminary behavioural study of Brazilian black tarantulas, *Grammostola pulchra*, in *PLoS ONE* in December 2024. Counterbalanced design, Standard versus Complex housing, Complex being roughly **six times larger with six times as many structural elements**.

**Activity did not change.** Budgets were approximately equivalent in both, and **inactivity accounted for nearly all observed behaviour**.

**Space use did change.** Home ranges were **77 percent larger** and core areas **113 percent larger** in the complex enclosure. Given more, the spiders used more.

**Boldness changed.** In a novel environment test, tarantulas had **150 percent greater odds of being active** after living in the complex condition, which the authors read as a more neophilic response.

**Preference did not appear.** Offered both simultaneously, the spiders showed **no clear preference**, splitting their time roughly evenly.

The authors' own conclusion is that welfare may be improved by complexity, on the strength of space use and the novelty response, while noting plainly that broad behaviour patterns were similar, no preference emerged, and the sample was small.

<FunFact>
The preference test is the result that ought to make everyone cautious. It is the closest thing to asking the animal directly, and the answer was a shrug. That does not mean complexity is worthless, and it does mean the case for it rests on inferred measures rather than on the spider choosing it.
</FunFact>

## Substrate Depth, Which Beats Everything Above

Nothing in that study displaces this. The most common genuine welfare failure in tarantula keeping is a terrestrial burrowing species kept on a shallow layer of substrate in an enclosure designed to be looked into.

Depth is species-dependent: an obligate burrower needs far more than a terrestrial species that mostly sits under a piece of bark, and an arboreal species needs height and a vertical retreat instead.

## A Retreat

Every tarantula wants somewhere to be out of sight. <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3SKmfOx" product="Cork bark round hide">Cork bark</AffiliateLink> laid down as a starter burrow for a terrestrial species, or mounted vertically for an arboreal one, gives the spider a structure to web into and modify.

Let it modify things. A tarantula that rearranges substrate and webs up the entrance is doing the closest thing to construction this animal does, and an enclosure it cannot alter is one less thing to do.

## Water, Always

A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/44hAzR9" product="Small/shallow water dish">shallow water dish</AffiliateLink>, kept full, for every species including arid ones. Chronic dehydration is a far more common cause of death than anything else on this page, and it is entirely preventable.

Overflowing the dish to dampen one corner is how most keepers manage humidity, rather than misting the whole enclosure into a swamp.

## Size, With the Study's Caveats

The space result is real: the spiders used 77 percent more home range when they had it. Go larger than the minimum where you sensibly can.

Two limits. An enclosure so large a terrestrial spider can fall a significant distance is dangerous, since a tarantula's abdomen ruptures easily and a fall can kill it. And a very large enclosure can make a small spider hard to find and hard to feed. Height is the risk, floor area is the benefit.

## Structure to Move Through

The other half of the complexity finding. Cork flats, leaf litter, low branch, moss, sphagnum, anchored so nothing can shift onto the animal.

Keep it low for terrestrial species. Everything about tarantula enclosure design comes back to not letting the spider fall.

## Feeding

Live prey moving in the enclosure, which is what the ambush is for. <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4uFr6hU" product="Dubia Roaches (feeder bag)">Feeder roaches</AffiliateLink> or crickets released rather than presented, and vary timing.

Do not leave uneaten live prey in with a spider approaching a moult, since crickets will chew on a soft tarantula and that is a real killer. Our [moulting guide](/blog/invertebrate-molting-guide/) covers the signs, and our [feeding guide](/blog/tarantula-feeding-guide/) covers sizing and frequency.

## Do Not Read Stillness as a Problem

Worth stating outright, because the study makes it unavoidable. Inactivity was nearly all of the observed behaviour in both conditions. A tarantula sitting motionless for days is a tarantula.

The trap runs the other way too. Because a still spider looks the same whether the setup is right or wrong, the husbandry has to be judged on substrate, humidity, ventilation and water rather than on how busy the animal looks.

## Priority Order

1. Substrate depth and enclosure type matched to the species
2. Water, always available
3. A retreat the spider can web into and modify
4. Correct humidity and ventilation
5. Floor area beyond the minimum, without adding fall height
6. Low structure to move through
7. Live prey released rather than presented

## What Not to Do

Do not keep an obligate burrower on shallow substrate for the sake of visibility. Do not give a terrestrial species height it can fall from. Do not let the water dish run dry. Do not leave live prey in with a pre-moult spider. Do not handle, covered in our [handling guide](/blog/tarantula-handling-guide/), since the risk to the spider from a fall is far greater than the risk to you. And do not treat a motionless tarantula as evidence of anything at all.
