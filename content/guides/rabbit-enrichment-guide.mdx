# Rabbit Enrichment: A Companion Beats Everything Else You Could Buy

<AffiliateDisclosure />

The rabbit enrichment aisle is full of things to put in a hutch. The evidence points somewhere much less commercial. For everything else about them, see our complete [Rabbit care guide](/guides/rabbit/).

<KeyTakeaway>
In privately owned rabbits, solitary animals showed **significantly less behavioural diversity and more awake inactivity** than socially housed ones. Space is the second finding: run access and hutch size measurably changed behaviour and faecal corticosterone in pair-housed pet rabbits. Company and room, then everything else.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Research Actually Says

Three threads, all on rabbits, which is a luxury after the reptile half of this series.

**Companionship.** A study of social housing conditions in privately owned rabbits found solitary rabbits expressed **less behavioural diversity** and **more awake inactivity**. Socially housed rabbits were more active. The same work found a **larger age difference between paired rabbits was associated with a lower friendship index**, which is a practical steer when choosing a companion.

**Space, in the physiology.** A study of pair-housed pet rabbits found **run access, hutch size and time of day all affected welfare-relevant behaviour and faecal corticosterone**. Space is not a nicety here, it registers in a stress hormone metabolite.

**Structure.** In group-housed rabbits, an elevated platform let animals move up and down, rest more comfortably and **increase exploratory behaviour**. Getting up onto something is worth building in.

<FunFact>
Awake inactivity is one of the more uncomfortable welfare measures to learn about, because a still rabbit reads as a contented rabbit to most owners. The measure exists precisely because those two states look identical from the sofa.
</FunFact>

## A Companion, First

The single highest-value change and the one the evidence most directly supports. A neutered, bonded pair does things a single rabbit does not: grooming, resting in contact, and simply more behaviour of every kind.

Neuter both, bond them properly rather than putting two rabbits in a room, and prefer animals closer in age given the friendship index finding. Bonding takes weeks and going too fast is how it fails: short supervised sessions on ground neither rabbit has claimed, ended before either animal is tired of it, repeated daily until they rest in contact. Our [handling guide](/blog/rabbit-handling-guide/) covers the safe pick-up and support you will need if a session has to be broken up, which is the part of bonding people are least prepared for.

A single rabbit with an attentive owner is a compromise, not an equivalent.

## Space and Run Access

The second finding, and the one that costs money. A hutch is a bedroom, not a house. Permanent access to a run, or free range of a rabbit-proofed room, is what the corticosterone result is pointing at.

Permanent beats scheduled. A rabbit let out for an hour a day spends the rest of it confined, and the study measured time-of-day effects for a reason: rabbits are crepuscular and want the space at dawn and dusk, not when it suits the household. Our [tank setup guide](/blog/rabbit-tank-setup-guide/) covers minimum dimensions.

## Digging

Burrowing animals. Digging is normal, not naughty, and a rabbit with nowhere to do it will pick somewhere you would rather it did not.

A dig box, a large container of safe soil, shredded paper or hay, gives the behaviour an outlet. Cardboard boxes to shred and tunnel through cover the same instinct cheaply.

## Height and Vantage Points

The platform finding. Something to hop up onto and survey from, plus somewhere to hide underneath, lets a prey animal choose between exposure and cover.

Both matter. A rabbit that can only be exposed or only be hidden has one option, and the choice is the enrichment.

## Chewing

Continuously growing teeth. <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0FNM64GL1?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Natural Sweet Bamboo Chew Sticks for Rabbits and Small Animals">Bamboo chew sticks</AffiliateLink>, untreated willow and plain cardboard cover it. Hay does most of the dental work and chews handle the rest.

## Priority Order

1. A neutered, bonded companion, close in age
2. Permanent run access or a rabbit-proofed room
3. Unlimited hay, presented so it takes time to eat
4. A dig box
5. Platforms to get on and cover to get under
6. Chewing and shredding material
7. Novel objects, last, and they are the cheap part

## What Not to Do

Do not keep a rabbit alone and expect toys to close the gap, since the research measured that gap and toys were not what changed it. Do not treat a hutch as housing. Do not schedule exercise for the household's convenience when the animal is crepuscular. Do not feed a muesli-style mix instead of hay. Do not pair rabbits without neutering both first. And do not treat digging as a behaviour problem, covered in our [feeding guide](/blog/rabbit-feeding-guide/) alongside the hay side of the picture.
