# How Much Does Corydoras Catfish Ownership Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Corydoras catfish are genuinely inexpensive, and like the [guppy](/blog/guppy-cost-guide/) and [neon tetra](/blog/neon-tetra-cost-guide/) already covered on this site, the real budgeting consideration isn't the individual fish, it's that you need to buy a group, not one or two. For the full care picture beyond budgeting, see our full [Corydoras Catfish care guide](/guides/corydoras-catfish/).

## The Fish: $3 to $30 Depending on Species

Common species, panda, peppered, bronze, and albino cories among them, run $3 to $8 each. Specialty species run considerably more, corydoras barbatus around $30, orange laser corydoras $15 to $20. Since this species needs to be kept in groups of six or more, more on why in our [Corydoras Catfish handling guide](/blog/corydoras-catfish-handling-guide/), budget for the whole school, not a single fish.

<FunFact>
Corydoras catfish are a longer commitment than they look. Most live 5 to 10 years, and occasional reports have individuals reaching up to 15 years under genuinely ideal conditions, an unusually long lifespan for a fish this small and unassuming at the bottom of the tank.
</FunFact>

A 20-gallon long tank is the practical starting size for a proper school. Plants, hides, and a water conditioner round out the list on top of the equipment above, together landing the full setup in the $150 to $215 range. Soft sand substrate is genuinely important for this species, and our [Corydoras Catfish tank setup guide](/blog/corydoras-catfish-tank-setup-guide/) covers exactly why it matters more than it looks like it should.

## Ongoing Costs: Roughly $10 to $20 a Month

Cories feed at the bottom and need food that actually reaches them, not just surface flakes, which is why a dedicated sinking food is worth budgeting for specifically rather than assuming leftovers will do.

Water conditioner, occasional filter media, and modest electricity make up the rest of the monthly total.

## Vet Costs

Aquatic vets are genuinely uncommon, and most cory health issues are managed directly by the keeper through water quality correction and appropriate medication rather than professional visits. Our [Corydoras Catfish health issues guide](/blog/corydoras-catfish-health-issues-guide/) covers what's actually manageable at home.

## Lifespan

5 to 10 years is typical, with some individuals living well past that, occasional reports reach up to 15 years under genuinely ideal conditions. This is a longer-lived fish than many people expect from a small bottom dweller, worth factoring into your long-term tank planning.

## The Real Budgeting Lesson: Buy the School, Not the Fish

Since cories are genuinely stressed and behave abnormally when kept in groups smaller than six, the actual cost of "a corydoras" is really the cost of at least six of them, plus a tank sized to comfortably hold that group. Planning for the full school from the start, rather than starting with two or three and adding more later, is both more humane and more cost-effective overall.

This is a familiar pattern for anyone who's priced out a proper neon tetra school, covered in our [Neon Tetra cost guide](/blog/neon-tetra-cost-guide/), the individual fish is cheap, the group is the real number to plan around.
