# Jackson's Chameleon Enclosure Setup: Considerably Cooler Than the Veiled Chameleon

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Jackson's chameleon husbandry looks similar to the [veiled chameleon's](/blog/veiled-chameleon-tank-setup-guide/) on paper, tall screen enclosure, misting, UVB, but the actual temperature numbers run meaningfully cooler, a direct result of this species' high-altitude East African origin. This species is one of four covered in our recent [Jackson's Chameleon, Canary, Giant Millipede & Corydoras Catfish compared](/blog/jacksonschameleon-canary-millipede-corydoras-overview/) roundup.

<Figure src="/assets/images/jacksons-chameleon-tank-setup-guide-1.jpg" alt="Labeled diagram of a Jackson's chameleon enclosure showing a halogen basking bulb with required UVB tube, a genuine 50-65°F night drop, and two or three solid enclosure sides" caption="Jackson's chameleon husbandry looks similar to the veiled chameleon's on paper, but the actual temperature numbers run meaningfully cooler." shareable />

## Enclosure Size

24 inches long by 24 inches wide by 48 inches tall for a single adult, larger is better. Juveniles under about 10 months can do well temporarily in something smaller, around 16x16x30 inches. House one chameleon per enclosure, males will fight, covered further in the [handling guide](/blog/jacksons-chameleon-handling-guide/). A <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B001PHABI8?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Zoo Med Reptibreeze Open Air Screen Cage 24x24x48">tall screen enclosure</AffiliateLink> sized correctly from the start saves a later upgrade, the full cost breakdown is in our [cost guide](/blog/jacksons-chameleon-cost-guide/).

## Temperature: The Defining Difference From the Veiled Chameleon

Basking spot around 85°F, similar to the veiled chameleon, but ambient air should stay considerably cooler, 68 to 75°F, with a genuine night drop to 50 to 65°F. Supplemental night heat usually isn't needed and can work against this species, unlike some reptiles that need warmth around the clock, Jackson's chameleons are adapted to cool mountain nights. Use a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B000YS3H0I?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Exo Terra Sun Glo Halogen Basking Spot Lamp, 50W">halogen basking bulb</AffiliateLink> on one side during the day only, run through a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08R29S2WB?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Exo Terra Dimming and Pulse Proportional Thermostat for Reptile Terrariums">dimming thermostat</AffiliateLink> to keep that gradient accurate.

<FunFact>
The night drop here isn't a nice-to-have, it's the point. Jackson's chameleons come from cool, high-altitude forest in East Africa, and mimicking that genuine overnight chill, rather than propping the enclosure up to a constant temperature, is part of what keeps this species healthy long term.
</FunFact>

## Humidity

Daytime humidity around 30 to 50%, rising to 75 to 100% overnight, achieved through morning and evening misting, an <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4fibBY2" product="Automatic misting system/fogger">automated mister</AffiliateLink> for consistency, and a cool-mist humidifier on a humidistat for the overnight spike. <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4grZYPq" product="Live terrarium plants">Live plants</AffiliateLink> help maintain this naturally. Note that humidity guidance varies somewhat across sources, some recommend a flatter 60 to 80% range throughout, but the day-low, night-high pattern is the most consistently supported approach.

## Substrate

Bare bottom with paper towel is the simplest, most manageable choice, though a bioactive substrate setup works too if you prefer. Avoid loose, particulate substrate that could be accidentally ingested and cause impaction, one of the health risks covered in the [health issues guide](/blog/jacksons-chameleon-health-issues-guide/).

## Lighting

UVB is required, a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3TiqeC4" product="UVB - Arcadia Forest 6% (ProT5 kit, 36&quot;/39W)">Zoo Med ReptiSun 5.0 T5 HO or Arcadia Forest 6% bulb</AffiliateLink>, replaced every 6 to 12 months.

## The Non-Obvious Requirements

Favor an enclosure with two or three solid sides rather than full mesh, this helps hold humidity and genuinely reduces stress compared to an all-screen setup. Use distilled water in misters and foggers to avoid mineral buildup and equipment damage, and disinfect misting equipment regularly. Never run a fogger while the heat lamp is on, the combination can create dangerous conditions inside the enclosure.
