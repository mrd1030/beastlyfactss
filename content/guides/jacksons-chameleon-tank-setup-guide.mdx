# Jackson's Chameleon Enclosure Setup: Considerably Cooler Than the Veiled Chameleon

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Jackson's chameleon husbandry looks similar to the veiled chameleon's on paper, tall screen enclosure, misting, UVB, but the actual temperature numbers run meaningfully cooler, a direct result of this species' high-altitude East African origin.

<Figure src="/assets/images/jacksons-chameleon-tank-setup-guide-1.jpg" alt="Labeled diagram of a Jackson's chameleon enclosure showing a halogen basking bulb with required UVB tube, a genuine 50-65°F night drop, and two or three solid enclosure sides" caption="Jackson's chameleon husbandry looks similar to the veiled chameleon's on paper, but the actual temperature numbers run meaningfully cooler." shareable />

## Enclosure Size

24 inches long by 24 inches wide by 48 inches tall for a single adult, larger is better. Juveniles under about 10 months can do well temporarily in something smaller, around 16x16x30 inches. House one chameleon per enclosure, males will fight. ## Temperature: The Defining Difference From the Veiled Chameleon

Basking spot around 85°F, similar to the veiled chameleon, but ambient air should stay considerably cooler, 70 to 80°F and kept toward the low end, since heat stress sets in once the air reaches the 80s, with a genuine night drop to 50 to 65°F. Supplemental night heat usually isn't needed and can work against this species, unlike some reptiles that need warmth around the clock, Jackson's chameleons are adapted to cool mountain nights. Use a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B000YS3H0I?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Exo Terra Sun Glo Halogen Basking Spot Lamp, 50W">halogen basking bulb</AffiliateLink> on one side during the day only, run through a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08R29S2WB?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Exo Terra Dimming and Pulse Proportional Thermostat for Reptile Terrariums">dimming thermostat</AffiliateLink> to keep that gradient accurate.

<FunFact>
The night drop here isn't a nice-to-have, it's the point. Jackson's chameleons come from cool, high-altitude forest in East Africa, and mimicking that genuine overnight chill, rather than propping the enclosure up to a constant temperature, is part of what keeps this species healthy long term.
</FunFact>

### Holding the Room in Range

The enclosure can only be as cool as the room it sits in. A screen cage exchanges air with the room constantly, so a room that reaches the 80s in summer puts the whole enclosure there too, and heat stress starts in the 80s for this species. For many keepers that makes an air conditioner part of the setup: keep the room in the low 70s through the day so the basking bulb can create the only warm spot, and let the room fall at night. A single higher-wattage bulb mounted farther from the branch gives a wider, gentler warm zone than a small bulb close in, which helps keep the rest of the enclosure cool. Check both ends of the gradient with a thermometer at the branch and another at the bottom of the cage, especially on the first hot day of the year.

## Humidity

Relative humidity of 60 to 100%, held with misting every 4 to 8 hours or an automated misting system, and a cool-mist humidifier where the room cannot hold it. Live plants help maintain it naturally. The misting is also how the animal drinks, since Jackson's chameleons take water as droplets from leaves rather than from a dish. Across the day the reading swings: it can dip toward 50% as the enclosure dries between mistings, then climbs to 90 to 100% overnight, which keeps the daily average inside the veterinary band. Keep a fogger off while the heat lamp is on.

## Substrate

Bare bottom with paper towel is the simplest, most manageable choice, though a bioactive substrate setup works too if you prefer. Avoid loose, particulate substrate that could be accidentally ingested and cause impaction.

## Lighting

UVB is required, a Zoo Med ReptiSun 5.0 T5 HO or Arcadia Forest 6% bulb in a T5 HO fixture, replaced every 6 to 12 months. The target is a UVI of 3.0 to 4.0 at the top basking branch, and distance sets it. With the fixture sitting on a mesh top, put the highest branch 9 to 11 inches below the bulb. With nothing between bulb and branch, 11 to 13 inches. An Arcadia ShadeDweller 7% gets the same reading much closer, 4 to 6 inches through mesh or 6 to 8 inches without. A UVI meter at the branch settles it for any particular fixture.

## Diet Basics

Live insects, varied: crickets, roaches, silkworms, superworms, mealworms, grasshoppers and wax worms, along with pesticide-free snails, slugs, caterpillars and flies.

Offer no more than five to seven insects at a feeding, none larger than the space between the chameleon's eyes. Overfeeding is its own risk here, since gout in this species traces back to too much protein as readily as to too little water.

Gut-load the insects before they go in. What the cricket ate is what the chameleon gets.

Supplement lightly, on a weekly rhythm: calcium dusted onto an adult's non-breeding diet once weekly, and a general vitamin and mineral supplement that may go on once weekly too. This is a species where more is a real risk: avoid products high in vitamin D, which can reach toxicity, and go carefully with synthetic vitamin A, which this species is especially sensitive to and which drives the [edema and gout](/blog/jacksons-chameleon-health-issues-guide/) it is prone to.

## The Non-Obvious Requirements

Favor an enclosure with two or three solid sides rather than full mesh, this helps hold humidity and genuinely reduces stress compared to an all-screen setup. Use distilled water in misters and foggers to avoid mineral buildup and equipment damage, and disinfect misting equipment regularly. Never run a fogger while the heat lamp is on, the combination can create dangerous conditions inside the enclosure.
