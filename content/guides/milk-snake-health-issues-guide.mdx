# Common Milk Snake Health Issues

<VetDisclaimer />
<AffiliateDisclosure />

Milk snakes are hardy, and most of what shows up on this list traces back to enclosure moisture and temperature, both fully within an owner's control. See our [Milk Snake tank setup guide](/blog/milk-snake-tank-setup-guide/) for the husbandry side, and our full [Milk Snake care guide](/guides/milk-snake/) for everything else.

## Respiratory Infection

Wheezing, nasal or oral discharge, and open-mouth breathing point here. From temperatures running too cool, humidity that's off, or poor ventilation. Always see a vet, this needs antibiotics and won't clear up on its own.

## Scale Rot (Blister Disease)

Red, inflamed skin and fluid-filled blisters, typically on the belly, from <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4vNgpe1" product="Cypress mulch substrate">substrate</AffiliateLink> kept too wet or dirty. This is well documented by veterinary sources as a real risk that can progress to septicemia and become fatal if left untreated. Always see a vet, and address the underlying substrate and cleaning routine at the same time, treatment alone doesn't fix a setup that's still too damp.

## Mites and Parasites

Tiny black or red specks around the eyes and scales point to mites, while restlessness and excessive soaking can be signs of either. External mites are treatable with a reptile-safe product and a deep enclosure clean. Internal parasites need a fecal exam and a vet's guidance.

## Retained Shed (Dysecdysis)

From low humidity or dehydration. Manageable at home with a properly maintained <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4bpnvNm" product="Sphagnum moss (for moist hide)">humid hide</AffiliateLink> and correct overall humidity, covered in our [Milk Snake tank setup guide](/blog/milk-snake-tank-setup-guide/). One handling note worth remembering here: avoid handling a snake mid-shed, when its skin is more fragile and easily damaged, covered in more detail in our [Milk Snake handling guide](/blog/milk-snake-handling-guide/).

## Mouth Rot and Obesity

Less common but both reported in this species. Mouth rot, infectious stomatitis, needs a vet. Obesity is manageable through appropriate feeding frequency and portion size relative to your snake's actual size and activity level.

<FunFact>
Three of the conditions on this list trace back to just one variable, moisture. Substrate that's too wet causes scale rot, air that's too dry causes retained shed, and the two sit at opposite ends of the same dial. Getting the humidity and substrate moisture right isn't just one husbandry task among many for milk snakes, it's close to the central skill of keeping this species healthy long-term.
</FunFact>

## The Husbandry Pattern Worth Remembering

Substrate kept too wet is the direct cause of scale rot, one of the more serious conditions on this list. Low humidity, the opposite problem, causes retained shed. And incorrect temperature or ventilation contributes to respiratory infection. Getting the moisture balance right, not too wet, not too dry, is really the central skill in keeping this species healthy long-term.
