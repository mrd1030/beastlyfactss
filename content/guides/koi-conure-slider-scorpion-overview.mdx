# Four Pets People Get Wrong Before They Even Bring One Home

Each of these gets sold on an assumption that turns out to be backwards. A "small pond fish." A "quiet, easy bird." A "simple starter turtle." A "dangerous, scary invertebrate." None of those four descriptions hold up once you actually look at the animal.

<Figure src="/assets/images/koi-conure-slider-scorpion-overview-1.jpg" alt="Portrait infographic comparing a koi, green cheek conure, red-eared slider, and emperor scorpion, showing the backwards assumption sold with each one and the reality" caption="Each of these gets sold on an assumption that turns out to be backwards." shareable />

## Koi: Not a Pond Fish, a Decades-Long Infrastructure Project

A koi bought as a few inches of color in a garden center can outgrow a small pond, live 25 to 50 years, and eventually demand serious filtration built for a fish that's heavier and messier than most people plan for. They're never really handled, this is an observation and water-management pet from day one.

## Green Cheek Conure: Not the Quiet, Low-Effort Parrot It's Marketed As

Quieter than a sun conure, sure, but quiet doesn't mean low-maintenance. These are social, intelligent birds that need daily out-of-cage time and real interaction, and a bored, under-stimulated conure turns loud and destructive fast.

[Cost](/blog/conure-cost-guide/) · [Handling](/blog/conure-handling-guide/) · [Health Issues](/blog/conure-health-issues-guide/) · [Cage Setup](/blog/conure-tank-setup-guide/)

## Red-Eared Slider: The Hatchling That Becomes a 100-Gallon Commitment

Sold cheap and small, this turtle grows into a 20 to 30 year, 75 to 100-plus gallon animal with genuinely heavy filtration needs. It's one of the most commonly bought, and most commonly outgrown, pet reptiles out there for exactly this reason.

[Cost](/blog/red-eared-slider-cost-guide/) · [Handling](/blog/red-eared-slider-handling-guide/) · [Health Issues](/blog/red-eared-slider-health-issues-guide/) · [Tank Setup](/blog/red-eared-slider-tank-setup-guide/)

## Emperor Scorpion: The "Scary" One That's Actually the Mildest

Big pincers, a real sting, and a reputation that scares people off, but emperor scorpions lean on those pincers over their sting, the venom is mild for a healthy adult, and at 6 to 8 years they're the shortest-lived, least demanding pet in this whole group.

<FunFact>
Line up the lifespans and the range is bigger than it looks at first glance: an emperor scorpion may only be around for 6 to 8 years, while a well-kept koi can outlive the pond it started in and reach 25 to 50 years. Two animals, both routinely sold as casual starter pets, on completely opposite ends of the commitment scale.
</FunFact>

## The Common Thread

Three of these four are multi-decade commitments sold as casual pets, and the fourth gets treated as dangerous when it's actually the easiest exit of the group. Know which one you're actually signing up for before the tank, cage, or pond gets built.
