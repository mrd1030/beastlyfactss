# Four Pets People Get Wrong Before They Even Bring One Home

Each of these gets sold on an assumption that turns out to be backwards. A "small pond fish." A "quiet, easy bird." A "simple starter turtle." A "dangerous, scary invertebrate." None of those four descriptions hold up once you actually look at the animal.

## Koi: Not a Pond Fish, a Decades-Long Infrastructure Project

A koi bought as a few inches of color in a garden center can outgrow a small pond, live 25 to 50 years, and eventually demand serious filtration built for a fish that's heavier and messier than most people plan for. They're never really handled, this is an observation and water-management pet from day one.

The size gap is the part garden centers leave out. Most ornamental fish farmed in Florida stay under 4 inches, and koi are the named exception, routinely passing 24 inches. A fish that size produces far more waste than a [goldfish](/blog/goldfish-tank-setup-guide/), so the pond is a filtration system with fish in it, sized for the adults the fry will become.

## Green Cheek Conure: Not the Quiet, Low-Effort Parrot It's Marketed As

Quieter than a sun conure, sure, but quiet doesn't mean low-maintenance. These are social, intelligent birds that need daily out-of-cage time and real interaction, and a bored, under-stimulated conure turns loud and destructive fast.

The good news is real. At about 10 inches, the green cheek is one of the smallest conures, owners prize it for being quieter than other conures, and it is not known for nipping. It is playful to the point of acrobatics, hanging upside down and clambering along the cage bars waiting for someone to play. The catch is time: a spacious cage, a great deal of time out of it, and a lifespan of up to 25 years or more. Some learn a fair number of words, but a green cheek is not bought for talking.

## Red-Eared Slider: The Hatchling That Becomes a 100-Gallon Commitment

Sold cheap and small, this turtle grows into a 20 to 30 year, 75 to 100-plus gallon animal with genuinely heavy filtration needs. It's one of the most commonly bought, and most commonly outgrown, pet reptiles out there for exactly this reason.

The numbers explain why. An adult slider grows to about 12 inches of shell, sometimes larger, and lives 20 to 30 years, with 40 not unheard of. Outgrown sliders released into ponds and lakes have made the species prolifically invasive. Every tank and filter decision is a decision about the 12 inch adult.

## Emperor Scorpion: The "Scary" One That's the Mildest

Big pincers, a real sting, and a reputation that scares people off, but emperor scorpions lean on those pincers over their sting, the venom is mild for a healthy adult, and at 6 to 8 years they're the shortest-lived, least demanding pet in this whole group.

They also come with more personality than most invertebrates. Emperor scorpions are native to the forests of West Africa, from Nigeria and Ghana to the Congo region, where they live in burrows under leaf litter and in termite mounds, and they are social enough to live in colonies of up to 15 animals. Adults average about 20 cm (8 inches). And like other scorpions, an emperor glows a fluorescent bluish green under UV light.

<FunFact>
Line up the lifespans and the range is bigger than it looks at first glance: an emperor scorpion may only be around for 6 to 8 years, while a well-kept koi can outlive the pond it started in and reach 25 to 50 years. Two animals, both routinely sold as casual starter pets, on completely opposite ends of the commitment scale.
</FunFact>

## The Common Thread

Three of these four are multi-decade commitments sold as casual pets, and the fourth gets treated as dangerous when it's actually the easiest exit of the group. Know which one you're signing up for before the tank, cage, or pond gets built.
