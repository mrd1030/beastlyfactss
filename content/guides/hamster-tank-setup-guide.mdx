# Hamster Enclosure Setup: Bigger and Deeper Than the Starter Kit Suggests

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Hamster housing standards have shifted significantly in recent years, and the small wire cages sold as "hamster starter kits" fall well short of current welfare recommendations. Getting size and bedding depth right is most of what matters here. For everything else about day-to-day care, see our complete [Hamster care guide](/guides/hamster/), or our companion guides on [cost](/blog/hamster-cost-guide/), [handling](/blog/hamster-handling-guide/), and [health issues](/blog/hamster-health-issues-guide/).

## Enclosure Size

Current welfare guidance calls for a genuinely large unbroken floor space, roughly 700 to 775 square inches for a Syrian, around 600 square inches for dwarf species, with more always being better. A <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B074FH47MQ?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Prevue Pet Products Universal Small Animal Home (Model 528)">properly sized cage</AffiliateLink> or a 40-gallon breeder tank works well for dwarfs; Syrians do better in something larger still, a 75-gallon tank or equivalent. If you're using a wire cage, bar spacing should be no more than half an inch for Syrians and a quarter inch for smaller species, Roborovski especially, since they can squeeze through surprisingly small gaps.

## Temperature

65 to 75°F is the comfortable range, kept stable. Below 60°F risks torpor, a hibernation-like state that can be dangerous, above 80°F risks heat stress. No supplemental heat source is needed in a normal indoor environment, but a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3QHrM81" product="Digital thermometer/hygrometer combo">digital thermometer</AffiliateLink> makes it easy to confirm the room is actually staying inside that range rather than guessing.

## Bedding: Deeper Than You'd Guess

At least 6 inches of dust-free, <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4voH9Ap" product="Small-pet paper bedding">paper-based bedding</AffiliateLink> (Carefresh is a common choice) or aspen shavings. This isn't decorative, it's a genuine welfare requirement. Avoid cedar and pine entirely, the aromatic oils in both irritate the respiratory system, more on why in our [Hamster health issues guide](/blog/hamster-health-issues-guide/).

<FunFact>
Six inches of bedding is the minimum, not the ceiling. Veterinary guidance specifically notes that bedding depths of roughly 15 to 30 inches enhance welfare for Syrian hamsters by supporting natural burrowing behavior, deep bedding lets a hamster express instincts that shallow bedding simply doesn't allow, far deeper than most owners would ever guess a cage liner needs to be.
</FunFact>

## Lighting

No special lighting or UVB is required. A normal day and night cycle is sufficient, just remember this is a nocturnal species, and bright light during their active hours is more disruptive than helpful.

## The Wheel: Solid, Not Wire, and Properly Sized

A <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08FC8JG1B?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Niteangel Super-Silent Hamster Exercise Wheel, 10 inch">solid-surface wheel with no rungs</AffiliateLink> is essential, wire wheels cause foot injuries. Size matters too: Syrians need at least an 8 to 11 inch wheel, dwarfs 6 to 8 inches. A wheel that's too small forces an unnatural, arched running posture that isn't great for the spine over time.

## The Non-Obvious Requirement: A Sand Bath

A dust-free, non-clumping sand bath is a genuine species essential, not an optional extra. Hamsters use it to manage coat and skin oils the way a bird uses a dust bath, and skipping it isn't a minor omission.

## Rounding Out the Enclosure

A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3T761Pz" product="Small-pet hideout/igloo">hideout</AffiliateLink>, a water bottle, and a food dish complete a basic setup. None of it needs to be elaborate, but all of it needs to be there, a hamster with nowhere to hide is a chronically stressed one.
