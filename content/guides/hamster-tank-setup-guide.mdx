# Hamster Enclosure Setup: Bigger and Deeper Than the Starter Kit Suggests

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Hamster housing standards have shifted significantly in recent years, and the small wire cages sold as "hamster starter kits" fall well short of current welfare recommendations. Getting size and bedding depth right is most of what matters here.

<Figure src="/assets/images/hamster-tank-setup-guide-1.jpg" alt="Labeled diagram of a hamster enclosure showing deep bedding, a solid exercise wheel, a sand bath, and a hideout" caption="Getting size and bedding depth right is most of what matters here." shareable />

## Enclosure Size

Current welfare guidance calls for a genuinely large unbroken floor space, roughly 700 to 775 square inches for a Syrian, around 600 square inches for dwarf species, with more always being better. A <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B074FH47MQ?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Prevue Pet Products Universal Small Animal Home (Model 528)">properly sized cage</AffiliateLink> or a 40-gallon breeder tank works well for dwarfs; Syrians do better in something larger still, a 75-gallon tank or equivalent. If you're using a wire cage, bar spacing should be no more than half an inch for Syrians and a quarter inch for smaller species, Roborovski especially, since they can squeeze through surprisingly small gaps.

## Temperature

65 to 75°F is the comfortable range, kept stable. Below 65°F the room is out of range and appetite and activity start to drop. Below about 41°F (5°C), particularly with short days, a hamster can enter torpor, a hibernation-like state that can be dangerous. Above 80°F risks heat stress. No supplemental heat source is needed in a normal indoor environment, but a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3QHrM81" product="Digital thermometer/hygrometer combo">digital thermometer</AffiliateLink> makes it easy to confirm the room is actually staying inside that range rather than guessing.

## Bedding: Deeper Than You'd Guess

At least 6 inches of dust-free, <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4voH9Ap" product="Small-pet paper bedding">paper-based bedding</AffiliateLink> (Carefresh is a common choice) or aspen shavings, and treat that as the floor: 15 to 30 inches (40 to 80 cm) is the depth that measurably improves a Syrian's welfare. In the bedding-depth study, every hamster given 40 cm or more built its own burrow and used it. This isn't decorative, it's a welfare requirement. Avoid cedar and pine entirely, the aromatic oils in both irritate the respiratory system.

<FunFact>
Hamsters kept on 10 cm of bedding, about 4 inches, gnawed the cage wire significantly more and ran the wheel harder than those given 40 or 80 cm. In a shallow cage, wire-gnawing is a depth problem before it is a toy problem, which is what the [bedding-depth study](/blog/hamster-enrichment-guide/) settles.
</FunFact>

## Lighting

No special lighting or UVB is required. A normal day and night cycle is sufficient, just remember this is a nocturnal species, and bright light during their active hours is more disruptive than helpful.

## The Wheel: Solid, Not Wire, and Properly Sized

A <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08FC8JG1B?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Niteangel Super-Silent Hamster Exercise Wheel, 10 inch">solid-surface wheel with no rungs</AffiliateLink> is essential, wire wheels cause foot injuries. Size matters too: Syrians need at least an 8 to 11 inch wheel, dwarfs 6 to 8 inches. A wheel that's too small forces an unnatural, arched running posture that isn't great for the spine over time.

## The Non-Obvious Requirement: A Sand Bath

A dust-free, non-clumping sand bath is a genuine species essential, not an optional extra. Hamsters use it to manage coat and skin oils the way a bird uses a dust bath, and skipping it isn't a minor omission.

## Cleaning: Spot Daily, Deep Less Often

A hamster's cage smells of its own scent marks and its food stores, and a cleaning routine has to keep enough of both to leave the animal settled.

- **Daily:** remove soiled bedding and uneaten fresh food, and wash and rinse the water bottle or bowl.
- **Weekly for a small cage:** move the hamster to a carrier, take out the bedding, wash the cage and accessories with a small-animal habitat cleaner, rinse off every trace of it, and let everything dry fully before fresh bedding goes in. A large enclosure with deep bedding may only need this every few weeks, with spot cleaning in between.
- **Every time:** set aside a handful of clean nesting material and any dry food hoard, and put them back afterward. Leave an established burrow in place during spot cleans. Return the nest, wheel and bowls to the same spots, since a hamster relies on the layout staying put.

A total strip-out erases the scent marks and the hoard at once, and it can leave a hamster unsettled for days. Clean in the evening, once the hamster is already awake.

## Rounding Out the Enclosure

A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3T761Pz" product="Small-pet hideout/igloo">hideout</AffiliateLink>, a water bottle, and a food dish complete a basic setup. None of it needs to be elaborate, but all of it needs to be there, a hamster with nowhere to hide is a chronically stressed one.

This is also the part of hamster ownership most first-time buyers underestimate: the animal is cheap, but a welfare-appropriate enclosure genuinely isn't, worth keeping in mind if you're also comparing setups for a [blue-tongue skink](/blog/blue-tongue-skink-tank-setup-guide/), a [White's tree frog](/blog/whites-tree-frog-tank-setup-guide/), or a [Pacman frog](/blog/pacman-frog-tank-setup-guide/) side by side.

A [chinchilla](/blog/chinchilla-vs-hamster-guide/) is the step up in both space and commitment: a tall multi-level cage instead of a deep floor, and 10 to 20 years instead of two or three.
