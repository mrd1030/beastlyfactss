# Green Iguana Enrichment: Hatchlings Are Social, Adults Are Not

<AffiliateDisclosure />

The green iguana is one of the best-studied lizards in the pet trade and one of the worst-kept. For everything else about them, see our complete [Green Iguana care guide](/guides/green-iguana/).

<KeyTakeaway>
Wild hatchling iguanas that slept in larger groups grew significantly faster, and hatchlings from different clutches join up and travel together. That is real evidence of sociality in a species the hobby files as asocial. It also stops at the juvenile stage: adults are territorial, and cohabiting them is how iguanas get hurt.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Research Actually Says

Burghardt and colleagues watched hatchling green iguanas emerge at a communal nesting site on a Panamanian islet and published the result in *Science* in 1977. The animals came out of the ground **in small groups**, **joined up across different clutches**, and moved around and off the islet **socially**. Their conclusion was that saurian social organization had been badly underestimated.

Follow-up work sharpened it. Hatchlings tracked on Barro Colorado Island across three years **grew faster when they slept in larger groups**. Separate work found **kinship recognition** in hatchling grouping, meaning the animals were not simply piling up at random.

There is also a small captive welfare assessment worth knowing about: eight iguanas observed over 22 days in an exotic animal facility, where **three of the eight were housed below minimum recommended requirements** and **all eight carried endoparasites**. A tiny sample, and a fair picture of how this species tends to be kept.

<FunFact>
Growth rate is an unusually hard outcome for a sociality study. It is not an observer scoring behavior on a scale, it is body mass over three years, and the animals sleeping in bigger groups put on more of it.
</FunFact>

## What This Does Not Mean

Adults are territorial. Adult males will fight each other, and a breeding-season male can be genuinely dangerous to the person keeping him. The grouping evidence is about hatchlings in the wild, with the entire island available to spread out across, which is not a shared enclosure.

Take from it that a young iguana is not an inert object, and keep the adults apart.

## Space, Which Is the Whole Problem

Everything else on this page is secondary to it. A green iguana reaches five to six feet, and the animal is sold at six inches to people who buy a tank. There is no enrichment item that compensates for an adult iguana in a vivarium, and the honest version of this guide is that most of the work is a custom enclosure or a dedicated room.

## Height and Real Climbing Routes

Arboreal animals. They want to be **up**, and an iguana at height is a calmer iguana than one with nowhere to retreat to.

Anchor everything properly. A structure that shifts under a six-foot lizard teaches the animal that height is unsafe, which is the opposite of the point.

Give more than one route up, so movement involves a choice rather than a single ladder.

## Foraging, for a Herbivore

Herbivore feeding gets treated as a nutrition question and stops there. Green iguanas in the wild spend their day working through foliage, and a bowl of chopped salad in the same corner every morning deletes all of it.

Clip whole leaves at height so feeding happens where the animal already is. Rotate greens across the week so the salad is not identical every day, and dust with a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0D82DPB1D?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Repashy SuperVeggie, 6 oz Jar">plant-eater supplement</AffiliateLink>, since a herbivorous reptile under artificial light needs the calcium and D3 side handled deliberately.

## Basking Choice

A large-bodied basking lizard needs a real thermal gradient and a basking platform sized for a whole adult iguana rather than the front half of one. Thermal choice is the item that ranks first in almost every reptile enrichment study that has bothered to test it, and for an animal this size it is also a heating problem worth solving properly.

## Supervised Free-Roam

Realistically part of keeping an adult. A warm, iguana-proofed room with climbing furniture and no exposed cables is genuine enrichment and is how many long-term keepers manage the space problem.

Supervised, warm, and hazard-checked. A large lizard loose in a cold room with nothing to climb is not being enriched, it is being left out.

## Priority Order

1. An enclosure or room sized for a six-foot adult
2. Height, with anchored routes and platforms
3. A real thermal gradient with an adult-sized basking site
4. Varied, scattered, elevated foraging
5. Visual barriers and a low-traffic location
6. Supervised free-roam in a warm, safe room
7. Rearranging the layout, last

## What Not to Do

Do not house adults together, and do not cite the hatchling grouping work to justify it. Do not buy a hatchling without a plan for the adult, which is the single failure this species is defined by. Do not treat a bowl of salad as feeding done.
