# White's Tree Frog Enclosure Setup: What Your Frog Actually Needs

<AffiliateDisclosure />

White's tree frogs are a forgiving amphibian to set up correctly once you understand the one detail that trips up a lot of first-time keepers: humidity that's too constantly high causes more problems than humidity that dips a bit low between mistings.

## Enclosure Size

18 inches long by 18 inches wide by 24 inches tall is the standard minimum for one adult, sized for a vertical, arboreal species. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4vlb6Bp" product="18x18x24 in glass terrarium (arboreal)">20-gallon-equivalent enclosure</AffiliateLink> is comfortable for a single frog, and 24x18x24 inches works well for a small group of two to four.

## Temperature

Keep a gradient: cool side around 70 to 75°F, a basking area around 80 to 85°F, dropping into the mid-70s at night. A low-wattage bulb or a side-mounted heat mat provides the warmth, this species doesn't need intense heat, just a gentle, reliable gradient.

## Humidity: The Detail Worth Getting Right

Keep a baseline around 50 to 60%, with misting spikes up to 70 to 80% once or twice daily, then let it drop back down between mistings. Mist in the evening, when this nocturnal frog wakes up. Constant, unbroken high humidity is directly linked to bacterial and red-leg issues, so resist the instinct to keep things perpetually damp.

Use only <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3Rg5gTT" product="Water conditioner">dechlorinated tap water or spring water</AffiliateLink> for misting and drinking, never distilled or reverse-osmosis water. The [amphibian quarantine and water guide](/blog/amphibian-quarantine-and-water-guide/) has the reason: distilled and RO water are usually not electrolyte-balanced, and can be fatal to an amphibian without rebalancing.

## Substrate

Coarse orchid bark, coco fiber or coco husk, or a bioactive soil mix, kept lightly moist rather than soggy. Avoid fine, loose substrate that's easy to accidentally ingest during feeding.

## Lighting

Low-level UVB is recommended, this species survives without it but genuinely does better with it. A <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00M9ONY1W?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Zoo Med 5.0 T5-HO 14&quot; Terrarium Hood with ReptiSun T5 HO 5.0 UVB Lamp">low-output T5 bulb in the 5 to 7% range</AffiliateLink> works well, positioned so the basking branch sits at least 6 inches below the fixture.

<FunFact>
A T5 UVB bulb can keep glowing visibly long after its actual UVB output has crashed. That's exactly why the bulb gets replaced every 9 to 12 months on a schedule, regardless of whether it still looks like it's working.
</FunFact>

## Furnishings

Sturdy <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3SKmfOx" product="Zoo Med Natural Cork Rounds Medium (7&quot;-10&quot; Long) (3)">branches for climbing</AffiliateLink>, since this is an arboreal species that spends real time off the ground, along with <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4gmXC4j" product="Artificial/silk terrarium plants">broad leaves or platforms</AffiliateLink> for resting, and a water dish that's easy to keep clean. Make it wide enough for the frog to sit in and soak, but shallower than the frog is tall when it sits with its legs folded: tree frogs are weak swimmers, and a deep dish can drown one.

## Cleaning Routine

- **Daily:** spot clean, wiping droppings and waste off the leaves, branches and glass, and pull uneaten insects within a few hours of feeding. Empty, rinse and refill the water dish every day, or at the very least every other day, because the frog soaks and defecates in it.
- **As needed, the more often the better:** take out bark and moss and wash them. Paper towel lining, if used, is changed often.
- **Soil:** replace it when it starts to smell or stays waterlogged, since wet, dirty substrate is what builds ammonia.

Clean with plain hot water. Soap and disinfectant can leave a residue the frog absorbs through its skin, so when something does need disinfecting, rinse it very well and dry it completely before it goes back. Refill the dish with dechlorinated water, never fresh tap water. Do the washing in a laundry sink or bathtub rather than the kitchen, and wash your hands afterward: a healthy frog can still carry Salmonella.

Once this setup is dialed in, this is a species that, unlike the [Pacman frog](/blog/pacman-frog-tank-setup-guide/), tolerates being picked up.

UVB is the one item on this list where the bulb distance matters as much as the bulb, and the [complete UVB lighting guide](/blog/uvb-lighting-complete-guide/) sets both by bulb type.
