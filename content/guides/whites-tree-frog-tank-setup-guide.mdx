# White's Tree Frog Tank Setup: What Your Frog Actually Needs

<AffiliateDisclosure />

White's tree frogs are a forgiving amphibian to set up correctly once you understand the one detail that trips up a lot of first-time keepers: humidity that's too constantly high causes more problems than humidity that dips a bit low between mistings. For the full care picture, see our complete [White's Tree Frog care guide](/guides/whites-tree-frog/).

## Enclosure Size

18 inches long by 18 inches wide by 24 inches tall is the standard minimum for one adult, sized for a vertical, arboreal species. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4vlb6Bp" product="18x18x24 in glass terrarium (arboreal)">20-gallon-equivalent enclosure</AffiliateLink> is comfortable for a single frog, and 24x18x24 inches works well for a small group of two to four.

## Temperature

Keep a gradient: cool side around 70 to 75°F, a basking area around 80 to 85°F, dropping into the mid-70s at night. A low-wattage bulb or a side-mounted heat mat provides the warmth, this species doesn't need intense heat, just a gentle, reliable gradient.

## Humidity: The Detail Worth Getting Right

Sources genuinely differ here, some recommend a higher constant range, but the more health-conscious consensus, echoed by exotic vets and amphibian specialists, favors a baseline around 50 to 60% with misting spikes up to 70 to 80% once or twice daily, then letting it drop back down between mistings. Constant, unbroken high humidity is directly linked to the bacterial and red-leg issues covered in our [health issues guide](/blog/whites-tree-frog-health-issues-guide/), so resist the instinct to keep things perpetually damp.

Use only <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3Rg5gTT" product="Water conditioner">dechlorinated tap water or spring water</AffiliateLink> for misting and drinking, never distilled or reverse-osmosis water.

## Substrate

Coarse orchid bark, coco fiber or coco husk, or a bioactive soil mix, kept lightly moist rather than soggy. Avoid fine, loose substrate that's easy to accidentally ingest during feeding.

## Lighting

Low-level UVB is recommended, this species survives without it but genuinely does better with it. A <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00M9ONY1W?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Zoo Med 5.0 T5-HO 14&quot; Terrarium Hood with ReptiSun T5 HO 5.0 UVB Lamp">low-output T5 bulb in the 5 to 7% range</AffiliateLink> works well, positioned so the basking branch sits at least 6 inches below the fixture.

<FunFact>
A T5 UVB bulb can keep glowing visibly long after its actual UVB output has crashed. That's exactly why the bulb gets replaced every 9 to 12 months on a schedule, regardless of whether it still looks like it's working.
</FunFact>

## Furnishings

Sturdy <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01F654UHM?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Zoo Med Natural Cork Rounds Medium (7&quot;-10&quot; Long) (3)">branches for climbing</AffiliateLink>, since this is an arboreal species that spends real time off the ground, along with <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4gmXC4j" product="Artificial/silk terrarium plants">broad leaves or platforms</AffiliateLink> for resting, and a shallow water dish that's easy to keep clean.
