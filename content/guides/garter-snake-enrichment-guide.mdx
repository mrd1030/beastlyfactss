# Garter Snake Enrichment: The One I Had to Correct

<AffiliateDisclosure />

This guide starts with a correction, because the mistake is common and we made it ourselves before checking.

<KeyTakeaway>
There is no garter snake enrichment research. The study usually credited to garter snakes is Almli and Burghardt (2006), and it was done on **ratsnakes**. *Elaphe* is the old genus name for what is now *Pantherophis*, so that paper belongs to the corn snakes, not to *Thamnophis*.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Research Says

Nothing, on *Thamnophis*.

The paper frequently attached to this species is **Environmental Enrichment Alters the Behavioral Profile of Ratsnakes (*Elaphe*)**, which found enriched-housed ratsnakes showed different behavioral profiles and performed differently on problem solving, feeding and field tasks. A genus rename is what makes it easy to misfile: *Elaphe* became *Pantherophis*, and *Pantherophis* is the corn snakes and ratsnakes.

The nearest usable evidence is therefore the colubrid work covered in our [Corn Snake enrichment guide](/blog/corn-snake-enrichment-guide/): given enclosures longer than their body, corn snakes **stretched out fully and preferred the larger enclosure**, added enrichment affected behavior and welfare measures, and enrichment altered odor discrimination performance.

<FunFact>
Garter snakes are among the few pet snakes that are genuinely diurnal active foragers rather than ambush predators. That makes them one of the better enrichment candidates in the group and one of the least studied, which is a frustrating combination.
</FunFact>

## Build an Enclosure They Can Hunt In

This is where garter snakes differ from every other snake on this site. A ball python needs somewhere to wait. A garter snake needs somewhere to search.

Dense low <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4grZYPq" product="Live terrarium plants">planting</AffiliateLink>, leaf litter, and varied ground texture give an active forager a landscape to work through. Scatter prey instead of presenting it, and vary where it goes.

## Water Big Enough to Swim In

Many garter snake populations are semi-aquatic and hunt in water. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eKyN0W" product="Large soak-able water dish">container large enough to swim in</AffiliateLink> rather than merely soak is a genuine enrichment item here in a way it is not for most snakes.

Keep it clean and stable, since a snake that swims in it will also foul it.

## Cover Across the Floor

Active does not mean fearless. <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3SKmfOx" product="Cork bark round hide">Cork tubes and flats</AffiliateLink>, leaf litter and planting mean the animal can forage across the whole enclosure without being exposed, which is what makes it actually use the space.

## Scent Trails

Chemosensory hunters that follow trails. Dragging prey across the substrate before offering, hiding food in new places, and introducing scent-marked objects all engage the sense this animal leads with, and none of it costs anything.

## Priority Order

1. Floor space and cover, so an active forager can actually forage
2. Scattered and varied prey presentation
3. A container large enough to swim in
4. Dense planting and varied ground texture
5. Scent trails
6. Rearrangement, last

## What Not to Do

Do not repeat the ratsnake misattribution, which is the reason this guide leads with it. Do not keep a diurnal active forager in a bare tub. Do not feed a group in the same space, since one snake taking another's prey is how garter snakes injure each other, covered in our health issues guide. Do not rely on a single prey item type, because dietary variety is unusually important in this species.
