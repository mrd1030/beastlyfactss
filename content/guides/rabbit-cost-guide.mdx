## Spay or Neuter: Budget This Separately

This is the line item new owners most often underestimate. Expect roughly $150 to $500 or more, a neuter typically runs $200 to $300, while a spay costs more since it's a more invasive procedure, and exotic or metro-area clinics charge on the higher end. Low-cost nonprofit programs sometimes bring this down to around $139 to $150.

Worth knowing: adoption fees, often just $25 to $100, frequently already include the spay or neuter plus an initial vet check. For many people, adopting an already-altered rabbit ends up being the cheapest overall path, not the pet store route.

<FunFact>
In some rabbit strains, 80% of unspayed females develop uterine cancer by 3 years of age. That is part of why spay/neuter is treated as close to mandatory in rabbit care, not a cost-saving nicety.
</FunFact>

Food and litter together add up to $62 to $135 a month. Routine vet care is $60 to $100 per visit with twice-yearly wellness checks recommended, so two exams add $120 to $200 a year, and the RHDV2 booster below adds $60 to $75 more. Together that makes a year after the first $924 to $1,895. The first year runs $1,219 to $2,697: setup at $125 to $197, a spay or neuter at $150 to $500, food and litter, two exams, and the $80 to $180 RHDV2 starter series. That first-year range leaves out the adoption fee.

## RHDV2 Vaccination: A Yearly Line Item

Rabbit hemorrhagic disease virus 2 has been spreading through US wild and pet rabbits since 2018, and vaccination is the main protection. A rabbit's first course is two doses 21 days apart, given from 4 weeks of age, and after that one booster a year. At rabbit welfare group clinics and exotic practices, the two-dose starter series runs about $80 to $180 and each annual booster about $60 to $75. The higher prices usually bundle a full exam, a nail trim or a microchip, so compare what each price includes.

Vaccine prices last checked September 2026 at House Rabbit Society chapter clinics and exotic practices in California, Connecticut, Massachusetts and Ohio. Prices vary by region; treat them as a snapshot.

## Large and Giant Breeds Cost More to Feed

The monthly table assumes an average-sized rabbit. Pellets are measured at about 1/4 cup and vegetables at 2 cups or more per 6 pounds of body weight, so an 18-pound giant eats about three times the pellets and vegetables of a 6-pound rabbit, and the fresh vegetable line can land well above the top of the range above. Hay is unlimited at any size, and a big rabbit gets through it faster.

## The Number That Catches People Off Guard

Rabbits need an exotic or [rabbit-savvy vet](/blog/rabbit-health-issues-guide/), not a standard small-animal clinic, and a single serious illness can be expensive. A [GI stasis](/blog/rabbit-gi-stasis-guide/) workup alone can exceed $1,500. Keep an emergency fund of at least $500 to $1,000 set aside separately from your routine budget, and more if you can, since one stasis workup can exceed it. Rabbits hide illness well, and when something does go wrong, it tends to go wrong quickly.
