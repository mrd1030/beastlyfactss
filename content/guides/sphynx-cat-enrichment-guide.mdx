# Sphynx Cat Enrichment: Warmth Is a Welfare Requirement

<AffiliateDisclosure />

The five pillars framework assumes a cat can regulate its own temperature. Remove the coat and the first pillar changes shape.

<KeyTakeaway>
For a sphynx, **a safe place has to also be a warm place**. That is not a comfort note, it is the thing that determines whether the cat does anything else at all. A cat spending its day chasing heat is not playing, exploring or using its territory.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Research Says

The framework is the **AAFP and ISFM Feline Environmental Needs Guidelines**, covered in full in our [cat enrichment guide](/blog/cat-enrichment-guide/): a safe place, multiple and separated key resources, opportunity for play and predatory behavior, positive and predictable human interaction, and an environment that respects the cat's sense of smell.

Nothing in it is breed-specific. What the sphynx adds is a **thermal constraint on the first pillar**, from the simple physical fact of having no insulating coat.

The guidelines' central claim, that environmental adequacy is linked to physical health rather than only to behavior, lands harder here than usual. A chronically cold cat is a cat under a physiological load.

<FunFact>
Sphynx cats are not truly hairless. They have a fine down that feels like suede and does essentially nothing for insulation, which is why the cat feels so warm to the touch: you are feeling skin temperature with nothing in the way.
</FunFact>

## Heat, Distributed Around the Home

The organizing decision. Not one warm bed, several warm places at different heights and in different rooms, so warmth and territory are not a trade-off.

Heated beds, covered and enclosed beds that trap the cat's own heat, warm spots near radiators, and a generally warmer ambient temperature than you would otherwise run. Clothing suits some sphynx cats and not others, and it is not a substitute for a warm environment.

Put the warm places **on the vertical route**, so the cat can be high, hidden and warm at once. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4vlaH1R" product="Cat tree / scratching post">cat tree</AffiliateLink> with an enclosed heated cubby at the top does all three pillars simultaneously.

## Sun, Which Is the Trap

Sphynx cats seek warmth and will lie in direct sun, and unpigmented skin burns. Sunburn in this breed is common and it is a skin cancer risk over time.

The fix is upstream: give enough indoor heat that the cat is not driven to the window. Then limit direct sun exposure, and use UV-filtering film on the windows the cat favors.

## Skin Care as Cooperative Training

Sphynx cats need a lot of handling: bathing, ear cleaning, nail fold cleaning, and dental care, since periodontal disease is common, and it is covered in the [periodontal and dental disease guide](/blog/periodontal-dental-disease-guide/).

Turn the maintenance into enrichment. That converts the most tedious part of owning this breed into something that serves the fourth pillar.

## The Standard Pillars, Still

Everything from the general guide applies. Key resources **separated** across the home, never stacked. One litter tray per cat plus one, sited apart. Several short **predatory play** sessions a day ending in a catch. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4vg6egE" product="Puzzle feeder">puzzle feeder</AffiliateLink> for part of the ration.

Sphynx cats are active and food-motivated and take to puzzle feeding readily. They also have a higher metabolic demand from heat loss, so feeding is not a place to be stingy, and weight still needs watching.

## Company

They have a reputation for being unusually social and a good deal of it is thermal: a cat that gets warmth from contact seeks contact.

Whatever the cause, they cope poorly with long isolation. Company, human or feline, is part of the plan, and a second cat provides both social contact and a heat source.

## Watch the Heart

Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy is over-represented in the breed, covered in the [cardiomyopathy in cats and dogs guide](/blog/cardiomyopathy-in-cats-and-dogs-guide/).

That belongs in an enrichment guide because it bears on activity. Regular screening, and if a cat tires unusually or breathes hard after play, that is a veterinary question rather than a fitness one.

## Priority Order

1. Multiple warm places, at height and across rooms
2. Enough indoor warmth that the cat is not driven into direct sun
3. Sun limitation and UV film on favored windows
4. Cooperative care training for bathing, ears, nails and teeth
5. Key resources separated, with one litter tray per cat plus one
6. Short daily predatory play ending in a catch, plus puzzle feeding
7. Company over long isolation, and cardiac screening

## What Not to Do

Do not provide one warm bed and consider the thermal question answered. Do not let a sphynx sunbathe unrestricted. Do not force handling for skin care instead of training it. Do not stack the resources in one corner. Do not under-feed a cat losing heat continuously. Do not skip dental care in a breed prone to periodontal disease. And do not push a tiring cat into more play without ruling out the heart.
