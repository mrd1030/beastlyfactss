# Ball Python Tank Setup: Humidity Is the Detail That Makes or Breaks It

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Ball python husbandry has shifted over the past several years, bigger enclosures and more clutter are now the standard, replacing the old advice about keeping them in small tubs.

<Figure src="/assets/images/ball-python-tank-setup-guide-1.jpg" alt="Labeled diagram of a ball python enclosure showing a PVC enclosure, thermostat-controlled heat, and heavy clutter" caption="Bigger enclosures and more clutter are now the standard, replacing the old advice about keeping them in small tubs." shareable />

## Enclosure Size

The current standard for an adult is a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4f8WVcx" product="4x2x2 ft PVC enclosure">4x2x2 ft (48x24x24 inches, roughly 120 gallons, about 8 square feet of floor space)</AffiliateLink> setup, with the enclosure at least as long as the snake itself. Scale down for younger snakes: hatchlings up to about 300 grams do well in a 10-gallon or 20x11x13 inch enclosure, and juveniles under 3 feet can move into a 36x18x18 inch setup.

Bigger, more furnished enclosures are now recommended over the old "small tub reduces stress" approach. PVC is the better material choice for this species since it holds humidity far more effectively than glass, if you do go with glass, cover three sides to help retain moisture. Whatever material you choose, make sure it's secure and locking, ball pythons are excellent escape artists. House one snake per enclosure.

Every single heat source needs to run through <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/44hAxJ1" product="Under-tank heat mat + thermostat kit">a thermostat</AffiliateLink>, no exceptions. This isn't a suggestion, it's what prevents burns and fire hazards, which is the whole reason [reptile thermostats](/blog/reptile-heating-thermostats-guide/) exist. Skip hot rocks entirely.

## Humidity: The Part That Determines Success

This is where most ball python problems start. Keep ambient humidity at 55 to 65%, and raise it to 70 to 80% specifically during shedding cycles. Too dry leads to bad sheds and raises respiratory infection risk. Too wet without adequate ventilation swings the other way, toward scale rot and respiratory issues from the opposite direction.

A few things that help maintain a stable range: a moisture-retaining substrate, an appropriately sized water bowl, a dedicated humid hide packed with <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4bpnvNm" product="Sphagnum moss (for moist hide)">damp sphagnum moss</AffiliateLink>, and periodic misting when needed. Check humidity with <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3QHrM81" product="Digital thermometer/hygrometer combo">a real digital hygrometer</AffiliateLink>, not a cheap analog dial, those are notoriously inaccurate.

<FunFact>
A ball python enclosure can look perfectly fine at a glance, correct temperature, clean substrate, a snake that seems fine, while quietly running too dry. Humidity problems tend to show up weeks later as a bad shed rather than immediately, which is exactly why a real digital hygrometer rather than a guess is worth having.
</FunFact>

## UVB: Not Required, But Not Without Benefit Either

Ball pythons have been kept successfully for decades with zero UVB, so it's genuinely optional. That said, providing a low-level UVB gradient is a reasonable piece of enrichment if you want to include it. Separately, run a 12-hour light, 12-hour dark photoperiod using an LED on a timer, and avoid bright white or colored bulbs at night, a ceramic heat emitter is the better choice if you need supplemental night heat.

## Substrate

<AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4vNgpe1" product="Cypress mulch substrate">Cypress mulch</AffiliateLink> or coconut coir/husk, 3 to 4 inches deep, are the best choices for holding humidity at the level this species needs. Paper towels work fine temporarily, for [quarantine setups](/blog/reptile-quarantine-guide/), for example, where a bare floor is the point, but won't hold humidity well long-term. Avoid aspen entirely, it tends to mold at the humidity level ball pythons require. And never use pine or cedar, both contain aromatic oils that are toxic to reptiles.

## Furnishings

Provide at least two hides, one on the warm side and one on the cool side, and size them snugly. Ball pythons feel most secure in a hide where their body is touching the sides on multiple points, not floating in open space. Add a heavy, tip-resistant water bowl large enough for the snake to soak in when it wants to.

Clutter matters more for this species than people expect. Plants, branches, and leaf litter aren't just decoration, ball pythons are naturally shy, and an enclosure that feels too open and exposed causes chronic stress, which shows up as feeding refusal and general skittishness. Add sturdy, well-anchored branches too, braced firmly enough that a snake can push against one without it shifting, and they will use them.

## Cleaning

Snake urates and feces soil a lot of substrate at once, so spot clean as soon as waste appears, scooping out the wet substrate around it. Change the water daily, since the snake soaks in it. Once a week, scrub the water bowl and any soiled decor with a reptile habitat cleaner or a 3% bleach solution left on for at least 10 minutes, then rinse until no smell remains and let everything dry.

Replace the whole substrate monthly if the snake is messy, and at least once a quarter even if it is not. At each full change, move the snake to a secure tub, strip the enclosure, disinfect every surface the same way, and let it dry completely before new substrate goes in. Wash hands after every clean and never rinse the bowl in a kitchen sink, the two [Salmonella rules](/blog/reptile-salmonella-hygiene-guide/) that hold for every reptile.

## Diet, Briefly

Ball pythons are carnivores, fed appropriately sized frozen-thawed rodents with tongs, directly in the enclosure rather than in a separate feeding tub, since that added stress is a common cause of regurgitation. Prey size, the schedule by age, and how to thaw and warm a rodent so a snake will actually take it are all in the [ball python feeding guide](/blog/ball-python-feeding-guide/). Resist the urge to overfeed, obesity is a real and common issue in captive ball pythons, and [weighing the snake](/blog/snake-sexing-growth-body-condition-guide/) catches it earlier than eyeballing it does.
