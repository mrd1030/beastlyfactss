# Red-Eared Slider Feeding: Schedule, Diet, and Why They Stop Eating

<AffiliateDisclosure />

A red-eared slider's diet shifts as it grows, and it can only swallow underwater.

## Feeding schedule

Juveniles eat daily. Adults eat every 2 to 3 days. Offer what the turtle finishes in about 15 to 20 minutes, then pull leftovers. Sliders will overeat an open-ended pile, and leftover food fouls the water. For a juvenile, a portion about the size of its own head, or whatever it clears in 10 to 15 minutes, is the tighter cue.

## What red-eared sliders eat

Sliders are omnivores. The split is a shift, not a percentage: more animal protein while growing, more plants as an adult. Do not keep an adult on a hatchling protein load. That is how you get pyramiding and a fat turtle.

**Commercial pellets** made for aquatic turtles are the staple. Larger floating pellets for adults, smaller sinking pellets for juveniles. **Animal protein:** earthworms, snails, crickets, mealworms, and occasional small amounts of cooked, never raw, chicken or beef. Live feeder fish can carry parasites; use caution. **Plant matter:** dark leafy greens, romaine, collard, mustard, kale, dandelion, shredded carrot, squash, green beans. Fresh first. Frozen vegetables work occasionally.

## Safe treats and foods to avoid

Safe: kale, collard, mustard, and dandelion greens, carrots and carrot tops, squash, green beans, berries, melon, apple in moderation, earthworms, crickets, and gut-loaded feeder insects.

Avoid: avocado, onion, and rhubarb. Iceberg lettuce is empty. Skip dairy. Skip bread and pasta. Raw grocery-store meat and anything wild-caught carry parasite risk. Spinach binds calcium if it is a staple; keep it occasional.

## Why red-eared sliders stop eating

1. **Brumation.** Cold-season slowdown, roughly October through March. Indoor sliders kept warm often do not fully brumate, so refusal in a warm tank is more likely something else.
2. **Water or basking temperature too low.** Target roughly 75 to 80°F water with an 85 to 95°F basking spot. Water below about 72°F slows metabolism enough that digestion essentially stops.
3. **Stress from a new or changed environment.** A week or two is common.
4. **Poor water quality.** High ammonia or nitrite from an undersized filter or skipped water changes.
5. **Shedding or pickiness.** Less eating while shedding scutes, or a new food they do not recognize.
6. **Gravidity.** A female carrying eggs often loses her appetite. A week or more past expected laying while weak or straining is egg binding.
7. **Illness.** Refusal plus wheezing, nasal discharge, swollen eyelids, or abnormal floating.

## When to worry

Outside of brumation, 2 to 3 weeks of total refusal is the vet line, sooner if warning signs appear. Appetite loss plus lethargy in a warm, stable tank is not brumation.

See a vet regardless of duration if refusal comes with wheezing, clicking, open-mouth breathing, swollen or closed eyes, floating unevenly, a soft or discolored shell, or significant lethargy at proper temperature. A gravid female straining without eggs, or weak a week past expected laying, is an emergency.
