# Blue-Tongued Skink Enrichment: Mealworms Made Them Hide Less

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Most enrichment studies measure one thing. This one changed three, and the third is the one worth building around.

<KeyTakeaway>
Nine eastern blue-tongue lizards, housed with a brick to bask on and a pipe to hide in, were given feeding enrichment. Mealworms increased time spent feeding and increased liveweight gain. They also reduced time spent hiding. One intervention, three measured outcomes.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the study found

The paper *Environmental enrichment for captive eastern blue-tongue lizards (Tiliqua scincoides)* looked at nine animals kept individually in cages with a brick for basking and a pipe for hiding.

Mealworms **increased the time the lizards spent feeding** and **increased liveweight gain**. They also **reduced the time the lizards spent hiding**.

<FunFact>
A skink that spends its day in a hide is usually called settled or shy. Here, less hiding traveled with more feeding. That reads as an animal that found something to do, not one that stopped being nervous.
</FunFact>

## Move the food off the plate

A bowl of chopped salad is a two-minute event for an animal that forages across ground for hours.

Scatter food across the substrate. Hide portions under cork and leaf litter. Vary where the food appears.

Mealworms were the study prey. They are fatty. Use them as the enrichment item, not as the everyday protein. The [feeding guide](/blog/blue-tongue-skink-feeding-guide/) holds the portion and the schedule.

## Floor space and substrate

Blue-tongues are terrestrial. Footprint beats height. A tall enclosure adds almost nothing. Floor space is what makes scatter feeding possible.

Give them enough <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4vNgpe1" product="Cypress mulch substrate">cypress mulch</AffiliateLink> to push through and dig into.

## A dig box

A tub of damp diggable material lets a skink excavate without converting the whole floor. A storage tub is the product.

## Cover that is not just one pipe

The study animals had a single pipe. That was the baseline, not a target. Multiple <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00SLM5GWQ?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Exo Terra Reptile Cave XL">large hides</AffiliateLink> at both ends of the gradient, plus cork and leaf litter, mean the animal can be concealed at any temperature it wants.

## Priority order

1. Scatter and varied feeding presentation, the part with a study behind it
2. Floor space, since it is what makes foraging possible
3. Substrate deep enough to dig into, plus a dedicated dig box
4. Multiple hides across the thermal gradient
5. Scent novelty
6. Target training
7. Novel objects, last

## What not to do

Do not bowl-feed a foraging omnivore and call the enclosure enriched. Do not build up when you could build out. Do not read a permanently hidden skink as a calm one. Do not overfeed while making food more interesting: liveweight gain was a study outcome and these animals get obese easily. Hold the portion in the feeding guide steady while you change the presentation. Do not rely on handling for stimulation.
