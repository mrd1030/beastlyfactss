# Blue Tongue Skink Enrichment: Mealworms Made Them Hide Less

Most enrichment studies measure one thing. This one changed three, and the third is the one worth building an article around. For everything else about the species, see our complete [Blue Tongue Skink care guide](/guides/blue-tongue-skink/).

<KeyTakeaway>
Nine eastern blue-tongue lizards, housed with a brick to bask on and a pipe to hide in, were given feeding enrichment. Mealworms increased time spent feeding and increased liveweight gain. They also reduced time spent hiding. One intervention, three measured outcomes.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Research Actually Says

The study looked at environmental enrichment for captive eastern blue-tongue lizards (*Tiliqua scincoides*), a species kept in zoos and increasingly common as a pet. Nine animals were kept individually in cages furnished with a brick for basking and a pipe for hiding.

Mealworms **increased the time the lizards spent feeding** and **increased liveweight gain**. They also **reduced the time the lizards spent hiding**.

<FunFact>
Reduced hiding is the result that changes how you read your own animal. A skink that spends its day in a hide is usually described as settled or shy. Here, less hiding travelled together with more feeding, which reads as an animal that found something to do rather than one that stopped being nervous.
</FunFact>

There is also a review of welfare assessment methods in reptiles that applied the Welfare Quality protocol to the endangered pygmy blue-tongue skink, useful for framing how thin reptile welfare measurement still is compared with mammals.

## Move the Food Off the Plate

This is the finding, applied. A bowl of chopped salad is a two minute event for an animal that forages across ground for hours in the wild.

Scatter food across the substrate. Hide portions under cork and leaf litter. Use <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0FB3PRD2Y?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Short soft-tip feeding tongs, 4 pack">soft-tipped tongs</AffiliateLink> to make prey items move rather than sit. Vary where the food appears from day to day so the search is real rather than habitual. Our [feeding guide](/blog/blue-tongue-skink-feeding-guide/) covers the diet composition that all of this is delivering.

## Floor Space and Substrate

Blue tongues are terrestrial and cover ground, so footprint beats height every time. A tall enclosure adds almost nothing for this species, and floor space is what makes scatter feeding possible at all.

Give them enough <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4vNgpe1" product="Cypress mulch substrate">cypress mulch</AffiliateLink> to push through and dig into rather than walk across. Other bag sizes are available and a decent depth wants the larger one. Our [tank setup guide](/blog/blue-tongue-skink-tank-setup-guide/) covers the gradient and humidity.

## A Dig Box

Separate from general substrate depth: a tub of damp diggable material gives a skink somewhere to excavate properly without you converting the whole floor. There is no purpose-made product for this, because a storage tub is the product.

## Cover That Is Not Just One Pipe

The study animals had a single pipe. That is the baseline the enrichment was added on top of, not a target. Multiple <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00SLM5GWQ?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Exo Terra Reptile Cave XL">large hides</AffiliateLink> at both ends of the gradient, plus cork and leaf litter breaking up the floor, mean the animal can be concealed at any temperature it wants.

## Priority Order

1. Scatter and varied feeding presentation, the part with a study behind it
2. Floor space, since it is what makes foraging possible
3. Substrate deep enough to dig into, plus a dedicated dig box
4. Multiple hides across the thermal gradient
5. Scent novelty, which suits a species that leads with tongue-flicking
6. Target training
7. Novel objects, last

## What Not to Do

Do not bowl-feed a foraging omnivore and then call the enclosure enriched. Do not build up rather than out, because height is close to wasted on a terrestrial lizard. Do not read a permanently hidden skink as a calm one, since hiding is what the study's less-enriched condition produced. Do not overfeed while making food more interesting, because liveweight gain was a study outcome and these animals get obese easily, which our [health issues guide](/blog/blue-tongue-skink-health-issues-guide/) covers. And do not rely on handling for stimulation.
