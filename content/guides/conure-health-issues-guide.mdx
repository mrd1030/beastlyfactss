# Common Green Cheek Conure Health Issues and What Causes Them

<VetDisclaimer />
<AffiliateDisclosure />

Most health problems in this species trace back to diet, environment, or a lack of mental stimulation rather than pure genetics. Get those three things right and this is a genuinely resilient bird. See our [Green Cheek Conure cage setup guide](/blog/conure-tank-setup-guide/) for the husbandry side, and our full [Green Cheek Conure care guide](/guides/conure/) for everything else.

## Nutritional Disorders

Fatty liver disease (hepatic lipidosis) and vitamin A deficiency both stem from the same root cause: an all-seed or high-fat diet with too little vegetable variety and not enough exercise. Watch for obesity, lethargy, unusual or green-tinted droppings, a swollen abdomen, and poor feather quality.

Advanced liver disease is life-threatening and needs veterinary care. Seed-only diets are one of the most common, and most preventable, causes of chronic illness in this species, covered from the budgeting side in our [Green Cheek Conure cost guide](/blog/conure-cost-guide/).

## Feather-Destructive Behavior

Plucking or chewing at feathers, especially on the chest, legs, or wings, often with visible skin irritation. Boredom, stress, hormonal changes, and poor diet all contribute, and underlying medical issues, allergies, skin disease, or systemic illness, need to be ruled out by an avian vet before assuming it's purely behavioral.

## Respiratory Infections and Aspergillosis

Sneezing, nasal discharge, labored breathing, a change in voice, lethargy, and tail-bobbing are all signs. Poor ventilation, a dusty or moldy environment or food source, stress, and vitamin A deficiency weakening the immune system all raise risk. A roomy, well-ventilated <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4wuT808" product="Large bird flight cage">flight cage</AffiliateLink> kept clean helps reduce that risk from the environment side.

This can progress quickly, treat it as potentially serious and see a vet promptly. Aspergillosis specifically can be genuinely difficult to clear once established.

## Viral Diseases

Psittacine Beak and Feather Disease (PBFD), Proventricular Dilatation Disease (PDD), and polyomavirus are the ones to know. Signs vary by virus: abnormal feather or beak growth, weight loss, regurgitation, neurological symptoms, or in severe cases sudden death.

These are contagious, spreading through new birds, contaminated equipment, or breeding facilities. Prevention through quarantine, testing new birds, and sourcing from reputable breeders is critical, there's no reliable cure for most of these.

## Hypocalcemia and Metabolic Bone Issues

Less common in this species than in larger parrots like African greys, but possible, tied to seed-heavy diets low in calcium and insufficient UV exposure. Severe cases can show weakness, tremors, or seizures.

## Emergencies vs. Manageable at Home

Difficulty breathing, an inability to perch, severe lethargy, seizures, significant bleeding, or a sudden refusal to eat all mean an immediate trip to an avian vet. Mild dietary adjustments, increasing enrichment for early-stage feather issues, and minor cuts, with proper first-aid knowledge, are generally manageable at home. Annual wellness exams remain the best early-warning system for this species, birds are good at masking illness until it's fairly advanced.

## The Husbandry Pattern Worth Remembering

An all-seed diet leads directly to fatty liver disease and vitamin deficiencies. Insufficient mental or physical stimulation, or isolation, leads to feather plucking and screaming, more on that pattern in our [Green Cheek Conure handling guide](/blog/conure-handling-guide/). Poor cage hygiene or moldy food leads to respiratory fungal infections. And skipping quarantine for new birds is how contagious viruses get introduced.
