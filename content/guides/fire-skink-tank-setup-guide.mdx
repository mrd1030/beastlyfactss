# Fire Skink Enclosure Setup: Deep, Moist Substrate Above Everything Else

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Fire skink care comes down to one central skill more than any other: keeping deep substrate genuinely, permanently moist for a species that spends most of its time burrowed within it. For the full care picture, see our complete [Fire Skink care guide](/guides/fire-skink/), and for what it all costs, our [cost guide](/blog/fire-skink-cost-guide/).

## Enclosure Size

36 inches long by 18 inches wide by 18 inches tall as a minimum for a single adult, larger is genuinely worth it since this is an active species. Floor space matters more than height. Don't house multiple fire skinks together, especially two males, real fighting risk.

## Temperature

Ambient 80 to 86°F, with a basking spot around 92 to 96°F, and a night drop to about 70°F, 65°F is acceptable. A basking bulb, halogen works well, provides daytime heat, and some keepers add an under-tank heater on the warm side as well.

## Humidity

60 to 70% ambient, higher overnight. Achieved through misting once or twice daily, keeping the substrate itself consistently moist, and providing a humid hide lined with sphagnum moss. An <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4fibBY2" product="Automatic misting system/fogger">automated misting system or fogger</AffiliateLink> is a genuinely useful addition at night if you want to maintain humidity without constant manual misting. Monitor with a digital probe hygrometer rather than guessing, this connects directly to the respiratory infection and shedding problems covered in the [health issues guide](/blog/fire-skink-health-issues-guide/).

## Substrate: The Single Most Important Decision Here

This is worth real attention since it's genuinely the defining requirement for this fossorial species. Provide 4 to 6 inches of a loose, moisture-retentive tropical mix, a DIY blend of roughly 40% topsoil, 40% coconut fiber, and 20% fine sand works well, topped with leaf litter or sphagnum moss. The substrate needs to stay damp, not waterlogged, and needs to hold a burrow shape. Avoid dry, sand-only setups entirely, they simply don't support how this species actually lives.

<FunFact>
Reptiles Magazine frames the fire skink as a lower-UVI species (Ferguson Zone 2, UVI 0.7 to 1.0) while other husbandry sources target UVI 3.0 to 4.0 at the basking area, a real disagreement worth knowing about. The 5 to 6% linear T5 recommendation below sits toward the more measured, detailed end of that range.
</FunFact>

## Lighting

UVB offers real benefit though is technically optional if diet is properly supplemented. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3TiqeC4" product="UVB - Arcadia Forest 6% (ProT5 kit, 36in/39W)">lower-output UVB tube in the 5 to 6% range</AffiliateLink>, positioned so the basking area sits 7 to 10 inches below the lamp, replaced every 12 months, is a reasonable, well-supported choice.

## The Non-Obvious Requirement: Build for a Species You Won't Always See

Because this is a burrowing animal, a fire skink kept in a sparse, minimally furnished enclosure will simply stay hidden most of the time, not because something's wrong, but because there's nowhere secure to be visible. Deep substrate, real cover, and permanent moisture aren't just about physical health, they're what actually lets you see and enjoy this genuinely striking lizard rather than staring at empty substrate.
