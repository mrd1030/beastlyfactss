# Common Ackie Monitor Health Issues and What Causes Them

<VetDisclaimer />
<AffiliateDisclosure />

Ackie monitors are hardy animals, and the health issues that do show up trace back almost entirely to a small number of preventable husbandry gaps. See our [tank setup guide](/blog/ackie-monitor-tank-setup-guide/) for the husbandry side of prevention, and our full [Ackie Monitor care guide](/guides/ackie-monitor/) for everything else.

## Metabolic Bone Disease (MBD)

ReptiFiles describes this plainly as a highly preventable disease of neglect, one a properly kept ackie should never actually develop. Caused by inadequate UVB, insufficient calcium, or both together. Watch for swollen limbs, soft or malformed bones, lethargy, and difficulty moving normally. Always see a vet, and treat correct <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3TipZXE" product="UVB - Arcadia Desert 12% (ProT5 kit, 22in/24W)">UVB</AffiliateLink> and calcium supplementation as non-negotiable from day one, this is genuinely one of the more avoidable conditions on this list.

## Obesity

A captivity-specific risk for this species. Ackies are naturally so active that they burn far more energy in the wild than most captive setups allow for, combined with their strong food motivation, this creates real overfeeding risk. The fix, per ReptiFiles, is straightforward: reduce feeding frequency and amount, provide a genuinely large enclosure that encourages movement, and weigh your monitor weekly with a kitchen scale to track trends. See a vet if a month of adjustment doesn't produce any weight change.

<FunFact>
Ackie monitors are active enough in the wild to burn through far more energy than most captive setups ever ask of them, which is exactly why obesity, not a disease or parasite, is one of the most common health issues keepers actually see in this species. A genuinely large enclosure and a weekly kitchen scale check do more preventive work here than almost anything else on this list.
</FunFact>

## Internal and External Parasites

Decreased appetite, lethargy, weight loss, and irregular stool are the signs to watch for. Quarantine any new ackie until it's cleared two separate clean fecal checks before introducing it to an established collection, and maintain good general hygiene. See a vet for diagnosis and treatment.

## Impaction

Can become a genuine emergency if severe. Linked to inappropriate substrate ingestion or temperatures too low to support proper digestion, best monitored with an <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4flGgUg" product="Infrared temp gun">infrared thermometer</AffiliateLink> rather than guesswork. This connects directly to the substrate and temperature choices covered in the [tank setup guide](/blog/ackie-monitor-tank-setup-guide/).

## Respiratory Infection

Linked to incorrect temperature or humidity. Always see a vet.

## The Husbandry Pattern Worth Remembering

Weak or absent UVB, combined with poor calcium supplementation, is the direct cause of MBD, the most serious and most preventable condition on this list. An enclosure too small to support this species' natural activity level contributes directly to obesity. And getting temperature and substrate right, covered in full in the [tank setup guide](/blog/ackie-monitor-tank-setup-guide/), prevents both impaction and digestive issues. Nearly everything here comes back to building the ambitious setup this species actually needs.
