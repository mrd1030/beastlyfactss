# How to Handle an Ackie Monitor

Ackie monitors are a genuinely different kind of lizard to interact with, compared to the sit-and-bask species already covered on this site. If you've kept a bearded dragon or a blue-tongued skink, expect this species to feel like a completely different level of energy. For the full care picture, see our complete [Ackie Monitor care guide](/guides/ackie-monitor/).

## What Makes This Species Different

Ackies are active, bold, intelligent, and strongly food-motivated, keepers frequently describe them doing something close to backflips while chasing crickets. Where a [bearded dragon](/blog/bearded-dragon-handling-guide/) tolerates passive handling and a [blue-tongued skink](/blog/blue-tongue-skink-handling-guide/) stays calm and ground-bound, an ackie is busy, curious, and constantly investigating its environment. This is a genuinely more engaging lizard to keep, but it's also a more demanding one.

## The Right Way to Build Trust

Never grab an ackie from above or chase it out of a hide, this triggers a real prey-flight response rather than building trust. Let the monitor voluntarily climb onto you instead, and use tong-feeding as your main bonding tool, offering a feeder insect as a reward for approaching or climbing on calmly. Tame ackies become genuinely interactive over time, but they're also fast and can scratch or nip with sharp teeth and claws if startled or handled carelessly.

<FunFact>
Keepers frequently describe ackie monitors doing something close to backflips while chasing down a cricket. It's a level of food-driven energy that's hard to picture until you've actually watched a lizard do it, and it's a big part of why this species reads as so much more interactive than a typical pet lizard.
</FunFact>

## First-Timer Mistakes

- Undersizing the enclosure, which leaves an animal this active with nowhere to burn energy.
- Providing shallow substrate that can't hold a proper burrow, undermining a core part of this species' natural behavior.
- Chasing or grabbing to force handling instead of letting the monitor approach on its own terms.
- Overfeeding into obesity, easy to do with such a food-motivated animal.

## How This Compares to More Sedentary Lizards

This is really the central fact to understand before choosing an ackie. Bearded dragons and blue-tongued skinks are calm, largely stationary lizards well suited to relatively passive interaction. An ackie is neither calm nor stationary, it's an active, intelligent predator that needs genuine enrichment and space to express that energy. That's exactly why ackies are classed as an intermediate reptile rather than a true beginner one, the temperament itself isn't difficult, but the husbandry demands that come with supporting it genuinely are.
