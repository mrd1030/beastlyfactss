# How Much Does Cardinal Tetra Ownership Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Cardinal tetras cost a little more than neon tetras to buy and about the same to keep, and the budgeting variable is the same one: this is a schooling fish, so you're never pricing out a single animal.

## The Fish: $3.30 to $8 Each

Tank-bred cardinal tetras run about $3.30 to $8 a fish from online sellers, a little less per fish in a larger pack. Since this is a schooling species, you're buying a group, not one fish. A starting school of 10, the number this species needs to settle, runs about $35 to $80 before shipping. Eight is the least to buy.

That is noticeably more than ten [neon tetra](/blog/neon-tetra-cost-guide/), which sell for $1 to $3 each. It's worth budgeting for rather than discovering after the fact.

<FunFact>
Cardinal tetra sometimes prices a touch higher than neon tetra, and there's a supply-chain reason behind it. Neon tetra is bred at scale and rarely wild-caught. Cardinal tetra is harder to breed at commercial scale, so a meaningful share of the trade still comes from a documented wild fishery on Brazil's Rio Negro. Cardinal tetra alone has made up roughly 80% of the ornamental fish exported from that region.
</FunFact>

Food and conditioner are low, close to what [neon tetra](/blog/neon-tetra-cost-guide/) costs to run. The test kit is the one item worth budgeting for on purpose: cardinal tetras are sensitive to nitrite and need ammonia at zero, both of which show up in an incompletely cycled tank. Confirming your cycle before stocking, not after, is the cheapest insurance this species has, and [what a finished cycle reads on a test kit](/blog/aquarium-cycling-guide/) is a different question from how long the tank has been running.

## Vet Costs

Not applicable in the traditional sense. Fish health management here is entirely about water quality and, when needed, over-the-counter treatments, not veterinary visits.

## Lifespan

A well-kept cardinal tetra lives about five years in captivity under good, stable conditions. That is dramatically longer than what this species gets in the wild, where it lives about a year in its native blackwater streams and wild fish probably die after spawning. An aquarium with stable water is an easier life than the one this fish evolved for.

## Why the School Sets the Bill

Cardinal tetra and neon tetra are both priced per fish, so the schooling requirement sets the total. Budgeting for a full group from the start, rather than starting with two or three and adding more later, is both the more humane approach and the more cost-effective one, since an understocked, stressed school is more prone to health issues, which cost more to correct than simply stocking correctly on day one.
