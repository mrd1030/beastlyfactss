# Common Jackson's Chameleon Health Issues

<VetDisclaimer />

Jackson's chameleons face many of the same core reptile health risks as the [veiled chameleon](/blog/veiled-chameleon-health-issues-guide/) already covered on this site, with a couple of details specific to this species worth knowing. Good husbandry, covered fully in the [tank setup guide](/blog/jacksons-chameleon-tank-setup-guide/), prevents most of what follows. This species is also part of our recent [Jackson's Chameleon, Canary, Giant Millipede & Corydoras Catfish compared](/blog/jacksonschameleon-canary-millipede-corydoras-overview/) roundup.

## Metabolic Bone Disease (MBD)

Lethargy, rubbery or bent bones, swollen limbs, and tremors. Caused by inadequate calcium, vitamin D3, or UVB exposure. Always see a vet, early cases respond considerably better to treatment than advanced ones.

## Dehydration

Genuinely the most common problem in this species, as with most chameleons, which won't reliably drink from a standing water dish. Watch for sunken eyes and lethargy. Manageable at home early, increase misting and dripper output immediately. See a vet if your chameleon doesn't respond or shows real lethargy. The [tank setup guide](/blog/jacksons-chameleon-tank-setup-guide/) covers the humidity and misting numbers that prevent this in the first place.

<FunFact>
Chameleons, Jackson's included, don't reliably recognize still water in a dish as something to drink. They're wired to respond to moving droplets instead, which is exactly why dehydration tops this list and why a misting or dripper setup matters so much more here than a water bowl ever could.
</FunFact>

## Gout and Kidney Disease

From chronic dehydration, overfeeding, or a diet too high in protein, leading to uric acid buildup. Watch for swollen joints. Always see a vet, and know that full resolution can be genuinely difficult once this develops, prevention through proper hydration and diet matters more than treatment here.

## Edema

Jackson's chameleons are particularly prone to this specific issue, fluid-filled sacs developing under the throat, neck, or chest. Often linked to over-supplementing vitamin A or D. See a vet, and review your supplement routine, more isn't always better with this species.

## Vitamin A Deficiency

Chameleons don't convert plant-based beta-carotene into usable vitamin A very effectively and need a source of preformed vitamin A in their diet. Watch for eye problems, poor coordination, a weakening grip, and respiratory issues. Always see a vet.

## The Husbandry Pattern Worth Remembering

Insufficient misting causes the dehydration that's the single most common issue in this species. Weak UVB and calcium cause MBD. Overfeeding and dehydration together drive gout. And oversupplementing, specifically vitamin A or D, can cause edema, a genuinely species-specific risk worth watching for. Getting hydration and supplementation calibrated correctly, not just present, prevents most of what's on this list.
