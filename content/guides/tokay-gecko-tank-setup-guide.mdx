# Tokay Gecko Enclosure Setup: Warm, Vertical, and Genuinely Humid

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Tokay gecko care runs warm and humid, closer to a rainforest setup than the arid conditions many other geckos prefer.

<Figure src="/assets/images/tokay-gecko-tank-setup-guide-1.jpg" alt="Labeled diagram of a tokay gecko enclosure showing grouped basking and UVB fixtures, full-height climbing structures, and high humidity misting" caption="Tokay gecko care runs warm and humid, closer to a rainforest setup than the arid conditions many other geckos prefer." shareable />

## Enclosure Size

Published minimums for one adult run from a 20 gallon tank to 24 by 24 by 48 inches tall. Build toward the larger: 18 inches long by 18 inches wide by 36 inches tall is the least to buy, and 24 by 24 by 48 is the target. A particularly large tokay needs more again, 36 by 24 by 48. A juvenile can start in as little as a 10 gallon tank, but it grows into the adult enclosure, and moving a defensive gecko is its own job, so setting up the adult size from the start saves a move. This is an arboreal species, so vertical space matters more than floor space. House one gecko per enclosure unless you are deliberately setting up a [pair](/blog/tokay-gecko-enrichment-guide/), since tokays are highly territorial and two males will fight.

## Temperature

Basking area 90 to 105F, cooler zone 80 to 85F, and nighttime should stay warmer than many homes naturally run, 75 to 80F, not dropping below about 70F. Use a halogen basking bulb during the day, and pair it with a lightless <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0002AQCQO?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Zoo Med Repticare Infrared Ceramic Heat Emitter (150 watt)">ceramic heat emitter</AffiliateLink> or deep heat projector on a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08R29S2WB?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Exo Terra Dimming and Pulse Proportional Thermostat for Reptile Terrariums">thermostat</AffiliateLink> for overnight warmth, this species genuinely needs supplemental night heat more than most.

## Humidity: 60 to 80%, Not a Desert Setup

60 to 80% is the target range, considerably more humid than what suits a desert species. Achieve this with daily or twice-daily misting using a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4vPKMjX" product="Fine mist spray bottle">fine mist spray bottle</AffiliateLink>, moisture-retentive substrate, <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4bpnvNm" product="Sphagnum moss (for moist hide)">sphagnum moss</AffiliateLink>, and leaf litter. Good ventilation matters here too, high humidity without airflow invites mold.

<FunFact>
A tokay gecko's basking area can run as hot as 105F, while its nighttime enclosure still needs to stay warmer than most homes naturally run, 75 to 80F, a combination that makes this one of the more demanding heating setups among commonly kept geckos.
</FunFact>

## Substrate

Coconut fiber or a bioactive-style mix, at least 2 to 3 inches deep, with moss and leaf litter layered on top. Avoid pine and cedar entirely, both release fumes that are toxic to reptiles.

## Lighting

As a nocturnal species, tokays can technically survive without UVB if their diet supplies enough vitamin D3, but [low-level UVB](/blog/uvb-lighting-complete-guide/), a 5% or forest-strength T5 bulb, offers real benefit. Replace the bulb every 6 to 12 months. Keep nights genuinely dark.

## Diet Basics

Tokay geckos are insectivores, and there is no powdered fruit diet for them the way there is for a crested gecko. Gut-loaded crickets, dubia roaches, and similar feeder insects make up the diet, with nothing offered wider than the gecko's own head. Juveniles eat daily and full-grown adults every other day, within a feeding window of about five minutes rather than food left standing in the enclosure. Dust feeders with calcium and vitamin D3, and keep fresh, dechlorinated water in a shallow dish at all times. What the insects themselves ate in the days before the feeding does real nutritional work, and [gut-loading the feeders](/blog/gut-loading-feeder-insects-guide/) is where that work happens.

## Keeping It Clean

A warm, humid enclosure invites mold, so the routine runs tighter than it does for a desert gecko. Spot clean every day, removing droppings and uneaten insects, and wash the water dish and any food dish daily. Clean and disinfect the enclosure thoroughly at least once a week. Tokays climb every surface, so check the glass, the cork and the top of each branch for droppings, not just the floor. The disinfectant, its contact time, and the full daily, weekly and monthly schedule are in the [reptile enclosure cleaning schedule](/blog/reptile-salmonella-hygiene-guide/). Plan the weekly clean around the gecko's temper: a front-opening enclosure lets you work from the side, with the tokay shut in a hide or moved to a holding tub first.

## The Non-Obvious Requirement: Climbing Structures at Every Level

Provide branches, cork bark, and foliage spanning the full height of the enclosure, not just near the top or bottom. This lets your tokay move along the vertical temperature gradient to thermoregulate properly.

A [leopard gecko](/blog/leopard-gecko-tank-setup-guide/) or an [African fat-tail](/blog/african-fat-tail-tank-setup-guide/) runs dry and warm rather than humid and warm, which is why two geckos in the same room can need opposite readings on the hygrometer.
