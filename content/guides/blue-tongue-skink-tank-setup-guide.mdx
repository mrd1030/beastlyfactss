# Blue-Tongued Skink Enclosure Setup: Northern vs. Indonesian, the Difference That Actually Matters

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Enclosure size and lighting are nearly identical between the two most commonly kept blue-tongued skink types. Temperature and humidity are where they diverge, and getting that split right matters more than almost anything else in this species' care.

<Figure src="/assets/images/blue-tongue-skink-tank-setup-guide-1.jpg" alt="Labeled diagram of a blue-tongued skink enclosure showing UVB lighting, basking temp, humidity, and substrate for Northern versus Indonesian types" caption="Enclosure size and lighting are nearly identical between Northern and Indonesian skinks. Temperature and humidity are where they genuinely diverge." shareable />

## Enclosure Size

Adult minimum is 4x2x2 feet, roughly 8 square feet of floor space, for either type. Bigger is always better. Front-opening <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4f8WVcx" product="4x2x2 ft PVC enclosure">PVC enclosures</AffiliateLink> are preferred over glass since they hold humidity more effectively, which matters more for Indonesian skinks specifically, covered below. That's the same adult minimum recommended in our [Bearded Dragon enclosure setup guide](/blog/bearded-dragon-tank-setup-guide/), if you're weighing the two starter lizard options against each other, base footprint requirement is one of the few things that doesn't change.

## Temperature: Hotter for Northerns

Northern basking surface: 105 to 115°F. Indonesian basking surface: slightly cooler, 100 to 105°F. Cool side (both): 70 to 80°F. Nighttime: stays above roughly 70°F for both types. A heat source (halogen or a deep heat projector) is required, mounted with a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/44eEEFH" product="Basking dome/fixture">basking dome fixture</AffiliateLink> and run on a [thermostat](/blog/reptile-heating-thermostats-guide/), which is what holds the surface inside those ranges instead of letting an unregulated bulb climb past them and burn the animal. In a 24-inch-tall 4x2x2, start with at least two 75-watt halogen floods clustered in the dome over the basking spot, then go up in wattage if the surface reads cool or down if it runs hot.

## Humidity: The Dividing Line Between the Two Types

This is the single biggest care difference between Northern and Indonesian skinks. Northerns need relatively low humidity, around 40%. Indonesians need considerably more, 60 to 80%. Achieve the higher Indonesian range through moisture-retentive substrate, regular misting, and a larger water bowl, standard low-humidity setups that work fine for a Northern will run too dry for an Indonesian.

<FunFact>
Two skinks that look nearly identical side by side, a Northern and an Indonesian, need humidity levels as much as double apart, roughly 40% for one and 60 to 80% for the other. It's an invisible difference, but it matters as much as anything visible in the enclosure.
</FunFact>

## Substrate

A naturalistic soil mix works for both, roughly 60% topsoil to 40% play sand for Northerns, with Indonesian setups benefiting from added moisture-retentive elements, leaf litter, sphagnum moss, or a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CY23K32S?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="VCEPJH Loose Coconut Fiber Substrate, 2 Pack">coco-fiber-based product</AffiliateLink>, to help hold the higher humidity they need. Keep it 4 to 6 inches deep. Avoid loose substrate for any sick or injured skink specifically, since normal movement is more limited during recovery. A bed that deep hides waste, so remove droppings as soon as they appear, taking the soiled soil with them, and strip the whole vivarium for a [full clean with a reptile-safe disinfectant](/blog/reptile-salmonella-hygiene-guide/) once a month.

## Lighting

UVB is required for this species to thrive, not just survive. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3TiqeC4" product="UVB - Arcadia Forest 6% (ProT5 kit, 36in/39W)">T5 HO bulb</AffiliateLink> anywhere from 5 to 12% output, picked against how high above the basking area it will hang, spanning at least half the enclosure on the warm side, replaced every 12 months regardless of whether it still visibly lights up. Keep the skink no closer than about 10 inches from the bulb. Run a photoperiod of roughly 11 to 13 hours depending on season for either type.

## The Non-Obvious Requirement: Quarantine for Wild-Caught Animals

If you've acquired an Indonesian skink, or any skink of uncertain origin, quarantine and a fecal test before it's treated as a healthy, established animal isn't optional extra caution, it's a standard part of responsible setup for this specific situation. It belongs in the setup conversation, since [quarantine](/blog/reptile-quarantine-guide/) is really the first step of housing a wild-caught animal properly.

Furnish the enclosure with at least one solid <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3SKmfOx" product="Cork bark round hide">hide</AffiliateLink> and a soakable water dish large enough for either type to fully submerge in, since Indonesians in particular will use it to help manage humidity on their own terms.
