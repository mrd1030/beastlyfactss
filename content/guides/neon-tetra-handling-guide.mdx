# Neon Tetras Aren't Handled, But the School Size Genuinely Isn't Optional

Like most fish, neon tetras are an observation pet, not a hands-on one. The real welfare consideration with this species isn't handling technique at all, it's group size, and getting that number right changes everything about how these fish actually behave. For the full care picture, see our complete [Neon tetra care guide](/guides/neon-tetra/).

## The Number That Matters: Six, Minimum

Neon tetras are obligate schooling fish, meaning they don't just tolerate being in a group, they genuinely need one to feel secure and behave normally. Six is the absolute floor, and ten to twelve or more is considerably better if your tank allows it. A group smaller than six produces fish that are pale, stressed, constantly hiding, and more vulnerable to illness. This isn't a minor behavioral preference, it's close to a core welfare requirement for the species.

## What a Properly Schooled Group Actually Looks Like

Confident, brightly colored fish swimming together in a loose group, exploring the mid-water areas of the tank actively rather than hiding in corners. This is genuinely one of the more rewarding things to watch in a community aquarium, and it only happens reliably once the school size is adequate.

## First-Timer Mistakes

- Buying just two or three neon tetras, thinking that's "enough" for a small tank, this is the single most common mistake with this species.
- Adding them to a tank that hasn't been properly cycled yet, which stresses an already-sensitive fish further.
- Mixing them with fin-nipping tankmates, which disrupts normal schooling behavior even in an otherwise adequate group.

That second mistake in particular is a big part of what drives the disease risk covered in our [Neon tetra health issues guide](/blog/neon-tetra-health-issues-guide/).

## How Neon Tetras Compare to the Guppy Already Covered on This Site

The two fish look superficially similar in a beginner's mental shopping list, small, colorful, community-friendly, but their care needs diverge in a genuinely important way. Guppies are hardy livebearers, tolerant of harder, more alkaline water, and they breed readily without much intervention. Neon tetras are egg-scattering fish that need soft, acidic, and genuinely stable water to thrive, they're more sensitive to water quality swings than guppies are. If you've kept guppies successfully and assume neon tetras will be just as forgiving, that assumption is worth double-checking before you buy, our [Guppy handling guide](/blog/guppy-handling-guide/) covers what makes that species more tolerant.

<FunFact>
Neon tetras are also sometimes confused with the closely related cardinal tetra, easiest to tell apart by the red stripe. It runs the full body length on a cardinal, but only the back half on a neon.
</FunFact>
