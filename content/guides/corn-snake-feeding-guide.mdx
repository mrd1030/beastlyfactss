# Corn Snake Feeding: Schedule, Diet, and Why They Stop Eating

Corn snakes are reliable eaters. A sudden refusal worries new owners more than it usually should.

## Feeding schedule by age and weight

- **Hatchlings (0 to 3 months, under about 30g):** every 5 to 7 days, pinky mice.
- **Young juveniles (3 to 9 months, 30 to 100g):** every 7 days.
- **Juveniles (9 to 18 months, 100 to 300g):** every 7 to 10 days.
- **Sub-adults (18 to 36 months, 300 to 600g):** every 10 to 14 days.
- **Adults (3+ years, 600g+):** every 14 to 21 days.

Prey about 1 to 1.5 times the width of the snake at its widest, or about 10 to 15% of body weight. Two smaller items can stand in for one larger one. Monthly weigh-ins beat the calendar. Feeding an adult too often produces obesity inside 12 to 18 months.

## What corn snakes eat

Whole frozen-thawed rodents. Pinky mice, then fuzzies and hoppers, then adult mice or small rats. Frozen-thawed, not live. Buy commercial or captive-bred, not wild-caught.

Thaw in the refrigerator overnight, then warm in warm, not boiling, water to about 98 to 102F. Never microwave. Never re-freeze refused prey.

A whole-prey diet covers nutrition. If you dust at all, it is hatchlings on pinkies, a light calcium and multivitamin every 3 to 4 feeds, then stop. They do not need UVB the way many lizards do. Extra D3 and extra calcium cause their own problems.

## What to avoid

Live prey. Wild-caught prey. Meat scraps or cooked food. Thawed-then-refrozen prey. Insects. Do not feed again inside about 24 hours of the last meal.

## Why corn snakes stop eating

1. **Shedding.** Appetite drops 5 to 7 days before a shed, when the eyes cloud, and sometimes just after.
2. **Brumation.** Drop temperatures over 1 to 2 weeks, then 2 to 4 months without food. A 10 to 14 day fast at normal temperature has to come first. Undigested food in a cooling gut can kill the snake.
3. **Breeding season, mainly in males.** Weeks to months after brumation. Fine if the snake stays active and holds condition.
4. **Environmental stress.** Rehoming, cage changes, too much handling, no hides. Wait about 48 hours after a disturbance before offering food.
5. **A warm side that is too cool.** They need about 82 to 85F on the warm side to digest.
6. **Prey presentation.** Cold prey, or a snake used to live food. Scenting against live-prey bedding or a bit of broth can help the switch.
7. **Illness or parasites.** Refusal plus other signs. The [health issues guide](/blog/corn-snake-health-issues-guide/) is that page.

## When to worry

Weigh monthly. An adult that is active and holding weight can miss 1 to 2 months outside brumation or a pre-laying fast. Two to three months is how long a healthy adult can physically last, not how long you wait. A hatchling past about a week without food is already a problem.

See a vet if refusal comes with weight loss, lethargy, wheezing or discharge, open-mouth breathing, vomiting, diarrhea, a swollen abdomen, or sores. Give more slack to a snake that is out and holding weight than to one that is thinning or still.
