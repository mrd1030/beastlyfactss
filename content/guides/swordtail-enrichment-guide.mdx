# Swordtail Enrichment: One Male, or Space for Several

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Most livebearer advice is about males and females. With swordtails, the male-to-male question matters just as much.

<KeyTakeaway>
Male swordtails compete hard with each other, and **more than one male in a small tank tends to produce a hounded loser**. Keep one, or keep several only where the tank is large and planted enough that the aggression spreads, and give them the horizontal distance a large active livebearer actually uses.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Research Says

Nothing on swordtails specifically. The applicable thread is livebearer sexual harassment, covered in full in our [Guppy enrichment guide](/blog/guppy-enrichment-guide/).

The research identifies **costs to females** of persistent male attention. In the molly *Poecilia mexicana*, females become **more vigilant with hampered feeding opportunities** when males court them. A guppy study found effects reaching into the next generation.

The male-to-male aggression in swordtails is not from that literature, and it is the thing most likely to cause a visible problem in a home tank, so it leads here.

<FunFact>
Some female swordtails are reported to develop a sword and male-like features, though whether that is true sex change or a late-developing male is an open question. It regularly convinces people their shop sold them the wrong fish.
</FunFact>

## One Male in a Small Tank

The most useful stocking rule for this species. With exactly two males, every contest is between the same pair and the loser has nowhere to be.

**One male** with a group of females is the simplest answer. **Several males** in a large, well-planted tank works because the aggression distributes, and it needs the space to be real rather than nominal.

## Sex Ratio

Two or three females per male minimum, three or four better, for the same reasons as any livebearer. A pursued female feeds less and rests less.

Combined with the rule above: one male and four or more females is a clean, easy setup.

## Length, and a Lid

Swordtails are the largest and most active of the common livebearers, and strong swimmers that use horizontal distance. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eSbKl2" product="20 gallon long tank">20 gallon long</AffiliateLink> is a stopgap rather than a floor, since 36 inches long is the shortest tank to consider, and [48 inches is the better buy](/blog/swordtail-tank-setup-guide/) for a group that will use the length it's given.

They are also excellent jumpers. A tank without a lid will lose fish, and it is one of the more common and entirely avoidable losses in this species.

## Cover and Broken Sight Lines

Dense <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B008HXF16K?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="SubstrateSource Anubias Nana Live Aquarium Plant, 1 Pot">live planting</AffiliateLink> along the sides and back, with open water through the middle.

Planting does double duty here: cover for pursued females, and broken sight lines that stop a dominant male from tracking a subordinate across the whole tank. If you are keeping several males, this is what makes it work.

## Flow and Filtration

Active fish from moving water that use current, and big enough to need a filter sized for the tank. A <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B000260FUW?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="AquaClear 70 Power Filter (40-70 gal)">hang-on-back filter</AffiliateLink> rated above the tank volume handles both.

Aim the outflow along the length so there is a faster end and a slower end.

## Feeding Across the Tank

Spread food across the tank, never into one corner, so chased females and subordinate males still eat. Include vegetable content, since swordtails graze on algae and biofilm.

## Plan for the Fry

Mixed-sex swordtails breed steadily and the fish are large enough that overstocking hits water quality quickly. Decide in advance: a single-sex group, a rehoming plan, or tank mates that will thin the fry.

## Priority Order

1. One male in a small tank, or several only with the space for it
2. At least two or three females per male, three or four where the tank allows
3. A long tank with a lid
4. Dense planting that breaks sight lines, with open water through the middle
5. Filtration and flow suited to a large active fish
6. Food spread across the tank, with vegetable content
7. A plan for the fry

## What Not to Do

Do not keep more than one male in a small tank. Do not run the tank without a lid, since swordtails jump. Do not use a small tank for the largest common livebearer. Do not keep a bare tank where a dominant fish can see everything. Do not let the population run unmanaged.

The [platy, swordtail and molly overview](/blog/platy-swordtail-molly-overview/) sets the swordtail beside the other two common livebearers, where its need for swimming length is the trait that separates it.
