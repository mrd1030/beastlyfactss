# Swordtail Enrichment: Two Males Is the Number That Causes Trouble

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Most livebearer advice is about males and females. With swordtails, the male-to-male question matters just as much.

<KeyTakeaway>
Male swordtails compete hard with each other, and **two males in one tank is the arrangement that reliably produces a hounded loser**. Keep one, or keep enough that the aggression spreads, and give them the horizontal distance a large active livebearer actually uses.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Research Says

Nothing on swordtails specifically. The applicable thread is livebearer sexual harassment, covered in full in our [Guppy enrichment guide](/blog/guppy-enrichment-guide/).

The literature identifies **direct costs to females** of persistent male attention: **energy expenditure and reduced foraging ability**. In the molly *Poecilia mexicana*, males attempt to mate almost constantly, females are largely non-responsive, and females become **more vigilant with hampered feeding opportunities**. A guppy study found effects reaching into the next generation.

The male-to-male aggression in swordtails is not from that literature, and it is the thing most likely to cause a visible problem in a home tank, so it leads here.

<FunFact>
Older female swordtails sometimes develop a sword and male-like features. It is a genuine and well-documented quirk of the species and it regularly convinces people their shop sold them the wrong fish.
</FunFact>

## Not Two Males

The single most useful stocking rule for this species. With two, every contest is between the same pair and the loser has nowhere to be.

**One male** with a group of females is the simplest answer. **Several males** in a large, well-planted tank works because the aggression distributes, and it needs the space to be real rather than nominal.

## Sex Ratio

Two to three females per male minimum, more better, for the same reasons as any livebearer. A pursued female feeds less and rests less.

Combined with the rule above: one male and four or more females is a clean, easy setup.

## Length, and a Lid

Swordtails are the largest and most active of the common livebearers, and strong swimmers that use horizontal distance. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eSbKl2" product="20 gallon long tank">20 gallon long</AffiliateLink> is a sensible floor rather than a generous provision, and [29 or 30 gallons is the more realistic target](/blog/swordtail-tank-setup-guide/) for a group that will use the length it's given.

They are also excellent jumpers. A tank without a lid will lose fish, and it is one of the more common and entirely avoidable losses in this species.

## Cover and Broken Sight Lines

Dense <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4grZYPq" product="Live terrarium plants">live planting</AffiliateLink> along the sides and back, with open water through the middle.

Planting does double duty here: cover for pursued females, and broken sight lines that stop a dominant male from tracking a subordinate across the whole tank. If you are keeping several males, this is what makes it work.

## Flow and Filtration

Active fish from moving water that use current, and messy enough to need real filtration. A <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B000260FUW?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="AquaClear 70 Power Filter (40-70 gal)">hang-on-back filter</AffiliateLink> rated above the tank volume handles both.

Aim the outflow along the length so there is a faster end and a slower end.

## Feeding Across the Tank

Spread food across the tank, never into one corner, so chased females and subordinate males still eat. Include vegetable content, since swordtails graze more than guppies.

## Plan for the Fry

Mixed-sex swordtails breed steadily and the fish are large enough that overstocking hits water quality quickly. Decide in advance: a single-sex group, a rehoming plan, or tank mates that will thin the fry.

## Priority Order

1. One male, or several with the space for it, but not two
2. At least two to three females per male
3. A long tank with a lid
4. Dense planting that breaks sight lines, with open water through the middle
5. Filtration and flow suited to a large active fish
6. Food spread across the tank, with vegetable content
7. A plan for the fry

## What Not to Do

Do not keep exactly two males. Do not run the tank without a lid, since swordtails jump. Do not use a small tank for the largest common livebearer. Do not keep a bare tank where a dominant fish can see everything. Do not let the population run unmanaged.

The [platy, swordtail and molly overview](/blog/platy-swordtail-molly-overview/) sets the swordtail beside the other two common livebearers, where its need for swimming length is the trait that separates it.
