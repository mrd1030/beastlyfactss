# Neon Tetra Tank Setup: Soft Water, Dim Light, and a Blackwater Feel

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Neon tetras come from soft, dim, tannin-stained blackwater streams, and the closer your tank setup gets to that origin, the better this fish looks and behaves. This covers the full setup, for what it all costs, see our [Neon tetra cost guide](/blog/neon-tetra-cost-guide/), or the full [Neon tetra care guide](/guides/neon-tetra/) for everything else.

## Tank Size

10 gallons is the practical minimum for a proper school of 6. If you're aiming for the better group size of 10 to 15 fish, step up to a 20-gallon long tank. Schooling behavior plays out horizontally, so favor a longer tank over a taller one.

<AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4pjiyeT" product="10 gallon tank">A 10-gallon tank</AffiliateLink> is a solid starting point for a school of 6. If you're planning for the larger 10 to 15 fish school size from the start, a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eSbKl2" product="20 gallon long tank">20-gallon long tank</AffiliateLink> is worth the upsize.

## Temperature

70 to 81°F works, with 72 to 78°F being a solid, commonly recommended target. As a genuinely tropical species, a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eQY1L5" product="Submersible aquarium heater">heater</AffiliateLink> is necessary for any tank of 10 gallons or more, and keeping the temperature stable matters as much as hitting the right number, swings are more stressful than a slightly imperfect but steady setting.

## Water Chemistry: The Detail That Separates This Fish From a Lot of Beginner Picks

Neon tetras want soft, slightly acidic water, a pH of 6.0 to 7.0 and low hardness. This is genuinely different from the harder, more alkaline water tolerance of species like guppies, and it's worth testing your source water before assuming your setup is automatically suitable. Our [Guppy tank setup guide](/blog/guppy-tank-setup-guide/) covers that species' more forgiving water chemistry for comparison. Ammonia and nitrite both need to read zero, this species is more sensitive to water quality instability than many hardier community fish.

## Filtration

Keep the flow gentle, neon tetras dislike strong current. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3T8xyAd" product="Sponge filter">sponge filter</AffiliateLink> is a common, effective choice, and a hang-on-back filter works too if you can baffle or reduce its output.

## Substrate

Fine gravel or sand, and a darker color genuinely makes neon coloring pop more against the background.

## Lighting

Dim to moderate, roughly 8 to 10 hours a day. Bright, intense lighting stresses this species, they come from shaded, plant-covered streams, not open water.

## The Non-Obvious Requirement: Recreate Blackwater Conditions

This is the detail that takes a neon tetra tank from adequate to genuinely great. <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eR0Z2l" product="Driftwood">Driftwood</AffiliateLink> and Indian almond leaves release natural tannins that stain the water slightly and lower pH, mimicking the blackwater habitat this species evolved in.

<FunFact>
Fish kept in tannin-stained, driftwood-conditioned water consistently show better color and confidence than fish in a bright, tannin-free setup, even though the water itself and diet are otherwise identical. Pair this with dense midground <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4grZYPq" product="Live terrarium plants">live plants</AffiliateLink> and some floating plants to dim the light further, and this species goes from surviving to genuinely thriving.
</FunFact>

Always use a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3Rg5gTT" product="Water conditioner">water conditioner</AffiliateLink> on tap water before it goes in the tank, dechlorinating and neutralizing chloramine and heavy metals that are otherwise harmful to fish.
