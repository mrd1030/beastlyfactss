# Neon Tetra Tank Setup: Soft Water, Dim Light, and a Blackwater Feel

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Neon tetras come from soft, dim, tannin-stained blackwater streams, and the closer your tank setup gets to that origin, the better this fish looks and behaves.

<Figure src="/assets/images/neon-tetra-tank-setup-guide-1.jpg" alt="Labeled diagram of a neon tetra aquarium showing soft acidic water, dim lighting, and blackwater conditions from driftwood and tannins" caption="The closer your tank setup gets to this species' soft, dim, tannin-stained blackwater origin, the better it looks and behaves." shareable />

## Tank Size

10 gallons is the practical minimum for a proper school of 6. If you're aiming for the better group size of 10 to 15 fish, step up to a 20-gallon long tank. Schooling behavior plays out horizontally, so favor a longer tank over a taller one.

If you're planning for the larger 10 to 15 fish school size from the start, a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eSbKl2" product="20 gallon long tank">20-gallon long tank</AffiliateLink> is worth the upsize.

Tankmates have to share the soft water and the 72 to 78°F band and be too small to eat a neon. The pairings that fail are [named species by species](/blog/aquarium-stocking-and-tankmates-guide/): an adult angelfish, which eats neons once it passes 3 inches, any fin-nipper, and anything else with a mouth big enough. Ten neons also make a calmer tank than six neons and six rasboras, where each half-school sits at the floor.

## Temperature

70 to 81°F works, with 72 to 78°F being a solid, commonly recommended target. As a genuinely tropical species, a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eQY1L5" product="Submersible aquarium heater">heater</AffiliateLink> is necessary for any tank of 10 gallons or more, and keeping the temperature stable matters as much as hitting the right number, swings are more stressful than a slightly imperfect but steady setting.

## Water Chemistry: The Detail That Separates This Fish From a Lot of Beginner Picks

Neon tetras want soft, slightly acidic water, a pH of 6.0 to 7.0 and low hardness. This is genuinely different from the harder, more alkaline water a [guppy](/blog/guppy-tank-setup-guide/) tolerates, and it's worth [testing your source water](/blog/freshwater-ph-gh-kh-guide/) before assuming your setup is automatically suitable. Hard tap water comes down by blending it with RO water, never by dosing a pH-down product against a high KH, which only swings the pH back. Ammonia and nitrite both need to read zero, this species is more sensitive to water quality instability than many hardier community fish.

## Filtration

Keep the flow gentle, neon tetras dislike strong current. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3T8xyAd" product="Sponge filter">sponge filter</AffiliateLink> is a common, effective choice, and a hang-on-back filter works too if you can baffle or reduce its output.

## Substrate

Fine gravel or sand, and a darker color genuinely makes neon coloring pop more against the background.

## Lighting

Dim to moderate, roughly 8 to 10 hours a day. Bright, intense lighting stresses this species, they come from shaded, plant-covered streams, not open water.

## The Non-Obvious Requirement: Recreate Blackwater Conditions

This is the detail that takes a neon tetra tank from adequate to genuinely great. <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eR0Z2l" product="Driftwood">Driftwood</AffiliateLink> and Indian almond leaves release natural tannins that stain the water slightly and lower pH, mimicking the blackwater habitat this species evolved in.

<FunFact>
Fish kept in tannin-stained, driftwood-conditioned water consistently show better color and confidence than fish in a bright, tannin-free setup, even though the water itself and diet are otherwise identical. Pair this with dense midground live plants and some floating plants to dim the light further, and this species goes from surviving to thriving.
</FunFact>

Always quarantine new fish for 2 to 4 weeks before adding them, given the disease risk this species carries, and keep them well fed on a proper micro-pellet or micro-crisp food sized for their small mouths. Always use a water conditioner on tap water before it goes in the tank, dechlorinating and neutralizing chloramine and heavy metals that are otherwise harmful to fish.
