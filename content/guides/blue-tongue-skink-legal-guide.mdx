# Is It Legal to Own a Blue-Tongued Skink? State Guide

Almost no US regulation mentions the blue-tongued skink by name. It gets reached, where it gets reached at all, as a member of Family Scincidae, and that is the useful thing to understand before checking any individual state. A keeper who looks up how their state handles geckos and assumes the answer carries over is reading the wrong line of the same list.

## Regulated as a Skink, Not as This Skink

New Jersey is the clearest illustration. Its permit list handles reptiles clause by clause, and skinks sit at 7:25-4.3(a)3.x as "Skinks - Family Scincidae", taken whole with no species named or excepted. Tiliqua scincoides is inside it.

Geckos sit two paragraphs away at 3.xiv, under their own entry. Both groups need a permit, so the outcome happens to match, but the mechanism is separate and there is no reason for the two entries to move together if New Jersey ever revises one. A keeper who checked the gecko line because a forum post mentioned it has not actually checked their animal.

Maine does the same thing more generously, clearing all species in Family Scincidae with nothing excepted. Tiliqua scincoides is rated least concern by the IUCN and appears on none of the four lists Maine's asterisk points at, so the clearance holds.

## Maine's Condition Is Not the Asterisk

The asterisk on Maine's entry draws attention because it looks like the restrictive part. For this species it does nothing. The condition that actually bites sits higher up the page, where the entire Reptilia section is headed captive bred origin only.

That turns the clearance into a conditional one. A captive-bred blue-tongued skink is cleared. A wild-caught animal of the same species is not, regardless of the family entry below. A Maine keeper needs provenance documented, not just species.

## Hawaii Named the Whole Pet-Trade Group

Hawaii lists Tiliqua scincoides individually on Part A of its Restricted Animal List, and several other Tiliqua are listed beside it. That breadth is deliberate: the group most likely to turn up in the pet trade is covered rather than just the single most common species, so there is no near-relative to substitute.

Part A is headed for research and exhibition, which means a licensed exhibitor or a university can hold the animal and a private keeper cannot. It is the same list and the same mechanism that catches the [leopard gecko](/blog/leopard-gecko-legal-guide/), reached by individual naming rather than by family.

The District of Columbia is the other absolute. Section 8-1808(j)(1) bars wildlife with seven exceptions, and none of them is a lizard. There is no permit behind the bar.

## West Virginia Regulates the Shipment, Not the Species

West Virginia never named the blue-tongued skink, and the rule that reaches it is not on any species list. W. Va. Code § 20-2-13 says that "no person shall transport into or have in his possession within this state any live wildlife or viable eggs thereof from without the state, except as authorized by an importation permit issued by the director." Wildlife is defined in § 20-1-2 as wild birds, wild animals, game and fur-bearing animals, fish, reptiles, amphibians, mollusks and crustaceans, and the reptile and amphibian clause carries no nativity qualifier and no captive-bred exception. A blue-tongued skink shipped in from a breeder is therefore live wildlife from without the state.

The argument that an out-of-state animal sits outside the chapter is closed before it can be made: § 20-2-4(b) says wildlife lawfully taken outside of this state is subject to the same laws and rules as wildlife taken within it. What is missing is the other half. No rule in Title 58 implements § 20-2-13 outside aquaculture, the Division of Natural Resources publishes no application for an importation permit, and the only penalty the section spells out is written for coyotes. The requirement is clear on the page and invisible in practice, which is the honest thing to tell someone moving to Huntington with a tank in the car. This is the one place on the skink’s map where the rule is not about skinks at all, or about any taxonomic group.

## What to Check Before Buying

Northern, Indonesian and several other blue-tongue types are sold under the same common name and are not all the same species, which can matter where a rule names one rather than the family.
