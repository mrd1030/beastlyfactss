# How Much Does a Ball Python Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Ball pythons have one of the best cost-to-lifespan ratios of any commonly kept reptile. They're not free to set up, but once you're past the first year, ongoing costs are low, especially for an animal that can live 20 to 30 years.

## The Snake Itself

A standard or wild-type ball python runs $40 to $100. Common single-gene morphs land around $50 to $150. Designer or rare morphs climb fast, anywhere from $250 to $5,000 or more depending on the genetics. Rescues and adoptions are often the cheapest route, typically $25 to $150.

## What a Morph Price Does Not Tell You

Price follows genetics, and one of the most widely bred genes carries a known health cost. The spider morph is linked to wobble syndrome, a heritable neurological disorder first described from a survey of breeders and keepers in 2014. Affected snakes show side-to-side or twisting movements of the head, poor coordination, and trouble striking or constricting prey. A 2022 imaging study of spider morphs found malformed balance organs in the inner ear of every spider it examined, and an independent study it cites found all spider morph pythons affected, to a variable degree.

In practice that means a spider, or any combination morph carrying the spider gene, is a snake that may struggle to feed, and any offspring bred from it can inherit the condition. A cheaper wild-type or a single-gene morph without a known defect is the lower-risk first snake at almost any budget. Whatever the morph, ask to see the snake eat, or for its feeding record, before paying: a ball python that is already taking frozen-thawed rodents on a regular schedule is worth more than one that is not.

## Vet Costs

A ball python should see a vet once a year. That wellness exam, $50 to $150, is the last line of the yearly table. If something goes wrong, sick or emergency visits run $100 to $500 or more, with imaging or surgery pushing that higher.

## The Long-Term Picture

The snake, the full setup and the first vet exam come to $560 to $1,450, before a year of rodents, substrate and electricity. After that first year, ball pythons are inexpensive to keep relative to their lifespan. Spread the setup cost over 20 to 30 years and it's one of the lowest per-year costs of any exotic pet, which is part of why they're such a popular first snake.

Weighed against a boa constrictor, a snake that demands considerably more space once fully grown, the [ball python vs. boa constrictor](/blog/ball-python-vs-boa-constrictor-guide/) comparison shows where the extra money goes.
