# Rabbit and Budgie: Two Classic Pets, Almost Always Kept Too Small

Both of these are among the most commonly kept pets outside of cats and dogs, and both are, more often than not, housed in something significantly smaller than they need. That's really the story that connects them.

## Rabbit: Bigger Space, Bigger Commitment Than the Hutch Suggests

The old-fashioned wire hutch that "rabbit cage" still calls to mind is considerably smaller than current welfare standards recommend. The House Rabbit Society sizes any cage at three to four times the rabbit's stretched-out length and treats that cage as a bedroom only. The living space is a 4 by 4 foot exercise pen with access to free-roam time, and the rabbit needs four or more hours of running play every day. A hutch in the yard also costs years: indoor house rabbits typically live 8 to 12 years, while rabbits kept outdoors average about 3 to 5.

Diet is the other half of the gap. Hay makes up about 85% of what an adult rabbit should eat, with fresh greens and a small measure of pellets on top, so the pellet-and-carrot bowl most people picture has the proportions upside down. That hay keeps a gut moving that cannot safely stop: a rabbit that goes 12 hours without eating or passing droppings is an emergency, the same always-on digestion that makes a skipped meal serious in a [guinea pig](/blog/guinea-pig-health-issues-guide/). The vet who handles that emergency has to be a rabbit-savvy exotic vet, which a standard small-animal clinic often is not.

## Budgie: Small Bird, Real Space Requirements

Budgies are often sold in cages barely bigger than the bird itself. The minimum usually quoted for a single bird is 18 by 18 by 18 inches, and that is a floor. Budgies fly side to side, so a long, wide flight cage does more for them than a tall one.

The seed mix sold beside the cage is the second problem. Commercial seed mixes run high in fat and carbohydrate and low in protein, vitamins and minerals, and budgies tend to pick out one or two favorite seeds from them, so an all-seed diet can shorten a budgie's life. A pelleted diet formulated for small birds, with a limited amount of fresh food, is the nutritionally complete version, and the switch takes patience because adult budgies resist new food. The [pellet conversion](/blog/bird-pellet-conversion-guide/) is slow by design. Kept well, a budgie lives 10 to 12 years.

Company matters too. Budgies are flock birds, and pairs are popular for exactly that reason, but a budgie kept with other budgies tends to bond with them instead of its owner. A single budgie needs a person who can supply most of that flock contact every day. Care for this bird also means an avian vet, a specialized field distinct from general small-animal practice.

## One Problem, Two Animals

The rabbit and the budgie share one problem completely: the standard starter setup sold for each is the thing most likely to shorten its life. The hutch and the starter cage are both too small, the carrot bowl and the seed mix are both the wrong diet, and both animals need a specialist vet that a general clinic cannot replace. A reader fixing the housing for one has, in practice, learned the fix for the other.

<FunFact>
Neither of these is a "starter pet" in the low-effort sense they're often marketed as. Both are genuinely rewarding animals once housed properly, the gap between the common version of their care and the correct version just happens to be wider than most new owners expect going in.
</FunFact>
