# Is It Legal to Own a Cockatoo? State-by-State Guide

Cockatoos are legal to own across nearly every US state, but "is a cockatoo legal here" turns out to be the wrong question in at least one place. Maine's parrot rules don't treat cockatoos as one group; they single out specific species by conservation status, which means the answer can depend on which cockatoo you're asking about. For everything else about keeping one, see our full [Cockatoo care guide](/guides/cockatoo/), and the [Cockatoo encyclopedia profile](/encyclopedia/animal/cockatoo/) for more on the family Cacatuidae itself.

### Cockatoos Are an Easy Yes in Most States

In the great majority of states, a pet cockatoo doesn't raise any legal questions at all. Idaho and Colorado clear pet birds as a category outright, Colorado by putting "all Psitticine birds" on its unregulated wildlife list, and Oklahoma's noncontrolled exotic-species exemption names cockatoos specifically, alongside cockatiels, macaws, and canaries. Others, including New Hampshire and Montana, name the order Psittaciformes or the family Psittacidae directly as unregulated. Where a state does restrict a parrot species, it's almost always the same one: the monk parakeet, named specifically in California, Connecticut, New Jersey, and Virginia, among others, and prohibited outright in Colorado on a separate list that overrides the parrot exemption, while every other parrot, cockatoos included, is left alone.

### Maine's CITES and IUCN Carve-Out: The Real Story

<FunFact>
Maine clears the entire parrot order, Psittaciformes, from its captivity-permit requirement in one broad stroke, then takes a chunk back out: any species also listed on CITES Appendix I or the IUCN Red List's Endangered, Critically Endangered, or Extinct in the Wild tiers still needs a permit. That single clause catches some of the most commonly kept pet cockatoos by name. The Moluccan and yellow-crested cockatoos are both CITES Appendix I. The umbrella cockatoo is IUCN Endangered, even though it's only CITES Appendix II, which means the IUCN half of Maine's rule catches it on its own. Smaller, more secure species like the cockatiel or the galah clear Maine's list with no permit at all. So "is a cockatoo legal in Maine" isn't a single yes-or-no, it depends entirely on which cockatoo you're asking about. Arkansas took the opposite approach: its unrestricted list names five cockatoo species outright, Moluccan and yellow-crested included, with no conservation-status carve-out at all, the same CITES Appendix I birds Maine treats as needing a permit.
</FunFact>

### Hawaii: A Process, Not a Ban

Hawaii doesn't ban cockatoos, but it doesn't treat them as an ordinary pet either. White cockatoos, along with the galah, gang-gang, black cockatoos, and palm cockatoo, are named on the state's list of conditionally approved animals. Getting one into the state legally means securing an import permit, completing pre-departure quarantine, providing health certification, and having the bird permanently identified, all before it's allowed to land. It's a real, working process rather than a ban, but it's a meaningfully higher bar than most states set.

## The Real Takeaway

Cockatoos are broadly legal pets in the US, and in most states that's the entire analysis. Maine is the exception worth remembering, not because it singles out cockatoos by name, but because its CITES Appendix I and IUCN Red List carve-out happens to catch several of the species people actually keep, the Moluccan, yellow-crested, and umbrella cockatoo among them, while leaving smaller parrots untouched. Arkansas shows the same birds can be regulated completely differently: named outright on an unrestricted list with no conservation-status test at all. Hawaii adds a real but manageable import process rather than a ban.
