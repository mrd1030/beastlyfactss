# Canary Enrichment: He May Be Singing Because He Is Alone

<AffiliateDisclosure />

The thing everyone loves about canaries is the thing most likely to be misread.

<KeyTakeaway>
In canaries, **the absence of a potential mate increases song production, and the presence of a mate greatly reduces it**. A male singing constantly is doing what an unpaired male does. That is not a reason to panic, and it is a reason to stop using song volume as your welfare readout.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Song and Stress Research Says

Canaries are one of the best studied songbirds in neuroscience, and most of that work is about the brain. Two threads are useful for the cage.

**Song is signaling, not mood.** Song production in canaries is strongly shaped by season and social context. Long days promote song, and **the absence of a mate increases the number of songs while the presence of one greatly diminishes singing**. There is a nice loop in it too: producing song promotes the incorporation of new neurons into the brain regions that control song output.

**Cage size shows up in corticosterone.** A study measuring stress hormone in canary droppings found a robust daily rhythm in birds on short-day cycles, and a **significant change in corticosterone excretion when group-bred birds were restrained in smaller cages**. That is a physiological reading on something owners treat as a matter of preference.

<FunFact>
Singing does something to the canary's brain, not only to its day. New neurons are incorporated into the song control system in response to song production, which is one of the findings that overturned the idea that adult vertebrate brains do not add neurons.
</FunFact>

## Flight Distance

The item the corticosterone result points at. Canaries fly rather than climb, and what they need is horizontal distance between perches, not height.

A long <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4wuT808" product="Large bird flight cage">flight cage</AffiliateLink> beats a tall decorative one by a wide margin. Leave the middle open so there is a flight path, and resist the urge to fill the center with toys.

If the cage is small, the study says the bird's stress physiology already knows.

## Perches at Varied Diameter

Perches are the item most likely to be quietly harming a small bird. Uniform dowel of one thickness holds the foot in a single position all day, which is how pressure sores start.

Site them at the ends of the cage so the flight path stays clear, and avoid sandpaper covers entirely. ## Bathing

The easiest win with this species. Canaries bathe readily and often daily, and a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B000I6ORHS?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Lixit Quick Lock Bird Cage Bath for Lovebirds, Canaries, Finches, Parakeets, and Cockatiels">shallow cage bath</AffiliateLink> is used enthusiastically.

Offer clean water in the morning and remove it later so it does not become drinking water contamination. Good feather condition follows from it, and the bird visibly wants to.

## Light Cycles That Mean Something

Photoperiod drives song, molt and reproduction in this species, so a canary kept under an artificial light schedule that never changes is being held in a permanent season.

A light cycle that tracks the natural year, or at least varies, is closer to what the bird is built for, and it matters for molting properly. Twelve hours of genuine dark for sleep, undisturbed, is the other half.

## Foraging, in a Small Way

Less elaborate than a parrot needs, and still worth doing. Millet sprays hung so they have to be worked at, greens clipped to the bars, seed scattered across a shallow tray, and safe branches with leaves to pick through.

Nothing complicated. Canaries do not solve puzzle boxes, and a bit of variety in how food arrives beats an identical cup every morning.

## Company, Decided Deliberately

Less socially dependent than parrots. A single canary in a good flight cage is a reasonable setup, unlike a single cockatoo.

Do not house two males together, since they are territorial and will fight. A male and female pair, or a group of females in a large enough flight, both work. And remember the song finding before you conclude a pair has made the bird miserable.

## Priority Order

1. A long flight cage with a clear flight path
2. Perches of varied diameter, sited at the ends
3. Daily bathing water
4. A light cycle that varies through the year, and twelve hours of dark
5. Varied food presentation, millet and greens to work at
6. Company chosen deliberately, never two males
7. Novel objects, last and modest

## What Not to Do

Do not read constant song as proof of contentment. Do not buy a tall narrow cage for a bird that flies horizontally. Do not fill the center of the cage with toys. Do not use uniform dowel perches or sandpaper covers. Do not house two males together. Do not keep the bird under an unchanging artificial light schedule. And see our handling guide before treating handling as enrichment, since canaries are watching birds rather than contact birds.
