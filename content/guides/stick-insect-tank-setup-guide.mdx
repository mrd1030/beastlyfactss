# Stick Insect Enclosure Setup: Height, Humidity, and the Right Host Plant

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Stick insect care is refreshingly simple once the enclosure height and humidity balance are right, this is a forgiving species to house well.

<Figure src="/assets/images/stick-insect-tank-setup-guide-1.jpg" alt="Labeled diagram of a stick insect enclosure showing the tall mesh top, host plant cuttings, and daily misting" caption="Enclosure height matters more than almost any other dimension here, this species molts by hanging from the mesh top." shareable />

## Enclosure Size

A useful rule: length and width should each be at least twice the adult's body length, and height should be at least three times body length. Height is the detail that actually matters most, since it's what allows a proper molt. A single Indian stick insect does fine in a roughly 8 by 8 by 12 inch <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0041P5PBE?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Exo Terra Glass Nano Terrarium Kit, 8 x 8 x 12 in">tall, mesh-topped enclosure</AffiliateLink>; a small group or larger species needs correspondingly more room.

## Temperature

Many commonly kept species, Indian stick insects especially, do perfectly well at normal room temperature, roughly 63 to 77F. Tropical species like the giant prickly stick insect prefer it warmer. Supplemental heat is only needed if your space runs consistently cold, and if you do add a small basking bulb, keep it off the mesh top, where the insect hangs, to avoid burns, and turn it off at night.

## Humidity

Varies by species, tropical types want 60 to 80%, while Indian stick insects are considerably more forgiving. Achieve this through daily light misting with dechlorinated or distilled water using a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4vPKMjX" product="Fine mist spray bottle">spray bottle</AffiliateLink>, combined with an inch or two of moisture-holding substrate, and track it with a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3QHrM81" product="Digital thermometer/hygrometer combo">digital hygrometer</AffiliateLink> rather than guessing. Avoid both extremes, too little humidity risks molting failures, too much invites mold.

## Substrate

<AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CY23K32S?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="VCEPJH Loose Coconut Fiber Substrate, 2 Pack">Coconut coir</AffiliateLink>, peat moss, or plain paper towel all work, holding humidity and absorbing waste. Replace it regularly rather than letting it build up.

## Lighting

No UVB is needed for this species. Normal ambient room light on a roughly 12-hour cycle is sufficient. A grow light is only worth adding if you're keeping live plants in the enclosure or the room is genuinely dark.

<FunFact>
An Indian stick insect lives at 63 to 77F with no lamp and no UVB. The entire heating budget for this pet is the house you already keep warm.
</FunFact>

## Diet Basics

Leaves, and that is the entire diet. Almost every commonly kept stick insect eats washed bramble, the blackberry stems that grow in any hedgerow, and it goes species by species from there: Indian stick insects also take privet and ivy, while rose, hawthorn, oak and eucalyptus each suit different species. Match the plant to the species you actually have.

Cut stems standing in water beat a pile of picked leaves, since foliage kept that way stays good for several days instead of crisping overnight. Block the opening of the water container so nothing can fall in, because a nymph drowned in a jar of stems is the routine avoidable loss in this hobby, and a lid that can rust is not the way to do it.

Wash foliage before it goes in, which takes off pesticide residue and any hitchhiking insects, and replace it before the leaves dry. The [invertebrate pesticide hazards guide](/blog/invertebrate-pesticide-hazards-guide/) covers where that residue comes from.

## The Non-Obvious Requirement: A Mesh Top

A mesh or netting lid is essential, not just for ventilation, but because stick insects hang from the top of the enclosure to molt, and shedding from mesh succeeds far more reliably than from a branch alone.
