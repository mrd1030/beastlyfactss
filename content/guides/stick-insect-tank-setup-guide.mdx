# Stick Insect Enclosure Setup: Height, Humidity, and the Right Host Plant

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Stick insect care is refreshingly simple once the enclosure height and humidity balance are right, this is genuinely one of the more forgiving pets on this site to house well. For what all of it costs, see our [cost guide](/blog/stick-insect-cost-guide/).

## Enclosure Size

A useful rule: length and width should each be at least twice the adult's body length, and height should be at least three times body length. Height is the detail that actually matters most, since it's what allows a proper molt. A single Indian stick insect does fine in a roughly 8 by 8 by 12 inch <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0041P5PBE?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Exo Terra Glass Nano Terrarium Kit, 8 x 8 x 12 in">tall, mesh-topped enclosure</AffiliateLink>; a small group or larger species needs correspondingly more room.

## Temperature

Many commonly kept species, Indian stick insects especially, do perfectly well at normal room temperature, roughly 63 to 77F. Tropical species like the giant prickly stick insect prefer it warmer. Supplemental heat is only needed if your space runs consistently cold, and if you do add a small basking bulb, keep it off the mesh top, where the insect hangs, to avoid burns, and turn it off at night.

## Humidity

Varies by species, tropical types want 60 to 80%, while Indian stick insects are considerably more forgiving. Achieve this through daily light misting with dechlorinated or distilled water using a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4vPKMjX" product="Fine mist spray bottle">spray bottle</AffiliateLink>, combined with an inch or two of moisture-holding substrate, and track it with a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3QHrM81" product="Digital thermometer/hygrometer combo">digital hygrometer</AffiliateLink> rather than guessing. Avoid both extremes, too little humidity risks the molting failures covered in our [health issues guide](/blog/stick-insect-health-issues-guide/), too much invites mold.

## Substrate

<AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CY23K32S?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="VCEPJH Loose Coconut Fiber Substrate, 2 Pack">Coconut coir</AffiliateLink>, peat moss, or plain paper towel all work, holding humidity and absorbing waste. Replace it regularly rather than letting it build up.

## Lighting

No UVB is needed for this species. Normal ambient room light on a roughly 12-hour cycle is sufficient. A grow light is only worth adding if you're keeping live plants in the enclosure or the room is genuinely dark.

<FunFact>
A stick insect's enclosure height matters more than almost any other dimension. This species hangs from the top of its home to molt, and shedding from a mesh lid succeeds far more reliably than shedding from a branch alone.
</FunFact>

## The Non-Obvious Requirement: A Mesh Top, and the Right Host Plant

A mesh or netting lid is essential, not just for ventilation, but because stick insects hang from the top of the enclosure to molt, and shedding from mesh succeeds far more reliably than from a branch alone. Diet is genuinely species-specific: bramble or blackberry, oak, rose, ivy, privet, and eucalyptus are all used by different species, matching the right host plant to your specific stick insect matters, and always wash foliage first to remove pesticide residue or hitchhiking insects.
