# Common Praying Mantis Health Issues (Molting Is Where Nearly Everything Goes Wrong)

<VetDisclaimer />
<AffiliateDisclosure />

Exotic vets rarely treat invertebrates, so this list leans almost entirely on prevention rather than treatment. Praying mantis health comes down almost entirely to two things: humidity and undisturbed molting. Get those right, and this is a genuinely low-maintenance pet for the short time you'll have it. See our [Praying Mantis enclosure setup guide](/blog/praying-mantis-tank-setup-guide/) for the husbandry side, and our complete [Praying Mantis care guide](/guides/praying-mantis/) for everything else.

## Mismolt (Failed Molt)

This is the single biggest cause of death in captive mantises, and it's largely preventable. During a molt, a mantis sheds its entire exoskeleton, and if anything goes wrong during that process, the outcome can range from a permanently twisted limb to death.

Watch for an incomplete shed, limbs that come out twisted or stuck, or a mantis that appears to be struggling and not progressing during what should be a molt.

Causes include humidity that's too low, dehydration, not enough vertical space for the mantis to hang freely while shedding, or physical disturbance, including handling, during the process. This is manageable primarily through prevention, correct humidity and adequate <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3QQNV3L" product="Climbing branch (Mopani wood)">space to hang from</AffiliateLink>, rather than treatment after the fact, there's little you can do once a molt has genuinely failed. A mantis that survives a mismolt with a crooked limb may need hand-feeding going forward, since the deformity can make normal hunting difficult, a pair of <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DCJXXMCT?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Entomology Forceps Set (fine-tip)">fine-tip feeding tongs</AffiliateLink> makes that much easier to do safely.

<FunFact>
A mantis mid-molt hangs upside down from a branch while its old exoskeleton splits and it pulls itself free entirely, soft-bodied and temporarily defenseless. If it loses its grip during that narrow window, the fall alone can be enough to cause a permanent deformity or worse.
</FunFact>

## Dehydration

From insufficient misting. Contributes directly to mismolt risk, and is manageable simply by correcting your misting schedule and overall humidity with a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4vPKMjX" product="Fine mist spray bottle">reliable mist bottle</AffiliateLink>, covered in more depth in our [Praying Mantis enclosure setup guide](/blog/praying-mantis-tank-setup-guide/).

## Digestive Infection From Excess Humidity

The opposite mistake causes its own problem. Over-misting combined with poor ventilation invites mold and internal infection, which is often undetectable until it's already fatal. Prevention is a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0041P5PBE?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Exo Terra Glass Nano Terrarium Kit, 8 x 8 x 12 in">mesh, not solid, enclosure</AffiliateLink> and a properly balanced humidity level, not simply misting as much as possible.

## Fall Injuries During Molting

From inadequate hanging space or branches that don't provide secure enough grip. Results in deformity or death depending on severity, and prevention comes down entirely to the enclosure setup, specifically the height and climbing surfaces covered in our [Praying Mantis enclosure setup guide](/blog/praying-mantis-tank-setup-guide/).

## Appetite Changes Worth Understanding Correctly

A mantis that stops eating or occasionally regurgitates isn't always sick. Refusing food is often completely normal pre-molt behavior, not illness. Overfeeding, on the other hand, can itself cause regurgitation. If your mantis seems reluctant to eat, offering a different type of prey sometimes helps, and a small drop of honey can provide a quick energy boost if needed.

## The Husbandry Pattern Worth Remembering

Humidity that's too low is the single biggest driver of the mismolt risk that accounts for most mantis deaths. Humidity that's too high, in the opposite direction, invites mold and internal infection instead. And handling or leaving live prey in during the pre-molt window, covered in our [Praying Mantis handling guide](/blog/praying-mantis-handling-guide/), directly risks injury during the animal's single most vulnerable moment. Nearly everything that goes wrong with a pet mantis traces back to getting that balance right.
