# Four Pets, Four Different Right Numbers

Ask "how many do I need" and each of these gives you a completely different answer, and getting that number wrong is the single biggest mistake you can make with any of them.

## Boa Constrictor: One Snake, But Not Always One Person

A single boa is the right number of snakes. What changes is the number of people needed to handle it safely. Once a boa passes about 6 feet, handling calls for a second person, not because the snake is aggressive, most are genuinely docile, but because a large constrictor is strong enough that a one-person rule stops being adequate.

## Sugar Glider: Never One, Always at Least Two

Sugar gliders are so social that a single glider is a genuine welfare problem, not just a lonelier pet. Budget for a pair from the start, on top of checking whether they're even legal where you live, several states ban them outright.

[Cost](/blog/sugar-glider-cost-guide/) · [Handling](/blog/sugar-glider-handling-guide/) · [Health Issues](/blog/sugar-glider-health-issues-guide/) · [Cage Setup](/blog/sugar-glider-tank-setup-guide/)

## Praying Mantis: Always Exactly One

The opposite rule, and just as strict. Mantises are solitary and cannibalistic, one per enclosure, always, with a single narrow exception for brief, supervised mating that still carries real risk. They're also the shortest-lived pet on this entire site, 6 to 12 months is typical, so the "right number" question resolves itself fast either way.

[Cost](/blog/praying-mantis-cost-guide/) · [Handling](/blog/praying-mantis-handling-guide/) · [Health Issues](/blog/praying-mantis-health-issues-guide/) · [Enclosure Setup](/blog/praying-mantis-tank-setup-guide/)

## Neon Tetra: Six Is the Floor, Not a Suggestion

A neon tetra kept alone or in a pair isn't really living as this species is built to live. Six is the minimum for genuine schooling behavior, ten or more is better, and a tank stocked below that number produces pale, stressed fish rather than the confident, colorful group this species is actually capable of.

<FunFact>
Line up the commitments and the contrast is almost absurd: a praying mantis is unlikely to see its own first birthday, while a well-kept boa constrictor can live 20 to 30 years, sometimes past 40. Two pets sold in the same kind of shop, on completely opposite ends of the lifespan scale, and neither one's "right number" has anything to do with how long you'll have it.
</FunFact>

## The Common Thread

None of these numbers are flexible guidelines you can round down from convenience. A boa under-handled by a person short is a safety risk. A solo sugar glider is a welfare problem. Two mantises in one enclosure is a death sentence for one of them. And a tetra school of three isn't really a school at all. Know the actual number before you buy, not after.
