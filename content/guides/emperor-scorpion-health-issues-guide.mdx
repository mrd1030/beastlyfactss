# Common Emperor Scorpion Health Issues and What Causes Them

<VetDisclaimer />
<AffiliateDisclosure />

Exotic vets experienced with invertebrates are uncommon, so this list leans heavily on prevention rather than treatment. Emperor scorpions are genuinely hardy when humidity and substrate depth are right, and most problems trace directly back to one of those two things being off.

## Dehydration

The most common thing that goes wrong with this animal, and it tracks directly back to where the species evolved: hot, humid forest, not the dry habitat most people picture when they hear the word scorpion. Watch for lethargy, a shriveled appearance, sunken features, and reduced activity. Humidity that's too low, inadequate misting, or overly dry substrate are the usual causes, and the [70 to 90% target](/blog/emperor-scorpion-tank-setup-guide/) is the number that keeps it from happening. Caught early, this is manageable by increasing humidity and providing a shallow water source, but advanced cases can be fatal. ## Failed or Incomplete Molts

Scorpions are at their most vulnerable during and immediately after molting, and this is genuinely life-threatening if it goes wrong. A 2024 study in the peer-reviewed journal *Insects* (Guo et al.), on a different scorpion species, *Mesobuthus martensii*, but describing biology that applies broadly across scorpions, found that "the new exoskeleton is too soft to provide protection against predators and conspecifics" immediately after a molt. Watch for difficulty shedding the old exoskeleton, stuck pieces of shed, and lethargy around molt time. Incorrect humidity, whether too low or fluctuating, poor nutrition, and disturbance during the vulnerable pre- and post-molt window all raise risk. A stable, thermostat-controlled setup removes one major variable, a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/44hAxJ1" product="Under-tank heat mat + thermostat kit">heat mat and thermostat</AffiliateLink> keeps temperatures from swinging at the worst possible time.

Remove live prey and avoid handling entirely during this period. An insect left in the enclosure can injure a molting scorpion that's unable to defend itself. Incomplete molts often need experienced intervention and are frequently fatal without it. The rule that holds for [every molting pet invertebrate](/blog/invertebrate-molting-guide/) holds here: no untrained hand pulls at the old exoskeleton. Stuck means half in and half out with no progress for hours, struggling that goes nowhere, or legs left twisted at the end, and that is the point to call an experienced keeper or an exotic vet.

## How Many Molts, and When They Stop

Scorpions molt an average of five times before reaching maturity, with a range of four to nine across species, and an emperor scorpion is sexually mature by about 4 years of age. Maturity ends it. There are no reports of a scorpion molting after reaching reproductive maturity, so an adult emperor scorpion bought at full size has finished molting for good. Molt problems are a juvenile and subadult risk, and a mature adult that stops eating, hides and turns dull is not building toward a molt. That adult needs its humidity, water and body condition checked instead.

## What a Keeper Can Do for a Stuck Molt

Very little, and the useful part is supportive:

- Check the temperature and humidity against the enclosure targets and correct them gently.
- Keep the enclosure dark and quiet, and stop handling.
- Take out every live feeder insect.
- Make sure a shallow water dish is in reach, if the scorpion can get to it without drowning risk.

Just as important is what not to try. Do not pull off the retained exoskeleton, do not soak the scorpion, and do not put oils, ointments or household products on it. Repeated failed attempts to stand, twisted limbs, obvious stuck exoskeleton, collapse after the molt, or weakness that worsens over several hours all mean an exotic animal clinic, the sooner the better.

## Mites and Other Parasites

Look for visible mites on the body or in the substrate, excessive grooming behavior, and lethargy. These typically arrive via substrate, feeder insects, or contaminated decor. ## Injuries From Falls, Pinching, or Cage-Mate Aggression

Visible wounds, limping, or missing appendages point here. Improper handling, a fall from height, or housing incompatible individuals together are the common causes. Severity varies widely. Minor injuries often heal on their own, and serious ones can lead to infection or death. This species' tolerance for cage mates is genuinely unusual among predatory arthropods, most of which will eventually kill each other when housed together, which is exactly why crowding or too few hides is the fastest way to turn that tolerance into a real injury. ## Fungal or Bacterial Issues

Less common, but linked to chronically soggy substrate or poor ventilation that lets mold take hold.

## Emergencies vs. Manageable at Home

A stuck molt, severe dehydration, open wounds showing signs of infection, or a complete refusal to eat for an extended period after a molt are all high-concern situations. Mild dehydration, minor mites, and early humidity-related lethargy are generally manageable once husbandry is corrected. Notably, the sting itself is rarely what sends an emperor scorpion to the vet. Severe toxicity is unlikely from this species and its relatives, with stings producing localized pain, and it's husbandry failures that drive the list above. Veterinary options for invertebrates remain limited, which is why prevention through correct humidity and substrate depth is the primary strategy here.

## The Husbandry Pattern Worth Remembering

Low or fluctuating humidity leads to dehydration and failed molts, the two most serious issues on this list. Substrate that's too shallow prevents proper burrowing, adding stress and humidity instability on top of that. Leaving live prey in the enclosure during a molt risks real injury or death to a scorpion that can't defend itself normally. And soaking-wet substrate, past properly damp, invites mold and secondary infection.

[Tarantulas](/blog/tarantula-health-issues-guide/) sit in the same position, with dehydration and molting complications at the top of their list too, for the same underlying reasons. Obesity from overfeeding is a preventable risk of its own.
