# Common Emperor Scorpion Health Issues and What Causes Them

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Exotic vets experienced with invertebrates are genuinely uncommon, so this list leans heavily on prevention rather than treatment. Emperor scorpions are genuinely hardy when humidity and substrate depth are right, and most problems trace directly back to one of those two things being off. ## Dehydration

The single most frequently cited issue in emperor scorpion care sheets. Watch for lethargy, a shriveled appearance, sunken features, and reduced activity. Humidity that's too low, inadequate misting, or overly dry substrate are the usual causes. Caught early, this is manageable by increasing humidity and providing a shallow water source, but advanced cases can be fatal. ## Failed or Incomplete Molts

Scorpions are at their most vulnerable during and immediately after molting, and this is genuinely life-threatening if it goes wrong. Watch for difficulty shedding the old exoskeleton, stuck pieces of shed, and lethargy around molt time. Incorrect humidity, whether too low or fluctuating, poor nutrition, and disturbance during the vulnerable pre- and post-molt window all raise risk. A stable, thermostat-controlled setup removes one major variable, a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/44hAxJ1" product="Under-tank heat mat + thermostat kit">heat mat and thermostat</AffiliateLink> keeps temperatures from swinging at the worst possible time.

Remove live prey and avoid handling entirely during this period, an insect left in the enclosure can injure a molting scorpion that's unable to defend itself normally. Incomplete molts often need experienced intervention and are frequently fatal without it.

<FunFact>
A molting emperor scorpion is effectively defenseless, its new exoskeleton is soft and its pincers and sting aren't yet functioning at full strength. That's exactly why keepers pull live feeder insects from the enclosure before a molt, a cricket or roach left behind can seriously injure or kill a scorpion that's temporarily unable to fight back.
</FunFact>

## Mites and Other Parasites

Look for visible mites on the body or in the substrate, excessive grooming behavior, and lethargy. These typically arrive via substrate, feeder insects, or contaminated decor. ## Injuries From Falls, Pinching, or Cage-Mate Aggression

Visible wounds, limping, or missing appendages point here. ## Fungal or Bacterial Issues

Less common, but linked to substrate that's chronically wet rather than properly damp, or poor ventilation that lets mold take hold.

## Emergencies vs. Manageable at Home

A stuck molt, severe dehydration, open wounds showing signs of infection, or a complete refusal to eat for an extended period after a molt are all high-concern situations. Mild dehydration, minor mites, and early humidity-related lethargy are generally manageable once husbandry is corrected. Veterinary options for invertebrates remain genuinely limited, which is exactly why prevention through correct humidity and substrate depth is the primary strategy here, not a backup plan. ## The Husbandry Pattern Worth Remembering

Low or fluctuating humidity leads to dehydration and failed molts, the two most serious issues on this list. Substrate that's too shallow prevents proper burrowing, adding stress and humidity instability on top of that. Leaving live prey in the enclosure during a molt risks real injury or death to a scorpion that can't defend itself normally. And substrate that's too wet, rather than properly damp, invites mold and secondary infection.
