# Madagascar Hissing Cockroach Enclosure Setup: Humidity, Substrate, and Keeping Them From Climbing Out

<AffiliateDisclosure />

A hissing cockroach enclosure is one of the simpler invertebrate setups to get right, with one detail that matters more than anything else: keeping them from climbing out.

<Figure src="/assets/images/madagascar-hissing-cockroach-tank-setup-guide-1.jpg" alt="Labeled diagram of a Madagascar hissing cockroach enclosure showing the petroleum jelly barrier, side-mounted heat mat, and hides" caption="The one detail that matters most here: a petroleum jelly barrier around the top of the enclosure, they can climb smooth glass with ease." shareable />

## Enclosure Size

A 5-gallon tank works as a minimum for a small group, which is how this species is kept: they are social, they huddle together, and a solitary roach is not how they live. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4pjiyeT" product="10 gallon tank">10-20+ gallon tank</AffiliateLink> or plastic tub suits a larger colony better, since floor space matters more than height for this ground-dwelling species. For stocking, healthy colonies run at 2 to 10 adults per gallon of enclosure, with the lower end the better choice for a group with several adult males, which each need room to hold a territory.

Place the enclosure away from fans, air vents, ultrasonic humidifiers and anything else that vibrates. Cockroaches are sensitive to vibration, and a tank on top of a humming appliance is a constant disturbance.

## Ventilation

A hisser enclosure has to hold humidity and move air at the same time, because humid, stagnant air is how mold takes hold. The spec is simple:

- **Every opening covered in fine mesh.** Hissers climb glass and plastic, and newborn nymphs are small, so any ventilation hole or screened panel needs mesh fine enough that a nymph cannot pass through it. Fine metal mesh over drilled holes in a tub lid works, as does a tight-fitting fine-mesh screen lid on a glass tank.
- **Fabric over the mesh if flies are a problem.** A layer of cloth over the vents keeps fruit flies and other small pests from getting in to the food.
- **Airflow matched to the misting.** The air should stay humid without going stale. Walls still beaded with condensation long after a misting, or a musty smell, mean the enclosure needs more mesh-covered openings.

The escape barrier below still matters with a mesh lid, since the lid is only a backup if the roaches reach it.

## Temperature

75 to 85°F for general pet-keeping, room temperature above about 70°F is genuinely fine if you're not actively trying to breed them. Breeding specifically gets triggered around 85 to 95°F, worth knowing if you want to avoid an accidental population boom, or if you're deliberately trying to encourage one. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/44hAxJ1" product="Under-tank heat mat + thermostat kit">thermostat-controlled heat mat</AffiliateLink> on one side of the enclosure creates a useful gradient if you do want warmer conditions.

## Humidity

60 to 70%, maintained through daily misting and a substrate that holds moisture well. A simple humidity chamber, a small container with a damp sponge inside the main enclosure, works well too. Keep the ventilation in step with the moisture: extended dryness hurts breeding, and stale, wet air grows fungus.

## Substrate

<AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01CN8ZRYA?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Zoo Med Eco Earth Loose Coconut Fiber Substrate (8 qt, 2-pack)">Coconut fiber</AffiliateLink>, peat moss, or orchid bark, 2 to 3 inches deep, supports this species' semi-burrowing habits well. Skip cedar chips, which can stunt nymph growth, and straw, which is often treated with insecticide. Baking moistened substrate in the oven above 212°F for at least 30 minutes, stirred now and then, kills most fungi, bacteria and parasites before it goes in. Provide egg-crate stacks or <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3SKmfOx" product="Cork bark round hide">cork bark</AffiliateLink> as hides, they'll use these constantly.

## Telling Males From Females

Worth knowing before you buy, since a mixed-sex group breeds and a same-sex group does not. Males carry a pair of large bumps, the pronotal horns, behind the head, and those structures are much smaller in females. Males also grow larger.

## Lighting

None needed. This species is nocturnal and photophobic, genuinely prefers darkness, and doesn't require any special lighting setup at all.

## The Non-Obvious Requirement: A Petroleum Jelly Barrier

This is the detail that matters most for a successful setup. Madagascar hissing cockroaches are excellent climbers, capable of scaling smooth glass with ease. Smear a band of <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B09HHH2Z9Y?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Amazon Basics Petroleum Jelly, Skin Protectant, 7.5 oz">plain petroleum jelly</AffiliateLink> around the top 2 to 3 inches of the enclosure interior, combined with a secure, tight-fitting lid, and escapes become a non-issue. Skip this step, and you'll likely find roaches outside the enclosure sooner or later, at which point finding them is the same room-by-room search that [any escaped invertebrate](/blog/invertebrate-quarantine-cleaning-and-escapes-guide/) calls for.

<FunFact>
Their sticky foot pads and leg hooks, the same grip that makes gentle handling important, are what lets them scale smooth glass with ease.
</FunFact>

One compliance note worth knowing: four jurisdictions bar this species outright and Florida requires a permit from its agriculture department rather than its wildlife agency, so [check where your state stands](/blog/madagascar-hissing-cockroach-legal-guide/) before ordering.
