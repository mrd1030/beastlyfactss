# Hognose Snake Feeding: Prey Size, Schedules, and the Picky Hatchling

<VetDisclaimer />

A western hognose hatchling can weigh three grams. At that size the question is not which mouse to buy but which part of one, and the sizing charts start exactly there: for a hog of 3 grams and under, a small pinky head.

## Prey Size

The chart runs by weight, which is more useful than by age for an animal that arrives at wildly different sizes.

- 3 grams and under: a small pinky head
- 4 to 6 grams: extra small pinks
- 7 to 9 grams: small to medium pinks
- Larger juveniles: large pinks to peach fuzzy, then peach fuzzy to fuzzy

Past the hatchling stage, sizing goes by proportion instead: a prey item slightly thicker than the snake's center mass. The progression runs from pinky mice at hatching up through fuzzies and hoppers. Where it ends depends on the animal: a 70 gram male may settle on a hopper or a couple of fuzzies, while a large adult female works up to considerably bigger prey.

Mice are the staple and the best diet for this species, with larger individuals sometimes taking smaller rats. Gerbils, hamsters, multimammate mice and chicks are fallbacks for a snake that will not take anything else.

## How Often, and Why Adults Eat Less

Young snakes eat every 5 to 7 days. Adult males of 70 grams and up take a hopper or a couple of fuzzies as their metabolism slows, and mature females move to every 10 to 14 days, with some large females going 14 to 21. The simpler version of the same schedule is hatchlings weekly and adults once every two weeks, because an adult fed weekly gets fat.

Every two weeks works on either. The reason they slow down is the most common husbandry mistake with this species, an adult kept on a hatchling's schedule getting fat, and the general reptile version of the point is that diets high in protein may predispose them to uric acid accumulation, which can cause gout.

## Frozen Thawed

Thawed prey over live, without exception.

The reason is straightforward: a live rodent can injure a snake that does not immediately subdue it, and a three gram hatchling taking a pinky head has no margin for that. Thawed prey also lets the keeper control temperature and size precisely.

## The Refusing Hatchling

This species has a reputation for it, and a hatchling that turns down a meal is not automatically in trouble.

One practical threshold: start scenting if a snake refuses its first three meal offerings. The scents that work are fish (sardine, tuna, salmon), chicken bone broth, and frog, toad, lizard or gecko, with commercial scent strips as a packaged option. The amphibian scents map onto what the species evolved eating, which is why they work on an animal ignoring a plain pinky.

Two other causes are seasonal. A male ready to breed may refuse, and seasonal change heading toward brumation can put a snake off food on its own. Neither is fixed by offering more food.

A refusal that continues past that, or comes with weight loss, is a vet question, no longer a feeding one.

## Feed Off the Substrate

Feed in a separate container, away from the loose, dry, diggable substrate this species needs. A snake that takes a mouse with a mouthful of substrate attached is the impaction case, and the [tank setup guide](/blog/hognose-snake-tank-setup-guide/) explains why the substrate has to be deep and loose in the first place.

That is also the one place where the display enclosure and the feeding setup should look nothing alike. Plain, lined, boring, easy to clean.
