# Savannah Monitor Enclosure Setup: The 8x4x4 Foot Reality

<AffiliateDisclosure />

A savannah monitor enclosure needs to support this species' size, digging instincts, and thermoregulation needs, cutting corners on any of these is what leads to the health and behavioral problems covered elsewhere in this series.

What all of it costs is in our [cost guide](/blog/savannah-monitor-cost-guide/).

## Enclosure Size

A hatchling can start around 36x24x20 inches, but a single adult needs an 8-foot by 4-foot by 4-foot enclosure at minimum, this is the current welfare standard and reflects genuine need, not excess. Floor space matters more than height for this ground-dwelling species. House one monitor per enclosure.

True adult-sized housing for this species is typically a DIY build or ordered direct from a manufacturer.

## Temperature

Basking surface temperature should reach 140 to 150F, measured directly with an <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4dEII7J" product="Etekcity Infrared Thermometer Laser Temperature Gun">infrared thermometer</AffiliateLink> rather than an air temperature reading. Cool side should run 75 to 85F, with nighttime dropping to around 70 to 75F. Use a cluster of <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DJRLWX1H?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Arcadia GoldenSun Basking Halogen Lamp 100W (PAR38 Heat Flood)">halogen flood bulbs</AffiliateLink> on a thermostat for basking heat, this species needs multiple bulbs working together to create adequate heat across a large enclosure. Avoid ceramic heat emitters, red or blue bulbs, or heat mats as the primary basking heat source, they don't provide the intensity this species needs.

## Humidity

Around 50% ambient, with access to a genuinely humid burrow where conditions run considerably wetter. Achieve this by pouring water directly into the deep substrate in one area and providing a humid hide, rather than trying to raise ambient humidity across the whole enclosure.

## Substrate

Deep is the operative word here, 12 to 24 inches or more of a diggable soil, sand, and clay mix that genuinely holds a burrow shape. A topsoil and play sand blend, or a product like Zoo Med Excavator Clay, both work. A <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07Z5CJ573?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="The Bio Dude Terra Sahara Bioactive Reptile Substrate, 36 qt">bioactive-style substrate</AffiliateLink> is a functional option here too, though multiple bags will be needed to reach adequate depth. Avoid shallow substrate or loose, particulate-only setups that collapse rather than holding tunnels.

## Lighting

Strong UVB is best practice for this species: a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3TipZXE" product="UVB - Arcadia Desert 12% (ProT5 kit, 22in/24W)">T5 HO bulb in the 10 to 12% range</AffiliateLink>, spanning roughly half the enclosure, positioned 14 to 16 inches from the basking area. Pair with a bright 6500K daylight bulb. Adjust photoperiod seasonally, around 11 hours in winter, 13 in summer.

## The Non-Obvious Requirement: A Real Water Basin

Provide a water basin large enough for your monitor to fully submerge its entire body, not just a shallow dish. Combined with the humid burrow, this genuinely matters for hydration and shedding, a big, dry enclosure with just a small water bowl isn't an adequate setup for this species regardless of how large the dry footprint is.
