# Common Madagascar Hissing Cockroach Health Issues (Prevention Does Almost All the Work)

<VetDisclaimer />
<AffiliateDisclosure />

Madagascar hissing cockroaches are hardy insects that live 2 to 5 years, and very few vets treat invertebrates at all, so their health care is mostly prevention. Nearly everything that goes wrong traces back to humidity, crowding or basic enclosure hygiene, all easy to get right once you know what to watch for.

## Desiccation (Too Dry)

Low humidity causes real problems with molting and can lead to death if left uncorrected. The fix is straightforward, maintain 60 to 70% humidity through regular misting and a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01CN8ZRYA?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Zoo Med Eco Earth Loose Coconut Fiber Substrate (8 qt, 2-pack)">moisture-holding coconut fiber substrate</AffiliateLink>, both of which are [set when the enclosure is built](/blog/madagascar-hissing-cockroach-tank-setup-guide/) rather than corrected later.

## Molting Complications

Nymphs molt six times on the way to adulthood, a process that usually takes 6 to 7 months, and each molt is the most vulnerable point in a hisser's life. A freshly molted cockroach is soft and pale white for several hours after shedding, until the new exoskeleton hardens and darkens. Never handle a roach during or immediately after a molt, since it can be injured easily while the exoskeleton is still soft. Overcrowding and low humidity both make molting problems more likely, and a hungry colony will eat a soft, freshly molted member, which is one more reason food should never run out. Adults do not molt, so a damaged adult carries the damage for life.

## Overcrowding

A colony that outgrows its enclosure shows it in the animals before anything else looks wrong. Crowding in cockroaches disturbs their daily rhythm, raises the rate of fighting, slows growth, and raises deaths. The signs to look for are undersized adults and roaches with damaged legs, antennae or exoskeletons, and in a well-kept colony adult size holds steady from one generation to the next. Healthy colonies run at 2 to 10 adults per gallon of enclosure. A colony past that needs a bigger enclosure, a split into two, or separate-sex housing to stop the births.

## Mold, and the Mites That Are Not a Problem

Mold comes from uneaten wet food combined with poor ventilation, and it can devastate a colony if it takes hold. Prevention is routine maintenance: remove any uneaten fresh food within 24 to 48 hours, keep the enclosure well ventilated, and do a periodic deep clean. For that, move the roaches out, disinfect the empty enclosure with a 10% bleach solution, and let it dry completely before they go back in.

Small mites clustered at the base of a hisser's legs are usually not a health problem. The species that lives on this cockroach, *Androlaelaps schaeferi*, does not parasitize it. It clusters between the legs, feeding on saliva and food debris, and by cleaning up that debris it removes a place where fungi can grow. The mites can look unsightly, but they do not seem to shorten a roach's life, and regular cleaning keeps their numbers down if they bother the keeper. What they do not call for is a mite spray: nearly every insecticide is as lethal to the roaches as to the mites, the same [pesticide hazard](/blog/invertebrate-pesticide-hazards-guide/) that applies to every pet invertebrate.

## Overheating

A heat mat used without a thermostat is a real risk, capable of overheating the roaches or even melting a plastic tub enclosure. If you're using supplemental heat, mainly relevant for breeding rather than casual pet-keeping, always run it through a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/44hAxJ1" product="Under-tank heat mat + thermostat kit">thermostat</AffiliateLink>.

## Injury From Mishandling

Not a disease, but the most common keeper-caused harm on this list. A dropped or forcefully pulled roach can be maimed. Most harm here is entirely preventable with gentle, unhurried handling.

## The Husbandry Pattern Worth Remembering

Humidity that's too low causes desiccation and molting problems, the most serious issues this species faces. Wet, uneaten food combined with poor airflow invites mold that can spread through an entire colony. Too many roaches for the space shows up as small, damaged adults. And unthermostatted heat is a real overheating risk.
