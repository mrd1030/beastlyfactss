# Common Corydoras Catfish Health Issues

<VetDisclaimer />
<AffiliateDisclosure />

Corydoras catfish are genuinely sensitive to two things above all else, substrate texture and water quality, and understanding that connection explains most of what shows up on this list. Our [Corydoras Catfish tank setup guide](/blog/corydoras-catfish-tank-setup-guide/) covers the prevention side in detail, and our full [Corydoras Catfish care guide](/guides/corydoras-catfish/) covers everything else.

## Barbel Erosion and Infection

This is genuinely the signature cory health issue. Watch for barbels, the whisker-like feelers around the mouth, that appear shortened, red, or are missing entirely. It's caused by a combination of sharp substrate causing physical damage and elevated nitrates allowing bacterial infection to take hold in the wound.

Manageable at home if caught reasonably early: switch to soft sand if you haven't already, do a significant water change, and keep nitrates under 20 ppm going forward. Barbels can regrow over 4 to 8 weeks once conditions improve. Left untreated, this can progress to the more serious condition below.

<FunFact>
Barbels can regrow over 4 to 8 weeks once conditions genuinely improve, a reminder that this species' most common health issue is largely reversible if you catch it before it progresses into red blotch disease.
</FunFact>

## Red Blotch Disease

Red or bloody-looking sores on the belly and flanks, often linked to a bacterial infection and frequently triggered by the stress of shipping or a new environment. Catch this early and treat promptly, fixing water quality alongside a broad-spectrum antibiotic, often dosed at half the standard rate given how medication-sensitive this species is, more on that below. A vet with aquatic experience is worth consulting if one's available.

## Ich (White Spot Disease)

Small, pinhead-sized white spots, triggered by stress, temperature swings, or a recently added fish. Treatable, gradually raise the tank temperature toward 82°F with strong aeration, but use reduced medication doses, this species doesn't tolerate full-strength treatments the way many hardier fish do.

## Fin Rot

Frayed fins with white or discolored edges, from poor water quality allowing bacteria to take hold. Manageable at home with water changes and an antibacterial treatment, alongside genuinely fixing whatever water quality issue caused it.

## A Genuinely Important Medication Note

Corydoras are armored catfish with sensitive, naked bellies, and they're notably more medication-sensitive than many community fish. When treating any illness, use reduced doses, sometimes half or even a quarter of the standard amount, and avoid full-strength copper, malachite green, or formalin treatments, which this species tolerates poorly.

## The Husbandry Pattern Worth Remembering

Sharp substrate combined with elevated nitrates causes the barbel erosion that's this species' most defining health risk. General stress (including from too small a school, see our [Corydoras Catfish handling guide](/blog/corydoras-catfish-handling-guide/) for the schooling requirement) and poor water quality drive red blotch disease and fin rot. Getting substrate and water quality right, both covered in our [Corydoras Catfish tank setup guide](/blog/corydoras-catfish-tank-setup-guide/), prevents nearly everything on this list before it starts.
