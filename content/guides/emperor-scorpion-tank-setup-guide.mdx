# Emperor Scorpion Enclosure Setup: Deep Substrate, High Humidity, No UVB

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Emperor scorpion husbandry is simple once you internalize two priorities: substrate depth for burrowing, and humidity that stays consistently high.

<Figure src="/assets/images/emperor-scorpion-tank-setup-guide-1.jpg" alt="Labeled diagram of an emperor scorpion enclosure showing deep burrowing substrate, high humidity, multiple hides, and a warm side heat mat" caption="Substrate depth for burrowing, and humidity that stays consistently high, are the two priorities that matter most." shareable />

## Size

A <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B09MQBB6CP?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Reptile Growth Reptile Terrarium, 10 Gallon Tank 20in x 10in x 11in for Invertebrates">10-gallon tank</AffiliateLink>, roughly 20x10x12 inches, is the commonly accepted minimum for one adult. For groups or extra enrichment, step up to 20 to 30 gallons or more, providing more horizontal floor space and, critically, more hides than the number of animals if you're cohabitating. A secure, tight-fitting lid is essential, these are capable climbers and genuine escape artists. Cohabitation is possible but not always successful. The University of Kentucky's Department of Entomology notes that emperor scorpions living together without hurting each other is "very unique" behavior, since "most predatory arthropods will kill each other when housed together," so monitor closely for aggression and make sure resources, hides, food, space, are genuinely sufficient for the group.

## Temperature

An ambient range of 75 to 85°F is the commonly cited target, with some sources extending that to 70 to 90°F as long as a gradient exists. Many keepers use a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/44hAxJ1" product="Under-tank heat mat + thermostat kit">side-mounted heat mat on a thermostat</AffiliateLink> to create a mild warm side, around 85 to 90°F at its warmest point, while leaving a cooler area available. Avoid high heat, these are tropical forest animals that prefer moderate, stable warmth rather than the intense basking many desert reptiles need.

## Humidity: The Priority That Matters Most

Target 70 to 90%, with many keepers aiming around 75 to 80% as a practical sweet spot. That range reflects the animal's native range: Animal Diversity Web (University of Michigan) describes emperor scorpions as typically found in "hot and humid forests," and the University of Kentucky's Department of Entomology likewise places them in warm, humid forests in western Africa, not the dry habitat most people associate with scorpions. Achieve this by keeping the substrate damp, not soggy, and misting as needed, often daily or every other day depending on your enclosure's ventilation and your local climate. Monitor with a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3QHrM81" product="Digital thermometer/hygrometer combo">digital hygrometer</AffiliateLink> rather than guessing. Condensation on the walls or visible mold means it's too wet, dry, cracked substrate means it's too dry. Sphagnum moss placed in the enclosure can help retain moisture between mistings.

## Substrate: Deep Enough to Burrow In

This is the other non-negotiable. Provide a minimum of 5 to 6 inches of <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CY23K32S?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="VCEPJH Loose Coconut Fiber Substrate, 2 Pack">substrate</AffiliateLink>, some keepers prefer deeper still, since this species genuinely uses that depth for burrowing, not just decoration. Coco fiber, peat, soil-based mixes, vermiculite blends, or commercial products all work well. Keep it damp but well-aerated, and refresh it periodically to prevent mold and mite buildup. Avoid pure sand or anything dry and dusty.

## Lighting

No UVB is required. Emperor scorpions are nocturnal and don't rely on UV exposure for vitamin D synthesis the way many reptiles do. A normal 12-hour light, 12-hour dark photoperiod works well, using ambient room light or a low-output LED if you want visibility for viewing or to support live plants in a bioactive setup. Avoid bright, continuous lighting, it genuinely stresses a nocturnal animal that's adapted to darkness for most of its active hours.

Some keepers add a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07TQKY1GC?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="uvBeast V3 385-395nm Black Light UV Flashlight">UV/black light</AffiliateLink> purely for viewing, not husbandry. It's entirely optional, but it does reveal one of the more striking things about this species.

<FunFact>
Emperor scorpions fluoresce a brilliant blue-green under UV/black light. Scientists aren't entirely sure why, but the compound responsible sits in the hyaline layer of their exoskeleton, and scorpions do this under natural UV from moonlight too, not just an artificial black light.
</FunFact>

## Furnishings and a Few Non-Obvious Details

Multiple hides are important, cork bark, half-logs, broken clay pots, or commercial hides all work, and if housing a group, provide more hides than animals. A shallow water dish, deep enough to drink from but not deep enough to risk drowning, changed regularly. Secure lid and smooth sides to prevent escapes, this species climbs more capably than its size suggests. Minimal disturbance during molting, remove live prey and avoid handling entirely for days to weeks around a molt. A 2024 study in the peer-reviewed journal *Insects* found that a freshly molted scorpion's new exoskeleton "is too soft to provide protection against predators and conspecifics," which is exactly why this is the single most vulnerable period in this animal's life. Bioactive setups work well for this species with a proper cleanup crew and adequate drainage. They rearrange decor, secure anything heavy so it can't tip or fall.
