# How Much Does a Zebra Finch Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Zebra finches are among the cheapest pet birds you can buy, but the detail that reshapes the budget is that you can never buy just one.

## The Birds: $15 to $60 Each

Zebra finch pricing varies a fair amount by seller. Online aviaries selling young, unweaned pairs price them lowest, The Animal Store lists very young pairs at $29 total, roughly $15 a bird. Independent sellers commonly land around $45 to $50 each, and big-box chains like Petco run closer to $60 per bird.

That per-bird price is one of the lowest of any pet bird. The real cost decision isn't the price tag, it's the minimum order.

## Why You Can't Buy Just One

Zebra finches are a flock-oriented species. Unlike a budgie or lovebird, which can bond one-on-one with a human keeper if kept solo, a lone zebra finch doesn't reliably transfer that social need onto a person. The RSPCA's guidance on zebra finch housing is direct about it: the species "is extremely social and gregarious, and should never be housed singly without compelling scientific or veterinary justification."

This is also where the species diverges most from the [canary](/blog/canary-cost-guide/), the other small finch-type cage bird. A canary is typically bought and kept solo (or a same-sex pair for males, since two males will fight). A zebra finch should never be kept solo at all. Budget for at least two birds from the start, roughly $30 to $120 for a starting pair depending on where you buy, more for a trio or small group.

<FunFact>
Zebra finches are monogamous and pair for life once bonded, and a mismatched or overcrowded group can turn stressful for the birds involved. That's part of why most care sources recommend starting with a same-sex pair or a proper male-female pair rather than guessing at group dynamics.
</FunFact>

The cage is the single biggest line item, and it's worth spending real money on. PetMD recommends at least 24 inches long by 14 inches wide by 18 inches tall for a pair, with bar spacing no wider than 3/8 inch, but a genuine flight cage that lets the birds fly the length of it rather than just hop between two perches is a meaningfully better setup than the bare minimum.

## Ongoing Costs: Roughly $10 to $25 a Month

Seed mix or pellet food, fresh greens, an occasional millet spray treat, and cuttlebone or mineral block replacement make up the monthly total for a pair.

## Vet Costs

Avian vets are a smaller, harder-to-find specialty than general small-animal vets, and pricing reflects that. VCA Hospitals recommends the first veterinary visit happen within 1 to 2 weeks of bringing a bird home, with at least annual checkups after that (twice yearly is their stated preference). Zebra finches are also a prey species that hides illness well, which is why routine checkups matter more than waiting for obvious symptoms.

## Lifespan

Commonly cited as 5 to 10 years in captivity with good care, occasionally longer, against just 2 to 3 years in the wild according to Animal Diversity Web. Lafeber's own species profile is more conservative, citing roughly 3 to 5 years typically, so treat the upper end of the range as achievable with excellent care rather than guaranteed. Either way, it's a genuine multi-year commitment for a bird that's often bought on impulse simply because it's so cheap.

## The Cost Decision: You Can't Buy Just One

The bird itself is the cheap part, and it's easy to walk into a pet store, see a $20 price tag, and buy a single finch on the spot. The actual budgeting decision that matters is committing to at least a pair from day one, since a solo zebra finch is a genuinely different, harder situation than a solo budgie or a solo canary.
