# Fire-Bellied Toad Enrichment: Plants Beat Pipes, and Someone Tested It

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Amphibian enrichment research is thin enough that a small preference test counts as a real result. This one is directly actionable.

<KeyTakeaway>
Given a straight choice between artificial plants, PVC pipes and open space, aquatic frogs picked the plants, by around 40 percent over the pipes, with both beating open water. PVC is the default hide in amphibian keeping because it is cheap and hoses out. The frogs disagreed.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Research Says

Nothing on *Bombina*. Two borrowed threads, both from other frogs.

**A preference test.** Ramos and Ortiz-Díez divided tanks into six sections and rotated enrichment elements, counting where African clawed frogs positioned themselves three times a day for six weeks. The frogs **preferred artificial plants over PVC pipes by around 40 percent**, and preferred both over open space, regardless of where the items were placed. The authors concluded plants should replace the PVC that laboratory housing defaults to.

**Cover does physiological work.** The tree frog plant-cover study found providing plants affected body size, growth rate and skin bacterial communities, covered in full in our [White's Tree Frog enrichment guide](/blog/whites-tree-frog-enrichment-guide/).

A clawed frog is fully aquatic and a fire-bellied toad is semi-aquatic, which makes this a shorter borrow than most in this series. The shared question is what an amphibian does with a water section, and the answer is that it wants planting in it.

<FunFact>
Preference tests are the honest way to ask an animal a question. You give it the options, you rotate them so position cannot explain the result, and you count. It cannot tell you why the frogs chose plants, and it can tell you they did, repeatedly, for six weeks.
</FunFact>

## A Real Land Section and a Real Water Section

The structural item. Fire-bellied toads use both, moving between them through the day, and the common failure is a token version of one of them.

## Plant the Water, Not Just the Land

Where the evidence points. <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4gmXC4j" product="Artificial/silk terrarium plants">Artificial plants</AffiliateLink> were the tested item, they clean easily, and they are what the frogs chose. Put them in the water section as well as on land, because open water is what the study's frogs avoided.

Live planting works too and adds humidity and biological filtration. Either way the target is cover throughout, so a toad in the water has somewhere to be that is not the middle of an empty pool.

## Water Quality as a Welfare Item

Amphibian skin is a respiratory and osmotic surface. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3Rg5gTT" product="Water conditioner">water conditioner</AffiliateLink> on everything that goes in, plus regular changes and gentle filtration, does more for this species than any object.

Untreated tap water is the fastest way to harm one.

## A Group, Which This Species Suits

One of the few amphibians in the hobby routinely kept in groups. Similar sizes, enough space, enough cover, and you get considerably more behavior to watch than a single animal provides.

The cover requirement is not incidental. A group in a bare enclosure has nowhere to spread out to.

## Feeding, Moved Around

Active diurnal hunters, which makes feeding presentation worth more here than in an ambush species. Release small live insects at the water's edge and into the planting, and vary where they go.

Changing where food appears is one of the few enrichment interventions actually tested in frogs: in dendrobatids, **moving the position of the food dish increased activity**. For an already active toad it is a free way to extend feeding into a search.

## Priority Order

1. A genuine land section and a genuine water section
2. Planting in the water as well as on land
3. Dechlorinated water, changed regularly
4. A group of similar-sized animals
5. Live prey released instead of presented
6. Varying where and when food appears
7. Novelty, last

## What Not to Do

Do not default to PVC pipe as the hide when the tested alternative won by 40 percent. Do not leave the water section as open water. Do not use untreated tap water. Do not mix sizes in a group. Do not handle beyond what is necessary, since they secrete skin irritants and handling damages the mucus layer either way, covered in our handling guide. And do not house them with other amphibian species, because those skin toxins are a real hazard in a shared water body.
