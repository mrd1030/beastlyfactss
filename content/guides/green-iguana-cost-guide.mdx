# How Much Does a Green Iguana Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Green iguanas are cheap to buy as babies, and that low starting price is exactly what leads so many owners to underestimate what they're taking on.

For the full care picture beyond budgeting, see our complete [Green Iguana care guide](/guides/green-iguana/).

## The iguana: $20 to $1,000+

Baby green iguanas are farm-raised and inexpensive, commonly $20 to $100. Color morphs push the price up considerably, albino, hypomelanistic, axanthic, and purple translucent lines commonly run $300 to $1,000 or more. A related but genuinely different, IUCN-endangered species, the blue iguana, can run into the tens of thousands, worth noting so you don't confuse the two when shopping.

## Upfront setup: $700 to $2,500 or more

This is where the real cost of iguana ownership shows up. A proper adult enclosure has to be custom-built or walk-in, no commercial tank is large enough, and by the time you add a cluster of <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DJRLWX1H?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Arcadia GoldenSun Basking Halogen Lamp 100W">halogen basking bulbs</AffiliateLink>, high-output <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/43AcIvA" product='Arcadia T5 UVB Reptile Light Fixture Kit (36" / 14% UVB Dragon)'>UVB</AffiliateLink>, an <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4fibBY2" product="Automatic misting system/fogger">automatic misting system</AffiliateLink>, and a large soaking tub, total setup cost commonly runs well past $700 and often past $2,000.

## Two enclosures, priced separately

The hatchling setup and the adult enclosure are two different purchases a year or two apart, and the second one is the trap. Price both on the day you buy the lizard.

The lighting and the mister carry over to the adult enclosure, so none of it is wasted.

LafeberVet puts an adult at 6 feet or more including the tail and calls for a 6 by 3 by 6 foot arboreal enclosure. ReptiFiles goes further, to 10 by 5 by 6 feet. The two ready made boxes on that table reach 6 or 8 feet long but stop at 2 feet deep and 4 feet tall, so they are a compromise on even the LafeberVet floor. That is what "no commercial tank is large enough" means above: you can buy a box, but not one that meets the minimum. The first enclosure that meets 6x3x6 outright is a made to order box at $3,740, and nothing ready made reaches the ReptiFiles number, which is walk-in territory. A walk-in build from plywood, welded mesh, and hardware runs several hundred dollars in materials by Reptile.Guide's estimate, and a custom builder starts around $1,000 and climbs from there. The [enclosure setup guide](/blog/green-iguana-tank-setup-guide/) covers what goes inside either one.

## Ongoing costs: roughly $60 to $150 a month

Fresh produce, <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0D82DPB1D?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Repashy SuperVeggie, 6 oz Jar">supplements</AffiliateLink>, substrate, and meaningful electricity to run heating and lighting for a large enclosure add up to a genuinely higher monthly cost than most reptiles on this site, produce alone commonly runs $30 to $50 a month, with electricity adding another $20 to $50. One cost aggregator models an all-in annual ownership cost around $2,140 a year once every category is included.

## Vet costs

Reptile vets are specialized, and iguana-specific issues, metabolic bone disease treatment or egg-binding surgery among them, can run into real money. Budget for an annual exam at minimum, and know that emergency care for this species tends toward the expensive end.

## Lifespan: 15 to 25 years, if the iguana survives its first year

Here's the number that should reframe this whole cost conversation. With excellent care, green iguanas live 15 to 20 years, sometimes 25. But inadequate diet, lighting, and housing are so common in this species that Wikipedia's entry on green iguanas in captivity, citing de Vosjoli (1992), states that inadequate care causes an estimated 70% of green iguanas in captivity to die within their first year of life. The low purchase price has nothing to do with what it actually costs, in setup and in animal welfare, to keep this species alive and thriving long-term.

## The honest budgeting takeaway

A green iguana's sticker price is almost irrelevant to what responsible ownership costs. This is one of the more expensive reptiles on this site to house correctly, and the setup investment isn't optional padding, it's the difference between an iguana that reaches its 20-year potential and one that becomes part of that 70% first-year statistic.
