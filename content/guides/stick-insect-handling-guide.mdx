# How to Handle a Stick Insect (And Why You Can Keep More Than One)

Stick insects are docile, slow, and genuinely low-drama to keep, relying entirely on camouflage and stillness rather than any real defense. This makes them one of the gentler invertebrates on this site to interact with.

## The Right Way to Handle One

Gently grasp the body or thorax if you need to move a stick insect, and never grab a leg. Most species practice autotomy, deliberately dropping a leg to escape a threat, and a leg lost this way doesn't fully regenerate in adults, only partially across molts in younger animals. Some species also have mildly irritating leg spines or can release a faint defensive scent, minor compared to almost anything else on this site, but worth a gentle approach regardless.

<FunFact>
Many stick insect species can deliberately drop a leg to escape a threat, a defense called autotomy, but that dropped leg only partially regrows across molts in younger animals and doesn't come back at all once the insect reaches adulthood.
</FunFact>

## A Genuinely Social Invertebrate, Unlike the Mantis

This is the detail that sets stick insects apart from the [praying mantis](/blog/praying-mantis-handling-guide/) already covered on this site. Mantises are solitary and cannibalistic, always housed one per enclosure. Stick insects are the opposite: herbivorous, non-aggressive toward each other, and Indian stick insects specifically can be housed in groups without issue. If you're looking for an invertebrate you can keep as a small colony rather than a single animal, this is genuinely the better fit.

## A Detail Worth Knowing: Reproduction Without Males

Several commonly kept species, Indian and Vietnamese stick insects among them, reproduce parthenogenetically, meaning females produce viable eggs without a male present at all. This means a single female, or a small group of females, can genuinely establish an ongoing colony on their own, worth knowing if you're not looking to breed but might end up with more stick insects than you started with regardless.

## First-Timer Mistakes

- Grabbing by a leg instead of the body
- Providing an enclosure too short for a proper molt, covered in our [tank setup guide](/blog/stick-insect-tank-setup-guide/)
- Leaving an open water dish, which can drown nymphs, mist for hydration instead
- Feeding the wrong host plant for your specific species
