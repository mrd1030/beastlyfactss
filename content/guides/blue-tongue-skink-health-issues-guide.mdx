# Common Blue-Tongued Skink Health Issues and What Causes Them

<VetDisclaimer />
<AffiliateDisclosure />

Most of what shows up on this list traces back to one of two things: incorrect UVB or temperature, or, specifically for Indonesian skinks, an untreated parasite load from before they ever reached you. In a real sense, a Northern and an Indonesian skink carry two very different health risk profiles under one common name, and knowing which one you have changes almost everything below.

## Metabolic Bone Disease (MBD)

Caused by inadequate UVB and or calcium. Watch for a soft or rubbery jaw, a kinked spine, tremors, and difficulty walking. ## Intestinal Parasites, Especially in Wild-Caught Indonesians

This is the health issue most directly tied to where your skink came from. Most Indonesian skinks sold in the US pet trade are wild-caught, and wild-caught animals commonly carry parasite loads picked up before import.

<FunFact>
Wolf and colleagues, comparing two coprological methods in Acta Veterinaria Scandinavica in 2014, examined reptile faecal samples and reported a total parasite occurrence of 93.2%, with wild-origin animals carrying a wide variety of invasive parasites. Northern skinks, being almost universally captive-bred, carry far lower parasite risk by comparison, one more way "blue-tongued skink" really means two different risk profiles depending on origin.
</FunFact>

Always see a vet for a fecal exam and appropriate deworming, fenbendazole for roundworms and pinworms, praziquantel for tapeworms and flukes, if you've acquired a wild-caught or unspecified-origin skink. This isn't optional preventive care, it's close to a certainty worth budgeting for upfront.

## Respiratory Infection

Nasal or oral discharge, bubbling, open-mouth breathing, and wheezing are the signs. Early cases often resolve once <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3QHrM81" product="Digital thermometer/hygrometer combo">temperature and humidity are corrected</AffiliateLink>, advanced cases need a vet and antibiotics. Incorrect temperature or humidity, or excess dust in the enclosure, are the usual causes.

## Obesity

Blue-tongued skinks are enthusiastic, food-motivated eaters that don't reliably self-regulate portions. This is manageable at home through portion control rather than free-feeding, and it's worth watching from early on rather than after weight becomes a visible problem. The portion that prevents it, roughly the size of the skink's own skull, is in our [feeding guide](/blog/blue-tongue-skink-feeding-guide/) alongside the schedule by age.

## Retained Shed and Scale Rot

Retained shed on the toes or tail comes from low humidity, scale rot on the belly comes from the opposite problem, substrate that's too damp. Both are manageable once humidity is corrected with the right <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CY23K32S?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="VCEPJH Loose Coconut Fiber Substrate, 2 Pack">substrate</AffiliateLink> and a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eKyN0W" product="Large soak-able water dish">properly sized water dish</AffiliateLink>, and a vet visit is warranted if either progresses to infection.

## The Husbandry Pattern Worth Remembering

Weak UVB leads directly to MBD. Skipping quarantine and a fecal exam on a new wild-caught skink is how a parasite problem goes undetected until it's serious. And getting humidity wrong in either direction causes shedding or skin problems. All of this is preventable with the right setup and with buying decisions made with full information about where your skink actually came from.

If wild-caught origin as a health risk is a new concept, it's worth checking the same question for other pets. [Hamsters](/blog/hamster-health-issues-guide/) and [Pacman frogs](/blog/pacman-frog-health-issues-guide/) come with their own version of it, in two very different directions.
