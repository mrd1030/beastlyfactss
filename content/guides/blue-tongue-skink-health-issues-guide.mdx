# Common Blue-Tongued Skink Health Issues and What Causes Them

<VetDisclaimer />
<AffiliateDisclosure />

Most of what shows up on this list traces back to one of two things: incorrect UVB or temperature, or, specifically for Indonesian skinks, an untreated parasite load from before they ever reached you. In a real sense, a Northern and an Indonesian skink carry two very different health risk profiles under one common name, and knowing which one you have changes almost everything below.

## Metabolic Bone Disease (MBD)

Caused by inadequate UVB and or calcium. Watch for a soft or rubbery jaw, a kinked spine, tremors, and difficulty walking. ## Intestinal Parasites, Especially in Wild-Caught Indonesians

This is the health issue most directly tied to where your skink came from. Most Indonesian skinks sold in the US pet trade are wild-caught, and wild-caught animals commonly carry parasite loads picked up before import.

<FunFact>
Wolf and colleagues, comparing two coprological methods in Acta Veterinaria Scandinavica in 2014, examined reptile fecal samples and reported a total parasite occurrence of 93.2%, with wild-origin animals carrying a wide variety of invasive parasites. Northern skinks, being almost universally captive-bred, carry far lower parasite risk by comparison, one more way "blue-tongued skink" really means two different risk profiles depending on origin.
</FunFact>

Always see a vet for a fecal exam and appropriate deworming, fenbendazole for roundworms and pinworms, praziquantel for tapeworms and flukes, if you've acquired a wild-caught or unspecified-origin skink. This isn't optional preventive care, it's close to a certainty worth budgeting for upfront.

## Respiratory Infection

Nasal or oral discharge, bubbling, open-mouth breathing, and wheezing are the signs. Early cases often resolve once <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3QHrM81" product="Digital thermometer/hygrometer combo">temperature and humidity are corrected</AffiliateLink>, advanced cases need a vet and antibiotics. Incorrect temperature or humidity, or excess dust in the enclosure, are the usual causes.

## Obesity

Blue-tongued skinks are enthusiastic, food-motivated eaters that don't reliably self-regulate portions. This is manageable at home through portion control rather than free-feeding, and it's worth watching from early on rather than after weight becomes a visible problem. The portion that prevents it, roughly the size of the skink's own skull, is in the [feeding guide](/blog/blue-tongue-skink-feeding-guide/) alongside the schedule by age.

## Body Condition and Weight

An adult Northern blue-tongue runs about 17 inches long on average, up to 22, and typically weighs 283 to 510 grams (10 to 18 ounces). Whatever the type, a skink's own weight log tells more than any one figure. Weigh on a gram kitchen scale, weekly for a youngster or a skink on a diet, and log it.

Shape tells the rest. A healthy skink has a straight jaw, smooth glossy scales, and a thick tail base, since the tail base is where it stores fat. An overweight skink shows it in proportion: heavy jowls of loose flesh under the chin, legs and a head that look too small for the body, labored breathing, and a lazy skink that moves little. The fix is fewer and smaller meals with more vegetables and less fruit, plus more room and time to move, checked on the weekly weigh-in. If the scale has not moved after a month, a vet should look for an underlying cause. A thinning tail base is the opposite signal, stored fat being used up. Parasites are one cause of weight loss, so a skink that loses weight while eating normally needs a vet and a fecal exam.

## Brumation: Which Skinks, and at What Temperature

Whether a blue-tongue should brumate depends on the species. Northern (*T. scincoides intermedia*), Halmahera, Indonesian (*T. gigas gigas*), Merauke and Irian Jaya skinks are tropical animals and should not be pushed into brumation. When one slows down on its own in late autumn, keep the heat on and let the warm side run down to about 80°F for the 1 to 4 months it lasts. Temperate species, including the eastern (*T. s. scincoides*), blotched, centralian, western and shingleback, can have the heat turned off entirely for a 4 to 6 month brumation, as long as the room never drops below 55°F (13°C).

Either way, the setup is the same. Stop feeding about two weeks before cooling so the gut is empty; food left in a cold gut rots. After the first week of that fast, cut the light to 6 hours a day. During brumation, refresh the water weekly and weigh the skink every two weeks. A brumating skink should lose little or no weight, so a loss of 10% of its starting weight is a reason to call the vet, and so is a skink that has not eaten a month after waking.

## Retained Shed and Scale Rot

Retained shed on the toes or tail comes from low humidity, scale rot on the belly comes from the opposite problem, substrate that's too damp. ## The Husbandry Pattern Worth Remembering

Weak UVB leads directly to MBD. Skipping quarantine and a fecal exam on a new wild-caught skink is how a parasite problem goes undetected until it's serious. And getting humidity wrong in either direction causes shedding or skin problems. All of this is preventable with the right setup and with buying decisions made with full information about where your skink actually came from.
