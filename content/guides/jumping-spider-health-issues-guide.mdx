# Common Jumping Spider Health Issues

<VetDisclaimer />
<AffiliateDisclosure />

Jumping spiders are hardy when humidity stays consistent, and the large majority of health problems in this species trace back to that one factor, either too little or, less commonly, too much.

## Dehydration

The single most common problem, and honestly the leading cause of death in captive jumping spiders. Watch for curled legs and general lethargy. It's caused by humidity running too low or an absence of drinkable water droplets. It's manageable at home, mist the enclosure regularly to the [humidity the enclosure is supposed to hold](/blog/jumping-spider-tank-setup-guide/), and for a spider showing real signs of dehydration, a damp cotton ball setup can help it recover.

## Retained Molt (Dysecdysis)

A spider that gets stuck in its old exoskeleton during a molt can lose limbs or die. This is caused by insufficient humidity in the lead-up to molting. Once a molt has genuinely failed, there's little that can be done. Never feed or disturb a spider that's in or approaching a molt.

<FunFact>
Low humidity alone is directly responsible for both dehydration and failed molts, the two most serious risks a captive jumping spider faces, part of why a simple digital hygrometer is one of the most useful tools a keeper can own.
</FunFact>

## Silk Impaction

A spider that stops producing silk normally. This is genuinely serious and often fatal, and it's tied to chronic low humidity over time.

## Pesticide Exposure

Jerky tremors and loss of coordination point here. It's caused by exposure to household sprays or residue from pet flea treatments, even indirect contact can affect a spider this small. There's no antidote, moving the spider to clean air immediately is the only real response.

## Heat Stress

From direct sunlight or an unregulated heat mat. It's manageable by correcting placement and always running supplemental heat through a thermostat if you use it at all.

## The Husbandry Pattern Worth Remembering

Low humidity is directly responsible for both dehydration and failed molts, the two most serious risks this species faces.
