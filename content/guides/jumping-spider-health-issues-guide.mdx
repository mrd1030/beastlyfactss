# Common Jumping Spider Health Issues

<VetDisclaimer />
<AffiliateDisclosure />

Jumping spiders are hardy when humidity stays consistent, and the large majority of health problems in this species trace back to that one factor, either too little or, less commonly, too much. For the regal jumping spider the target is 50 to 60%, and tropical species such as *Hyllus diardi* need 70 to 80%.

## Dehydration

The single most common problem, and honestly the leading cause of death in captive jumping spiders. Watch for curled legs and general lethargy. It's caused by humidity running too low or an absence of drinkable water droplets. It's manageable at home. Bring the enclosure back to 50 to 60% and mist once or twice a day, leaving droplets on the wall right in front of the spider. For a spider showing real signs of dehydration, use the damp cotton ball setup: a small deli cup lined with damp paper towel, and a cotton ball wetted with dechlorinated water placed so it touches the spider's front legs and pedipalps for 30 to 60 minutes. Mild cases usually recover within 12 to 24 hours.

Curled legs are the sign that dehydration is advanced. A jumping spider extends its legs hydraulically, by pumping hemolymph into them, so when fluid runs low the legs fold under the body.

## Retained Molt (Dysecdysis)

A spider that gets stuck in its old exoskeleton during a molt can lose limbs or die. This is caused by insufficient humidity in the lead-up to molting. Never pull at the old skin: the new one underneath is soft and tears easily. What helps is raising humidity to 70 to 75% by misting more heavily and partly covering the vents, which often softens the old skin enough for the spider to finish on its own within 12 to 24 hours. A molt still stuck after 12 hours of corrected humidity is a vet case. Never feed or disturb a spider that's in or approaching a molt, and remove uneaten prey so nothing chews on a molting spider.

<FunFact>
A jumping spider has no antidote for pesticide. A flea treatment on the family dog can reach a spider that never leaves its enclosure, and clean air is the only treatment there is.
</FunFact>

## Silk Impaction

A spider that stops producing silk normally. This is genuinely serious and often fatal, and it's tied to chronic low humidity over time.

## Pesticide Exposure

Jerky tremors and loss of coordination point here. It's caused by exposure to household sprays or residue from pet flea treatments, even indirect contact can affect a spider this small. There's no antidote, moving the spider to clean air immediately is the only real response.

## Heat Stress

From direct sunlight or an unregulated heat mat. It's manageable by correcting placement and always running supplemental heat through a thermostat if you use it at all.

## Finding a Vet Who Treats Spiders

Few vets see arachnids, so the search is worth doing before a spider is sick. The [Association of Reptile and Amphibian Veterinarians directory](https://arav.org/find-a-vet/) lists exotic vets by region, and some of them treat invertebrates. Call ahead and ask directly whether the practice has seen spiders. Exotic animal hospitals and university veterinary teaching hospitals are the other good starting points, and teaching hospitals sometimes run a service that covers fish, amphibians and invertebrates.

A vet visit makes sense when:

- The spider has not eaten for more than 14 days outside a premolt fast
- There are visible lesions, fungal growth, or leaking fluid
- A molt has been stuck for more than 12 hours despite corrected humidity
- Worms are visible, or the spider shows tremors, seizure-like movement, or partial paralysis
- It keeps declining after temperature, humidity, and ventilation have all been corrected

Bring photos or video of the symptoms, since behavior often stops at the clinic, plus the last molt date, last meal, temperature and humidity readings, and where the feeders came from. Transport the spider in a small ventilated deli cup with a little substrate and a misted paper towel, and keep it at a steady temperature. Expect a diagnosis and a supportive-care plan more than a cure: very few drugs have tested doses for spiders, and some conditions carry a poor outlook whatever the treatment.

## The Husbandry Pattern Worth Remembering

Low humidity is directly responsible for both dehydration and failed molts, the two most serious risks this species faces. Chemical exposure is entirely preventable by keeping cleaning products and treated pets away from the enclosure. And heat stress is avoidable simply by never using unregulated supplemental heat.
