# How Much Does a Leaf-Tailed Gecko Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Leaf-tailed geckos are among the most expensive geckos in the pet trade, and the price reflects both their striking camouflage and the skill involved in breeding them successfully.

## The Gecko: $250 to $800+

Satanic leaf-tailed geckos, the smallest and most dramatically leaf-mimicking species, commonly run $750 to $800. Larger species cost less per animal: Henkel's leaf-tailed geckos run roughly $290 to $500, mossy leaf-tailed around $250, and the giant leaf-tailed species around $350.

<FunFact>
Pricing runs backward from what you'd expect with this genus. The smallest species, the satanic leaf-tailed gecko, is also the most expensive, commonly costing more than double the larger Henkel's, mossy, or giant leaf-tailed geckos.
</FunFact>

## Species and Locality, Not Color Morphs

Unlike many reptiles in the trade, leaf-tailed geckos aren't sold by designer color morph. Pricing is driven by which species you're buying and whether it's captive-bred or wild-caught, not selectively bred color genetics. Natural variation, including bark mimics, dead-leaf mimics, and lichen-covered patterns, exists across the group, but there's no real morph market to speak of.

## Knowing Which Species You Are Buying

The species decides the enclosure size, the temperatures and the humidity, so the name on the listing has to be right. The ones most often offered are, roughly in order of availability, Henkel's (*U. henkeli*), the mossy (*U. sikorae*), the giant (*U. fimbriatus*), the satanic (*U. phantasticus*), the lined (*U. lineatus*) and *U. ebenaui*. Anything else is rare outside serious breeder collections, and a rare name at a common price deserves a second look.

The small leaf-mimics are where mislabeling happens. *U. kelirambo* looks similar to the satanic leaf-tail, and *U. fiera* resembles a large *U. ebenaui*. The satanic leaf-tail carries more and longer spines on the head and body than *U. ebenaui*, but at juvenile size a photo is not proof. A breeder who produced the animal can name the species of both parents and show them.

## Captive-Bred, and How to Tell

Madagascar has largely stopped exporting these geckos, and the few species still exported move under smaller quotas, so most animals for sale today are captive-bred. Wild-caught leaf-tails still appear in small numbers, and imports historically had poor survival, which is where the genus's reputation for being hard to keep came from. They are best left to experienced keepers adding new bloodlines.

A wild-caught adult cannot be aged, and even a good-looking one may be old. The practical test is paperwork: buy directly from the breeder, and expect a hatch date and a parent pair. A seller who cannot give either is selling an animal of unknown age and origin, whatever the listing says. The general checks for choosing a healthy reptile and quarantining it on arrival are in the [reptile quarantine guide](/blog/reptile-quarantine-guide/).

## Ongoing Costs: Roughly $20 to $40 a Month

Live insects, crickets, dubia roaches, and silkworms among the common choices, plus calcium and multivitamin supplements and modest electricity for misting equipment and lighting.

## Vet Costs

A routine exotic exam runs $60 to $135, with a fecal test adding $15 to $35. Emergency visits start around $150 to $300 for the exam alone, and a serious case with diagnostics and treatment can reach $500 to $1,500. Reptile-experienced vets aren't universally available, worth locating one before you need it.

## Lifespan

5 to 15 years in captivity. [Wild-caught individuals consistently live shorter lives](/blog/leaf-tailed-gecko-health-issues-guide/) than captive-bred ones, one more reason captive-bred is strongly worth the higher price for this species specifically.

## The Honest Budgeting Takeaway

The higher purchase price for this species reflects genuine rarity and breeding difficulty, not marketing. Combined with the specialized humidity and misting setup this species needs, leaf-tailed geckos are a real financial step up from the other commonly kept geckos, appropriate for a keeper ready to invest in getting the details right.

The genus is endemic to Madagascar's shrinking forests, and the [leaf-tailed gecko encyclopedia profile](/encyclopedia/animal/leaf-tailed-gecko/) covers the trade protection that followed.
