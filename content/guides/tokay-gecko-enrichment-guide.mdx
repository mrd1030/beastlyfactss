# Tokay Gecko Enrichment: A 2024 Study Says Keep Them in Pairs

<AffiliateDisclosure />

The standard line on tokays is that they are aggressive, solitary and best left alone. A 2024 study tested part of that and came back with the opposite answer.

<KeyTakeaway>
Researchers measured enclosure use, basking and hiding as captive-bred tokay geckos went from single to pair housing. Socially deprived lizards were more likely to move and hide before feeding, and the authors concluded that pair housing improves tokay welfare. This is the most current evidence on the species, and it points somewhere the hobby does not.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Research Says

The study, published in *Applied Animal Behaviour Science* in 2024, tracked how captive-bred tokays used their enclosure across the transition from being housed singly to being housed in pairs. The measured behaviors were space use, basking and hiding.

The finding: **when socially deprived, the lizards were more likely to move and hide before feeding**, and the results indicate pair housing improves welfare in this species. A separate body of work on tokay development looks at how environment and family shape captive welfare, pointing the same direction.

<FunFact>
Hiding is the easiest welfare signal to misread. A gecko that stays out of sight is usually described as shy, settling in, or simply nocturnal. In this study it was the measure that separated the socially deprived condition from the paired one.
</FunFact>

The honest caveat, and it needs stating in any guide: this is one study on captive-bred animals under observation. It is not a license to drop two wild-caught adult tokays into a tank and walk away. Pairing needs [quarantine](/blog/reptile-quarantine-guide/), careful introduction, enough space and cover for both, and a genuine plan for separating them.

## Vertical Structure That Is Usable

Tokays are arboreal and spend their lives on vertical surfaces. A tall enclosure with nothing in it is not enrichment, it is a taller empty box.

Live plants do a second job here by holding humidity, which this species needs, and by creating visual cover so the gecko can be somewhere other than inside a hide.

## Retreats at Every Level

A tokay should be able to be concealed at the top of the enclosure, not just at the bottom. Vertical cork tubes, tight gaps behind slabs, and planting dense enough to disappear into give the animal choices about where to be hidden rather than one correct answer.

That matters more for a defensive species than for a placid one. Defensiveness is what an animal reaches for when it has no better option, and the point of cover is to give it a better option.

## Feeding as Hunting

Tokays are ambush predators and will take prey from height. Varying where and when food appears, offering live prey it has to pursue and not simply collect, and feeding in the evening to match the activity window all follow the pattern that gecko feeding-enrichment research supports.

The leopard gecko work found feeding among the strongest enrichment types tested, and while that is a different genus, the mechanism transfers better than most.

## Priority Order

1. Enclosure height with real climbing structure in it
2. Social housing, treated carefully and with an exit plan
3. Retreats available at every level, not just the floor
4. Live planting for cover and humidity
5. Feeding presentation and prey pursuit
6. A low UVB gradient
7. Novel objects, last

## What Not to Do

Do not pair two adult tokays casually on the strength of one study, since the research was on captive-bred animals under observation and needs replicating in a home setting with care. Do not read constant hiding as the species being shy. Do not build height without filling it, because empty vertical space is not usable space. Do not handle for enrichment, since this species is enriched through its enclosure and stressed by interaction.
