# Leaf-Tailed Gecko Enclosure Setup: Cool, Genuinely Humid, and Precisely Misted

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Leaf-tailed gecko care flips a lot of typical reptile assumptions. This species wants to stay cool, not warm, and humidity management is genuinely the central skill in keeping one healthy. For what all of it costs, see our [cost guide](/blog/leaf-tailed-gecko-cost-guide/).

## Enclosure Size

Smaller species do fine in 12x12x18 inches, with 18x18x18 better. The satanic leaf-tailed gecko, the smallest commonly kept species, does well in an <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4vlb6Bp" product="18x18x24 in glass terrarium (arboreal)">18x18x24 inch enclosure</AffiliateLink> as an adult. Larger species like Henkel's need considerably more room. This is a vertical, arboreal species, so height matters more than floor space.

## Temperature: Cool, With No Basking Spot

Keep ambient temperature around 68 to 75F, with a night drop to around 64 to 68F. This species has thin skin, dehydrates easily, and genuinely does not handle heat stress well. A basking heat source is generally not needed and can be actively dangerous for this species, the opposite setup priority from most reptiles covered on this site.

## Humidity: The Defining Requirement

60 to 80% for most species, though the satanic leaf-tailed gecko specifically needs 90 to 100% overnight and dehydrates extremely easily if that's not met. Achieve this through morning and evening misting, live plants, and moisture-retentive substrate. An <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4fibBY2" product="Automatic misting system/fogger">automated misting system</AffiliateLink> is genuinely worth the investment for the more sensitive species. A useful check: a gecko kept at correct humidity sheds in one clean piece with no sunken eyes or a drooping tail afterward, a sign covered in more detail in our [health issues guide](/blog/leaf-tailed-gecko-health-issues-guide/).

<FunFact>
The satanic leaf-tailed gecko needs 90 to 100% humidity overnight, essentially saturated air, while its ambient daytime temperature tops out around 75F, a cool, damp combination that's nearly the opposite of what most reptiles on this site need.
</FunFact>

## Substrate

A moisture-retentive bioactive mix or coconut husk substrate supports both the humidity this species needs and some light burrowing behavior.

## Lighting

Low-output UVB, in the <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3TiqeC4" product='UVB - Arcadia Forest 6% (ProT5 kit, 36"/39W)'>2.0 to 5.0 range</AffiliateLink>, is increasingly recommended for long-term health, though not definitively required. Avoid high-output basking-style UVB entirely, this species doesn't use or tolerate intense light the way a desert lizard would. Run a standard 12-hour photoperiod on a timer.

## The Non-Obvious Requirement: Use Tap Water, Not Distilled

This is a detail specific to this species worth getting right. Use ordinary tap water for misting and drinking rather than distilled, softened, or heavily filtered water, tap water contains minerals genuinely important to this gecko's health. Provide water through a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0C4GFLSVZ?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="REPTI ZOO Magnetic Gecko Feeding Ledge, Acrylic Arboreal Feeding Ledge with 6PCS Cups">magnetic feeding ledge</AffiliateLink> with a small cup, and mist to keep the enclosure damp without letting water pool or standing moisture build up, over-misting invites mold just as reliably as under-misting causes dehydration.
