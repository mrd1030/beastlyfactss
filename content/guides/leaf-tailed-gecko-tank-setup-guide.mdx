# Leaf-Tailed Gecko Enclosure Setup: Cool, Genuinely Humid, and Precisely Misted

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Leaf-tailed gecko care flips a lot of typical reptile assumptions. This species wants to stay cool, not warm, and humidity management is the central skill in keeping one healthy.

<Figure src="/assets/images/leaf-tailed-gecko-tank-setup-guide-1.jpg" alt="Labeled diagram of a leaf-tailed gecko enclosure showing no basking spot, automated misting, and a tap water feeding ledge" caption="This species wants to stay cool, not warm, and humidity management is the central skill in keeping one healthy." shareable />

## Enclosure Size

Smaller species do fine in 12x12x18 inches, with 18x18x18 better. The satanic leaf-tailed gecko, the smallest commonly kept species, does well in an <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4vlb6Bp" product="18x18x24 in glass terrarium (arboreal)">18x18x24 inch enclosure</AffiliateLink> as an adult. Larger species like Henkel's need considerably more room. This is a vertical, arboreal species, so height matters more than floor space.

## Temperature: Cool, With No Basking Spot

Keep ambient temperature around 75 to 79F (24 to 26C), with a night drop to 64 to 72F. This species has thin skin, dehydrates easily, and genuinely does not handle heat stress well. A basking heat source is generally not needed and can be actively dangerous for this species, the opposite setup priority from most reptiles. Genus-level care sheets give a basking area of 80 to 84F for Uroplatus as a whole; that figure is for the lowland species and does not apply to this montane one.

## Humidity: The Defining Requirement

60 to 80% for most species, though the satanic leaf-tailed gecko specifically needs 90 to 100% overnight and dehydrates extremely easily if that's not met. Achieve this through morning and evening misting, live plants, and moisture-retentive substrate. An <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4fibBY2" product="Automatic misting system/fogger">automated misting system</AffiliateLink> is genuinely worth the investment for the more sensitive species.

<FunFact>
The satanic leaf-tailed gecko needs 90 to 100% humidity overnight, essentially saturated air, while its ambient daytime temperature tops out around 79F, a cool, damp combination that's nearly the opposite of what most reptiles need.
</FunFact>

## Other Species, Other Numbers

The cool, saturated-night setup above is built around the satanic leaf-tail. The larger species in the hobby are kept warmer, somewhat drier by day, and in bigger enclosures, all housed singly. Every one follows the same pattern of a drier day and a wetter night, which keeps mold down and matches the evening misting cycle.

Henkel's leaf-tail is also given a warmer spot of about 92°F at one end, where the satanic leaf-tail gets none, and the bark-mimicking species such as fimbriatus and lineatus likely receive more UVB in the wild than the leaf mimics. Size the enclosure and set the thermostat for the species you actually have, which means confirming the species before buying.

## Substrate

A natural substrate 2 to 4 inches deep holds humidity best: a mix of 60% topsoil and 40% peat moss, coconut fiber, or a bioactive kit, where cleanup crew species break down waste. Avoid bark and wood chips, pine, cedar and reptile carpet, which bring impaction and injury risks. Juveniles are the most vulnerable to impaction and go on paper towel.

## Lighting

Low-output UVB is strongly recommended for Uroplatus, even though these are crepuscular forest geckos in Ferguson zone 1. The target is a UVI of 0.6 to 1.4 at the highest branch, which a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3TiqeC4" product='UVB - Arcadia Forest 6% (ProT5 kit, 36"/39W)'>low-output T5 fixture</AffiliateLink> reaches at the right distance; check it with a UV meter. Avoid basking-strength bulbs entirely. Put the lights on a timer that follows the wild cycle, about 13 hours of light in summer and 11 in winter.

## Diet Basics

Strictly insectivorous. Dubia, discoid and red runner roaches, crickets, grasshoppers, hornworms, silkworms, mealworm and superworm beetles, and captive-bred snails all work, with the smaller species needing correspondingly smaller prey.

Juveniles eat daily and adults every other day, and the portion is as much as the gecko will take in one night. These are nocturnal hunters, so food goes in when the animal is becoming active rather than in the morning, and anything still loose in the enclosure by the next day comes out: live insects wandering a cage disturb a resting gecko, and on a freshly shed or unwell one they can do worse than disturb it.

Dust feeders lightly with calcium at every other feeding, and feed in the evening after misting so the water does not wash the powder off.

## The Non-Obvious Requirement: Use Tap Water, Not Distilled

This is a detail specific to this species worth getting right. Use ordinary tap water for misting and drinking rather than distilled, softened, or heavily filtered water, tap water contains minerals important to this gecko's health. Provide water through a <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0C4GFLSVZ?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="REPTI ZOO Magnetic Gecko Feeding Ledge, Acrylic Arboreal Feeding Ledge with 6PCS Cups">magnetic feeding ledge</AffiliateLink> with a small cup, and mist to keep the enclosure damp without letting water pool or standing moisture build up, over-misting invites mold just as reliably as under-misting causes dehydration. A digital hygrometer and thermometer combo makes it far easier to confirm you're hitting the target range.
