## Scale Rot

Brown, yellow, red, or greenish-black discoloration on the belly scales, from substrate that's kept too wet or dirty. Always see a vet, left untreated this can become systemic and turn fatal. Keeping the <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4vNgpe1" product="Cypress mulch substrate">substrate</AffiliateLink> dry and clean is the prevention, but a thorough enclosure cleaning alongside veterinary treatment is part of the fix, not a substitute for it.

## Mouth Rot (Infectious Stomatitis)

Swollen gums and visible pus in the mouth, usually tied to stress, poor husbandry, or an untreated injury. Always see a vet with reptile experience, severe cases need surgical cleaning alongside antibiotics.

## Inclusion Body Disease (IBD)

A viral disease causing neurological symptoms, head tilting, a corkscrewing motion, staring off into space, along with regurgitation and weight loss. There's no cure, and it's often fatal. The real prevention here happens at the point of purchase: buy captive-bred animals from reputable, established breeders, and quarantine any new snake before introducing it to others.

## Internal and External Parasites

Weight loss and lethargy point to internal parasites; small, dark specks resembling tiny ticks point to mites. Both are diagnosed and treated with a vet's help, routine fecal exams catch internal parasites before they become a bigger problem.

## The Husbandry Pattern Worth Remembering

Low temperature or humidity, or poor ventilation, causes respiratory infection. Wet, dirty substrate causes scale rot. Stress and poor husbandry contribute to mouth rot. And skipping quarantine on a new snake is how IBD and parasites get introduced to an otherwise healthy collection.
