# Common Neon Tetra Health Issues (Including the Disease Named After This Fish)

<VetDisclaimer />
<AffiliateDisclosure />

Most neon tetra health problems are treatable and trace back to water quality. One, though, is genuinely different, incurable, and worth understanding clearly since it's specific to this species.

## Neon Tetra Disease (NTD)

This condition is named after the species because neon tetras are especially associated with it, caused by a parasite that invades the fish's muscle tissue.

Watch for: restless or erratic swimming, often more noticeable at night, fading or patchy color, particularly along the back, visible lumps under the skin, a curved or deformed spine in advanced cases, and difficulty swimming normally.

This is highly contagious to other fish in the tank, spreading when fish consume infected tissue, including from a fish that's already died. It can also affect other species sharing the tank, cardinal tetras, guppies, and several others among them. Our [Guppy health issues guide](/blog/guppy-health-issues-guide/) covers what that species deals with, water quality driven for the most part, but worth knowing NTD can cross over. It is not transmissible to humans.

There is no cure. Once symptoms appear, the fish will not recover, and the honest, difficult recommendation from aquatic specialists is humane euthanasia for a confirmed case, both to end suffering and to protect the rest of the tank. Prevention is the only tool available: quarantine new fish for 2 to 4 weeks before adding them to an established tank, buy from sources with healthy stock, and never feed a tank the remains of a fish that died from an unknown cause.

<FunFact>
One caveat worth knowing: NTD is likely over-diagnosed in the hobby. True cases are relatively uncommon in a well-established, well-filtered tank, and symptoms genuinely overlap with more treatable bacterial infections, especially in newer or under-filtered setups. If your tank is well maintained and a fish shows these signs, it's worth considering other causes before assuming the worst.
</FunFact>

## Ich (White Spot Disease)

Small white spots resembling grains of salt, along with flashing against decor and rapid breathing. From stress or temperature instability. This one is treatable, gradually raise the temperature and use a standard commercial ich medication.

## Fin Rot

Ragged or bloody-edged fins, bacterial, from poor water quality or an untreated injury. Treatable with an antibacterial medication alongside correcting water conditions.

## Swim Bladder Disorder

Buoyancy problems from overfeeding or a poor diet. Manageable through proper feeding.
