# How Much Does Guppy Ownership Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Guppies are one of the most affordable pets on this entire site, cheap to buy, cheap to maintain, and easy to get started with. The one cost people don't budget for isn't money, it's more guppies than they planned on.

## The Fish: %%animal:guppy%%, More for Specialty Strains

Most guppies in the trade are bred varieties, since the wild form is seldom seen for sale. Named fancy strains from specialty breeders, Koi and similar patterns with elaborate color and fins, ran about $12 to $17 per fish and $23 to $28 per pair, and live fish ship overnight, so shipping adds to the bill.

<FunFact>
Underneath all that fancy coloring, a show-strain guppy and a plain one are the same species with the same basic needs. Many commercially bred guppies are weakened by inbreeding and over-production, so a higher price is no guarantee of hardiness.
</FunFact>

## Upfront Setup: Modest

Food, water conditioner, occasional filter media replacement, and a small amount of electricity for the heater and filter make up the entire monthly cost.

## Vet Costs

Fish rarely see a vet in any traditional sense. Most health issues are managed with over-the-counter medications and water quality correction rather than a clinic visit.

## Lifespan

Guppies are short-lived. In one lab study, females averaged about 13 months from the start of the experiment, and the longest-lived reached about 27 months. That short lifespan, combined with the low buy-in cost above, makes guppies about as far as you can get from the multi-decade commitment of a species like the sulcata tortoise, which routinely lives 70 to 100 or more years. Our [Sulcata tortoise cost guide](/blog/sulcata-tortoise-cost-guide/) covers what a pet that outlives its owner actually costs over a lifetime, a useful contrast if you're weighing pets at opposite ends of the commitment spectrum.

## The Cost to Plan For: More Guppies Than You Started With

This is the detail that catches new owners off guard. Guppies are livebearers, females give birth to live, free-swimming young rather than laying eggs, and they're genuinely prolific. A female can store sperm from a single mating and produce up to about 8 monthly broods from it without ever encountering a male again. Put males and females in the same tank with no plan, and you'll have far more guppies within months than you started with, which means [more tank space](/blog/guppy-tank-setup-guide/), more filtration, and more of every ongoing cost above.

Budgeting realistically for this species means budgeting for growth, not a fixed population.
