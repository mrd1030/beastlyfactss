# Rat Enclosure Setup: Multi-Level, and Never a Tank

<AffiliateDisclosure />

The single most important decision in rat housing isn't size, it's cage type: a glass tank traps exactly the ammonia buildup that drives the respiratory disease affecting nearly every pet rat.

## Why a Wire Cage, Not a Tank

It's tempting to reach for the same glass aquarium setup used for other small pets, but rats are a specific exception, and the reason ties directly to their single biggest health risk. VCA Animal Hospitals is direct about the underlying mechanism: a major cause of respiratory disease in pet rodents is poor environmental ventilation, which lets ammonia from urine build up and irritate the airways. A glass tank, even a large one, traps that ammonia far more effectively than an open wire cage does, and rats are unusually prone to exactly the chronic respiratory disease that kind of buildup drives, covered in depth in our Rat health issues guide. A well-built wire cage with a solid, sealed base solves this the way a tank simply can't: airflow that keeps ammonia from concentrating in the space a rat actually breathes.

<FunFact>
The link between cage type and respiratory disease isn't a loose correlation, it's a documented mechanism. Ammonia from urine builds up fastest in an enclosed space with poor airflow, and VCA Animal Hospitals specifically names that buildup as a major cause of respiratory disease in pet rodents. For a species where one survey found 95% of pet ratteries already carrying the bacterium behind chronic respiratory disease, more on that in our [Rat health issues guide](/blog/rat-health-issues-guide/), the cage you choose is doing real preventive work before a rat ever gets sick.
</FunFact>

## Enclosure Size

PetMD lists 24x24x24 inches as the minimum for a single rat and recommends the largest habitat possible beyond that. Because rats must be kept in pairs or small groups, not alone, that minimum is a starting point rather than a target, and PDSA's practical shorthand is worth repeating: cages built for ferrets or chinchillas are usually the right size for rats too. ## Bar Spacing

Keep bar spacing to about half an inch (1 to 1.5 cm) or less. The American Fancy Rat and Mouse Association is specific about why: spacing around 3/4 inch is wide enough that even an adult rat can get its head stuck trying to push through, a real injury risk rather than just an escape risk. RSPCA guidance frames the same target slightly differently but lands in the same place: spacing needs to be small enough that a rat can't poke its head through the bars at all. Cages marketed broadly for "small animals" sometimes ship with wider spacing that's fine for a guinea pig but genuinely unsafe for a rat, worth checking specifically rather than assuming.

## Temperature

RSPCA guidance puts the ideal range at 19 to 23°C, roughly 66 to 73°F, and rats generally do fine across a somewhat broader 64 to 79°F band. Rats can't sweat or pant, and they regulate body heat largely through their tails, which makes them more heat-sensitive than that broader range might suggest, heat stress becomes a real risk above about 86°F. ## Bedding

Paper-based or cellulose bedding is the safest, most widely recommended choice. Avoid dusty bedding and, per RSPCA guidance, sawdust specifically, along with cedar and pine, whose aromatic oils irritate a rat's already respiratory-sensitive system, more on why in our Rat health issues guide. <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4voH9Ap" product="Small-pet paper bedding">Paper-based bedding</AffiliateLink> changed at least twice a week keeps ammonia from accumulating between cleanings, which matters more for this species than almost any other on this site.

## The Wheel

A solid-surface exercise wheel with no wire rungs is essential, the same rule that applies across every small rodent on this site. Size matters more here than it does for hamsters or gerbils: most sources recommend at least 12 inches in diameter, with many experienced keepers and rescues recommending 14 to 16 inches for adult rats, especially males, since a wheel that's too small forces a hunched, arched running posture that isn't good for the spine over time. This runs meaningfully larger than the wheels typically sold for hamsters and gerbils, worth checking the actual diameter rather than assuming a "small pet" wheel will fit.

## Enrichment Tied to Intelligence

Rats are widely considered among the most intelligent commonly kept small mammals, and RSPCA behavior guidance backs that up directly, describing rats as animals that dig, climb, and learn routes and pathways quickly. A cage with just a wheel and a hideout under-serves that.
