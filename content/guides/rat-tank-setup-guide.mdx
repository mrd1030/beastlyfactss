# Rat Enclosure Setup: Multi-Level, and Never a Tank

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Cage type first. Size second. A tank fails the first test.

## Why a wire cage, not a tank

Ammonia from urine irritates airways. A glass tank holds that ammonia. Rats already carry Mycoplasma. The cage you pick is prevention, not decoration. A wire cage with a solid, sealed base is the setup.

<FunFact>
One northwestern US survey found 95% of pet ratteries positive for the bacterium behind chronic respiratory disease. The cage is doing work before the first sneeze.
</FunFact>

## Enclosure size

24x24x24 inches is the floor for one rat. They do not live alone, so that number is the start, not the pair target. ## Bar spacing

Half an inch or less. Three-quarter inch is how an adult wedges its head. "Small animal" cages often ship too wide.

## Temperature

66 to 73F. Workable 64 to 79F. Heat stress above about 86F. [Rapid or open-mouth breathing is the first sign](/blog/small-mammal-temperature-heat-stress-guide/). Act there, not at collapse. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3QHrM81" product="Digital thermometer/hygrometer combo">digital thermometer</AffiliateLink> beats guessing.

## Bedding

Paper or cellulose. Changed at least twice a week. No dusty bedding, no sawdust, no cedar, no pine. <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4voH9Ap" product="Small-pet paper bedding">Paper bedding</AffiliateLink> is the default.

## The wheel

Solid surface. No wire rungs. At least 12 inches. 14 to 16 for an adult, especially a male. A hamster wheel hunches the spine.
