# Tegu Enrichment: Borrowing From the Monitors, and Saying So

There is no enrichment study on this species. That is worth stating in the first line rather than burying, because most care writing would simply assert things instead. For everything else about them, see our complete [Argentine Tegu care guide](/guides/tegu/).

<KeyTakeaway>
Nothing has been published on tegu enrichment. The closest real evidence is the varanid problem-solving literature, and tegus are teiids, a separate lineage that converged on a similar shape and lifestyle. The husbandry that follows is sound. The evidence behind it belongs to a different animal, and this guide will not pretend otherwise.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Research Actually Says

For *Salvator merianae*, nothing. Searching returns care content, not studies.

The nearest thing is the monitor work covered in our [Ackie Monitor enrichment guide](/blog/ackie-monitor-enrichment-guide/): eight juvenile black-throated monitors were given a transparent food tube and **all eight opened it within ten minutes on the first trial**, with later longitudinal testing showing solving latencies dropping over time, which was read as learning.

<FunFact>
Monitors and tegus are not close relatives. Varanids and teiids are separate lineages that independently arrived at the same design: large, active, wide-ranging, burrowing carnivores that investigate everything. That convergence is why the husbandry transfers. It is also why the evidence is a borrow rather than a finding.
</FunFact>

Behind both sits Burghardt's review, which argued reptiles had been systematically under-enriched because assumptions about their cognition were wrong rather than because the animals did not respond.

## Substrate Depth, the Biggest Decision

Tegus dig, extensively, and will build a burrow they sleep in and brumate in. Depth is the largest single enclosure choice for the species, and a shallow layer is bedding rather than a burrow.

## Puzzle and Extraction Feeding

This is the item the borrowed evidence most directly supports. A container with visible food that has to be opened, food under a moveable object, food buried in a new spot: all of it is trivially easy to improvise and all of it is closer to the study apparatus than anything sold as reptile enrichment.

Our [feeding guide](/blog/argentine-tegu-feeding-guide/) covers the diet you would be delivering this way.

## Target and Station Training

Standard practice in zoo collections for large lizards, and widely done by private tegu keepers. A <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07GBC85T9?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Parrot Wizard Training Clicker and Target Stick">clicker and target stick</AffiliateLink> and a few minutes daily makes weighing, health checks and moving the animal far less stressful for both of you.

Be clear in your own head that this is practice rather than published evidence. It is well established practice, and the distinction still matters.

## A Soak Big Enough to Get Into

Tegus soak readily. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eKyN0W" product="Large soak-able water dish">container large enough to fully enter</AffiliateLink> supports hydration and shedding and is genuinely passive: you provide it, the animal decides. Larger tubs are worth it for an adult.

## Priority Order

1. Substrate deep enough for a real burrow
2. Enclosure floor area, since this is a wide-ranging forager
3. Puzzle and extraction feeding
4. Target and station training
5. A soaking container
6. Scent novelty and varied food placement
7. Rearrangement and objects, last

## What Not to Do

Do not present borrowed varanid evidence as if it were about tegus, which is the mistake this guide exists to avoid. Do not keep a digging animal on a shallow substrate layer. Do not bowl-feed an animal this investigative. Do not let enrichment distract from basking temperatures and UVB, which outrank all of it and which our [health issues guide](/blog/argentine-tegu-health-issues-guide/) covers the consequences of. And do not confuse handling with training, since our [handling guide](/blog/argentine-tegu-handling-guide/) covers the difference.
