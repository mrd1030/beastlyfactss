# How Much Does a Fire-Bellied Toad Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Fire-bellied toads are inexpensive, and the setup cost here goes toward something a bit different than most amphibians need, real aquatic filtration, not just humidity control.

## The Toad Itself: $10 to $25, More for Morphs

Standard oriental fire-bellied toads run $10 to $25, sometimes as low as $5 to $10. Most animals in the trade are wild-caught, captive breeding isn't especially profitable at this price point, worth knowing when you're choosing where to buy. Albino and other color variants can reach $100 or more.

Those four lines come to roughly $45 to $75. Everything the table does not price comes on top: plants, hides and cork bark for the land portion, a tight screen lid, and a thermometer. Their cost depends on how elaborate the paludarium is, so budget them separately from the core build.

<FunFact>
Most fire-bellied toads in the pet trade are wild-caught instead of captive-bred, since breeding simply isn't especially profitable at this price point. That is why a fecal exam on arrival is worth budgeting alongside the animal. It's worth asking a seller directly where an animal actually came from before you buy.
</FunFact>

## Ongoing Costs: Roughly $15 to $30 a Month

Feeder insects, water conditioner, and occasional filter media replacement make up the monthly total. This isn't a species with heavy recurring expenses once the enclosure is built.

## Vet Costs

A routine exotic or amphibian exam runs $50 to $135, with emergency visits starting around $150 to $300. This species needs a genuinely exotics-experienced vet; a general practice will not do. Budget one wellness exam a year, the interval vets advise for fire-bellied toads, and a fecal test at the first visit for any newly bought toad.

## A Group Costs Little More Than One

A 10 gallon tank houses one to three toads, and the species is social, so the second and third animals add only the purchase price and a little more food. The limit is the tank: more toads than that means a larger tank and a bigger filter, and the filter size follows the volume of the water section. A new toad joining an existing group also needs a quarantine tub for its first 6 to 8 weeks, which a plastic tub and a spare sponge filter cover.

## Lifespan: Longer Than You Might Expect

10 to 15 years is the figure to plan around, and the documented ceiling runs well past it: 20 years in human care is recorded, and the maximum on record is 30. This is a genuinely long-term amphibian commitment for a species often bought on impulse at a low price.

## A Safety Note Worth Knowing Before You Buy

Fire-bellied toads secrete genuine toxins from their skin, and while this isn't dangerous to people under normal handling, it does mean this species is best kept as a minimal-to-no-handling observation pet. This shapes the whole relationship you'll have with the animal, and it's worth knowing before you buy, not after.

## The Bottom Line

Fire-bellied toads are among the cheapest amphibians to buy and set up, but the long lifespan and the specific water quality demands mean the real cost is attention over a decade or more, not dollars upfront. Compared to a purely terrestrial amphibian like the [White's tree frog](/blog/whites-tree-frog-cost-guide/), which leans on humidity and has no real water section, this species' budget goes toward filtration instead of lighting.
