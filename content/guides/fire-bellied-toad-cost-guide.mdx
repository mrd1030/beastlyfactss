# How Much Does a Fire-Bellied Toad Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Fire-bellied toads are genuinely inexpensive, and the setup cost here goes toward something a bit different than most amphibians on this site, real aquatic filtration, not just humidity control. For everything else about day to day care, see our full [Fire-Bellied Toad care guide](/guides/fire-bellied-toad/).

## The Toad Itself: $10 to $25, More for Morphs

Standard oriental fire-bellied toads run $10 to $25, sometimes as low as $5 to $10. Most animals in the trade are wild-caught, captive breeding isn't especially profitable at this price point, worth knowing when you're choosing where to buy. Albino and other color variants can reach $100 or more.

Plants and hides for the land portion round out the build. Most complete setups land in the $150 to $300 range once the tank, filter, conditioner, and substrate are all accounted for. Our [Fire-Bellied Toad tank setup guide](/blog/fire-bellied-toad-tank-setup-guide/) walks through why the water section matters so much for this particular species.

<FunFact>
Most fire-bellied toads in the pet trade are wild-caught rather than captive-bred, since breeding simply isn't especially profitable at this price point. It's worth asking a seller directly where an animal actually came from before you buy.
</FunFact>

## Ongoing Costs: Roughly $15 to $30 a Month

Feeder insects, water conditioner, and occasional filter media replacement make up the monthly total. This isn't a species with heavy recurring expenses once the enclosure is built.

## Vet Costs

A routine exotic or amphibian exam runs $50 to $135, with emergency visits starting around $150 to $300. This species needs a genuinely exotics-experienced vet, not a general practice.

## Lifespan: Longer Than You Might Expect

10 to 15 years is commonly cited, with some sources putting the range as wide as 10 to 20 years, and dedicated keepers occasionally reporting animals reaching 20 or more with excellent care. This is a genuinely long-term amphibian commitment for a species often bought on impulse at a low price.

## A Safety Note Worth Knowing Before You Buy

Fire-bellied toads secrete genuine toxins from their skin, and while this isn't dangerous to people under normal handling, it does mean this species is best kept as a minimal-to-no-handling observation pet. This shapes the whole relationship you'll have with the animal, more detail in our [handling guide](/blog/fire-bellied-toad-handling-guide/), and it's worth knowing before you buy, not after.

## The Bottom Line

Fire-bellied toads are one of the cheapest amphibians to buy and set up on this site, but the long lifespan and the specific water quality demands mean the real cost is attention over a decade or more, not dollars upfront. Compared to a purely terrestrial amphibian like the [White's tree frog](/blog/whites-tree-frog-cost-guide/), which leans on humidity rather than a real water section, this species' budget goes toward filtration instead of lighting.
