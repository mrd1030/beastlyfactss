# How Much Does an Argentine Black and White Tegu Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Argentine tegus are one of the more expensive lizards on this site to set up properly, a direct consequence of just how large this species actually grows. For everything beyond budgeting, diet, enrichment, and the rest, see our full [Argentine Tegu care guide](/guides/tegu/).

## The Tegu: $200 to $1,200

Standard black and white tegus, along with blue and Chacoan morphs, typically run $200 to $500. Rare designer morphs, albino, high white, and various patterned lines, push considerably higher, $700 to $1,200 or more for the rarest combinations.

Where you live actually matters here before price does. Florida has banned new acquisition of pet tegus outright since 2021, no amount of budget fixes that. See our [Argentine Tegu legal guide](/blog/argentine-tegu-legal-guide/) before you start pricing enclosures if you're anywhere near that state.

## Upfront Setup: Often Exceeding $1,000 to $3,000

This is genuinely one of the most expensive setups on this entire site. A proper adult enclosure alone, custom built at 8 feet by 4 feet by 4 feet minimum, routinely runs into the thousands once you add powerful basking and radiant heat, a real thermostat, high output UVB, and 12 to 18 inches of deep substrate.

Worth being upfront about the enclosure line above: it's a ready made 8ft PVC modular unit, actually 8x2x2ft, a partial match on length only, well short of the full 8x4x4ft footprint an adult tegu needs. True adult sized housing at this species' scale is typically a DIY build or ordered direct from a manufacturer, similar to what we cover in the [savannah monitor cost guide](/blog/savannah-monitor-cost-guide/) for that other large monitor family lizard. The substrate line is similarly a starting point, not a total, reaching 12 to 18 inches of depth across an 8 by 4 foot floor takes considerably more than one or two packs.

## Ongoing Costs: Roughly $40 to $100+ a Month

A genuinely varied omnivore diet, rodents, insects, eggs, ground meats, fruit, and vegetables, plus meaningful electricity for heating and lighting a large enclosure, add up to a real monthly commitment, considerably more than any other lizard covered on this site.

<FunFact>
Some Argentine tegus have been documented living past 30 years old under excellent care, well beyond the 15 to 20 year typical lifespan. Combined with the setup cost above, this is a genuinely serious, multi-decade financial and space commitment, not a casual purchase.
</FunFact>

## Vet Costs

A routine exotic exam runs $50 to $135, with emergency visits starting around $150 to $300 before diagnostics. One practical note worth mentioning here: an adult tegu's genuine size makes transport to a vet logistically difficult, establishing a relationship with a reptile vet early, before you need one urgently, is worth doing. Our [health issues guide](/blog/argentine-tegu-health-issues-guide/) covers the conditions most likely to trigger one of those visits.

## Lifespan

15 to 20 years is typical, with some individuals documented living past 30 under excellent care. Combined with the setup cost above, this is a genuinely serious, multi-decade financial and space commitment.

## The Honest Budgeting Takeaway

If you're drawn to this species for its famously dog-like temperament, covered in our [handling guide](/blog/argentine-tegu-handling-guide/), go in understanding that the setup cost reflects genuine need, not luxury. An undersized enclosure isn't a reasonable way to save money here, it directly compromises the health and behavior of an animal that's specifically prized for how well it does with proper space and socialization.
