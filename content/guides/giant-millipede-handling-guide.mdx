# How to Handle a Giant Millipede (Genuinely Gentle, Genuinely Fragile)

Giant African millipedes are often called the gentle giants of the invertebrate world, and that reputation is earned. This is a docile, slow-moving, completely non-aggressive animal, genuinely one of the safest invertebrates for a keeper of any experience level to handle. The real risk here runs in a direction you might not expect.

## A Completely Different Kind of Animal From the Predatory Invertebrates on This Site

The [tarantula](/blog/tarantula-handling-guide/), [emperor scorpion](/blog/emperor-scorpion-handling-guide/), and [praying mantis](/blog/praying-mantis-handling-guide/) already covered here are all predators, and their defenses reflect that: bites, stings, urticating hairs. The giant millipede is a detritivore, eating decaying plant matter, and it can't bite or sting you at all. Its only defense is curling into a tight spiral and secreting a chemical fluid that smells unpleasant and can mildly irritate skin, eyes, or mucous membranes, and may leave a temporary yellowish-brown stain. Always wash your hands after handling, and keep that fluid away from your eyes and mouth.

<FunFact>
Unlike the tarantula, emperor scorpion, or praying mantis covered elsewhere on this site, the giant millipede has no offensive weapon at all, no bite, no sting, no venom. Its entire defense is a curl and a mildly irritating, foul-smelling secretion, which is exactly why it's often called the gentle giant of the invertebrate world.
</FunFact>

## The Real Risk: Falls, Not Bites

This is the detail that matters most. A giant millipede has a heavy body and a genuinely brittle exoskeleton, and a fall from even a modest height, higher than the animal's own body length, onto a hard surface can crack the shell and prove fatal. Handle over a soft surface close to the ground, support the full body, and never grab from above, let the millipede walk onto your hand instead.

## Never Handle During or Right After a Molt

A molting millipede has a soft, vulnerable exoskeleton. Leave it completely undisturbed during this period, and if you see your millipede burrow into the substrate and stay there, resist the urge to dig it up to check on it, this is very often normal molting behavior, not a problem. Our [Giant Millipede health issues guide](/blog/giant-millipede-health-issues-guide/) covers what can go wrong when a molt is disturbed or humidity isn't right beforehand.

## First-Timer Mistakes

- Dropping the animal, by far the most common and most serious handling risk with this species.
- Handling during a molt.
- Digging up a burrowed millipede out of curiosity rather than recognizing it as likely molting behavior.

## The Honest Comparison to Other Invertebrates on This Site

If you're weighing a first invertebrate pet, the giant millipede is arguably the most genuinely safe to handle, no venom, no bite, no sting, just a mild-smelling secretion as its worst-case defense. The tradeoff is fragility rather than danger, this animal needs to be handled carefully specifically to protect it, not you.
