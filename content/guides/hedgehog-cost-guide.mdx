# How Much Does a Hedgehog Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Hedgehogs are moderately priced to buy and inexpensive to keep month to month, but the first real decision with this species isn't financial or even husbandry-related, it's legal. That's an unusual thing to lead with in a cost guide, but for a hedgehog it comes before everything else.

## Where Hedgehogs Are Legal (Check Before You Buy)

Check this before you fall in love with a specific hedgehog. [Six places will not let you keep one](/blog/hedgehog-legal-guide/): California, Georgia, Hawaii, Pennsylvania, Washington DC and New York City. New Jersey is the only state in the country where a permit is the answer rather than a flat no. Legality also varies at the city level within otherwise-legal states, which is why New York City appears on that list while the rest of New York State does not.

Two states are widely reported as permit states and neither one is. Maine names the African pygmy hedgehog on its Unrestricted Species List, meaning no permit at all, and Wisconsin's exotic animal chapter does not reach hedgehogs.

These laws exist largely over concerns about disease carriage and ecological risk, and they do change over time. Verify current rules with your state wildlife agency and local animal control before you commit to buying one, not after.

## The Hedgehog Itself: $100 to $300

Breeder babies typically run $150 to $250, rescue adults $100 to $175. Color and markings affect price somewhat, unusual, very dark, or very light coloring costs more than the common gray or brown, but this isn't a species with dramatic morph-driven pricing the way some reptiles are.

Food, bedding, and occasional insects make up most of the monthly total, with heating electricity adding more during colder months, this species needs consistent warmth year round, not just during a cold snap. One thing you can safely leave off the shopping list is anything dairy, since hedgehogs are lactose intolerant and [milk and cheese cause real digestive problems](/blog/hedgehogs-are-lactose-intolerant-despite-what-cartoons-show/) rather than being a cheap treat.

<FunFact>
Hedgehog legality doesn't stop at the state line. New York City bans pet hedgehogs outright even though the rest of New York State allows ownership, and a handful of other cities layer their own restrictions on top of otherwise-permissive state law. It's a genuinely easy detail to miss if you only check your state's rules and assume that settles it.
</FunFact>

## Vet Costs

Hedgehogs genuinely need an exotic-experienced vet, and that is the price to plan around: $80 to $200 a visit, against $40 to $80 for a routine checkup at a general practice that may not see this species at all. Respiratory infection treatment with medication commonly reaches $200 to $400. An emergency fund of $500 or more is a reasonable planning number.

Three vet items belong in the plan from the start. The first exam should happen within about 48 hours of bringing a hedgehog home, since most health guarantees require it, and it includes a fecal test for parasites and a check of the animal's sex. After that, hedgehogs hide illness and have short lives, so vets recommend a full exam with baseline blood work every 6 to 12 months, usually under sedation because a balled-up hedgehog cannot be examined properly. That puts the realistic vet line at one or two visits a year, not one. And a female raises the spay question: uterine disease is common enough in pet hedgehogs that vets advise strongly considering a preventive spay, a surgery priced clinic by clinic and worth a written quote before you decide.

## Lifespan

3 to 6 years typically, with some individuals reaching 8 to 10 years under excellent care. Age 5 is generally considered senior for a hedgehog.

## The Honest Budgeting Takeaway

Once legality is settled, hedgehogs are one of the more predictable small pets to budget for, a modest purchase price, a one-time setup that's mostly about temperature control and a good wheel, and a manageable monthly cost. The real financial risk is skipping the legal check first and having to rehome an animal you've already grown attached to.

That legal-first order is what sets the hedgehog apart from a sulcata tortoise, a lovebird and a guppy, the [four pets decided before the tank goes up](/blog/sulcata-hedgehog-lovebird-guppy-overview/), where the other three start with money or husbandry.
