# How Much Does a Hedgehog Really Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Hedgehogs are moderately priced to buy and inexpensive to keep month to month, but the first real decision with this species isn't financial or even husbandry-related, it's legal. That's an unusual thing to lead with in a cost guide, but for a hedgehog it genuinely comes before everything else. For the full care picture beyond budgeting, see our complete [Hedgehog care guide](/guides/hedgehog/), or our companion guides on [handling](/blog/hedgehog-handling-guide/), [health issues](/blog/hedgehog-health-issues-guide/), and [enclosure setup](/blog/hedgehog-tank-setup-guide/).

## Where Hedgehogs Are Actually Legal (Check Before You Buy)

Check this before you fall in love with a specific hedgehog. Hedgehogs are banned outright in California, Georgia, Hawaii, and Washington D.C. Pennsylvania and Maine restrict ownership under specific conditions, and New Jersey and Wisconsin require a permit. Legality also varies at the city level within otherwise-legal states, New York City bans them even though the rest of New York State allows ownership, and some other cities have their own restrictions on top of state law.

These laws exist largely over concerns about disease carriage and ecological risk, and they do change over time. Verify current rules with your state wildlife agency and local animal control before you commit to buying one, not after.

If you're comparing this species against the other pets in this roundup, this is genuinely the odd one out. A [sulcata tortoise](/blog/sulcata-tortoise-cost-guide/), a [lovebird](/blog/lovebird-cost-guide/), and a [guppy](/blog/guppy-cost-guide/) all start with a financial or husbandry question first. A hedgehog starts with a legal one.

## The Hedgehog Itself: $100 to $300

Breeder babies typically run $150 to $250, rescue adults $100 to $175. Color and markings affect price somewhat, unusual, very dark, or very light coloring costs more than the common gray or brown, but this isn't a species with dramatic morph-driven pricing the way some reptiles are.

Food, bedding, and occasional insects make up most of the monthly total, with heating electricity adding more during colder months, this species needs consistent warmth year round, not just during a cold snap. One thing you can safely leave off the shopping list is anything dairy, since hedgehogs are lactose intolerant and [milk and cheese cause real digestive problems](/blog/hedgehogs-are-lactose-intolerant-despite-what-cartoons-show/) rather than being a cheap treat.

<FunFact>
Hedgehog legality doesn't stop at the state line. New York City bans pet hedgehogs outright even though the rest of New York State allows ownership, and a handful of other cities layer their own restrictions on top of otherwise-permissive state law. It's a genuinely easy detail to miss if you only check your state's rules and assume that settles it.
</FunFact>

## Vet Costs

A routine checkup runs $40 to $80. An exotic-vet visit, and hedgehogs genuinely need an exotic-experienced vet, runs $80 to $200. Respiratory infection treatment with medication commonly reaches $200 to $400. An emergency fund of $500 or more is a reasonable planning number for this species, our [Hedgehog health issues guide](/blog/hedgehog-health-issues-guide/) covers the specific conditions that drive those costs.

## Lifespan

3 to 6 years typically, with some individuals reaching 8 to 10 years under excellent care. Age 5 is generally considered senior for a hedgehog.

## The Honest Budgeting Takeaway

Once legality is settled, hedgehogs are actually one of the more predictable small pets to budget for, a modest purchase price, a one-time setup that's mostly about temperature control and a good wheel, and a manageable monthly cost. The real financial risk is skipping the legal check first and having to rehome an animal you've already grown attached to.
