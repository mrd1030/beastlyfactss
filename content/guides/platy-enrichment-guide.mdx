# Platy Enrichment: Forgiving Is Not the Same as Unaffected

<AffiliateDisclosure />

The platy is a beginner fish when the water is hard enough, which is why it is the fish that gets the beginner mistakes.

<KeyTakeaway>
Platies are easy beginner fish when the water is hard enough, which is why they end up in small tanks, bare tanks and tanks not yet cycled. Mass-bred stock is often weak, and a fish surviving is not evidence that the tank is fine. What a platy needs is undemanding and it is still a list.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Research Says

One comparison of nine livebearing species included the variatus platy and found that females spent significantly less time feeding with a male present than with another female. The wider thread is livebearer sexual harassment, covered in full in our [Guppy enrichment guide](/blog/guppy-enrichment-guide/).

Studies of guppies and related livebearers identify **direct costs to females from male harassment**, including **energy expenditure and reduced foraging ability**. In the molly *Poecilia mexicana*, males attempt to mate almost constantly while females are largely non-responsive, so females **flee, become more vigilant and feed less**. One guppy study found the effects carrying into the next generation.

Platy males can be relentless too, and the direction of the finding still applies. A male-heavy platy tank produces females that spend their day avoiding rather than eating.

<FunFact>
Many platies in the trade carry hybrid ancestry from the southern platy, the variatus and the swordtail, and have been bred for color for over a century. Mass-bred stock of that kind is often not very hardy, because of inbreeding and over-production.
</FunFact>

## Sex Ratio

Two to three females per male minimum, more females better. Or keep a single-sex group, which sidesteps both the harassment and the population question.

The trap is the usual one. Buying on color and finish produces a male-heavy tank without anyone deciding to make one.

## A Group

Peaceful, social fish that do better in numbers. Five or more spreads any chasing instead of focusing it on one individual, and produces more of the ordinary behavior that makes the fish worth watching.

## Cover That Breaks Sight Lines

Dense <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B008HXF16K?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="SubstrateSource Anubias Nana Live Aquarium Plant, 1 Pot">live planting</AffiliateLink> along the sides and back, plus floating plants.

In a livebearer tank cover does specific work: it lets a pursued female stop being pursued, and it gives fry a survival rate you can manage rather than none or all. Leave open swimming space through the middle, since platies are active mid-water fish.

## A Cycled, Stable Tank

The one that gets skipped because the fish survives it. A platy will live through an uncycled tank. It will do so while being exposed to ammonia.

Cycle first, use <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3Rg5gTT" product="Water conditioner">water conditioner</AffiliateLink> on everything that goes in, and change small amounts often.

## Feeding Across the Tank

Spread food out and do not drop it in one place, so females being chased still eat. Vary it with vegetable-based foods, since platies take more plant matter than guppies do.

## Decide About the Fry Before You Get Them

A mixed-sex platy tank produces broods roughly monthly. Within a year an unmanaged tank is overstocked, and overstocking is what finally overwhelms even a forgiving beginner fish.

Single-sex group, a plan for rehoming, or tank mates that will predate fry. Any of the three works. Not deciding is what does not.

## Priority Order

1. A cycled, stable tank before they arrive
2. At least two to three females per male, or a single-sex group
3. A group of five or more
4. Planting that breaks sight lines, including floating cover
5. Open swimming space through the middle
6. Food spread across the tank, with vegetable content
7. A decision about the fry

## What Not to Do

Do not use a beginner fish to cycle a tank. Do not stock on color and end up male-heavy. Do not keep a pair. Do not run a bare tank because platies will put up with one. Do not ignore the population.

The [platy, swordtail and molly overview](/blog/platy-swordtail-molly-overview/) sets the platy beside the other two common livebearers, where it is the smallest and the least demanding of the three.
