# Platy Enrichment: Hardy Is Not the Same as Unaffected

<AffiliateDisclosure />

The platy is the fish that survives the beginner mistakes, which is why it is the fish that gets them.

<KeyTakeaway>
Platies tolerate small tanks, wide temperature ranges, hard water and shaky cycling better than almost anything else in the community section. Tolerance is why they end up in those tanks, and it is not evidence that the tanks are fine. What a platy needs is undemanding and it is still a list.
</KeyTakeaway>

For everything else about them, see the [Platy care guide](/guides/platy/).

## What the Research Says

Nothing on platies specifically. The applicable thread is livebearer sexual harassment, covered in full in our [Guppy enrichment guide](/blog/guppy-enrichment-guide/).

Studies of guppies and related livebearers identify **direct costs to females from male harassment**, including **energy expenditure and reduced foraging ability**. In the molly *Poecilia mexicana*, males attempt to mate almost constantly while females are largely non-responsive, so females **flee, become more vigilant and feed less**. One guppy study found the effects carrying into the next generation.

Platy males are generally less relentless than guppy males, and the direction of the finding still applies. A male-heavy platy tank produces females that spend their day avoiding rather than eating.

<FunFact>
Almost every platy in the trade is a hybrid of two species, bred for color for over a century. The hardiness is partly a consequence: generations of tank-bred fish selected under exactly the conditions hobbyists keep them in.
</FunFact>

## Sex Ratio

Two to three females per male minimum, more females better. Or keep a single-sex group, which sidesteps both the harassment and the population question.

The trap is the usual one. Buying on color and finish produces a male-heavy tank without anyone deciding to make one.

## A Group

Peaceful, social fish that do better in numbers. Five or more spreads any chasing instead of focusing it on one individual, and produces more of the ordinary behavior that makes the fish worth watching.

## Cover That Breaks Sight Lines

Dense <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4grZYPq" product="Live terrarium plants">live planting</AffiliateLink> along the sides and back, plus floating plants.

In a livebearer tank cover does specific work: it lets a pursued female stop being pursued, and it gives fry a survival rate you can manage rather than none or all. Leave open swimming space through the middle, since platies are active mid-water fish.

## A Cycled, Stable Tank

The one that gets skipped because the fish survives it. A platy will live through an uncycled tank. It will do so while being exposed to ammonia.

Cycle first, use <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/3Rg5gTT" product="Water conditioner">water conditioner</AffiliateLink> on everything that goes in, and change small amounts often. Our [health issues guide](/blog/platy-health-issues-guide/) covers what chronic poor water produces in a fish that does not visibly complain.

## Feeding Across the Tank

Spread food out and do not drop it in one place, so females being chased still eat. Vary it with vegetable-based foods, since platies take more plant matter than guppies do. Our [feeding guide](/blog/platy-feeding-guide/) covers it.

## Decide About the Fry Before You Get Them

A mixed-sex platy tank produces broods roughly monthly. Within a year an unmanaged tank is overstocked, and overstocking is what finally overwhelms even a hardy fish.

Single-sex group, a plan for rehoming, or tank mates that will predate fry. Any of the three works. Not deciding is what does not.

## Priority Order

1. A cycled, stable tank before they arrive
2. At least two to three females per male, or a single-sex group
3. A group of five or more
4. Planting that breaks sight lines, including floating cover
5. Open swimming space through the middle
6. Food spread across the tank, with vegetable content
7. A decision about the fry

## What Not to Do

Do not use a hardy fish to cycle a tank. Do not stock on color and end up male-heavy. Do not keep a pair. Do not run a bare tank because platies will put up with one. Do not ignore the population. And see our [handling guide](/blog/platy-handling-guide/) before moving a pregnant female.
