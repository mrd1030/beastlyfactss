# Molly Enrichment: The Biggest Livebearer Needs Room to Graze

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Mollies get sold beside guppies and platies as if they were the same fish in a different color. They are noticeably bigger, considerably more herbivorous, and fussier about water.

<KeyTakeaway>
Two decisions carry this species. **Sex ratio**, because the harassment research that shapes livebearer stocking was partly done on a molly, and **grazing surface**, because a molly grazes algae and biofilm continuously and a spotless tank gives it nothing to do.
</KeyTakeaway>

For everything else about them, see the [Molly care guide](/guides/molly/).

## What the Research Says

The relevant thread is sexual harassment in livebearers, covered in full in our [Guppy enrichment guide](/blog/guppy-enrichment-guide/), and part of it is molly research directly.

In ***Poecilia mexicana***, a molly, males **attempt to mate almost constantly** while females are **mostly non-responsive** to male approaches. Females **flee from this harassment**, are **more vigilant in the presence of males**, and consequently have **hampered feeding opportunities**.

The wider guppy literature identifies **energy expenditure and reduced foraging ability** as direct costs to females, and one study found effects reaching the next generation, with high-harassment females producing smaller daughters and less successful sons.

<FunFact>
The vigilance finding is the one that connects to the grazing section below. A female molly spending her time watching for males is a female molly not spending it grazing, and for a fish whose whole feeding strategy is continuous grazing, that is a bigger loss than it would be for a fish that eats in bursts.
</FunFact>

## Sex Ratio

Two to three females per male minimum, and more females is better. An all-male group also works and avoids the fry entirely, with the caveat that male mollies squabble among themselves more than male guppies do, so it needs space and numbers rather than a trio.

The default failure is the same as with guppies: buying on color, ending up male-heavy.

## Grazing Surface

The molly-specific item. They are the most herbivorous of the common livebearers and work surfaces all day.

Dense <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4grZYPq" product="Live terrarium plants">live planting</AffiliateLink>, driftwood, rock and any surface that grows biofilm all give them something to graze. A tank scrubbed spotless every week and fed a pinch of flake is the impoverished version.

Supplement with vegetable-based foods and blanched vegetables, never a purely protein diet. Our [feeding guide](/blog/molly-feeding-guide/) covers the balance, and it leans further toward plants than guppy advice does.

## Hard Water, Not Necessarily Salt

The advice to add salt to a molly tank is a crude proxy for what they actually want: **hard, alkaline, mineral-rich water**.

Get the hardness right and the salt question mostly disappears. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4p1T7yd" product="Water test kit">water test kit</AffiliateLink> is worth having here, because most of this species' reputation for being delicate traces to being kept in water suited to a soft-water fish. Our [health issues guide](/blog/molly-health-issues-guide/) covers the conditions that follow.

## Space

Bigger than the livebearer shelf implies. Some varieties reach a size that makes a small tank inadequate before you count fry, and a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eSbKl2" product="20 gallon long tank">20 gallon long</AffiliateLink> is a sensible floor rather than a generous one.

Plan for the population. A mixed-sex molly tank produces broods regularly and mollies are big enough that overstocking hits water quality faster than it does with guppies. Our [tank setup guide](/blog/molly-tank-setup-guide/) covers dimensions.

## Cover for Females

Dense planting including floating plants, so a female being pursued can break line of sight and rest.

That is what cover is for in a livebearer tank. It also lets fry survive at a manageable rate, not all or none.

## Feeding Across the Tank

Spread food instead of dropping it in one corner, so females that are being chased still eat. It is the direct practical answer to the vigilance finding.

## Priority Order

1. At least two to three females per male, or an all-male group with space
2. Hard, alkaline, mineral-rich water
3. Grazing surface: planting, wood and biofilm left to grow
4. A tank sized for a large livebearer plus fry
5. Dense cover including floating plants
6. A vegetable-leaning diet, fed across the tank
7. A plan for the population

## What Not to Do

Do not stock on color and end up male-heavy. Do not keep mollies in soft water and conclude they are delicate. Do not scrub every surface spotless for a grazing fish. Do not keep them in a tank chosen for guppies. Do not feed a purely protein diet. And see our [handling guide](/blog/molly-handling-guide/) before moving a heavily pregnant female.
