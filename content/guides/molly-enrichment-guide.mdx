# Molly Enrichment: A Large Livebearer That Needs Room to Graze

<AffiliateDisclosure />

Mollies get sold beside guppies and platies as if they were the same fish in a different color. The sailfin in particular is a large fish that feeds mostly on plants and needs hard, alkaline water.

<KeyTakeaway>
Two decisions carry this species. **Sex ratio**, because the harassment research that shapes livebearer stocking was partly done on a molly, and **grazing surface**, because a molly grazes on algae and plant matter and a spotless tank gives it nothing to do.
</KeyTakeaway>

## What the Research Says

The relevant thread is sexual harassment in livebearers, which the [guppy enrichment guide](/blog/guppy-enrichment-guide/) works through, and part of it is molly research directly.

In surface-dwelling ***Poecilia mexicana***, a molly, females are **more vigilant in the presence of a male** than of another female, and that vigilance **detracts from the time they spend feeding**. In cave-dwelling populations, where sexual activity is lower, females are less vigilant and **do not feed less** when a male is near. In *Poecilia mexicana*, females also **flee from this harassment**.

In guppies, harassment effects reach the next generation, with high-harassment females producing smaller daughters and less successful sons. The guppy work shows the same cost in feeding: females spend **less time feeding** with a male present.

<FunFact>
The vigilance finding is the one that connects to the grazing section below. A female molly spending her time watching for males is a female molly not spending it grazing, and for a fish that feeds by grazing, that is a bigger loss than it would be for a fish that eats in bursts.
</FunFact>

## Sex Ratio

Two to three females per male minimum, and more females is better. An all-male group also works and avoids the fry entirely, with the caveat that male sailfin mollies can be aggressive toward each other, so it needs space and numbers rather than a trio.

The default failure is the same as with guppies: buying on color, ending up male-heavy.

## Grazing Surface

The molly-specific item. The sailfin is almost exclusively herbivorous in the wild, and the short-finned molly takes plenty of plant matter too. Mollies work surfaces all day.

Driftwood, rock and any surface that grows algae and biofilm give them something to graze. A tank scrubbed spotless every week and fed a pinch of flake is the impoverished version. Sailfins can destroy soft-leaved plants, so hardy planting such as <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B008HXF16K?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="SubstrateSource Anubias Nana Live Aquarium Plant, 1 Pot">anubias</AffiliateLink>, hornwort and floating plants is the safer choice.

Supplement with vegetable-based foods and blanched vegetables, never a purely protein diet.

## Hard Water, Not Necessarily Salt

The advice to add salt to a molly tank is a crude proxy for what they actually want: **hard, alkaline, mineral-rich water**.

Get the hardness right and the salt question mostly disappears. A <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4p1T7yd" product="Water test kit">water test kit</AffiliateLink> is worth having here, because mollies kept in soft or acidic water weaken fairly quickly, and general hardness of roughly 15 to 30 dGH is the target. Most of this species' reputation for being delicate traces to being kept in water suited to a soft-water fish. Hardness and alkalinity are separate numbers that move together, and the [freshwater pH, GH and KH guide](/blog/freshwater-ph-gh-kh-guide/) is where the difference between GH and KH is worked through.

## Space

Bigger than the livebearer shelf implies. Some varieties reach a size that makes a small tank inadequate before you count fry, and a <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eSbKl2" product="20 gallon long tank">20 gallon long</AffiliateLink> is a sensible floor rather than a generous one.

Plan for the population. A mixed-sex molly tank produces broods regularly, and an overcrowded tank fouls its water. Mollies are big enough that overstocking hits water quality faster than it does with guppies.

## Cover for Females

Dense planting including floating plants, so a female being pursued can break line of sight and rest.

That is what cover is for in a livebearer tank. It also lets fry survive at a manageable rate, not all or none.

## Feeding Across the Tank

Spread food instead of dropping it in one corner, so females that are being chased still eat. It is the direct practical answer to the vigilance finding.

## Priority Order

1. At least two to three females per male, or an all-male group with space
2. Hard, alkaline, mineral-rich water
3. Grazing surface: planting, wood and biofilm left to grow
4. A tank sized for a large livebearer plus fry
5. Dense cover including floating plants
6. A vegetable-leaning diet, fed across the tank
7. A plan for the population

## What Not to Do

Do not stock on color and end up male-heavy. Do not keep mollies in soft water and conclude they are delicate. Do not scrub every surface spotless for a grazing fish. Do not keep them in a tank chosen for guppies. Do not feed a purely protein diet.

The [platy, swordtail and molly overview](/blog/platy-swordtail-molly-overview/) sets the molly beside the other two common livebearers, where its salt tolerance is the trait that separates it.
