Glossary, A to L

Avian gastric yeastA yeast (Macrorhabdus ornithogaster), once called megabacteriosis, that disrupts digestion: a bird eating well and still losing weight. Page {{P:gut}}.
Avian polyomavirusA virus that kills unweaned chicks and that adults can carry and shed with no signs. A vaccine exists for selected parrots. Page {{P:viral}}.
Bar spacingThe gap between cage bars: half an inch (1.3 cm) or less for a cockatiel. A head-entrapment limit. Page {{P:enclosure}}.
Bird fancier's lungA form of hypersensitivity pneumonitis in people: an immune reaction to bird proteins in feather dust and dried droppings, breathed in over years. Page {{P:air}}.
Blood featherA feather still growing, with a blood supply running up the shaft. Breaking one can bleed heavily in a bird this small. Page {{P:grooming}}.
CircovirusThe family of virus behind PBFD. Page {{P:viral}}.
ClutchThe eggs a hen lays in one run: 3 to 8 for a cockatiel, 5 on average. Page {{P:laying}}.
Contact callA loud call a flock bird makes to locate the others when they are out of sight. The cockatiel's sharp call and wolf whistle are this. Page {{P:species}}.
CrestThe movable feathers on top of a cockatiel's head, raised and lowered with its emotional state. Page {{P:behavior}}.
CropThe pouch at the base of a bird's neck where food is held. A crop that stops emptying is a serious sign. Page {{P:viral}}.
CuttleboneThe calcium-rich internal shell of a cuttlefish, kept in the cage for calcium and for the beak. Page {{P:perches}}.
DNADeoxyribonucleic acid: the genetic code. A DNA test on a blood sample or a feather sexes a bird whatever its color or age. Page {{P:sexing}}.
DoxycyclineThe antibiotic used to treat a confirmed or strongly suspected case of psittacosis. Never a preventive. Page {{P:respiratory}}.
Egg bindingA formed egg the hen cannot pass. A same-day emergency. Page {{P:binding}}.
Fecal checkA vet's microscope check of a dropping for parasites such as Giardia, and for yeast. Page {{P:quarantine}}.
GiardiaA gut parasite that infects cockatiels and can cause loose stools, an intense itch, or both. Page {{P:gut}}.
Hand-raisedPulled from the nest and fed by a person. Usually tamer, and more dependent on people. Page {{P:arrival}}.
HEPAHigh-efficiency particulate air: a filter standard for air purifiers that traps very fine dust, including powder down. Page {{P:air}}.
Hepatic lipidosisFatty liver disease: fat building up in the liver, linked to an all-seed diet. Page {{P:nutrition}}.
HypocalcemiaLow blood calcium. Chronic laying drains calcium into eggshells and can cause weakness, fractures and seizures. Page {{P:laying}}.
Hypovitaminosis AVitamin A deficiency, from a seed-heavy diet. It shows in the nose, eyes, airway, kidneys and feathers. Page {{P:nutrition}}.
KeelThe blade of breastbone down the center of the chest. Felt, not seen, to score body condition. Page {{P:weight}}.
LutinoA color mutation with no dark pigment: a yellow and white cockatiel that cannot be sexed by plumage. Page {{P:sexing}}.
Reference

Glossary, M to Z

MoltThe gradual replacement of feathers, at least once a year. Slow, with whole feathers and no bald skin. Page {{P:molt}}.
Night frightSudden, panicked flapping in the dark that can injure a cockatiel on the bars. A night light or cover helps prevent it. Page {{P:climate}}.
Nutritional secondary hyperparathyroidismA bone condition that a long calcium shortfall can lead to. Cockatiels are especially prone to the calcium deficiency behind it. Page {{P:nutrition}}.
OviductThe tube an egg travels down. Without enough calcium it cannot contract hard enough to push an egg out. Page {{P:binding}}.
OxytocinA hormone a vet may give an egg-bound hen to make the oviduct contract. Page {{P:binding}}.
PasteurellaBacteria in cat saliva that can cause fatal blood poisoning in a bitten bird within hours. Page {{P:hazards}}.
PBFDPsittacine beak and feather disease: a circovirus that stunts feathers and damages the beak. No cure. Page {{P:viral}}.
PCRPolymerase chain reaction: a lab test that detects a germ's genetic material, even in a bird that looks healthy. Page {{P:quarantine}}.
PelletsA formulated food that packs balanced nutrition into every bite. About 75 to 80% of the diet. Page {{P:diet}}.
PhotoperiodHours of light per day. A long photoperiod is a major trigger for chronic laying. Page {{P:climate}}.
PiedA color mutation that scatters the pigments at random. The hardest cockatiel of all to sex by plumage. Page {{P:sexing}}.
Pin featherA new feather in its waxy sheath. Tender, and worked loose by preening. Page {{P:molt}}.
PolyuriaExtra clear urine with the solid fecal part still formed. Different from diarrhea. Page {{P:droppings}}.
Powder downFeathers that grow continuously and flake into a fine keratin dust. Cockatiels, cockatoos and African greys make it. Page {{P:air}}.
Primary feathersThe long outer flight feathers. A wing clip shortens four to seven of them on each wing. Page {{P:flight}}.
PsittacosisParrot fever, or avian chlamydiosis, caused by Chlamydia psittaci. It can spread to people. Page {{P:respiratory}}.
PTFEPolytetrafluoroethylene: the nonstick coating on many pans. Above 280°C (536°F) it releases particles and gases that kill birds. Page {{P:air}}.
QuarantineKeeping a new bird apart, in a separate room on separate equipment, for 30 to 45 days. Page {{P:quarantine}}.
Units°F, degrees Fahrenheit, and °C, degrees Celsius, for temperature; in (inches), ft (feet) and cm (centimeters) for size; g (grams) for weight; % (percent) for shares of the diet.
UratesThe white or cream chalky part of a dropping: uric acid, how a bird excretes nitrogen while saving water. Page {{P:droppings}}.
UVUltraviolet: light beyond what the eye sees, and the part of sunlight a cockatiel uses to make vitamin D. Window glass blocks it. Page {{P:climate}}.
VentThe single opening that droppings and eggs both leave through. Page {{P:droppings}}.
WhitefaceA color mutation with no yellow or orange, so the face cue for sexing is gone. Page {{P:sexing}}.
Zinc poisoningPoisoning from galvanized cage hardware, once called "new wire disease." Page {{P:hazards}}.
ZoonoticAble to spread from animals to people. Psittacosis is. Page {{P:respiratory}}.
