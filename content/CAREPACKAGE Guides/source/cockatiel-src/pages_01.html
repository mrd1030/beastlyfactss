Why this package exists The cockatiel is sold as the easy, gentle first parrot, and much of that is true. What follows from it is not: a lot of the advice in circulation was written for a bird nobody expected to keep for fifteen years. A seed bowl, a tall narrow cage bought for its looks, and a bedroom that stays pitch dark all night are still normal advice somewhere, and all three shorten a cockatiel's life or injure it. Every figure here is cross-checked against the veterinary and published sources listed on page {{P:sources}}.
Not a substitute for An avian veterinarian. This is a husbandry reference for day-to-day care. Anything urgent or health-related is a vet call, not a page in this book, and a cockatiel hides illness until it is far along. Find an avian vet before you need one and write the number on the emergency card.

Quick profile

Everything at a glance before you read the detail.
Scientific nameNymphicus hollandicus. The smallest member of the cockatoo family, and the only species in its genus.
Adult size12 to 13 in (30 to 33 cm) including the tail; 80 to 125 g, about 90 g on average
Captive lifespan12 to 15 years typically, with up to about 25 reported; the longevity record is 35 years
Native rangeOpen grassland, scrubland and lightly wooded country across Australia, in nomadic flocks that follow water and seeding grasses
Diet typeGrass seeds, grains, berries and insects in the wild; in captivity 75 to 80% pellets, with vegetables and a little fruit (page {{P:diet}})
Cage, one birdAbout 20×20×30 in (51×51×76 cm) as the minimum; 24×24×30 in (61×61×76 cm) for easier movement. Wider beats taller.
Bar spacingHalf an inch (1.3 cm) or smaller
Room temperatureAbout 70 to 80°F (21 to 27°C), with no drafts and no direct sun on the cage
Darkness and light10 to 12 hours of quiet, dark sleep every night, with a cover or a small night light against night frights. 10 to 12 hours a day of UV (ultraviolet) light exposure, or safe supervised time outdoors.
SexingBy plumage in a standard gray adult over 6 months old; most color varieties need a DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid) test (page {{P:sexing}})
Handling difficultyBeginner, built on trust over 7 to 14 days of settling and short sessions
Conservation statusLeast Concern, the lowest-risk grade on the global list of threatened species

Cost overview

Initial setup

$295 to $601 for a complete starter setup, bird included ($75 to $250 of it, depending on the color). The cage is often the biggest single line, more than the bird. Every line is on page {{P:budget}}.

Ongoing

About $200 to $350 a year for food, toy replacement and cage upkeep, roughly $17 to $29 a month. Avian vet exams come on top, at least once a year.

The line item people leave out Avian vets are a specialized field, harder to find and often pricier than a general small-animal clinic. Pet insurance for birds is uncommon, and reproductive emergencies like egg binding arrive with no warning. Set money aside for them from the start.

Fun facts worth knowing

  • Cockatiels were the first parrot species to be widely bred in captivity, and remain among the most popular pet birds in the world.
  • Males especially learn whistled tunes and will repeat them for hours, and many pick up short phrases too.
  • Australia bars the export of its native birds, so every cockatiel kept anywhere else descends from stock that was already outside the country.

The species, colors, size & lifespan

What you are buying, how big it gets, and how long it stays.

A small cockatoo

The cockatiel is Nymphicus hollandicus, the smallest member of the cockatoo family and endemic to Australia. In the wild it lives in open country and lightly wooded areas across the continent, moving with the water and with whichever grasses are seeding. Like the larger cockatoos it carries a movable crest and produces powder down, a fine feather dust that films the furniture near the cage (page {{P:air}}). It was the first parrot species to be widely bred in captivity, and it is prized for a gentle temperament, its whistling and how readily it trains.

Colors

The normal gray, with a yellow face and orange cheek patches, is the closest color to the wild bird and is usually the cheapest, because it needs no selective breeding to produce. Every other color carries a premium. Four mutations underpin nearly every variety on sale: pied, which appeared in California in 1949, lutino in 1958, cinnamon during the 1950s, and pearl in 1967. Color matters for one practical reason: the plumage sexing method works on the normal gray and fails on most of the rest (page {{P:sexing}}). The care in this package applies to every color.

Size and age

Length12 to 13 in (30 to 33 cm) including the long tail
Weight80 to 125 g, about 90 g on average. Your own bird's settled weight matters more than the range (page {{P:weight}}).
WeaningAround 42 to 52 days old. Most pet cockatiels are bought already weaned onto solid food.
Maturity6 to 12 months. Until about 6 months every young cockatiel is colored like a hen.

Lifespan

12 to 15 years is typical, a well-kept bird can reach about 25, and the longevity record is 35 years for one exceptional bird. Budget for the long end. A cockatiel bought for a 10-year-old can still be on its perch when that child finishes college, and the cage, the avian vet and the daily routine have to last all of those years.

Whistling and noise

Cockatiels are whistlers far more than talkers. They learn and repeat whole melodies, and some pick up a few words, but whistling is the real strength, and males are the ones known for it. Most of the day is whistling and chatter at a volume most households barely register. The exception is a sharp contact call or the well-known wolf whistle, which cuts through a quiet room in short bursts, especially during a hormonal phase. In an apartment with thin walls, that is worth weighing before you buy.

A prey bird first Wild cockatiels live in flocks where a predator picks off the bird that looks weakest. That one fact explains three things in this book: the bird wants company, it hides illness until it cannot, and a sudden movement in the dark sends it into a panic.
