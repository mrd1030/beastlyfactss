Setup checklist & targets

Tick each line before the snake goes in, and run through it again whenever something seems off.

Enclosure

Sized for the sex: at least 30×13×13 in (76×33×33 cm) for a male, 36×18×16 in (91×46×41 cm) for a female
Front-opening, with a lid that latches or clamps. No tape anywhere
Glass sides covered on three sides
Out of the kitchen and out of a sunny window

Heat and light

Low-wattage halogen over the screen at the warm end, flat stone beneath
Every heat source on a thermostat matched to it; probe placed per its instructions
Lamp at least 4 to 6 in (10 to 15 cm) above the highest reachable spot
Two digital thermometers, plus an infrared thermometer
Lights on a 12-hour timer. No light at night
Optional UVB: T5 HO 5.0 or 6%, half to two thirds of the length, over screen, not glass

Inside

3 to 6 in (8 to 15 cm) of dry soil and sand mix, deepest at the cool end
Warm hide, cool hide, and a humid hide
Cork, branches and leaf litter for cover
A water dish large enough to get into
Digital hygrometer reading 30 to 50%

Feeding and records

Separate lidded feeding tub, lined with paper towel
Feeding tongs and a snake hook
Frozen mice sized to the snake, in a freezer away from human food
Kitchen scale in grams, and the owner log

Before it comes home

State and local rules checked, pages {{P:legal}} to {{P:legal3}}
Reptile vet found, first exam booked within 72 hours
Quarantine room ready if you keep other reptiles

Emergency & quick targets

Call the vet now if Open-mouth breathing, wheezing, drooling or mucus. No droppings with bloating and lethargy. Belly scales discolored or blistered and spreading. Red spots, saliva or cheesy material in the mouth. Mites. A burn. Anything protruding from the vent: keep it moist, do not push it back. A meal brought back up twice. Weight down more than 10%. Limp or unresponsive at normal temperature. A female past her laying window who is weak.

First 30 days

Most first-month problems come from doing too much: handling too soon, feeding too often, or fixing a hood and hiss that needs no fixing.

Before it arrives

Enclosure set up and running, heat, light and thermostat checked with both thermometers for a full day
Humidity confirmed 30 to 50%
Legal check done, and the receipt names Heterodon nasicus, captive-bred
Vet chosen; first exam booked within 72 hours
Asked the seller: hatch date, last meal, prey size, plain or scented

Week 1

Snake straight into its enclosure, or into quarantine if you keep other reptiles
No handling. Two weeks to settle
First vet exam with a fresh stool sample; record the baseline weight
Read both thermometers and the hygrometer daily
Fresh water daily; spot-clean droppings

Week 2

First feeding, in the feeding tub, on the seller's schedule and prey size
Leave it alone for 48 hours after
Refused? No panic. Page {{P:refusals}}

Week 3

First handling session: lift out with a hook, from the side, no more than 5 minutes, end on a calm snake
Not within 48 hours of a meal, not during a shed
First weekly weigh-in in the log
Probe check: still where it belongs?

Week 4

Second weigh-in. Is the line flat, rising or falling?
Cross-section body condition check
Review the feeding record against page {{P:feeding}}
Look over the droppings and urates, page {{P:stool}}

Throughout

Wash hands with soap after every contact; nothing cleaned in the kitchen sink
No misting, ever
Hooding, hissing, playing dead: step back and let it settle
Quarantine running its full 3 to 6 months, if you keep other reptiles
Sitter sheet, page {{P:sitter}}, filled in before your first trip
The one thing to do every single day Read the thermometers and the hygrometer and write them down. A thermostat that has failed, a probe that has slipped, or a dish that has spilled into the substrate all show up in those numbers before they show up in the snake.

Daily, weekly & seasonal routine

A low-maintenance snake, once the routine is written down.

Daily

Read both thermometers and the hygrometer
Read the second thermometer away from the thermostat probe
Empty, wash and refill the water dish
Pull droppings and urates as you see them; note what they looked like
Check lights came on and went off on time
Wash hands with soap after any contact

Weekly

Weigh in grams and write it down
Cross-section body condition check
Check the thermostat probe has not moved
Spot-check surfaces with the infrared thermometer
One or two short handling sessions, never within 48 hours of a meal

Every feeding

Thaw in the refrigerator; never the microwave or the kitchen sink
Feed in the tub, from tongs; record the date, prey and result
Wash hands, tongs and tub

Every shed

Moisten the humid hide substrate when the eyes cloud
Leave it alone until the shed is off
Check the skin for both eye caps and the tail tip

Every three months

Replace all substrate and disinfect, if the bed stayed dry and clean. Monthly if it went damp or smelly
Check the frozen mouse stock date

Yearly

Wellness exam and fecal test
Replace the UVB tube, if you use one, on the maker's schedule
Review prey size against current weight

Seasonal

Fall: expect a slowdown; weigh every 1 to 2 weeks while it lasts
Winter: watch night temperatures as the house cools; add heat with no light below 75°F (24°C)
Spring: appetite should return. Still refusing by spring is a vet visit
Summer: a hot room pushes the basking surface up; check it is not well above 95°F (35°C)

Power outages, travel & transport

Heat is the one thing a snake cannot make for itself. For a hognose the line to hold is 75°F (24°C).

A healthy adult snake tolerates one ordinary cool night without harm. The risk is duration: sustained lower temperatures leave a reptile more open to infection and can stop it eating. For a hognose, the normal night low is 75 to 78°F (24 to 26°C), and 75°F (24°C) is the point to add heat, move the snake, or call the sitter.

When the power goes out: do

  • Put a battery thermometer in the enclosure so you know the real number.
  • Drape thick towels or a blanket over it, leaving a vent gap.
  • Or move the snake in a ventilated tub to the warmest room, usually one without outside walls.
  • A wrapped hot water bottle or hand warmer beside the snake, never touching it.
  • Stop feeding until heat is reliably back. Keep water available.
  • More than a day or two below 75°F (24°C): board it with a vet or a friend with power.

Don't

  • Use candles, a gas stove or any open flame near the enclosure: a fire and carbon monoxide risk.
  • Put a heat pack against the snake. A cold, sluggish snake cannot move away in time.
  • Feed to "keep its strength up." Food sitting in a cold gut is a risk.
  • Warm a cold, unresponsive snake in hot water.

Call the vet if

Open-mouth breathing, wheezing or clicking, mucus at the nose or mouth, or a snake that stays limp once it is back at normal temperature.

An adult fed every two weeks usually needs no feeding at all on a trip of a week. Time the meal before you leave, then let the sitter skip it.

Transport and vet trips

  • A ventilated plastic tub with a towel to grip, inside a dark bag or box. No loose substrate and no water dish.
  • Skip the meal before a trip. Warm the car first in cold weather, and on a trip over about 30 minutes below 65°F (18°C), add a wrapped hand warmer beside the tub.
  • Check it every 20 to 30 minutes at a stop. Never leave a snake in a parked car, hot or cold, even for a few minutes.
Outage kit, packed before you need it Two or three hand warmers or a hot water bottle, two thick towels, a battery thermometer, a ventilated tub, and the vet and emergency clinic numbers on paper, not only in a phone that might die.

Weight: same scale, same tub, every week. Meal: write "R" for a refusal and "Reg" for a regurgitation. Shed: the date, and "eye caps off" once you have checked the skin.

Why bother logging A hognose that is getting sick often shows it on the scale before it shows it anywhere else. Sixteen rows is four months, long enough to see a real trend rather than a bad week, and it is the first thing a vet will want to see. For an adult, it is also how you notice the slow climb toward obesity.
