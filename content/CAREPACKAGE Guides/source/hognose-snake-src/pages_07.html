Glossary, A to H

Albino, leucistic, xanthicBred color forms: albino lacks dark pigment, leucistic is pale overall, xanthic is yellow. Indiana exempts captive-bred animals in these forms. Page {{P:legal2}}.
Ambient humidityThe humidity of the enclosure air in general, as opposed to inside the humid hide. 30 to 50% for this species. Page {{P:humidity}}.
AnemiaToo few red blood cells, here from blood lost to mites. Page {{P:parasites}}.
AntiparasiticA drug that kills parasites. The vet chooses it and sets the dose.
Basking surfaceThe flat spot under the heat lamp where the snake warms up: 90 to 95°F (32 to 35°C).
BioactiveA planted substrate with a cleanup crew of tiny invertebrates that break down waste.
BrumationThe reptile version of a winter slowdown: less activity, less appetite, slower metabolism. Not needed by a pet. Page {{P:seasonal}}.
Captive-bredHatched from parents kept by people, rather than caught in the wild. Several state laws turn on it.
CellulitisAn inflamed infection of the skin and the tissue under it. A sign in the 2009 bite case. Page {{P:venom}}.
Ceramic heat emitterA screw-in heater that gives off heat with no visible light, usable at night.
Cloaca and ventThe cloaca is the single chamber that waste, urates and eggs all pass through; the vent is its opening under the tail.
Color formA line bred for a color or pattern, also called a morph. The care is identical; only the price changes.
ColubridA member of the snake family Colubridae, the family the hognose is filed under in New York City's rule. Page {{P:legal}}.
ConditionalA legal status: allowed without a permit only if a stated condition is met. Page {{P:legal2}}.
CryptosporidiumA single-celled stomach parasite of snakes with no reliable cure. Page {{P:parasites}}.
Death feigningPlaying dead: rolling belly up, mouth open, tongue out. Also called thanatosis. Normal. Page {{P:display}}.
DevenomizedVenom glands removed or disabled. New York City bans a hognose even if it has been. Page {{P:legal}}.
Dimming thermostatA thermostat that turns a heat lamp's power up and down like a light dimmer. The match for a halogen. Page {{P:heating}}.
Duvernoy's glandThe gland in the upper jaw that produces a hognose's mild venom, feeding the rear fangs. Page {{P:venom}}.
DysecdysisA bad or stuck shed. Page {{P:shedding}}.
DystociaEgg binding: a female unable to pass her eggs. An emergency. Page {{P:eggs}}.
EcdysisShedding the skin. Page {{P:shedding}}.
EnrichmentFeatures that let an animal do what it naturally does: for a hognose, above all, digging. Page {{P:enrichment}}.
EnvenomingVenom actually entering the body in a bite. Page {{P:venom}}.
Eye capThe clear scale over each eye, also called the spectacle. It sheds with the skin; one left on needs a vet.
Fecal testA lab check of a stool sample for parasites. Part of every new snake's first vet visit.
Ferguson zoneA ranking of reptiles by how much ultraviolet light they seek in the wild, zone 1 to zone 4. Hognoses are zone 2. Page {{P:humidity}}.
Frozen-thawedPrey bought frozen and thawed before feeding. The standard for this species. Page {{P:thawing}}.
Fuzzy, peach fuzzyA fuzzy is a young mouse with its first fur; a peach fuzzy sits between a pinky and a fuzzy. Page {{P:feeding}}.
GoutA painful disease from uric acid building up in the joints or organs. High-protein feeding and dehydration push toward it.
GradientThe spread of temperatures from the warm end to the cool end, so the snake can choose. Page {{P:temperature}}.
GravidCarrying eggs. Page {{P:eggs}}.
Halogen basking bulbA bright, focused bulb that heats the spot beneath it. The recommended day heat for this species.
HemipenesA male snake's paired reproductive organs, kept inside the base of the tail. What probing and popping detect.
HideA box, cave or piece of cork bark the snake can get fully inside, out of sight. Page {{P:enclosure}}.
HoodingFlattening the neck into a cobra-like hood as a bluff. Page {{P:display}}.

Glossary, H to Z

HopperA young, weaned mouse, bigger than a fuzzy.
Humid hideA hide with moistened substrate or moss inside, the one damp spot in a dry enclosure. Page {{P:humidity}}.
HygrometerA humidity meter. Digital, read at snake level.
ImpactionA gut blocked by swallowed substrate or other material. Page {{P:impaction}}.
IncubationKeeping eggs warm until they hatch. Page {{P:eggs}}.
Infrared thermometerA handheld point-and-read thermometer for surface temperatures.
Lymph nodesSmall glands that help fight infection; they swelled in the 2009 bite case.
MitesTiny blood-feeding parasites, Ophionyssus natricis in snakes. Page {{P:parasites}}.
MuskA foul-smelling fluid released from the vent in defense.
Native speciesA species that lives wild in that state. Native-wildlife law is what restricts hognoses most often.
Nongame speciesWildlife that is not hunted for sport or food. Several states cap or protect native nongame reptiles.
OrdinanceA city or county law. It can be stricter than the state.
PermethrinAn insecticide. Some permethrin products are highly toxic to hognoses: mite treatment is the vet's call.
PinkyA newborn, hairless mouse, the first food for a hatchling.
Probing and poppingThe two hands-on ways to sex a snake. Vet or experienced breeder only. Page {{P:growth}}.
ProlapseTissue pushed out through the vent. An emergency. Page {{P:shedding}}.
Pulse proportional thermostatA thermostat that sends short pulses of power. The match for a ceramic heat emitter.
PVCPolyvinyl chloride, the rigid plastic many enclosures are made of.
QuarantineKeeping a new animal apart from other reptiles for 3 to 6 months. Page {{P:arrival}}.
RadiographAn X-ray image.
Rear-fangedWith enlarged fangs toward the back of the upper jaw rather than at the front.
RegurgitationA swallowed meal brought back up. Page {{P:impaction}}.
Rostral scaleThe scale at the tip of the snout, upturned and shovel-shaped in a hognose for digging.
SalmonellaBacteria healthy reptiles carry and shed in droppings. Hands and surfaces to mouth is the risk. Page {{P:handling}}.
Scale rotA belly-skin infection from damp, dirty substrate; blister disease is its early stage, with pustules (small pus-filled bumps). Page {{P:respiratory}}.
ScentingRubbing prey with a scent such as fish or toad to tempt a refusing snake. Page {{P:refusals}}.
Species of special concern, of greatest conservation needState conservation listings below threatened. Several states bar keeping the eastern hognose because of them.
StomatitisMouth rot: infection of the tissue lining the mouth. Page {{P:shedding}}.
SubstrateThe loose material on the enclosure floor. Page {{P:substrate}}.
T5 HOA slim fluorescent tube 5/8 in across (T5) in the high-output (HO) format. The usual UVB source.
ThermostatA controller that cuts the heat at a set temperature. Non-negotiable. Page {{P:heating}}.
UltrasoundImaging with sound waves. A non-invasive way for a vet to sex a snake or find eggs.
Unitsin, inches; ft, feet; cm, centimeters; mm, millimeters; g, grams; °F and °C, degrees Fahrenheit and Celsius; %, percent.
UratesThe white, chalky paste of uric acid passed with a reptile's droppings. Page {{P:stool}}.
UVBUltraviolet B, the part of sunlight a reptile's skin uses to make vitamin D3. Page {{P:humidity}}.
UVIUltraviolet index, a measure of UVB strength at one spot. 1.1 to 3.0 where a hognose basks.
Wild-typeThe natural color and pattern, not a bred color form. The cheapest hognose to buy.

Research on this species

  • Weinstein and Keyler (2009), Toxicon 54(3): Local envenoming by the Western hognose snake (Heterodon nasicus): a case report and review of medically significant Heterodon bites. The bite case, its five-month recovery, and the conclusion that hognoses should not be considered dangerous.
  • Damm, Vilcinskas, Kreuels and Lüddecke (2026), Frontiers in Amphibian and Reptile Science: 34 reports of snakebite envenoming by the Western Hognose snake, a retrospective online survey. Bite counts, feeding context, hold time against symptoms, recovery times and the keeper recommendations.
  • Durso and Mullin, Ethology 120: Intrinsic and extrinsic factors influence expression of defensive behavior in Plains Hog-nosed Snakes. Death feigning by size, sex and temperature.
  • Nagabaskaran, Skinner and Miller (2022), Animals 12(23): Western hognose snakes prefer environmental enrichment. The preference study.
  • Kerns, Animal Diversity Web, University of Michigan: Heterodon nasicus. Range, habitat, length and weight, wild lifespan, toads in the diet, clutch size and hatching time, the winter season, and the state listings.
  • Galewood, Reptiles Magazine: Western Hognose Snake Care and Breeding. Adult lengths and weights by sex, hatchling weights, the pre-lay shed and laying box, incubation, and the warnings on permethrin and Cryptosporidium.
  • Healey, ReptiFiles: the hognose snake care guide and its pages on temperatures, humidity and lighting, handling and body language, brumation, and mites. The middle temperature band and 60°F (16°C) night floor, the basking stone, the UVB specification, cool water for a bite that holds on, and the brumation schedule.

Veterinary references

  • Merck Veterinary Manual, pet owner version: Disorders and Diseases of Reptiles. Respiratory infection, mouth rot, mites and the enclosure clean, blister disease, gout, prolapse, and egg binding diagnosis and treatment.
  • Merck Veterinary Manual: Parasitic Diseases of Reptiles. Cryptosporidium signs, diagnosis and treatment, the permethrin licensed for reptiles, and the role of quarantine.
  • VCA Animal Hospitals (originally Veterinary Centers of America): Common Snake Health Issues. Egg binding causes and signs, retained eye caps, burns and their causes, and the causes of a lost appetite.

The Beastly Facts guides cite further veterinary sources of their own, among them the Merck Veterinary Manual's reptile husbandry, nutrition and quarantine pages, VCA's snake housing page, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention on reptiles, amphibians and frozen feeder rodents, and Iowa State University's biosecurity guidance.
