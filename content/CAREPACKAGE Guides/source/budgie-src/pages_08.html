Glossary, A to L

Avian bornavirusA virus screened for at the first vet exam, along with psittacosis, polyomavirus and PBFD. Page {{P:quarantine}}.
Avian gastric yeastA yeast (Macrorhabdus ornithogaster), once called megabacteriosis, that disrupts digestion. The classic cause of "going light": a budgie eating well and losing weight. Page {{P:mites}}.
Avian polyomavirusA virus that kills unweaned chicks and leaves survivors with French molt. Called budgerigar fledgling disease in budgies. Page {{P:viral}}.
Bar spacingThe gap between cage bars: half an inch (1.3 cm) or less for a budgie. A head-entrapment and escape limit. Page {{P:enclosure}}.
Blood featherA feather still growing, with a blood supply running up the shaft. Breaking one can bleed heavily in a bird this small. Page {{P:grooming}}.
CankerThe disease caused by the trichomonas parasite: vomiting, sticky head feathers and a swollen crop. Page {{P:mites}}.
CereThe fleshy patch above the beak where the nostrils sit. Its color shows sex, age and sometimes illness. Page {{P:sexing}}.
CircovirusThe family of virus behind PBFD. Page {{P:viral}}.
ClutchThe eggs a hen lays in one run: 4 to 6 on average for a budgie. Page {{P:laying}}.
Contact callA call a bird makes expecting a flock-mate to call back and confirm both are safe. The root of noise at dawn and dusk. Page {{P:behavior}}.
CropThe pouch at the base of a bird's neck where food is held. A crop that stops emptying is a serious sign. Page {{P:viral}}.
CuttleboneThe calcium-rich internal shell of a cuttlefish, kept in the cage for calcium and for the beak to wear against. Page {{P:perches}}.
DNADeoxyribonucleic acid: the genetic code. A DNA test on a blood sample or a feather sexes a bird whatever its color or age. Page {{P:sexing}}.
DoxycyclineThe antibiotic used to treat a confirmed case of psittacosis, for 45 days, or 30 in a budgerigar. Page {{P:respiratory}}.
Egg bindingA formed egg the hen cannot pass. A same-day emergency. Page {{P:binding}}.
Fecal checkA vet's microscope check of a dropping for parasites and yeast. Pages {{P:quarantine}}, {{P:mites}}.
French moltThe chronic feather form of polyomavirus in budgies: abnormal flight and tail feathers, often a bird that cannot fly. Also called feather dusters. Page {{P:viral}}.
GoiterAn enlarged thyroid, from too little iodine in a seed diet. It sounds like a breathing noise, not a swelling. Page {{P:respiratory}}.
Gram stainA lab stain that shows abnormal bacteria or yeast in a swab or dropping. Page {{P:quarantine}}.
Hand-raisedPulled from the nest and fed by a person. Usually tamer, and more dependent on people. Page {{P:arrival}}.
Hepatic lipidosisFatty liver disease: fat building up in the liver, most often from an all-seed diet. Page {{P:obesity}}.
HypocalcemiaLow blood calcium. Chronic laying drains calcium into eggshells and can cause weakness, fractures and seizures. Page {{P:laying}}.
KeelThe blade of breastbone down the center of the chest. Felt, not seen, to score body condition. Page {{P:weight}}.
Knemidocoptes pilaeThe mite that causes scaly face and scaly leg. Page {{P:mites}}.
Leuprolide acetateA hormone drug given by injection to stop laying for three to four weeks. Prescribed by an avian vet. Page {{P:laying}}.
LipomaA benign fatty growth, linked to high-fat, seed-heavy diets. Page {{P:obesity}}.
Reference

Glossary, M to Z

MoltThe gradual replacement of feathers, at least once a year. Slow, with whole feathers and no bald skin. Page {{P:molt}}.
NeoplasiaTumors. Budgies have an unusually high rate, mostly of the kidney and gonads. Page {{P:obesity}}.
OviductThe tube an egg travels down. It can become diseased after months of chronic laying. Page {{P:laying}}.
PasteurellaBacteria in cat saliva that can cause fatal blood poisoning in a bitten bird within hours. Page {{P:hazards}}.
PBFDPsittacine beak and feather disease: a circovirus that stunts feathers and damages the beak. No cure and no vaccine. Page {{P:viral}}.
PCRPolymerase chain reaction: a lab test that detects a germ's genetic material, even in a bird that looks healthy. Pages {{P:quarantine}}, {{P:viral}}.
PelletsA formulated food that packs balanced nutrition into every bite. The base of the diet at 60 to 80%. Page {{P:diet}}.
PhotoperiodHours of light per day. A day over 12 hours is a risk factor for chronic laying. Page {{P:climate}}.
Pin featherA new feather in its waxy sheath. Tender, and worked loose by preening. Page {{P:molt}}.
PolyuriaExtra clear liquid urine with the solid fecal part still formed. Different from diarrhea. Page {{P:droppings}}.
Primary feathersThe long outer flight feathers. A wing clip shortens four to seven of them on each wing. Page {{P:flight}}.
PsittacosisParrot fever, caused by Chlamydia psittaci. It can spread to people. Page {{P:respiratory}}.
PTFEPolytetrafluoroethylene: the nonstick coating on many pans. Past 280°C (536°F) it gives off gases that damage a bird's lungs. Page {{P:air}}.
QuarantineKeeping a new bird apart, in a separate room on separate equipment, for 30 to 45 days. Page {{P:quarantine}}.
ThyroidThe gland that makes a hormone from iodine. It enlarges on an iodine-poor seed diet. Page {{P:respiratory}}.
TrichomonasThe parasite behind canker, a common cause of death in young budgies. Page {{P:mites}}.
Units°F, degrees Fahrenheit, and °C, degrees Celsius, for temperature; in (inches), ft (feet) and cm (centimeters) for size; g (grams) for weight; % (percent) for shares of the diet.
UratesThe white or cream chalky part of a dropping: uric acid, how a bird excretes nitrogen while saving water. Page {{P:droppings}}.
UVUltraviolet: light beyond what the eye sees. An indoor budgie away from unfiltered sunlight needs a full-spectrum UV light made for birds. Page {{P:climate}}.
VentThe single opening that droppings and eggs both leave through. Page {{P:droppings}}.
XanthomaA yellow, thickened, cholesterol-loaded patch of skin, linked to a high-fat diet. Page {{P:obesity}}.
Zinc poisoningPoisoning from galvanized cage hardware, once called "new wire disease." Page {{P:hazards}}.
ZoonoticAble to spread from animals to people. Psittacosis is. Page {{P:respiratory}}.
