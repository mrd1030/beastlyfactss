Glossary, A to G

°FDegrees Fahrenheit, the temperature scale on most United States thermometers. Page {{P:temperature}}.
°CDegrees Celsius; 0 is freezing, 100 is boiling water. Page {{P:temperature}}.
AlfalfaA hay made from a legume, not a grass, with more protein and calcium than a healthy adult needs. Suited to growing, pregnant and nursing guinea pigs. Page {{P:diet}}.
AnesthesiaMedicine that puts an animal into a controlled, unconscious state for surgery or a dental. Guinea pigs carry real risk under it and need a vet who does it regularly. Page {{P:dental}}.
Assisted feedingFeeding a guinea pig that will not eat by syringe, with a critical care food, placed into the side of the mouth. Done as the vet directs. Page {{P:stasis}}.
BoarA male guinea pig. Page {{P:sexing}}.
Boar buttAn impacted rectal pouch: a pocket behind the anus where droppings pack in, mostly in older intact males. Page {{P:skin}}.
BordetellaA bacterium, Bordetella bronchiseptica, that causes respiratory infection in guinea pigs. Rabbits and dogs can carry it without looking ill. Page {{P:respiratory}}.
BumblefootPododermatitis: inflammation and infection of the soles of the feet, from wire floors, dirty bedding, obesity, overgrown nails or vitamin C deficiency. Page {{P:skin}}.
C&CCubes and corrugated plastic: an enclosure built from wire storage cubes on a corrugated plastic base, the usual way to get real floor area for the price. Page {{P:enclosure}}.
Calcium carbonateThe mineral that forms most guinea pig bladder stones. Page {{P:urinary}}.
CavyAnother name for a guinea pig. A "cavy-savvy" vet is one who regularly treats them. Page {{P:profile}}.
CecumThe large pouch of the gut where bacteria ferment fiber. It is why hay and antibiotic choice both matter. Page {{P:antibiotics}}.
Cervical lymphadenitisAn infected lymph node in the neck, which shows as a large swelling on one side. Page {{P:skin}}.
cmCentimeter, one hundredth of a meter. 2.54 cm make an inch. Page {{P:enclosure}}.
Coat typesThe English or American shorthair, Abyssinian (swirled hair), Peruvian (long-haired), Silkie, Teddy and Texel. Page {{P:species}}.
CollagenThe structural protein that holds skin, joints, gums and blood vessels together. Vitamin C is needed to make it. Page {{P:vitc}}.
CystotomySurgery that opens the bladder to remove a stone. Page {{P:urinary}}.
EnterotoxemiaToxins from overgrown gut bacteria flooding the body, often after the wrong antibiotic. It can kill within one to two days. Page {{P:antibiotics}}.
ForagingSearching for food. A wild guinea pig can spend up to 80% of the day doing it. Page {{P:enrichment}}.
gGram, the unit a kitchen scale reads: 28 g make about an ounce, and 1,000 g make a kilogram. Page {{P:growth}}.
Gastrointestinal (GI)Of the stomach and intestines, the gut. Page {{P:stasis}}.
GI stasisGastrointestinal stasis: the gut slowing or stopping. The emergency this species is most likely to have. Page {{P:stasis}}.
GestationPregnancy. In guinea pigs 59 to 72 days. Page {{P:sexing}}.
Gram-positiveA broad group of bacteria that includes most of the ones in a guinea pig's gut. Antibiotics aimed at them can wreck the gut. Page {{P:antibiotics}}.
Grease glandA gland at the base of the spine, above the anus, more prominent in males, that can get greasy and matted. Page {{P:grooming}}.

Glossary, H to Z

HideA shelter, such as a hut, pipe or tunnel, where a prey animal can retreat. Page {{P:bedding}}.
inInch. 1 in is 2.54 cm. Page {{P:enclosure}}.
kgKilogram, 1,000 g, about 2.2 lb. Page {{P:vitc}}.
Kiln-dried pinePine dried in a kiln, which drives off most of the oils. The one wood bedding generally accepted. Page {{P:bedding}}.
lbPound, 454 g. Page {{P:profile}}.
Mange miteTrixacarus caviae, a burrowing mite that causes itching so intense it can look like a seizure. Page {{P:skin}}.
mgMilligram, a thousandth of a gram. Page {{P:vitc}}.
mg/kgMilligrams per kilogram of body weight: a dose that scales with the animal's size. Page {{P:vitc}}.
NeophobiaFear of anything new, including food. A reason to change a diet gradually. Page {{P:diet}}.
NeuteringSurgery on a boar so that he can live with sows without fathering litters. He stays able to father young for some weeks afterward. Page {{P:sexing}}.
NursingProducing milk for young. Page {{P:diet}}.
Obligate nasal breatherAn animal that cannot switch to breathing through the mouth when the nose blocks. An open-mouthed guinea pig is an emergency. Page {{P:respiratory}}.
Ovarian cystA fluid-filled sac on an ovary, very common in unspayed sows. Functional ones make hormones. Page {{P:repro}}.
OvariohysterectomySpaying: surgery that removes the ovaries and the uterus. Page {{P:repro}}.
PalpatingFeeling the abdomen with the hands for an enlarged organ. Page {{P:repro}}.
PopcorningRepeated bouncing hops and twists. A sign of excitement. Page {{P:behavior}}.
PVCPolyvinyl chloride, a hard plastic. PVC pipe makes a tunnel or hide. Page {{P:bedding}}.
QuarantineKeeping a new animal apart, behind a closed door, so it cannot spread illness. 2 to 3 weeks. Page {{P:arrival}}.
QuickThe blood vessel running through the center of a nail. Page {{P:grooming}}.
RingwormA fungal skin infection, not a worm, that spreads from guinea pigs to people. Page {{P:skin}}.
ScurvyVitamin C deficiency, in which collagen production fails. Page {{P:scurvy}}.
SowA female guinea pig. Page {{P:sexing}}.
sq ftSquare foot, an area of 12 in by 12 in. Floor space is counted in it. Page {{P:enclosure}}.
Styptic powderA powder that stops a nicked nail bleeding. Page {{P:grooming}}.
ToxemiaPregnancy toxemia: a metabolic emergency in a late-pregnant sow. Page {{P:repro}}.
UltrasoundImaging with sound waves, the more reliable way to confirm an ovarian cyst. Page {{P:repro}}.
UrethraThe tube that carries urine out of the bladder. A stone can block it. Page {{P:urinary}}.
VetShort for veterinarian. Page {{P:redflags}}.
WheekThe loud, rising food call. A learned response to expected food. Page {{P:behavior}}.

Veterinary references

  • Merck Veterinary Manual: the guinea pig pages on providing a home, housing and nutrition, selecting a guinea pig, restraint and physical examination, anatomy, physiology and behavior, clinical techniques, infectious diseases, noninfectious diseases and common health problems. Floor space, the 65 to 79°F range, vitamin C per kilogram, dental, urinary, ovarian and foot disease, the usable antibiotics and the short fast before anesthesia.
  • VCA Animal Hospitals (a United States veterinary hospital chain): guinea pig feeding, health problems, and bladder sludge and stones; health problems in rodents. The feeding list, the signs to act on and the antibiotics to avoid.
  • North Carolina State University Veterinary Hospital: Caring for Your Pet Guinea Pig. University of Missouri College of Veterinary Medicine: antibiotic-induced enterotoxemia in guinea pigs. University of Illinois College of Veterinary Medicine: reasons to spay your guinea pig. Massachusetts Society for the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals-Angell: urogenital disease in guinea pigs.
  • LafeberVet (a veterinary education publisher): care of the pet guinea pig, care of the pregnant guinea pig, and behavior essentials for the guinea pig; the Lafeber pet-owner pages on grooming, guinea pig sounds, and whether a guinea pig is overweight. Coat types, the vitamin C content of common greens, weekly weighing and the pregnancy warnings.
  • PetMD (a pet health site): gastrointestinal stasis and vitamin C deficiency in guinea pigs. Bonnie D. Wright, a veterinary anesthesiologist: anesthesia and pain relief in small mammals. The fasting rules.

Welfare organizations and husbandry references

  • Royal Society for the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals (RSPCA), United Kingdom: guinea pig company. RSPCA Australia knowledgebase: handling, heat, healthy guinea pigs, where to get guinea pigs, breeding and sexing, and enrichment. Heat stress from 75°F (24°C), sexing and the breeding timings.
  • Humane World for Animals: guinea pig housing requirements. NC3Rs (a United Kingdom research body): housing and husbandry of the guinea pig. Oxbow Animal Health (a small-animal feed maker): the guinea pig care guide.
  • GuineaLynx (a guinea pig rescue's information site): handling, introductions and quarantine.

Research

  • Elsbacher and colleagues (2025), Animals: guinea pig (Cavia porcellus) welfare, husbandry practices, human-animal interactions and behavior. The link between food enrichment and play, and between hiding places and lower stress.

The law

Library of Congress, Law Library: laws involving animals. The Local Switzerland: animals that cannot be kept alone.
