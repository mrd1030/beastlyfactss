How to use this package

Three ways to get value from it, depending on where you are right now.

Units used throughout Every temperature and size is given twice. Temperatures appear in degrees Fahrenheit (°F) with degrees Celsius (°C) beside them. Lengths appear in inches (in), with centimeters (cm) beside them. Floor space is in square feet (sq ft), which is length times width: 30 by 36 inches is 7.5 sq ft. A guinea pig and its weight log are measured in grams (g), the unit a kitchen scale shows, with pounds (lb) beside them. Vitamin C is counted in milligrams (mg) per kilogram (kg) of body weight, written mg/kg. Humidity is a percentage (%) of the moisture the air could hold. Prices are in United States dollars.

If you haven't brought any home yet

Check the law first, on page {{P:legal}}: a guinea pig is legal in every state, with one footnote. Then read pages {{P:profile}} to {{P:sexing}} in order before buying anything. If your guinea pigs are already home

Start with vitamin C on page {{P:vitc}}. It is the one requirement almost no other common small pet shares, and it is almost entirely preventable. Then audit the setup against the Setup Checklist, and if anything looks off, go to page {{P:redflags}}: the safe waiting window for a guinea pig that has stopped eating is measured in hours.

Cost overview

Initial setup

$100 to $250 for a properly sized cage, bedding, dishes and a hideout, plus $10 to $60 for each guinea pig from a shelter or about $50 at a pet store. An annual wellness exam runs $50 to $100. Every line is on page {{P:budget}}.

Monthly ongoing

$33 to $70 a month for one guinea pig and $60 to $135 for a pair, which is the recommended minimum. Hay, pellets and fresh vegetables are the recurring lines.

Budget for the worst case too A sick or emergency visit runs $100 to $300 or more, and complex problems such as gastrointestinal stasis can push well past that. Neuter and spay fees are set clinic by clinic. None of this is likely if you follow this package, but plan for it.

Fun facts worth knowing

  • The wheek, the loud squeal many owners know as the sound a fridge door brings on, is a learned response to expected food, not a fixed instinct.
  • Guinea pigs, humans and fruit bats are among the mammals that lost the ability to make their own vitamin C, and in each case the same gene is the one that stopped working.
  • Bell pepper carries more vitamin C by weight than an orange, which is why it tops the vegetable list for this species.
Section 01 · Profile

The species, coat types, size & lifespan

What you are buying, how big it gets, and how long it stays.

One species

The pet guinea pig is Cavia porcellus, a rodent that has never been found in the wild. It is a fully domesticated species descended from South American wild cavies and bred by Andean peoples for more than 3,000 years, first as livestock and for religious ceremonies and now as one of the world's most popular small pets. It is valued for being vocal, social and gentle. Guinea pigs reached Europe soon after the first European contact with South America, arriving as luxuries kept by the upper classes. The oldest known European remains date to the sixteenth century.

Its Andean ancestry explains most of its habits: it lives in groups, it grazes most of the day (a wild guinea pig can spend up to 80% of it foraging), and it reads a lifted hand as a predator.

Coat types

The most common types are the English or American shorthair, the Peruvian (long-haired) and the Abyssinian (swirled hair). Teddy and Silkie are two other types. Care is the same across them, with two differences: the long-haired types (Peruvian, Silkie and Texel) need brushing every day, and specialty types cost more, from $100 up, against $10 to $60 from a shelter or about $50 at a pet store for a standard animal.

Size and growth

Adult length8 to 12 in (20 to 30 cm).
Adult weightBoars 900 to 1,200 g (2 to 2.6 lb); sows 700 to 900 g (1.5 to 2 lb). Each animal has its own steady weight, and that figure is the one to track (page {{P:growth}}).
GrowthBabies start nibbling hay, pellets and vegetables within hours of birth while still nursing, and are fully weaned by 3 to 5 weeks. A guinea pig is growing, and eats differently, until about 6 months old (page {{P:diet}}).
Males and femalesBoars are larger. Telling the sexes apart is easy to get wrong in young animals (page {{P:sexing}}).

Lifespan

4 to 8 years is typical. That is a multi-year commitment, closer in scale to a rabbit or a cat than to a hamster, which lives 2 to 3 years. Plan for the whole span: the diet, the company and a vet who sees guinea pigs for every one of those years, and a second animal for as long as the first lives.

One animal is not the plan The most commonly skipped part of guinea pig care is the companion, and it quietly doubles most of the numbers in the budget. Guinea pigs are herd animals. Budget for two from day one rather than adding a second later and being surprised by the jump.
