Setup checklist & targets

Essential setup checklist

7.5 sq ft of solid floor for one guinea pig, 10.5 sq ft for a pair, roughly 2 to 4 sq ft more for each extra one (page {{P:enclosure}})
Sides at least 12 in (30 cm), good ventilation, no wire flooring
Paper, fleece or kiln-dried pine bedding; no cedar, raw pine or sawdust (page {{P:bedding}})
More than one hide, and more than one tunnel or chew in a shared cage
A digital thermometer and humidity gauge in the room, and the enclosure out of sun and drafts (page {{P:temperature}})
Out of reach of household cats and dogs, and never shared with a rabbit
Unlimited grass hay, roughly 80% of the diet, never allowed to run out (page {{P:diet}})
Guinea pig pellets, about 1/8 cup per animal, from a bag less than 90 days past its manufacture date
Fresh vegetables daily, bell pepper included (page {{P:vitc}})
Water in a bottle with a working nozzle, or a heavy bowl, and nothing added to it (page {{P:cleaning}})
Two guinea pigs, sexed by you or by a vet (page {{P:sexing}})
A gram scale, nail clippers with styptic powder, and a hard-sided carrier (page {{P:growth}})
A vet who sees guinea pigs found, and the number written down (page {{P:redflags}})

Power outages, heat waves, travel & transport

A cool room is a far smaller problem than a hot one, and an animal that stops eating is in more trouble than one that is a few degrees cool.

When the power goes out

The danger line does not move: heat stress can begin at 75°F (24°C). What changes is that you are now the climate system.

Summer outage: the one to worry about

  • Freeze bottles of water, wrap them in a towel and set them where a guinea pig can lie against one.
  • Use cool wet towels, shade the enclosure with cloths and darken windows against direct sun.
  • Keep unlimited fresh water in more than one container, and offer cucumber, melon or apple.
  • Move the enclosure to the coolest room, often a tiled bathroom or a basement, and get air moving with a battery fan or open windows on the shaded side.
  • Check the thermometer rather than guessing: a house without air conditioning climbs through the afternoon.
  • Move out early if the room will not stay safe: a friend's house, or a vet with power. Decide at hour two, not hour eight.

Winter outage: drafts and damp

  • Keep the enclosure in a draft-free spot away from windows and doors, and raise it off the floor, which is the coldest part of the room.
  • Give plenty of bedding hay to burrow into, and change it often enough to stay dry.
  • Add extra hay: it is insulation and the extra calories a cold animal burns.
  • Check that water has not frozen, including the ball in a bottle nozzle.
  • A bonded pair will huddle and do some of the work themselves.
Keep feeding them Unlike a reptile, a guinea pig must keep eating through an outage. Its gut has to keep moving, so hay and water stay in front of it the whole time.

Leaving town

A guinea pig cannot be left for days with a full bottle and a pile of food, because 8 to 12 hours without eating or droppings is already the time to call a vet. Arrange someone to look in daily to check appetite, droppings, water and the temperature, and fill in the pet-sitter sheet on page {{P:sitter}}.

Transport and vet trips

  • A hard-sided, ventilated carrier with a hide spot, a layer of hay, a toy or clean bedding from home, and the cage mate along when the visit allows it.
  • Strap the carrier in with a seatbelt or wedge it in a footwell, its side facing the direction of travel.
  • Keep the car cool and ventilated, the carrier out of direct sun and never in a closed trunk.
  • Never leave a guinea pig in a parked car, even briefly: a closed car crosses into heat stress well before it feels uncomfortable to you.
  • Keep the trip short where you can. Moving house: set the enclosure up and running at the new place before the guinea pigs arrive.
