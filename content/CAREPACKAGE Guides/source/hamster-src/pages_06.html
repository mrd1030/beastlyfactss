Essential setup checklist

Enclosure with 700 to 775 sq in (4,500 to 5,000 cm²) of unbroken floor for a Syrian, about 600 sq in (3,900 cm²) for a dwarf (page {{P:enclosure}})
Wire bars no wider than 1/2 in (13 mm) for a Syrian, 1/4 in (6 mm) for a dwarf

At least one hideout, and tunnels or connected cover
Water bottle with a valveless sipper tube, never glass for a Syrian

Chew items and timothy hay
Digital thermometer and humidity gauge at cage height

Daily, weekly & seasonal routine

Do it in the evening, once the hamster is up.

Daily

Remove soiled bedding and uneaten fresh food
Wash and rinse the water bottle or bowl, check the sipper, and refill
Feed the staple, or top up the bowl if it is always available
About 1 teaspoon of fresh vegetables, scattered or hidden
Look at the rear end, the eyes and how it moves
Read the thermometer, and check the room is not above 80°F (27°C)
A short handling session if it is awake and calm

Weekly, and regularly

Weigh it and write the number in the log
Check eyes, teeth, coat, breathing, lumps and the rear end
Fruit about 3 times a week, about 1 teaspoon
Treats, 1 to 2 times a week at most
Small cage: move it to a carrier and give the cage a full clean. Large deep cage: spot clean only

Every few weeks

Large deep-bedded enclosure: a full clean, with the nest and hoard set aside and returned
Replace the sand when it clumps or smells, and sift it at spot cleans
Check the bedding depth has not packed down below 6 in (15 cm)

Seasonal

Autumn: move the cage from windows and doors, and put the thermometer at cage height
Winter: keep the room 65 to 75°F (18 to 24°C), never near 41°F (5°C), including overnight
Summer: coolest room, fresh water, watch for 80°F (27°C)

Yearly

A small-mammal wellness exam, $80 to $115
Review the log, and the sitter sheet, for anything that changed
Do not Strip the whole cage out, dig out the burrow, or reach in while it sleeps.

Power outages, heat waves, travel & transport

Power outage

An outage takes the air conditioning, the fan and the heat at once. The thresholds do not move: 80°F (27°C) is the heat line and about 41°F (5°C) the cold one. You are now the climate system. Keep fresh water in front of the hamster and keep feeding it.

Summer: freeze bottles of water so it can lie up against them, use cool wet towels, shade any open side with cloths, darken windows against direct sun, and put water in more than one container. Cucumber, melon and apple help. Move the cage to the coolest room, such as a tiled bathroom or a basement, add whatever air movement you can, and check the thermometer rather than guessing.

Winter: the problem is drafts and damp, not the thermometer. Keep the cage in a draft-free spot away from windows and doors, off the floor, keep the bedding dry, and check the water has not frozen.

Relocate once the room stops responding and you cannot bring it back: a friend's house, a relative's basement, or a vet with power. Decide at hour two, not hour eight.

Heat wave

The same plan as a summer outage. Heat stress starts with rapid or open-mouth breathing and drooling, and the first sign, not the fourth, is the point to move. Cool gradually, and never plunge it into cold or ice water.

Transport to the vet

  • A sturdy, rigid, ventilated, hard-sided carrier the animal cannot chew through, with room to lie down and turn around. A wire cage is a poor travel choice.
  • A hiding place inside, such as a towel over the carrier or a small tunnel, and a handful of clean bedding from its own cage.
  • Strap it in with a seatbelt, or wedge it in the footwell, with a side facing the direction of travel.
  • A cool, well-ventilated car. Never direct sun, never a closed trunk, and never a parked car, even briefly.
  • A hamster travels alone. Keep the trip short, and choose a vet close to home where you can.
