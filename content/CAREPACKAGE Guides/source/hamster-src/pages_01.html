How to use this package

Three ways to get value from it, depending on where you are right now.

Units used throughout Every temperature and size is given twice: degrees Fahrenheit (°F) with degrees Celsius (°C), and inches (in) and square inches (sq in) with centimeters (cm) and square centimeters (cm²). A hamster is weighed in grams (g), with ounces (oz) beside it, and its water is measured in milliliters (ml). Humidity is a percentage (%) of the moisture the air could hold. Prices are in United States dollars.

If you haven't set up yet

Check the law first, on page {{P:legal}}: a hamster is legal in nearly every state, but two are exceptions. Then read the housing pages, starting at page {{P:enclosure}}, in order before buying anything. The enclosure and the bedding are expensive to redo, and the pet-shop starter kit gets both wrong: too small for the floor space and too shallow for the bedding. Buy the enclosure once, using the Budget & Shopping List on page {{P:budget}}, and buy it bigger than the kit.

If your hamster is already home

Audit what you have against the Setup Checklist below, starting with bedding depth, the highest-value change available. If your hamster chews the bars, that is a readout of the housing, not a personality trait. And if its rear end is wet or matted, stop reading and go to page {{P:wettail}}: a same-day emergency.

Quick profile

Everything at a glance before you read the detail.
Scientific nameMesocricetus auratus for the Syrian or golden hamster; the dwarfs are mostly Phodopus
Adult sizeSyrian 5 to 7 in (13 to 18 cm) and 3 to 5 oz (85 to 140 g). Every dwarf stays under 100 g
LifespanSyrians 2 to 3 years, Winter Whites often about 1. Median age at death in a large study: 1.75 years
Native rangeNorthern Syria and southern Turkey: arid steppe and farmland, in extensive burrows
Diet typeOmnivore. A complete pellet or lab block as the staple, small amounts of fresh food, and protein treats (page {{P:diet}})
Floor space minimum700 to 775 sq in (4,500 to 5,000 cm²) unbroken for a Syrian, around 600 sq in (3,900 cm²) for a dwarf
The one hard ruleBedding deep enough to burrow in: 6 in (15 cm) is the floor, and the research used 40 cm (page {{P:bedding}})
Temperature65 to 75°F (18 to 24°C), held steady. No heat lamp and no UVB (ultraviolet B light)
HousingOne per enclosure for a Syrian or Chinese hamster; some dwarfs can share (page {{P:choosing}})
Active hoursMainly nocturnal. Waking one by day is the commonest way owners get bitten (page {{P:handling}})
Legal statusLegal in 48 states, the District of Columbia and New York City. Hawaii bans it, and Oregon's rule is unclear
Conservation statusVulnerable on the IUCN (International Union for Conservation of Nature) global threatened-species list, for the wild Syrian population

Cost overview

The animal and the setup

About $26 for a Syrian, $16 to $24 for a dwarf. $111 to $226 to set up, animal included, before a water bottle, sand bath and chew items. Every line is on page {{P:budget}}.

Monthly ongoing

$10 to $18 for food, vegetables, bedding and chews, or $125 to $215 a year. Most of it is bedding.

Budget for the vet too A small-mammal wellness exam runs about $83 to $115, and an emergency exam about $200 before any tests or treatment. Wet tail is the one to plan for.

Fun facts worth knowing

  • Nearly every pet Syrian descends from one litter: the species was brought into captivity from a single wild-caught female found near Aleppo, Syria, in 1930.
  • Cheek pouches stretch back as far as the shoulders and can hold roughly a fifth of the hamster's own body weight, which is why a day without seeing it eat may be nothing.

The species, size & lifespan

What you are buying, how big it gets, and how long it stays.

Five pet species

Five species are kept as pets: the Syrian (the largest and the most common), the Winter White, the Campbell's, the Chinese and the Roborovski. The Winter White, Campbell's and Roborovski are the "dwarfs" of the pet trade, and the Chinese hamster is sold alongside them though it does not share their tolerance for company. Temperament varies more by species than most people expect: a Syrian and a Roborovski, both sold under the same label, are genuinely different pets to hold. Where the species differ, the page says which one it means.

In the wild a Syrian is a solitary burrower that digs extensive tunnel systems and is fiercely territorial toward other hamsters, a trait that carries into captivity and is why a Syrian lives alone as an adult.

Varieties

Color and coat vary: short-haired, long-haired, satin and curly "rex". They rarely change the price, and this is not a species with morph-driven pricing. The one care difference is the coat: some long-haired hamsters need weekly brushing to take out mats, bedding and debris.

A short commitment, and a short margin That is brief next to almost any other pet. It cuts both ways: less time to spread the setup cost across, but less total expense, and a much smaller commitment to take on. It also leaves little room for wait-and-see with a health problem, which is why the health section is built around same-day action.
