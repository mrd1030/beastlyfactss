Glossary, A to H

AbscessA pus-filled swelling, usually from an infected injury or bite. Needs a vet. Page {{P:lumps}}.
Bar spacingThe gap between the bars of a wire cage: no wider than 1/2 in (13 mm) for a Syrian and 1/4 in (6 mm) for a dwarf. Page {{P:enclosure}}.
CacheA stash of food a hamster carries off in its cheek pouches and hides in the enclosure, also called a hoard. Page {{P:fresh}}.
Cheek pouchA stretchy pocket on each side of the mouth, reaching back to the shoulders, that holds roughly a fifth of the hamster's body weight in food. Pages {{P:fresh}}, {{P:teeth}}.
cm, cm²Centimeter, and square centimeter: the metric units for length and for floor area. 1 in is 2.54 cm. Page {{P:enclosure}}.
ClostridiumToxin-producing bacteria that take over the gut when an antibiotic wipes out the normal bacteria. Page {{P:antibiotics}}.
ContraindicatedA drug a vet should not give to a particular animal because it is known to cause harm. Page {{P:antibiotics}}.
DehydrationToo little water in the body. Shows as dull, sunken eyes, slow movements, very concentrated urine and a skin tent. Page {{P:signs}}.
Dwarf hamsterThe small species sold as pets: the Winter White, the Campbell's and the Roborovski. The Chinese hamster is sold alongside them but lives alone. Pages {{P:species}}, {{P:choosing}}.
EnterotoxemiaA fatal diarrhea caused by toxin-producing bacteria overgrowing in the gut, most often after certain antibiotics. Hamsters can develop it three to five days after a dose. Page {{P:antibiotics}}.
°C, °FDegrees Celsius and degrees Fahrenheit, the two temperature scales. 65 to 75°F is 18 to 24°C. Page {{P:temperature}}.
gGram: the metric unit of weight a kitchen scale shows. 28 g is about 1 oz. Page {{P:growth}}.
Gram-positiveA large group of bacteria. A hamster's gut bacteria are overwhelmingly gram-positive, so antibiotics aimed at them can wipe the gut out. Page {{P:antibiotics}}.
Hemolytic anemiaA shortage of red blood cells because they break down. The risk behind raw onion and raw garlic. Page {{P:treats}}.
Hindgut fermenterAn animal that digests plant fiber by fermenting it in an enlarged gut full of bacteria, as a hamster does. Page {{P:antibiotics}}.
HumidityThe share of the moisture the air could hold, in percent (%). The target for the room is 40 to 60%. Page {{P:temperature}}.
Reference

Glossary, I to Z

ImpactionA blockage. In a cheek pouch, soft or moist food that clumps and lodges there. Page {{P:teeth}}.
in, sq inInch, and square inch: the units for length and floor area in this book, each with the metric figure beside it. Page {{P:enclosure}}.
IncisorsThe front teeth. A hamster's grow continuously and are worn down by chewing. Page {{P:teeth}}.
IUCNInternational Union for Conservation of Nature, which keeps the global list of threatened species. The wild Syrian hamster is listed as Vulnerable. Page {{P:profile}}.
Lab blockA formulated, complete diet pressed into a hard block, nutritionally complete and something to gnaw. Page {{P:diet}}.
LCMVLymphocytic choriomeningitis virus: a virus Syrian hamsters have a reputation for carrying, which can cause neurological disease in people. Page {{P:signs}}.
MalocclusionTeeth that no longer meet properly, so they overgrow. Page {{P:teeth}}.
MitesTiny skin parasites that cause itching, redness and hair loss. Page {{P:lumps}}.
mlMilliliter: a metric unit of liquid, about a fifth of a teaspoon. A hamster drinks about 9 to 12 ml a day. Page {{P:water}}.
NocturnalActive at night. A hamster is mainly nocturnal, so it sleeps by day. Page {{P:handling}}.
ozOunce: about 28 g. A Syrian weighs 3 to 5 oz. Page {{P:growth}}.
PDSAPeople's Dispensary for Sick Animals, a United Kingdom veterinary charity whose hamster advice is among this package's sources.
Proliferative ileitisThe medical name for wet tail. Page {{P:wettail}}.
RexA curly coat variety, one of several coat types. Page {{P:species}}.
RSPCARoyal Society for the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals, a United Kingdom animal welfare charity whose hamster pages are among this package's sources.
Sand bathA dish of dust-free, non-clumping sand a hamster rolls in to work coat and skin oils out. A species essential. Page {{P:wheel}}.
Scatter feedingScattering or hiding food around the cage instead of serving it in a bowl, which encourages natural foraging. Page {{P:fresh}}.
Sipper tubeThe spout of a water bottle. A valveless one has no ball inside, so a hamster can drink from it easily. Page {{P:water}}.
Skin tentSkin that stays up after being gently lifted, a sign of dehydration. Page {{P:signs}}.
SyrianMesocricetus auratus, the golden or teddy bear hamster. The largest and easiest to handle, and strictly solitary. Pages {{P:species}}, {{P:choosing}}.
TorporA cold-induced, hibernation-like state, below about 41°F (5°C), with minimal activity and little eating. It can look like death. Page {{P:torpor}}.
TumorA growth. Common in older hamsters, particularly females, and growing fast. Page {{P:lumps}}.
UVBUltraviolet B: the part of sunlight reptiles need for bone health. A hamster needs none. Page {{P:temperature}}.
VCAVeterinary Centers of America, now VCA Animal Hospitals: an animal hospital chain whose pet care articles are among this package's sources.
VetShort for veterinarian, an animal doctor. Page {{P:redflags}}.
Wet tailA bacterial gut infection tied to stress, with watery diarrhea and a wet, matted rear end. A same-day emergency. Page {{P:wettail}}.
Wire-gnawingChewing the cage bars. A standard behavioral measure of housing quality in small rodents. Page {{P:bedding}}.

  • Merck Veterinary Manual: Hamsters (biology, husbandry, physical examination, infectious diseases, tumors and zoonotic risk), and Providing a Home for a Hamster.   • LafeberVet: Basic Information Sheet: Hamster, and Sexing or Gender Determination in Small Mammals. The humidity target, water intake, weights, cheek pouch impaction and the sexing method.
  • VCA Animal Hospitals (Veterinary Centers of America): Owning a Pet Hamster, and Feeding Rodents. Daytime activity, pouch caching, escaping, the wheel and what makes a hamster bite, and the feeding figures.
  • Chicago Exotics Animal Hospital: hamster care. Dehydration signs and a full cheek pouch mistaken for a growth. Dial A Vet: hamster health and diseases. Abscesses, mites, diabetes and overgrown teeth.
  • Exotic practices that publish their fees: Erlanger Veterinary Hospital and Colorado Exotic Animal Hospital. The wellness and emergency exam fees.

Research

  • O'Neill and colleagues (2022), Journal of Small Animal Practice 63(10): demography, disorders and mortality of pet hamsters under primary veterinary care in the United Kingdom in 2016. The median age at death of 1.75 years, the wet tail figures and the aging changes.
  • Hauzenberger, Gebhardt-Henrich and Steiger, Applied Animal Behaviour Science: the influence of bedding depth on behavior in golden hamsters. The 80, 40 and 10 cm groups and the wire-gnawing result.

Public health

  • Centers for Disease Control and Prevention: Healthy Pets, small mammals. Hand washing, keeping animals out of the kitchen, and the higher risk to children under 5.

Husbandry and welfare references

  • Royal Society for the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals (RSPCA): the hamster behavior, company, diet and health pages, and the taking-your-new-hamster-home sheet. Pickup, the dwarf group conditions, the first 24 hours, the ten-day diet change, cage placement and the exercise-ball warning. Prices were last checked in September 2026.
  • Woodgreen Pets Charity: the introduction to hamsters and the health check pages. Species temperament, taming steps and what to check in a hamster.
  • PetMD: the hamster lifespan page, the five most popular hamster breeds, and the Syrian hamster care sheet. Lifespan and length by species. People's Dispensary for Sick Animals (PDSA): hamsters as pets. Cleaning frequency.
  • Merck Veterinary Manual: Lincosamides Use in Animals; VCA Animal Hospitals: Health Problems in Rodents; University of Missouri College of Veterinary Medicine: antibiotic-induced enterotoxemia. The antibiotic classes, the three to five day window and the heat line. RSPCA transport pages: the carrier and car rules.

The law

  • Hawaii Administrative Rules § 4-71-6; California Code of Regulations Title 14 § 671; District of Columbia Code § 8-1808(j); Alaska Administrative Code title 5, section 92.029; Rhode Island Rules and Regulations Governing the Importation and Possession of Wild Animals; New Jersey Administrative Code 7:25-4; Oregon Administrative Rules 635-056; Wyoming, Georgia, Kentucky and Colorado rules on domestic or exempt animals; and each other state's rules, read on 1 October 2026.
