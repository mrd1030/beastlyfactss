Glossary, A to F

AcclimateTo bring a fish gradually to a new tank's temperature and water chemistry, by floating the sealed cup and, where the waters differ a lot, adding tank water to the cup a little at a time. Page {{P:arrival}}.
Air stoneA porous stone on an air line that releases fine bubbles. It adds oxygen and moves the water surface, which is how a tank takes in air. Pages {{P:ichvelvet}}, {{P:meds}}.
Ammonia (NH3)The first and most toxic product of fish waste and decaying food. NH3 is its chemical formula: one nitrogen and three hydrogens. The target is 0 ppm, always. Pages {{P:cycle}}, {{P:water}}.
Aquarium saltPlain sodium chloride sold for aquarium use, not table salt. A treatment dosed into the water for scaled fish, never a food. Pages {{P:ichvelvet}}, {{P:meds}}.
Brine shrimp naupliiNewly hatched baby brine shrimp, a first food for fry. Page {{P:breeding}}.
Bubble nestA raft of saliva-coated air bubbles a male builds at the surface to hold eggs. Males build them with no female present. Pages {{P:behavior}}, {{P:breeding}}.
°CDegrees Celsius, the metric temperature scale, on which water freezes at 0 and boils at 100. Page {{P:heat}}.
ChloramineChlorine bonded to ammonia, used by many water utilities. It does not gas off the way chlorine does, so a conditioner has to be labeled for both. Page {{P:changes}}.
Clamped finsFins held tight against the body instead of spread. A posture that signals stress or illness, not damage to the fin itself. Pages {{P:behavior}}, {{P:finrot}}.
cmCentimeter, one hundredth of a meter, about 0.4 in.
ColumnarisA fast, highly contagious bacterial infection of fish, from Flavobacterium columnare, that makes pale or gray saddle-shaped patches. It is not a fungus. Page {{P:columnaris}}.
ConditionerA water treatment, also called a dechlorinator, that neutralizes the chlorine and chloramine in tap water. Page {{P:changes}}.
CortisolA stress hormone. Researchers measure it after a mirror display to see how stressed a betta is. Page {{P:behavior}}.
CycledA tank whose bacteria colonies keep pace with the waste: a full ammonia dose reads zero within 24 hours, nitrite reads zero, and nitrate is present. The single prerequisite for adding a fish. Pages {{P:cycle}}, {{P:fishin}}.
Dissolved oxygenThe oxygen mixed into the water, which gills take up. It falls as water warms and when the surface stops moving. Pages {{P:heat}}, {{P:signs}}.
DropsyNot a disease but a sign: fluid building up inside the body from organ failure, with scales standing out like a pinecone. Page {{P:internal}}.
EugenolThe sedative in clove oil, which is why clove oil is the humane method where no vet can be reached. Page {{P:meds}}.
°FDegrees Fahrenheit, the temperature scale used in the United States, on which water freezes at 32 and boils at 212. Page {{P:heat}}.
Fasting dayOne day a week with no food, to support digestion and reduce bloating and constipation. Page {{P:diet}}.
Fin rotA bacterial infection that frays and discolors the fins, often secondary to poor water, a chill or a physical fin injury. Page {{P:finrot}}.
Fish-in cycleCycling a tank with the fish already living in it, using daily testing and large water changes to keep ammonia and nitrite low. A rescue, not a plan. Page {{P:fishin}}.
Fishless cycleCycling an empty tank by dosing pure ammonia to feed the bacteria before any fish arrives. The safer path. Page {{P:cycle}}.
FlashingA fish rubbing or scratching against decor or the substrate, usually a sign of an irritant or a parasite. Pages {{P:behavior}}, {{P:signs}}.
Reference

Glossary, G to M

galGallon, the United States unit of liquid volume, about 3.8 L. Page {{P:tank}}.
GHGeneral hardness: the amount of dissolved calcium and magnesium in the water, a mineral supply that does not buffer pH. Page {{P:water}}.
Gravel vacuumA siphon with a wide tube that pulls waste out of the substrate during a water change. Page {{P:changes}}.
Hospital tankA bare tank with a seeded sponge filter, an air pump and a heater, used to quarantine new fish and to treat sick ones without dosing the main tank. Pages {{P:quarantine}}, {{P:meds}}.
IchWhite spot disease, caused by the parasite Ichthyophthirius multifiliis, which shows as grains of white salt across the body and fins. Page {{P:ichvelvet}}.
inInch, 2.54 cm.
InfusoriaA mixture of very small aquatic organisms, the first food of newly hatched fry. Pages {{P:diet}}, {{P:breeding}}.
IUCNInternational Union for Conservation of Nature, which keeps the global Red List of threatened species. The betta is listed Vulnerable in the wild. Page {{P:profile}}.
KHCarbonate hardness: the water's buffering capacity, its ability to neutralize acid and hold pH steady. The bacteria that clear ammonia use it up. Pages {{P:fishin}}, {{P:water}}.
LLiter, the metric unit of liquid volume, about 0.26 gal.
Labyrinth organA structure above the gills that lets a betta take oxygen from air at the surface. It is why clear access to the surface is a physiological need. Pages {{P:species}}, {{P:tank}}.
Malachite green, formalinTwo standard medications for ich in fish that cannot take heat or salt, or in a heavy outbreak. Used to the label or a vet's direction, in a hospital tank. Page {{P:ichvelvet}}.
MethemoglobinThe form of the blood's oxygen carrier that cannot carry oxygen. Nitrite makes it, which is why nitrite poisoning is called brown blood disease. Page {{P:signs}}.
mg/LMilligrams per liter, a weight of a substance in a volume of water, which for water is the same as ppm. For dissolved oxygen, 5 mg/L supports optimum fish health. Page {{P:signs}}.
mlMilliliter, one thousandth of a liter. Page {{P:meds}}.
MycobacteriosisFish tuberculosis: a rare bacterial infection that is zoonotic. Pages {{P:cleaning}}, {{P:internal}}.
Reference

Glossary, N to Z

Nitrate (NO3)The end product of a healthy cycle. Far less harmful than ammonia or nitrite, and removed by water changes. Keep it under 20 ppm. Pages {{P:cycle}}, {{P:water}}, {{P:changes}}.
Nitrite (NO2)The middle step of the nitrogen cycle. It blocks oxygen uptake in the blood. The target is 0 ppm. Pages {{P:cycle}}, {{P:water}}.
Nitrogen cycleThe chain in which bacteria convert ammonia to nitrite and then to nitrate. Cycling a tank means growing both bacteria groups. Page {{P:cycle}}.
Obligate carnivoreAn animal that needs meat to live. A betta in the wild eats insects, larvae and small crustaceans. Page {{P:diet}}.
pHThe standard scale of how acidic or basic water is, from 0 to 14, with 7 neutral. For a betta, 6.5 to 7.5 and steady. Pages {{P:fishin}}, {{P:water}}.
PipingA fish swimming at the surface to gulp air, a warning sign of low oxygen or poisoned water. Page {{P:signs}}.
ppmParts per million: the unit a test kit prints, one part of a substance in a million parts of water. Pages {{P:cycle}}, {{P:water}}.
QuarantineKeeping a new or sick fish in a separate tank for a set time, at least 30 days, before it joins others. Page {{P:quarantine}}.
Seeded mediaFilter sponge or gravel taken from an established, healthy tank, carrying a living bacteria colony. The most reliable way to shorten a cycle. Pages {{P:cycle}}, {{P:quarantine}}.
Slime coatThe mucus layer over a fish's body, a barrier against infection and parasites. Bare hands strip it. Page {{P:handling}}.
SororityA group of female bettas kept together, which needs at least 10 gal and cover. Page {{P:tankmates}}.
Sponge filterAn air-driven filter whose sponge is the surface the bacteria live on. The standard for bettas because it filters with almost no current. Page {{P:filter}}.
Surface agitationMovement of the water surface, from a filter, air stone or hand pouring, which is how oxygen enters the tank. Pages {{P:heat}}, {{P:signs}}.
Swim bladderThe gas-filled organ that controls buoyancy. A swollen gut pressing on it makes a fish float, sink or list. Page {{P:internal}}.
TanninsCompounds released by driftwood that tint water faintly amber and soften the light. Cosmetic, and closer to the blackwater the species comes from. Pages {{P:decor}}, {{P:enrichment}}.
VCAVeterinary Centers of America, a United States veterinary hospital group, named only on the Sources page.
VelvetGold dust disease, caused by the freshwater parasite Piscinoodinium, which dusts the body with a fine gold or rust sheen. Fast and dangerous. Page {{P:ichvelvet}}.
WWatt, the unit of power. A heater of 15 to 25 W suits a 5 gal tank. Page {{P:heat}}.
ZoonoticAble to pass from animals to people. Fish tuberculosis can, through broken skin. Pages {{P:cleaning}}, {{P:internal}}.
