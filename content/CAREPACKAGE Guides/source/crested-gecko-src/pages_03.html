Section 03 · Feeding

Diet & feeding by age

The powder is the meal. Insects are the extra.

The biggest feeding mistake with this species is not a wrong schedule. It is treating live insects as the main event, when the complete crested gecko diet powder is the nutritional foundation and insects are a supplement for extra protein and the enrichment of hunting. Offer insects before a gecko is reliably eating its powder and it can learn to hold out for them and refuse the complete diet, which is a real route to malnutrition.

AgeDiet powderLive insectsPortion of mixed powder
Hatchling and juvenile, 0 to 12 monthsFresh, daily1 to 2 times a weekAbout 1/2 tsp
Adult, 12 months and upFresh, every 2 to 3 daysAbout once a weekAbout 1 tsp

Adjust the portion by how much actually gets eaten.

Timing and placement

  • Evening. Crested geckos feed at night, so food goes in around lights-out and the leftovers come out the next morning, when the dish is washed and disinfected like every food dish, every day. Offering only what is cleared within about 4 hours is another option.
  • High up. They eat off the ground in the wild, so the dish sits on an elevated or magnetic wall ledge, not the floor.
  • Insects sized to the eyes. No insect wider than the space between the gecko's eyes. Anything not eaten within 15 to 20 minutes comes back out, so it can't stress or nip a sleeping gecko overnight.

Water

A crested gecko takes most of its water as droplets licked off leaves and glass after the evening mist, so the mist is the main drink of the day. Keep a shallow dish anyway as a backup, filled with tap, filtered or spring water: distilled water carries no minerals and is not a good sole source. Wash and refill it daily. Water runs on a much shorter clock than food: a crested gecko can go only about 3 days without it.

Never feed Avocado, rhubarb, citrus fruit, onion, garlic or eggplant. Fireflies, which are toxic. Any wild-caught insect. The full list is on page {{P:treats}}.
Powder first, then insects Get a new gecko reliably eating its complete diet before offering any live insect. Insects are the extra in both directions: the gecko does not need them to be well fed, and they cannot replace the powder.
Section 03 · Feeding

Complete diet powder: mixing, rotation & refusals

The base of the diet. Get this right and the rest of the food pages are extras.

A complete crested gecko diet powder is a meal replacement: mixed with water, it supplies protein, fruit, calcium and vitamin D3 in one product. That is unusual among reptiles, and it is why most health problems in this species trace back to the setup rather than to the food. Several established brands make one.

Mixing and serving

  • Consistency: mix with water to a smooth, ketchup-like texture.
  • Rotate 2 to 3 flavors or brands, both for variety and because crested geckos can be genuinely picky. A watermelon flavor has unusually high acceptance among fussy eaters.
  • Serve high, in a small dish on a ledge, at about 1/2 tsp for a juvenile and 1 tsp for an adult.
  • Fruit alongside, never instead: fed too often, fruit teaches a gecko to reject the food that is actually complete.

Feeder insects & gut-loading

A supplement for protein and hunting, not the base diet. Commercially raised, gut-loaded and dusted.

Mealworms and superwormsSkipHard to digest, and linked to impaction
FirefliesNeverToxic to reptiles. Not a question of how many
Any wild-caught insectNeverPesticide and parasite risk, however clean the garden looks

Feeding them out

Juveniles get insects 1 to 2 times a week, adults about once at most, fewer if putting on fat. No insect wider than the space between the gecko's eyes. Rather than dropping insects on the floor, release a few into the upper branches after dark, where a crested gecko naturally hunts.

Why gut-loading matters

Feeder insects are a poor calcium source, and the problem is the ratio of calcium to phosphorus, because phosphorus competes with calcium for absorption. The target for a reptile eating insects is roughly 2 parts calcium to 1 of phosphorus, or at least 1:1. Unsupplemented crickets sit near 1 part calcium to 9 of phosphorus. Gut-loading means feeding the insects a calcium-rich diet for 24 to 72 hours before they are offered, so what is in their gut goes into the gecko too.

Gut-load withNotes
GoodA commercial gut-load sold on its calcium content, not one that just says gut-load on the front. Collard, mustard, turnip and dandelion greens, and butternut squash
Not enoughCarrot, potato and apple keep insects alive and watered, but do nothing for the calcium ratio
AvoidSpinach, beet greens and Swiss chard: high in oxalic acid, which binds calcium so it cannot be absorbed
Keeping feeders A ventilated tub, not crowded, with the gut-load always available and a calcium-fortified water gel, since insects drown in an open dish. Remove fresh produce before it molds. Feed insects out within a few hours of taking them off the gut-load: they start emptying their gut as soon as the food is gone.

Why fruit stays occasional

Fruit is not dangerous in itself. The risk is habit: a gecko fed fruit too often starts rejecting its nutritionally complete powder, and then it is living on the part of the diet that carries the least. A small smear alongside the dish, now and then, is a treat. A daily bowl of fruit is a diet problem.

When a refusal becomes a vet visit

A healthy adult at a good weight can typically go about 2 to 3 weeks without eating and be physically fine, but a vet consult is worth considering once the second week passes, especially with visible weight loss. Juveniles have far less reserve: a young gecko refusing food for more than a few days is more urgent than an adult doing the same. Water runs on a shorter clock, about 3 days.

Go regardless of the day count for Visible weight loss · a bony spine, hips or tail base · sunken eyes · lethargy or loss of grip strength · a soft or rubbery lower jaw, an early sign of metabolic bone disease.

Weighing at least monthly, and every few days while the gecko is off its food, catches a real downward trend instead of reacting to one skipped meal. The log is on page {{P:ownerlog}}.

The never-feed list, in one place Avocado. Rhubarb. Citrus fruit. Onion, garlic and eggplant. Fireflies. Any wild-caught insect. Any insect wider than the space between the gecko's eyes.

Calcium, vitamin D3 & the supplement jar

Calcium builds bone. Vitamin D3 is what lets the body use it. The question is where each one comes from.

SourceWhat it supplies
Diet powderCalcium and vitamin D3 already mixed in, with everything else a gecko needs. The powder is complete as it comes: the supplement jar is for the insects.
InsectsVery little calcium for the phosphorus they carry. Gut-loading and dusting are what make them worth feeding (page {{P:insects}}).
UVB lightVitamin D3, made in the gecko's own skin. Optional on a complete powder, still worth providing at a low level (page {{P:uvb}}).
The calcium sacsTwo small sacs at the back of the mouth where a crested gecko stores calcium. A laying female draws on them with every clutch.

Dusting the insects

Lightly dust feeder insects with a reptile calcium supplement before each insect feeding. Occasionally skipping a dusting is fine. Dust immediately before feeding, not in advance: powder falls off, and an insect that has sat dusted in a tub for an hour has groomed much of it away. Gut-loading and dusting are separate jobs, not two ways of doing one. Gut-loading improves the insect itself; dusting is the insurance, because gut-load products vary and you can never be sure how much any insect ate.

Laying females

A mature female can lay infertile eggs with no male present, and every clutch draws on her calcium. Keep calcium in her diet, and look at the calcium sacs at the back of her mouth: sacs that shrink noticeably over a season mean her reserves are running down, and a vet should check her.

When calcium is not the answer

A gecko that is gut-loaded and dusted properly and still shows signs of calcium trouble usually has a light or heat problem rather than a calcium one. A gecko too cool to digest properly cannot use calcium it has eaten, and a UVB tube loses its output well before it stops glowing.

The prevention is three things A genuinely complete diet powder as the base, calcium on the insects, and low-level UVB. That combination is how metabolic bone disease is prevented in this species.

Growth, weight & body condition

A gram scale is one of the most useful tools you can own for this species.

Crested geckos are tracked by weight rather than length: a gram scale is precise, and a jumpy climbing gecko on a ruler is not. Weigh at least once a month and log it, and every few days while the gecko is off its food or slowing down in winter. A slow loss is often the earliest sign that something is wrong, well before any other symptom appears.

Body condition at a glance

Healthy

A rounded body with no projecting hips, spine or ribs, a full tail base if the tail is still there, and a strong grip.

Underweight

A bony spine, hips or tail base, sunken eyes, a loose grip on your hand. Check the powder is actually being eaten rather than drying in the dish, then the heat, the humidity and the stool.

Overweight

A round or swollen belly with no eggs in it, fat at the base of the legs or along the flanks, less climbing. Usually a high-fat diet or insects too often: cut back on insect meals. It is linked to fatty liver disease and trouble with eggs.

When a weight change is a vet visit Ongoing weight loss across several weigh-ins, or any visible loss in a gecko that is also off its food. Bring the Owner Log: a trend line tells a vet more than a single reading.
