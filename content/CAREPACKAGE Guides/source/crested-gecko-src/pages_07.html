Glossary, A to G

ArborealTree-dwelling. Why height beats floor space for this species, and why the food dish belongs on a ledge. Page {{P:enclosure}}.
AutotomyDropping the tail on purpose as a defense. The tail breaks at built-in fracture points and wriggles to distract a predator. In a crested gecko it never grows back. Page {{P:fts}}.
BioactiveA living enclosure with a drainage layer, a soil mix, leaf litter and a cleanup crew of springtails and isopods, tiny invertebrates that break down waste. Page {{P:substrate}}.
Calcium sacsTwo small sacs at the back of a crested gecko's mouth where it stores calcium. Shrinking sacs in a laying female mean her reserves are running down. Page {{P:supplements}}.
CGDCrested gecko diet: the complete powdered food, mixed with water, that is the staple of this species' diet, with protein, fruit, calcium and vitamin D3 in one. Page {{P:powder}}.
CITESThe Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna and Flora: the treaty that controls international trade in listed species. The crested gecko is not listed. Page {{P:legal}}.
Cryptosporidiosis"Crypto": a serious, often hard-to-treat infection of the gut by a single-celled parasite. One reason for a fecal test at the first vet visit. Page {{P:impaction}}.
D3, vitamin D3The vitamin a reptile needs to absorb and use calcium. A crested gecko gets it from its diet powder, and makes some in its skin under UVB. Page {{P:supplements}}.
Dimming thermostatA thermostat that turns a heat bulb's power down as the target nears, the way a light dimmer does, instead of switching it fully off and on. Suits a strict ceiling. Page {{P:temperature}}.
DysecdysisStuck or incomplete shedding, most often on the toes and the tail tip. Almost always a humidity problem in this species. Page {{P:shed}}.
DystociaEgg binding: a female unable to pass the eggs she is carrying. A same-day vet visit. Page {{P:binding}}.
EcdysisThe normal shed: the whole old skin peeling away so the gecko can grow and clear damaged skin. Page {{P:seasonal}}.
Fecal testA vet's microscope check of a stool sample for parasites. Bring a fresh sample in a sealed bag. Page {{P:arrival}}.
Femoral poresA row of tiny pores on the underside of the thighs. Some breeders use them to sex young geckos early, but the method is not always accurate. Page {{P:eggs}}.
Ferguson ZoneOne of four groups that match a species' natural sun exposure to a UVB strength. Crested geckos are Zone 1, the shade dwellers, the lowest. Page {{P:uvb}}.
Frog buttKeeper slang for a crested gecko that has lost its tail. It lives a completely normal, healthy life. Page {{P:fts}}.
FTSFloppy tail syndrome: a tail that hangs over the head or sits at an odd angle when the gecko rests upside down, linked to long-term resting on glass. Page {{P:fts}}.
GravidCarrying eggs. A female can be gravid without ever meeting a male. Page {{P:eggs}}.
Grow-out enclosureA small enclosure, 12×12×12 in (30×30×30 cm), where a hatchling lives until it passes about 12 to 13 g and feeds reliably. Page {{P:enclosure}}.
Gut-loadingFeeding insects a calcium-rich diet for 24 to 72 hours before they are offered, so what is in their gut goes into the gecko too. Page {{P:insects}}.
Reference

Glossary, H to Z

Hemipenal bulgeThe swelling at the base of a male's tail, just behind the vent, usually visible from 18 to 25 g. The only dependable way to sex a crested gecko. Page {{P:eggs}}.
Humid hideA hide lined with damp sphagnum moss, where the gecko can sit in moist air without the whole enclosure staying wet. Pages {{P:substrate}}, {{P:seasonal}}.
HygrometerA humidity gauge. Use a digital one with a probe; dial gauges drift and read wrong in moist air. Page {{P:humidity}}.
ImpactionA blockage in the digestive tract, usually from loose substrate, oversized prey, or shed skin that was never passed. Page {{P:impaction}}.
IUCNThe International Union for Conservation of Nature, which keeps the Red List grading how threatened each species is. It rates the crested gecko Vulnerable. Pages {{P:profile}}, {{P:legal}}.
K (kelvin)The scale for the color of light. A 6500 K lamp gives white, daylight-colored light, the kind live plants need. Page {{P:substrate}}.
Lay boxA container with a few inches of slightly moist coconut fiber and fir bark, half and half, where a female lays. Every adult female needs one. Page {{P:eggs}}.
MBDMetabolic bone disease: soft, weakened, deformed bones from too little usable calcium or vitamin D3, properly called nutritional secondary hyperparathyroidism. Page {{P:mbd}}.
MorphA captive-bred line that looks different from the wild type in color, pattern or crest. Care is the same for all. Page {{P:species}}.
Mouth rotAn infection of the gums and mouth, one of the medical reasons a gecko stops eating. A vet visit. Page {{P:impaction}}.
Oxalic acidA compound in spinach, beet greens and Swiss chard that binds calcium so it cannot be absorbed. Why they are the wrong gut-load. Page {{P:insects}}.
ProbeThe thermostat's sensor. At the gecko's level, held with a clip or suction cup, never tape. Page {{P:temperature}}.
Proportional thermostatA thermostat that sends power in short pulses to hold a steady temperature, instead of switching fully on and off. Page {{P:temperature}}.
PVCPolyvinyl chloride: a rigid, waterproof plastic used for enclosure panels. A front-opening PVC or glass enclosure holds humidity well. Page {{P:enclosure}}.
QuarantineKeeping a new reptile fully apart from others, in a separate room with separate tools, for 3 to 6 months. Page {{P:arrival}}.
SalmonellaBacteria that healthy reptiles carry in the gut and shed in their droppings without being ill. Spread hands to mouth. Page {{P:cleaning}}.
T5 HOA fluorescent tube 5/8 inch across (T5), in the high-output (HO) version. A low-output 12 in tube is the right UVB source here. Page {{P:uvb}}.
TreadmillingLetting a gecko walk from hand to hand, cupping the free hand in front each time, so it is never held. Page {{P:handling}}.
Units°F, degrees Fahrenheit, and °C, degrees Celsius, for temperature; in (inches), ft (feet) and cm (centimeters) for size; g (grams) for weight; tsp (teaspoons) for food; W (watts) for bulbs; % (percent) for humidity.
UrateThe white to cream, paste-like part of reptile waste, made from uric acid instead of liquid urine. Hard, gritty or yellow means dehydrated. Page {{P:stool}}.
UVBUltraviolet B: the part of sunlight a reptile's skin uses to make vitamin D3. Glass and clear plastic block most of it. Page {{P:uvb}}.
UVIUltraviolet index: a measure of UVB strength at one spot. About 0.6 to 1.4 near the top of a crested gecko's enclosure. Page {{P:uvb}}.
VCAVCA Animal Hospitals, originally Veterinary Centers of America: an animal hospital chain whose pet care articles are among this package's sources.
VentThe single opening under the base of the tail that waste and eggs pass through. Page {{P:arrival}}.
