Heat waves, power outages, travel & transport

For this species the summer plan matters as much as the winter one.

In a heat wave

The ceiling is 85°F (29°C) anywhere in the enclosure, and in summer the room itself can do the heating. Read the top of the enclosure through the day with a digital probe, not a stick-on strip, and check the bottom stays cooler. A thermostat can switch a bulb off but cannot cool anything, so turn the lamp off once the room itself reaches 82°F (28°C). Keep the enclosure out of direct sun, move it to a cooler room if this one climbs, and keep the vents open. A gecko limp, gaping or unresponsive after a hot spell goes to a vet right away.

When the power goes out

A normal night low is 65 to 72°F (18 to 22°C), so one cool night is not an emergency. Act once the room heads for 72°F (22°C) or below day and night, which brings on lethargy and appetite loss.

Do

  • A battery thermometer in the enclosure, to read the real number.
  • Thick towels over the enclosure, with a vent gap, to hold the heat in.
  • Move the enclosure, or the gecko in a ventilated tub, to the warmest room.
  • A wrapped hot-water bottle or hand warmer beside the gecko, never touching it.
  • An automatic mister stops with the power: mist by hand on schedule.
  • No insects until heat is back. Past a day or two too cool, board it with a vet or a friend with power.

Don't

  • Don't use candles, gas stoves, or open flame near the enclosure.
  • Don't put a heat pack in contact with the gecko; a cold animal can't move away fast enough and will burn.
  • Don't feed insects to "keep its strength up." Undigested food in a cold gut rots.
  • Don't warm a cold gecko in hot water.
Outage kit Hand warmers or a hot-water bottle, two thick towels, a battery thermometer, a ventilated tub, a hand mister, and your vet numbers on paper.

Leaving town

The nightly mist is a daily need, so plan one step stricter than for a typical reptile.

Trip lengthAdultJuvenile, or a gecko sick, mid-shed or gravid
1 to 2 nightsA sitter every 1 to 2 days for the mist, water, a stool check and a temperature check.A sitter daily, with pre-measured powder and written instructions.
3 nights or moreA daily sitter who has done a practice visit with you, or boarding with a reptile-experienced vet or shop. Never a general dog-and-cat boarding facility.

Transport and vet trips

  • A ventilated plastic tub or small carrier with a towel to grip, in a dark, room-temperature bag or box. No loose substrate, no water dish.
  • Skip the meal before a trip, and never leave the gecko in a parked car, hot or cold. Below 65°F (18°C), warm the car first, and on trips over about 30 minutes put a wrapped hand warmer beside the tub. Check it every 20 to 30 minutes.

Enrichment checklist & log

Setup

A climbing network from floor to ceiling, mostly thin branches, with real gaps
At least two hides high up, at different heights
Dense foliage that holds droplets after misting
A raised feeding ledge, so the gecko never comes down to eat
Lay box for an adult female through the season (page {{P:eggs}})

Monthly review

Change one branch angle or rotate one plant. Leave the core structure and hides alone
Release insects into the upper branches after dark at least once
Rotate to a different powder flavor for a week
Try one occasional fruit from the safe list (page {{P:treats}})
Check every perch is still anchored

Enrichment safety Anchor everything: a perch that collapses mid-jump is a fall, or a dropped tail that never grows back. Take out insects not eaten within 15 to 20 minutes, never use wild-caught insects, and never overhaul a shy gecko's whole enclosure at once. Nothing here replaces the temperature ceiling and the humidity cycle.
