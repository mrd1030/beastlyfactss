Glossary, A to L

AmmoniaThe first waste product of an axolotl and of rotting food, and highly toxic. The target reading is 0 ppm. Page {{P:water}}.
AscitesFluid swelling in the abdomen. A sign of heat stress above about 75°F (24°C) or of a bacterial infection, and a vet visit. Page {{P:infection}}.
BaffleAnything that breaks up a filter's outflow, such as foam or a spray bar, so the current reaching the axolotl is gentle. Page {{P:filtration}}.
BdBatrachochytrium dendrobatidis: a chytrid fungus that causes the skin disease chytridiomycosis in more than 200 amphibian species. Page {{P:arrival}}.
BsalBatrachochytrium salamandrivorans: a chytrid fungus that kills salamanders, axolotls included. The reason for the 2025 federal listing of genus Ambystoma. Pages {{P:arrival}}, {{P:legal2}}.
Captive-bredHatched from parents kept by people rather than caught in the wild. Pet axolotls are captive-bred. Page {{P:profile}}.
ChillerA device that actively cools aquarium water. The only cooling method that holds a set temperature whatever the weather. Page {{P:cooling}}.
ChloramineA compound of chlorine and ammonia added to many city water supplies. Unlike chlorine it does not leave water that is left standing, so a dechlorinator that handles it is needed. Page {{P:water}}.
Chytrid fungiA group of fungi that infect amphibian skin. Bd and Bsal are the two that matter for pets. Page {{P:arrival}}.
CloacaThe single vent behind the back legs, used for waste and breeding. Swollen in a mature male. Page {{P:sexing}}.
CyclingGrowing the filter bacteria that turn ammonia into nitrite and nitrite into nitrate. Four to six weeks is typical, slower in cold water. Page {{P:water}}.
DechlorinatorA water conditioner that neutralizes chlorine and chloramine in tap water before it reaches the axolotl. Page {{P:water}}.
Drip-matchingAdding small amounts of the destination water to a new arrival's container over time, after the temperatures match, so the chemistry shifts gradually. Page {{P:handling}}.
Fecal testA vet's microscope check of a fresh stool sample for parasites, best done during quarantine. Pages {{P:arrival}}, {{P:burns}}.
GFPGreen fluorescent protein: a protein from a jellyfish gene that glows green under ultraviolet light. Put into axolotls for research in 2006, and now a pet morph. Page {{P:species}}.
GHGeneral hardness: the amount of dissolved minerals in water. No published target exists for axolotls. Page {{P:water}}.
ImpactionA gut blockage from something swallowed that cannot pass, most often gravel or small decor. Page {{P:impaction}}.
Injurious wildlifeAnimals the federal government lists under the Lacey Act as harmful, which limits importing and shipping them. Page {{P:legal2}}.
IUCNInternational Union for Conservation of Nature: the body that keeps the Red List of threatened species, which grades the axolotl Critically Endangered. Page {{P:profile}}.
KHCarbonate hardness: the water's buffering capacity, which steadies pH. No published target exists for axolotls. Page {{P:water}}.
Lacey ActThe federal wildlife law under which genus Ambystoma was listed as injurious wildlife in 2025. Page {{P:legal2}}.
Lateral lineA row of pressure-sensing organs along the body that lets an axolotl feel movement in the water, including food. Page {{P:behavior}}.
LeucisticThe mostly white morph with dark eyes. Not the same as albino, which has no black pigment at all. Page {{P:species}}.
Reference

Glossary, M to Z

MelanoidA solid dark morph, with many black pigment cells and none of the reflective ones. Page {{P:species}}.
MorphA color variety. Morphs differ in looks only, never in care. Page {{P:species}}.
Mosaic and chimeraAn axolotl built from two genetically different cell lines, carrying patches of two colors. It does not breed true. Page {{P:species}}.
NitrateThe end product of the nitrogen cycle. Far less toxic than ammonia or nitrite, kept under 10 ppm by water changes. Page {{P:water}}.
NitriteThe middle product of the nitrogen cycle, and toxic. The target reading is 0 ppm. Page {{P:water}}.
Nitrogen cycleThe chain in which filter bacteria turn ammonia into nitrite and then nitrate. Page {{P:water}}.
PaedomorphosisReaching adult size and breeding while keeping the larval body: gills, a finned tail and a fully aquatic life. Page {{P:species}}.
pHHow acidic or alkaline water is, on a scale where 7 is neutral. 7.4 to 7.6 for an axolotl. Page {{P:water}}.
ppmParts per million: how many parts of a substance there are in a million parts of water. The unit of a water test reading. Page {{P:water}}.
PVCPolyvinyl chloride: a rigid plastic. Wide pipe sections with sanded edges make safe hides. Pages {{P:substrate}}, {{P:budget}}.
QuarantineKeeping a new axolotl in its own tank or tub, with its own tools, for 6 to 8 weeks before it meets an established one. Page {{P:arrival}}.
RegenerationRegrowing lost body parts. Axolotls regrow limbs, the tail, and parts of the eye, brain, spinal cord and heart. Page {{P:burns}}.
ROReverse osmosis: a filtering process that strips almost everything out of water. Never used straight for an axolotl without remineralizing. Page {{P:water}}.
Salt bathA short bath in salted water. The published figures, 10 to 15 g/L for about 10 minutes, are a ceiling, so a vet sets the dose. Page {{P:tubbing}}.
Sand saucerA shallow dish of sand under 1 mm with food hidden in it, for searching without loose sand across the floor. Page {{P:foods}}.
Spray barA bar with a row of small holes on a filter or chiller return, which spreads the flow out. Page {{P:filtration}}.
Sponge filterAn air-driven filter that pulls water through a foam block. Gentle by design, the standard choice for axolotls. Page {{P:filtration}}.
TubbingKeeping an axolotl in a bare, unfiltered container with a full daily water change, for isolation, observation or support during illness. Page {{P:tubbing}}.
Un-ionized ammoniaThe more toxic of ammonia's two forms. Its share rises with temperature and pH. Page {{P:temperature}}.
Units°F, degrees Fahrenheit, and °C, degrees Celsius, for temperature; in (inches), cm (centimeters) and mm (millimeters) for size; gal (US gallons) and L (liters) for tank volume; mL (milliliters) and g (grams) for small amounts; g/L (grams per liter) for a salt bath; ppm for water tests.
VCAVeterinary Centers of America, now VCA Animal Hospitals: an animal hospital chain whose pet care articles are among this package's sources.
Water moldThe white, cotton-like fungal growth that is the most common fungal infection in axolotls. Page {{P:infection}}.
