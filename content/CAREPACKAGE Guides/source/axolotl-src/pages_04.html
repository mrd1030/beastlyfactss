Section 04 · Handling & Behavior

Handling & moving an axolotl

Why the answer is almost always no, and how to move one when you must.

Axolotls are not a hands-on pet, and that is about their bodies, not a preference. The skin is scaleless and sensitive, with no robust slime layer to fall back on the way a fish has. The feathery external gills do the actual work of breathing, so a snagged filament affects how the animal takes in oxygen, not just how it looks. And the skeleton is largely cartilage rather than solid bone. Handling risks skin and gill damage, real stress, and infection at any point of contact.

When a move is genuinely necessary

SituationHow
Tank maintenance, a rehouse, a health checkGuide the axolotl into a submerged container while it is still underwater, then lift the container. Keep it in water the whole time: air exposure and pressure are both real risks.
If a net is the only wayAvoid nets if you can, since gill filaments and toes snag in mesh. If nothing else works, use a soft, fine mesh net and have the axolotl in it for seconds, not minutes.
If it has to be touchedWear clean, disposable nitrile gloves. The oils and residues on bare hands harm its skin.
A vet visitA small, clear, watertight container filled with its own tank water, kept cool on the way (page {{P:outage}}).

At the vet

A vet visit is the most common reason an axolotl leaves its tank, and the container does most of the work. In a clear container the vet can look it over through the side without taking it out, and much of an axolotl exam happens that way. When a vet needs to restrain one for a closer look, a sample or a procedure, the standard is sedation in the water with an anesthetic drug, rather than holding a struggling animal by hand.

Bringing a new axolotl home

  1. Temperature first. Float or stand the travel bag or container beside the destination water until a thermometer reads the same in both.
  2. Then chemistry, slowly. Drip or add small amounts of the destination water to the container over time, so the chemistry shifts gradually. This is drip-matching, and it settles the animal with no handling at all.
  3. Then the move. Guide it across in a submerged container. Know the targets in advance: 60 to 64°F (15.6 to 17.8°C) and pH 7.4 to 7.6.

If another axolotl already lives in the tank, the newcomer goes into its own quarantine tub instead, and the acclimation happens into that tub (page {{P:arrival}}).

The relationship is real, it is just not touch If part of what you want from a pet is physical contact, an axolotl will not offer it, and pushing for it harms the animal. What it does offer is a lot to watch, and an animal that learns to take food from your tongs.

Body language & normal behavior

What a healthy axolotl does all day, and what its gills and posture tell you.

What normal looks like

Most of an axolotl's day is spent lying nearly motionless on the bottom. That is normal for the species, not a sign that the tank is boring or the animal is ill. It notices food through its lateral line, a row of pressure-sensing organs along the body, and will snap at a pellet dropped in front of it as it sinks. It breathes four ways: through the skin, the lining of the mouth, the gills, and simple lungs it fills by gulping air at the surface. So an occasional trip up for a gulp of air, then back down, is normal too.

Enrichment & common mistakes

Not toys. Choice: somewhere to rest, cover that feels secure, and food that takes some finding.

What has been tested

Billingham's 2017 study gave laboratory axolotls rocks, plants, plastic shelters, old compact discs and tunnels, and recorded what they did. The animals nudged, rubbed against and moved the items, in one case shifting a disc into a position that made a shelter. Tunnels came out as the item most worth adding. Research colonies land on the same short list: plants, places to hide, controlled temperature and a set day length. A timer on a dim light, on the same schedule every day, costs nothing.

What to offer

  • Structure: 3 to 4 hides of different shapes, one of them a tunnel, most of them kept stable and never moved.
  • A choice of current: a gentle flow on one side from a baffled filter, and a genuinely still corner on the other.
  • Foraging: tong feeding, scattered pieces, or a sand saucer, with the feeding spot moved every few days.
  • Novelty: a mild rearrange of non-critical decor about once a month, or one new smooth item in for 5 to 7 days, then rotated out.
  • Shade: floating plants or a dimmed light for the dappled shade this species is drawn to.

Frequent full overhauls remove the security the tank is supposed to give. Cohousing is not enrichment either: two axolotls bite at limbs and gills. Strong current and bright light are just stress.

Section 05 · Arrival & Life Stages

Choosing an axolotl, quarantine & the first vet visit

What to look at before you pay, and the weeks before a newcomer meets an established tank.

Checks before you buy

CheckWhat you want, and why
The waterFeel it or ask its temperature. An axolotl kept above about 75°F (24°C) turns sluggish and open to infection, and carries that home.
The gillsFull and feathery, held out to the sides. Curled forward is a stress sign.
The skinNo white, cotton-like tufts (water mold, the most common fungal infection), and no red patches or open sores.
How it sitsResting on the bottom. Not floating at the surface, and no swollen, fluid-filled belly.
AppetiteAsk to see it eat. Poor appetite is one of the first signs of illness.
The tank floorSkip animals kept on gravel. One that has lived on pebbles may already have swallowed some.

Quarantine

If you already keep an axolotl, a new one never goes straight into the established tank. It spends 6 to 8 weeks in its own tank or tub first, typically adequate for a new amphibian. Zoos run 30 days as a minimum and prefer 60. Land closer to 8 weeks than to the shortest end, and restart the clock, not just pause it, if the animal shows any illness partway through.

  • A second tank or a large tub with a secure, ventilated lid, kept bare or close to it so you can watch the animal and its waste.
  • Its own net or scoop, siphon and cleaning tools, never shared with the main tank.
  • A fecal test (a vet's microscope check of a stool sample for parasites) through an exotics vet at some point in the window.
Why stricter than for a fish Two fungal diseases target amphibians. Bd (Batrachochytrium dendrobatidis) affects more than 200 species. Bsal (Batrachochytrium salamandrivorans) kills salamanders, axolotls included, and reached Europe through the pet trade. Over 750,000 salamanders were imported into the United States between 2010 and 2014 alone. Never release an axolotl or its tank water outdoors.

The first vet visit

Find an aquatic or exotic-amphibian vet before you need one: general small-animal clinics often are not equipped for this species. The find-a-vet directory of the Association of Reptile and Amphibian Veterinarians lists candidates; ask each practice directly whether it sees axolotls. Bring the animal in a container of its own tank water, plus a separate sample of tank water in a clean container and your latest test readings.

Sexing, pairs & breeding

How to tell males from females through the glass, and why a pair is a bigger commitment than it sounds.

Sexing, with no handling at all

FeatureMature maleMature female
Cloaca (the vent behind the back legs, used for waste and breeding)Swollen: a visible bulge behind the back legsSlimmer there, with no bulge
NailsDarkNot dark
Age at maturityAbout 10 months12 to 18 months

An axolotl younger than that cannot be sexed reliably by anyone, so a juvenile sold as a particular sex is a guess.

A pair will breed

A mature male and female kept together will breed. The female lays her eggs 12 to 20 hours after mating, spread over one or two days. In managed care a female can lay 200 to 1,500 eggs every 3 to 6 months. At 70°F (21°C) the eggs hatch in about 2 weeks, sometimes 3 to 4, into tiny larvae that live on live food, 2 to 3 times a day.

Raising them is the real commitment. Juveniles are cannibalistic and are raised apart, so a single clutch means hundreds of animals to house, feed live food and rehome, and they keep coming every few months.

If you do not want hundreds of larvae Keep one axolotl, or two of the same sex, confirmed after maturity.

What sets off breeding

In one research vivarium, natural breeding followed a cool spell of 12 to 14°C (54 to 57°F) with about 11 hours of light a day. It is a reminder that the light cycle is part of the environment too.

No metamorphosis

An axolotl reaches maturity and breeds while still in its larval body, and it never leaves the water. The seasonal job in this species belongs to the keeper: getting the tank through summer heat, which is planned on the routine page.
