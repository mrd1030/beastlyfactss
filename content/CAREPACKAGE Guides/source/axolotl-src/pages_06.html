Daily, weekly & seasonal routine

About a minute a day, and one fixed day a week for the water.

Daily

Read the water temperature
A look at the gills in good light
Filter running, surface moving
Feed on schedule; leftovers out within 15 to 30 minutes

Weekly

Test ammonia, nitrite, nitrate and pH, before the change
Change 20 to 30 percent, dechlorinated and temperature-matched
Siphon waste and leftovers off the floor
Rinse the mechanical sponge or floss in the old water as it needs
One row in the owner log

Monthly

Rearrange one piece of non-critical decor, or rotate in one new smooth item
Run a hand over the decor for new rough edges
Read back through the log for slow trends
Check the chiller or fan still does what it did last month

Seasonal

Spring: test the cooling plan against a genuinely hot day, before summer
Spring: have the fan, bottles for freezing and a battery air pump ready
Summer: lights off on hot days, blinds closed
Yearly: update the sitter sheet and confirm your vet still sees axolotls
Fix the day, not the intention Pick one day a week and always test and change water on it. A routine tied to a day survives a busy month. By the time an axolotl tank looks like it needs attention, the readings moved days ago.
What is not on this list Handling, full teardowns and medication. None belong in a routine. Biological filter media is rinsed barely ever, never all at once.

Power outages, heat waves, travel & transport

For an axolotl, a summer power outage is an oxygen problem and a heat problem at once.

When the power goes out

Oxygen is the fast risk. Once the filter stops, the surface stops moving, gas exchange slows, and dissolved oxygen starts falling within a couple of hours. So the first move is aeration, not the thermometer.

Do

  • Run a battery air pump with an airstone. Own one before you need it.
  • No pump: scoop water out and pour it back from a few inches up for a minute or two, every 10 to 15 minutes in a small tank, 20 to 30 in a larger one.
  • Unplug the filter, so you decide when it restarts.
  • In summer, keep heat out: blinds closed, the tank away from windows, the room as cool as you can.

Don't

  • Don't feed until the filter is back and stable.
  • Don't restart a filter that sat dead for hours without checking it: rinse the media in old tank water first, or decomposing waste flushes into the tank.
  • Don't use loose ice. Sealed frozen bottles, swapped as they melt.
  • Don't bring an open flame or a heat source near the tank.

Heat waves

The emergency runs in reverse for an axolotl: heat is the danger, not cold. Above about 75°F (24°C) it goes off food, develops ascites and floats. Use the cooling steps on page {{P:heat}}; for an animal already in crisis, a dish in the refrigerator is the recognized stopgap while the tank is cooled.

Travel

A sitter needs the pet-sitter sheet, walked through in person, and a thermometer they will actually read. In summer, the cooling has to work without you: test it before you leave.

Transport and moving house

  • Move the axolotl in a small, clear, watertight container filled with its own tank water, kept cool.
  • In warm weather: an insulated container in an air-conditioned car, never a hot trunk or a parked car.
  • For a move, pack the filter media in a sealed bag or tub of tank water, never tap water, with a battery airstone on a long drive. Set the filter up again as soon as the tank is filled, using that reserved water.
  • On arrival, match temperature before anything else, then move the animal in a submerged container.

The one thing that matters most This animal must stay cold. The thermometer should read 60 to 64°F (15.6 to 17.8°C). Above 72°F (22°C) is an emergency: close the blinds, turn the light off, put the fan across the water, float a sealed bottle of frozen water, and call me. Each visit

Read the thermometer and write the number down
Check the filter is running and the water surface is moving
Look at the axolotl: resting on the bottom, gills feathery and out to the sides, not curled forward
Feed one pre-portioned meal, only on the days listed above
Take out anything not eaten after 15 to 30 minutes

Please don't

  • Feed extra or on another day. An adult eats every 2 to 3 days and always looks interested.
  • Touch, lift or net the axolotl for any reason. Its skin and gills damage very easily.
  • Add tap water that has not been dechlorinated, clean the filter, or add anything to the tank.
  • Turn on a bright light over the tank. The axolotl has no eyelids.
Call me if The thermometer reads above 72°F (22°C) · the filter is silent · the axolotl is floating and cannot get down · there is white fluff, a red patch or a sore on it · the power has been out more than an hour or two. If I don't answer, call the vet.

Why bother logging The water readings are this animal's vital signs, and they take minutes with no handling at all.
