How to use this package

Three ways to get value from it, depending on where you are right now.

Units used throughout Every temperature and size is given twice. Temperatures appear in degrees Fahrenheit (°F) with degrees Celsius (°C) beside them. Sizes appear in inches (in) with centimeters (cm) beside them, and a tarantula is measured by its leg span, diagonally from the tip of a front leg to the tip of the opposite back leg. Humidity is a percentage (%) of the moisture the air could hold. Sling containers are sold by volume, in drams for vials and ounces for cups. Prices are in United States dollars.

If you haven't set up yet

Check the law first, on page {{P:legal}}: a tarantula is legal with no permit in most of the United States, but a few places bar it and one state clears only three species. Then read pages {{P:profile}} to {{P:cleaning}} before buying anything. The enclosure shape is the decision that kills tarantulas: a ground-dweller in a tall enclosure has somewhere to fall from. Use the Budget & Shopping List on page {{P:budget}} to buy once.

If your tarantula is already home

Audit the setup against the checklist below, paying most attention to fall height, substrate depth and the water dish. If your spider has stopped eating, read page {{P:fasting}} before you worry: for a tarantula, weeks without food are usually normal. A shrunken, wrinkled abdomen is the real emergency, and it is on page {{P:dehydration}}.

Which tarantulas this package fits More than 900 species exist, and they do not share one set of numbers. Quick profile

Everything at a glance before you read the detail.
FamilyTheraphosidae, the tarantulas: more than 900 species, on every continent except Antarctica
Species in this packageChilean rose hair, curly hair, Brazilian black, Chaco golden knee and Mexican red knee on the ground; the pink toe in the trees
Adult sizeAbout 6 in (15 cm) leg span for a rose hair or a pink toe, about 6.5 in (17 cm) for a curly hair. Across the whole family, 2 to 12 in (5 to 30 cm)
LifespanSet by sex and species. A female rose hair commonly 15 to 20 years or more; a male about 5, 10 at the outside
Diet typeCarnivore: live insects only, with no formulated diet (page {{P:diet}})
Enclosure (terrestrial adult)About 20×10×10 in (51×25×25 cm), roughly a 10-gallon tank, floor about 3× the leg span, under about 12 in (30 cm) tall
The one hard ruleKeep a ground-dweller low. Height is the risk, floor area is the benefit, and a fall can rupture the abdomen (page {{P:enclosure}})
HousingOne per enclosure. Most species are solitary and cannibalistic, and no enclosure size fixes that
HandlingLook, don't touch. The risk runs in both directions (page {{P:handling}})
ConservationVaries by species. Many are listed under CITES, the Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna and Flora. Buy captive-bred

Cost overview

Initial setup

$25 to $100 for a beginner species, plus $50 to $170 for the enclosure, substrate, hide, decor and water dish: $75 to $270 all in. Every line is on page {{P:budget}}.

Ongoing

$5 to $15 a month, nearly all of it feeder insects: $60 to $180 a year. No insurer covers a tarantula, and routine vet visits are not part of the budget.

The real number to sit with Not the money, the years. A female rose hair is a 15 to 20 year commitment on a budget most pets spend in a month, so the time is the thing to think through before you buy.

Fun facts worth knowing

  • They digest food outside the body. Enzymes injected through the fangs liquefy the prey, a muscular sucking stomach pumps the meal in, and the empty shell is left behind as a small pellet.
  • Females keep molting for life. Males stop at maturity, which is a large part of why a female can outlive a male by a decade or more.
  • Their blood, called hemolymph, is not held in vessels. It flows freely around the organs, which is why a ruptured abdomen turns fatal so fast.

The species covered, size & lifespan

"Tarantula" covers animals with different needs. Know which one is in the enclosure.

Ask the seller for the species in Latin, and write it on the emergency card. It decides the enclosure, the humidity, the lifespan, and in one state whether you need a permit. New World tarantulas come from the Americas and defend themselves by flicking irritating hairs; every species below is one. Old World tarantulas, from Africa and Asia, are faster, more defensive and carry a stronger bite, and this package does not cover them.

Lifespan: the sex decides it

Females keep molting their whole adult lives. Males stop at the molt that makes them mature, and most die within about a year of reaching maturity and mating. That is why two spiders of one species can differ by a decade or more. A male rose hair lives about 5 years in all; a female 15 to 20 or more. Slings are usually sold unsexed, so an unsexed spiderling is a coin flip on lifespan. How sexing works is on page {{P:sexing}}.

CITES and buying captive-bred CITES is the treaty that controls trade in listed animals between countries. The genera Brachypelma, Tliltocatl and Poecilotheria are on its Appendix II, but most tarantulas are not listed at all, so unlisted species move through the trade with far less scrutiny. The Chilean rose hair is one of the species most often raised as a collection concern. Buying captive-bred takes pressure off wild populations, and a captive-bred spider is less likely to carry parasites (page {{P:arrival}}).
