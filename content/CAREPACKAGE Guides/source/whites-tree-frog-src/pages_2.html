Section 02 · Housing & Environment

UVB, light & the day length

Not strictly required. The frog survives without it, and does better with it.

Low-level UVB is the recommendation for this species. It is nocturnal, so it never needed the strong lamps a basking lizard does, but a frog under working UVB makes its own vitamin D3, and vitamin D3 is how it uses the calcium in its food. Missing UVB is one of the two causes of metabolic bone disease on page {{P:mbd}}, a condition veterinary references describe as frequently seen in captive amphibians. A low-output tube is cheap insurance against it.

SettingNumber
TubeLow-output T5, 5 to 7%: T5 is the slim fluorescent tube size, and the percentage is its UVB output. In a reflector fixture across the top
DistanceThe basking branch at least 6 in (15 cm) below the fixture, and the tube within about 18 in (46 cm) of where the frog sits
Strength at the frogA UV index (UVI), the standard scale for UVB strength, of roughly 0.7 to 1.6 where it basks, if you own a UV meter. Most keepers set the distance and never measure
Day length12 hours on, 12 hours off, on a timer. A regular cycle matters more than the exact hour
Replace the tubeEvery 6 to 8 months, on a schedule, whether or not it still lights
At nightDark. A nocturnal frog needs no night light, and the room's own dim light is enough to see by
Glass blocks it Ordinary glass and most plastics filter out UVB, so a tube sitting on a glass lid, or a terrarium near a sunny window, delivers far less than it appears to. Mount the fixture over mesh. And a window is never the UVB source: through glass it is only a heat trap, and page {{P:enclosure}} explains why that matters more.

The bulb that still glows

A T5 UVB tube keeps producing visible light long after its UVB output has dropped. That is why it is replaced on a date rather than when it looks tired, and why the date it went in belongs on the equipment log on page {{P:equiplog}}. Amphibian guidance runs shorter than the 6 to 12 months often quoted for reptile tubes, so do not borrow a lizard keeper's schedule.

UVB and the supplement jar are one decision

Whether the calcium dust needs to carry vitamin D3 depends on the lighting. A frog under a working tube makes its own; a frog without one does not, and needs calcium with D3. More is not better in the other direction either: an excess of vitamin A has been suggested to interfere with vitamin D metabolism, which is one reason to dose a multivitamin on schedule rather than generously. The schedule is on page {{P:feeders}}.

Keep the light gentle This is a frog that sleeps through the day against cover. Bright light with nowhere to get out of it is stress, not enrichment. The broad leaves and cork on page {{P:substrate}} are what make a lit enclosure livable, and a frog that is always fully exposed under the tube needs more cover, not less light.

Substrate, plants & furnishings

The floor holds the humidity, and everything above it is where the frog actually lives.

Substrate

ChoiceVerdictNotes
Coarse orchid barkGoodLarge pieces hold moisture without packing down
Coco fiber or coco huskGoodHolds humidity well. Keep it lightly moist, never soggy
Bioactive soil mixGoodA planted, living floor. More work to set up, less to maintain
Sphagnum moss on topAddA layer over any of the above helps hold the humidity cycle
Paper towelSimpleEasiest to clean and to check droppings on, harder to hold humidity. Good for quarantine
Fine, loose substrate, small gravel, small barkNeverEasy to swallow along with an insect. Page {{P:minor}}
Cedar or pine mulchNeverToxic to amphibians

Soil is replaced when it starts to smell or stays waterlogged, because wet, dirty substrate is what builds ammonia. Bark and moss can be taken out and washed as needed, and the more often the better.

Plants

Dense, layered planting is the one enrichment item with real evidence behind it: in the closest study, tree frogs given plant cover grew larger and faster and chose the planted side. Page {{P:enrichment}} has the detail. Use broad-leaved plants sturdy enough to hold a heavy frog. Live plants must be free of fertilizer and pesticide residue, on the leaves and in the soil, which means rinsing new plants and repotting them; keeping them in small pots makes cleaning easier. Artificial plants fill the dense parts cheaply and wipe clean, but they do not hold humidity or freshen the air the way live ones do. A mix is the sensible answer.

Furnishings

  • Sturdy branches and cork. This is a stocky frog, and anything it climbs has to take its weight without sagging or shifting.
  • Broad perches near the top. Wide leaves, flat branches and cork flats rather than thin stems, at several heights, with the best spots high up.
  • A daytime sleeping spot. A large piece of bark set diagonally a couple of inches from the back wall lets the frog tuck in behind it to sleep. Dark paper on the outside of the back glass adds a dim, secluded wall.
  • The water dish, low and stable, from page {{P:water}}.
The visibility test If the frog is always immediately visible, there is not enough in there. A well-furnished enclosure is one where you sometimes have to look for it.
A frog rubbing its nose on the glass Some frogs push along the bottom of the glass trying to get out, and a nose rubbed raw is an open door for infection, covered on page {{P:skin}}. A strip of paper a couple of inches tall around the outside base of the glass often stops it.
Section 02 · Housing & Environment

Cleaning without soap, and household chemicals

A frog's skin absorbs what touches it, and the water dish collects whatever lands in it.

The cleaning routine

WhenWhat
DailySpot clean: wipe droppings off leaves, branches and glass. Pull uneaten insects within a few hours of feeding. Empty, rinse and refill the water dish, or every other day at the very least
As neededTake out bark and moss and wash them, the more often the better. Change paper towel lining often
When it smellsReplace soil when it starts to smell or stays waterlogged
Plain hot water, not soap Clean with plain hot water. Soaps and disinfectants can leave a residue the frog absorbs through its skin. When something genuinely needs disinfecting, rinse it very well and dry it completely before it goes back. Refill the dish with treated water, never fresh tap water.

When disinfecting is the right call

After an illness, before reusing a quarantine setup, or when an enclosure comes to you secondhand. Veterinary husbandry guidance gives household bleach at 30 mL per liter of water (about 2 tablespoons per quart), left in contact for at least 30 minutes, then rinsed thoroughly and dried completely. Do it with the frog in another room, and let the item air out until no smell is left.

Household chemicals and scents

HazardWhat to do
Phenol cleaners such as Pine-Sol and Lysol, and formaldehyde and other aldehydesHighly toxic to amphibians. Keep them out of the frog's room entirely
Other disinfectants: iodine, chlorhexidine, quaternary ammonium, chlorine, ammoniaMost disinfectants harm amphibians. None of them goes on anything the frog touches unless it is rinsed and dried afterward
Air fresheners, sprays and fumesVentilate the room whenever anything has to be sprayed nearby. The fumes are part of the hazard
Pesticides, including bug sprays and flea treatmentsToxic to amphibians. Never in the frog's room, and never on a plant going into the enclosure
Cigarette smokeListed among the toxins that harm amphibians. Not in the frog's room
Lotion, sanitizer, soap on your handsThe handling rules on page {{P:handling}}

Where you wash it

Not the kitchen sink, and not a bathtub people use. Amphibians carry Salmonella without looking sick, and the bacteria travel on the dish, the decor and the water. A utility sink or a plastic tub kept only for the frog is the right place. If a bathtub has to be used, clean it thoroughly afterward. Wash your own hands with soap and running water when you finish, away from the frog.

Keep a frog-only kit A spray bottle, a sponge, a dish brush, paper towels and a jug of treated water, kept apart from household cleaning supplies. Nothing in it is ever used with a cleaner, so nothing in it can carry residue back.
Section 03 · Feeding

Feeding by size & age

A sedentary frog with an appetite that does not switch off. Overfeeding is the default unless someone watches its shape.

Two facts about this species meet at the food dish. It spends most of the day sitting still, and it eats whatever is put in front of it. Put those together and a well-meaning keeper produces an overweight frog without ever feeling they overdid it. So the schedule below is a starting point, and the frog's body condition on page {{P:bodycondition}} is what corrects it.

StageHow oftenWhat and how muchDusting
Froglet, up to about 1.6 in (4 cm)DailyOne to four pinhead cricketsTwice a week
Growing juvenileDailyCrickets sized to the frog, a few at a sittingTwice a week
Smaller adult, under about 3 in (7.5 cm)2 to 3 times a week3 to 4 insects, about three-week-old cricketsOnce a week
Adult, over 3 in (7.5 cm)2 to 3 times a week3 to 4 insects, a few larger cricketsOnce a week

Larger items, an earthworm or a fuzzy mouse, come no more than once a month for an adult. Waxworms and superworms are once a week at most. Page {{P:feeders}} has the full list.

How a feeding works

  • Evening. This frog wakes and hunts after dark, so feed in the evening, around the time of the main misting.
  • Loose or from forceps. Insects can go loose into the enclosure or be offered from blunt, round-tipped forceps, whichever the frog responds to. Forceps let you count exactly what each frog ate, which matters in a group.
  • Prey it can find at its level. Insects that climb the planting suit it better than ones that vanish into the substrate. Releasing them into cover rather than presenting them is also enrichment, page {{P:enrichment}}.
  • Clear up. Pull any insects still loose a few hours later, before they drown in the water dish or scatter into the planting.
  • Never free-feed. A bowl of insects left in permanently is how this species gets fat.
The amount matters more than the interval Whether an adult eats on Monday and Thursday or Tuesday and Saturday makes little difference. Whether it gets four crickets or ten makes a great deal. Write the counts on the owner log on page {{P:ownerlog}} for the first couple of months, and compare them against the ridge test.
A frog that stops eating Check the temperature and humidity first, since a cold or badly set up frog eats less. If they read right and the frog still refuses food, treat it as a health sign, not a preference: loss of appetite is among the first signs of both chytrid and red-leg. Page {{P:redflags}}.

Supplements: two jobs, not one

An unfed cricket delivers very little, and an unsupplemented cricket carries about 1 part calcium to 9 parts phosphorus. Fixing that takes two separate steps, and neither replaces the other.

StepHow
Gut-loadFeed the insects a calcium-rich gut-load for 24 to 72 hours before they go to the frog, 48 hours being the figure most often given. Feed them out within a few hours of taking them off the food, before they empty their guts
Dust, adultCalcium and a multivitamin about once a week
Dust, juvenileCalcium and a multivitamin about twice a week
D3 or notUnder working UVB the frog makes its own vitamin D3. Without UVB, the calcium needs to carry D3. Page {{P:uvb}}

Dust immediately before feeding, not in advance: powder falls off, and an insect left sitting in a dusted container grooms most of it away. Shake the insects in a bag or cup with a pinch of powder until they look lightly floured, not caked.

Why the multivitamin matters for a frog Amphibians cannot make their own carotenoids, so these have to come in through the diet, and veterinary guidance names vitamin and mineral supplementation as necessary to prevent nutritional disease. Vitamin A deficiency in particular shows up as a frog that can no longer catch prey with its tongue, page {{P:mbd}}.
Section 03 · Feeding

Body condition: reading the tympanum ridges

This species is round by design. Obesity is read off the head, not the waistline.

White's tree frogs are nicknamed dumpy tree frogs for their round, waxy-skinned build, and a moderate fat roll over the shoulders is normal for the species. So general roundness tells you very little. What tells you something is the ridge of fat that sits just above the tympanum, the round external eardrum behind each eye, and fat building in the armpits.

The ridge test, three readings

What you seeReadingWhat to do
No visible ridges above the eardrumLikely underweightFeed more, or feed more often. Check the frog is actually eating what goes in
Ridges present and definedRightKeep the current schedule and keep checking
Ridges prominent, sagging or folding over, fat bulging over the tympanum and in the armpitsObeseFeed less: fewer insects per sitting, fatty items cut out, and more room and reason to climb. Page {{P:obesity}}

When it is past a diet fix

In an overweight frog the excess fat starts to show clinically: a cloudy look to the eyes from lipid deposits in the cornea, a body cavity that looks enlarged from fat laid down inside it, and fat in the crests above the eyes heavy enough to interfere with vision. Any of those is a vet visit, not just a smaller dinner, because sustained obesity raises the risk of fatty liver disease.

Make it a monthly habit Once a month, look at the head from the side in good light, take a photo from the same angle, and note the reading on the owner log on page {{P:ownerlog}}. If you own a kitchen scale that reads in grams, weigh the frog in a small lidded tub at the same time. A series of photos makes a slow change visible in a way no single look at the frog does.
Section 04 · Handling, Company & Behavior

Handling with plain water & no soap

The risk runs the direction people do not expect: not the frog hurting you, but you hurting the frog.

This is one of the few frogs that tolerates being picked up, and most White's tree frogs handle well once they are used to it, more so than the great majority of pet frogs and toads. What makes technique matter is the skin. Amphibian skin is permeable and absorbs what it touches, including oils, lotions, soap residue and chemicals from your hands. Even the natural oils and salts on clean human skin can damage it.

The technique

StepHow
HandsWash thoroughly with plain water only, no soap, immediately before handling, and rinse well so no lotion, sanitizer or soap remains
Or glovesPowder-free disposable gloves, wetted with dechlorinated water. After lotion, cleaning chemicals or a manicure, gloves are the safer choice however well you have washed
Pick-upLet the frog walk onto a flat, wet hand rather than grabbing it, and support its body. Sit low, over something soft, in case it jumps
SessionRoughly 5 to 15 minutes, and no more than about twice a week
AfterwardBack into the enclosure, then wash your hands with soap and running water, away from the frog

First-timer mistakes

  • Handling with lotioned or chemical-exposed hands. By far the most common and most damaging mistake.
  • Handling too often, which adds stress even to a tolerant species.
  • Wetting hands or gloves with tap water straight from the faucet instead of treated water.
  • Treating handling as enrichment. For an amphibian it mostly is not; it is something the frog tolerates.
Salmonella, and children under 5 Healthy-looking amphibians carry Salmonella in the gut with no sign of illness, and it spreads hands and surfaces to mouth rather than by bites. Wash with soap and running water after touching the frog, its water or anything in the enclosure. Keep the frog and its equipment out of the kitchen. Children younger than 5 should not handle the frog or touch its enclosure at all, since they are the most likely to become seriously ill. Older children are supervised and wash every time.
What to expect A sedentary animal, so do not expect a lot of movement during a session. This is a calm, low-drama frog by amphibian standards. A frog that struggles or leaps away every time is asking for shorter sessions or a break, and a new frog gets none at all until it is through quarantine on page {{P:arrival}}.
Section 04 · Handling, Company & Behavior

Group housing by size, sexing & breeding

They tolerate company well. The one rule is not optional.
Every frog in a group is a similar size A White's tree frog will attempt to swallow anything it can fit in its mouth, including a smaller frog. A mixed-size group is a feeding accident waiting to happen. Juveniles grow at different rates, so a group that started matched needs checking as it grows.

Setting up a group

  • Space: 24×18×24 in (61×46×61 cm) for two to four frogs of a similar size, against 18×18×24 in for one.
  • Cover: enough planting and cork that each frog can be out of the others' line of sight, and enough high perches that nobody is pushed to the floor.
  • Feeding: forceps feeding lets you see that each frog eats its share, so a dominant eater does not get fat while a shy one thins out. Check every frog against the ridge test on page {{P:bodycondition}}, not just the group.
  • A new frog goes through quarantine before it meets the group, page {{P:arrival}}. If one frog falls ill, it comes out to its own tub until the vet has seen it.

Most frogs for sale are juveniles, and a juvenile usually cannot be sexed: the adult features may not have appeared yet, and the species takes about two years to mature.

What happens if a pair breeds

A male and female kept together can breed. In captivity breeding follows the wet season the frogs get in the wild, and keepers who want eggs trigger it by imitating heavy summer rain. The male grips the female from behind in amplexus, which can last for days, and she lays 150 to 300 eggs in still water. Hatching starts 28 to 36 hours after laying.

The tadpoles are the long part. In good conditions some finish metamorphosis in 2 to 3 weeks, most are out of the water within 2 months, and a few stay tadpoles for up to a year. The froglets then take about two years to reach breeding age. That is hundreds of animals needing homes, food and space.

Company without the project Keep two of the same sex. Because juveniles are hard to sex, that usually means buying adults whose throats show which is which. Two males will call; two females are the quiet option.
