Glossary, A to H

ADVAtadenovirus: a contagious, incurable virus of bearded dragons, worst in young ones, spread by contact and droppings. Stargazing is the sign to know. Pages {{P:arrival}}, {{P:respiratory}}.
Arm-waveA slow, circular roll of one front leg, most likely a sign of submission to a more dominant animal. Page {{P:behavior}}.
Basking surfaceThe platform under the heat lamp where the dragon warms up: 95 to 110°F (35 to 43°C) for an adult, 105 to 115°F (41 to 46°C) for a juvenile, read with an infrared thermometer. Page {{P:temperature}}.
BioactiveA planted enclosure with a living substrate and a cleanup crew of isopods and springtails, tiny invertebrates that break down waste. Page {{P:substrate}}.
BrumationA reptile's natural winter slowdown: less activity and appetite for weeks to months, distinct from illness. Page {{P:brumation}}.
CANVChrysosporium anamorph of Nannizziopsis vriesii: an old umbrella name for the fungi now split into several species, including the one behind yellow fungus. Page {{P:respiratory}}.
Captive-bredHatched from parents kept by people rather than caught in the wild. Every bearded dragon lawfully sold outside Australia is captive-bred. Pages {{P:species}}, {{P:legal}}.
Ceramic heat emitterA screw-in heater that gives off heat with no visible light, so it can run at night. Page {{P:thermostats}}.
CoccidiaSingle-celled gut parasites. Common, and able to make a juvenile seriously ill fast. Found with a fecal test. Page {{P:parasites}}.
D3, vitamin D3The form of vitamin D a dragon makes in its skin under UVB and needs to absorb calcium. It builds up in the body, so more from supplements is not safer. Pages {{P:uvb}}, {{P:supplements}}.
Deep heat projectorA heater like a ceramic emitter that sends its heat down in a more focused beam, with no visible light. Page {{P:thermostats}}.
Dig boxA tub of slightly damp topsoil and play sand, 4 to 6 in (10 to 15 cm) deep, for digging. Page {{P:enrichment}}.
Dimming thermostatA thermostat that lowers the power to a heat bulb instead of switching it off and on. The right kind for halogen and incandescent basking bulbs. Page {{P:thermostats}}.
DysecdysisIncomplete or "stuck" shedding, most often on the toes, the tail tip and around the eyes. Page {{P:shedding}}.
DystociaEgg binding: a female unable to pass her eggs. A same-day emergency. Page {{P:binding}}.
Fatty liver diseaseFat building up in the liver, the long-term risk of an overweight dragon fed like a juvenile. Page {{P:growth}}.
Fecal testA vet's microscope check of a stool sample for parasites. Bring a sample less than 24 hours old in a sealed bag. Pages {{P:arrival}}, {{P:parasites}}.
Femoral poresA row of waxy pores along the underside of the thighs, larger in adult males. Page {{P:eggs}}.
Ferguson ZoneOne of four groups that match a species' natural sun exposure to a UVB strength. Bearded dragons are Zone 3, open or partial-sun baskers. Page {{P:uvb}}.
Glass surfingRepetitive pacing or scratching along the glass, usually a sign something in the setup is wrong. Page {{P:behavior}}.
GoitrogensCompounds in kale, cabbage and broccoli that interfere with the thyroid in large, regular amounts. Fine in rotation, not as a daily staple. Page {{P:greens}}.
GravidCarrying eggs. Females can be gravid without ever meeting a male. Page {{P:eggs}}.
Gut-loadingFeeding feeder insects a calcium-rich diet for 24 to 72 hours before offering them, so the nutrition passes through. Page {{P:insects}}.
Hemipenal bulgesThe two bumps at the base of a male's tail, visible with a flashlight from about 4 to 6 months of age. Page {{P:eggs}}.
HypomelanisticA morph with reduced dark pigment. Like silkback and translucent morphs, it burns more easily under UVB and gets a UVI of 3.0 or lower. Page {{P:species}}.
Reference

Glossary, I to Z

ImpactionA blockage in the digestive tract, often from loose substrate, oversized prey, dehydration, or a basking spot too cool to digest. Page {{P:impaction}}.
Infrared thermometerA handheld gun that reads the temperature of a surface from a distance. The way to check the basking surface. Page {{P:temperature}}.
K (kelvin)The scale for the color of light. A 6500 K lamp gives white, daylight-colored light. Page {{P:uvb}}.
Lay boxA deep box of damp topsoil and sand, 6 to 12 in (15 to 30 cm), where a female digs and lays. Page {{P:eggs}}.
LEDLight-emitting diode: a low-heat, long-life lamp. A daylight LED adds bright light, but no UVB and no basking heat. Page {{P:uvb}}.
MBDMetabolic bone disease: weakened, deformed bones from too little UVB or calcium, the most common serious illness in pet bearded dragons. Page {{P:mbd}}.
MorphA captive-bred line that looks different from the natural dragon in color, pattern or skin. Page {{P:species}}.
Mouth rotInfectious stomatitis: an infection of the gums and mouth that spreads to the jawbone if ignored. Page {{P:parasites}}.
OxalatesCompounds in spinach, chard, beet greens and parsley that bind calcium so it can't be absorbed. Why those foods are rare, not staple. Page {{P:greens}}.
PCRPolymerase chain reaction: a lab test that detects a germ's genetic material from a swab. How a vet tests a dragon for ADV. Page {{P:arrival}}.
PhotoperiodThe number of hours of light per day: 12 to 14 in summer, 10 to 12 in winter, set by a timer. Page {{P:thermostats}}.
ProbeThe thermostat's sensor. At the dragon's level at the basking platform, held with a clip or suction cup, never tape. Page {{P:thermostats}}.
ProlapseTissue pushed out of the vent that doesn't go back in within minutes. Keep it moist and see a vet the same day. Page {{P:impaction}}.
Pulse proportional thermostatA thermostat that sends short pulses of power to hold a steady temperature. Suits a ceramic heat emitter or deep heat projector. Page {{P:thermostats}}.
PVCPolyvinyl chloride: a rigid, waterproof plastic used for enclosure panels. Holds heat better than glass. Pages {{P:enclosure}}, {{P:budget}}.
QuarantineKeeping a new reptile fully apart from others, in a separate room with separate tools, for 3 to 6 months. Page {{P:arrival}}.
SalmonellaBacteria that healthy reptiles carry in the gut and shed in droppings without being ill. Spread hands to mouth. Page {{P:cleaning}}.
StargazingHolding the head tilted up and back for long periods. A neurological sign, most often linked to ADV. Urgent, not wait-and-see. Page {{P:respiratory}}.
T5 HOA fluorescent tube 5/8 inch across (T5), in the high-output (HO) version. The standard UVB source for this species; coil and compact bulbs are not equivalent. Page {{P:uvb}}.
T8An older fluorescent tube 1 inch across, weaker than a T5 HO and replaced every 6 months. Page {{P:uvb}}.
Units°F, degrees Fahrenheit, and °C, degrees Celsius, for temperature; in (inches), ft (feet) and cm (centimeters) for size; g (grams) for weight; % (percent) for humidity and UVB strength.
UrateThe white, paste-like part of reptile waste, made from uric acid instead of liquid urine. Its color and texture are a hydration gauge. Page {{P:stool}}.
UVBUltraviolet B: the part of sunlight a dragon's skin needs to make vitamin D3 and absorb calcium. Ordinary bulbs and window glass give none. Page {{P:uvb}}.
UVIUltraviolet index: a measure of UVB strength at one spot, read with a handheld meter. 4.0 to 4.5 at the basking spot for this species. Page {{P:uvb}}.
VCAVeterinary Centers of America, now VCA Animal Hospitals: an animal hospital chain whose pet care articles are among this package's sources.
Yellow fungusNannizziopsis guarroi (historically grouped as CANV), a contagious, aggressive fungal skin infection. Page {{P:respiratory}}.
