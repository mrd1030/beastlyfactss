Glossary, A to I

ASFAfrican soft-furred rat: a different rodent species from the common feeder rat, milder-smelling and thinner-skinned. Used to restart a stubborn feeder, not as a diet. Page {{P:prey}}.
BrumationA reptile's seasonal slowdown in activity, appetite and metabolism, tied to temperature and daylight. Not true hibernation: the snake can still wake, move and drink. Page {{P:winter}}.
Captive-bredHatched from parents kept by people rather than taken from the wild. The better pet: less likely to bite, refuse food or carry parasites. Page {{P:species}}.
CBSColumbia Broadcasting System: a United States television network whose news service reported the 62-year-old zoo female in this package's sources.
Ceramic heat emitterA screw-in heater that gives off heat with no visible light, so it can run at night. Page {{P:temperature}}.
CITESConvention on International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna and Flora: the treaty that tracks international trade in species that trade could threaten. Ball pythons are listed on its Appendix II. Page {{P:species}}.
CloacaThe single opening under the tail that waste, urates and eggs all leave through. The vent is its outer opening. Page {{P:handling}}.
Cloacal spursSmall claw-like points either side of the vent in boas and pythons. A real sex-linked trait in some populations, but no way to sex a single snake. Page {{P:sexing}}.
Cryptosporidium serpentisA gut parasite that thickens the stomach lining and causes repeated regurgitation. No reliably effective treatment, and can be fatal. Page {{P:parasites}}.
Deep heat projectorA heater like a ceramic emitter that sends its heat down in a more focused beam, with no visible light. Page {{P:temperature}}.
Dimming thermostatA thermostat that turns the power to a heat bulb down instead of switching it off and on. The right kind for halogen and incandescent bulbs. Page {{P:thermostats}}.
DysecdysisIncomplete or "stuck" shedding, where the skin comes off in pieces. Low humidity is the most common single cause, one of six. Page {{P:shedding}}.
Egg bindingAlso called dystocia: eggs a female cannot pass. Weakness or lethargy around laying time is the sign, and it needs a vet promptly. Page {{P:eggs}}.
Eye capThe clear scale over each eye, also called the spectacle, shed with the rest of the skin. A retained cap is easy to miss and is a vet's job to remove. Page {{P:shedding}}.
Fecal testA vet's microscope check of a stool sample for parasites. Bring a fresh sample in a sealed bag. Page {{P:arrival}}.
FollicleA yolk-filled egg in waiting on the ovary. Follicles grow in the breeding season and are normally reabsorbed if not fertilized. Page {{P:eggs}}.
Ferguson ZoneOne of four groups that match a species' natural sun exposure to a UVB strength. Ball pythons are Zone 2, partial-sun and occasional baskers. Page {{P:humidity}}.
Frozen-thawedPrey frozen, then thawed and warmed before feeding. The standard diet, and far safer than live prey. Page {{P:thawing}}.
GravidCarrying eggs. Used for reptiles in place of pregnant. Page {{P:species}}.
Heat pitsOpenings along the upper lip that detect the body warmth of prey, letting a ball python strike accurately in total darkness. Why prey is warmed before feeding. Page {{P:thawing}}.
HemipenesA male snake's paired reproductive organs, held inverted in pockets at the base of the tail. Probing and popping both work by finding them. Page {{P:sexing}}.
Humid hideA closed hide packed with damp sphagnum moss: a humid pocket the snake can enter and leave, kept year round. Page {{P:humidity}}.
HygrometerA humidity gauge. Use a digital one; analog dials are notoriously inaccurate. Page {{P:humidity}}.
IBDInclusion body disease: a fatal virus of boas and pythons, caused by reptarenaviruses, that shows as nerve signs such as stargazing. No cure; quarantine is the only defense. Page {{P:ibd}}.
Infrared temperature gunA handheld tool that reads the temperature of a surface from a distance. How you check what the snake actually lies on. Page {{P:temperature}}.
Reference

Glossary, L to Z

LEDLight-emitting diode: a low-heat, long-life lamp. Used on a timer for the 12-hour day. Page {{P:humidity}}.
MorphA selectively bred line with a different color or pattern. Some genes carry a health cost, such as the spider morph's wobble. Page {{P:species}}.
Mouth rotInfectious stomatitis: an infection of the gums and mouth, usually riding on stress or poor husbandry. Needs a vet. Page {{P:mites}}.
MuskingA foul-smelling release from the cloaca. A clear sign the snake feels seriously threatened and the session is over. Page {{P:handling}}.
NematodesRoundworms. Some turn up often in snake stool and range from minor to a cause of gut blockage or pneumonia. Page {{P:parasites}}.
On/off thermostatA thermostat that cuts power fully at the set point and restores it below. Suits an under-tank mat. Page {{P:thermostats}}.
PCRPolymerase chain reaction: a lab test that detects a germ's genetic material. Used with a biopsy to diagnose IBD. Page {{P:ibd}}.
PLOSPublic Library of Science: the publisher of PLOS ONE, the journal several of this package's research sources appeared in.
ProbeThe thermostat's sensor. On the floor of the warm hide, held with a clip or suction cup, never tape. Page {{P:thermostats}}.
Probing and poppingThe two hands-on ways to sex a snake. Both are for a vet or an experienced breeder; done wrong, either can injure the snake. Page {{P:sexing}}.
ProlapseInternal tissue pushed out through the vent. Keep it moist, never push it back, and see a vet at once. Page {{P:ibd}}.
ProtozoaSingle-celled creatures. Most found in reptile stool are harmless; a few, such as Entamoeba invadens, cause serious illness. Page {{P:parasites}}.
Pulse proportional thermostatA thermostat that sends short pulses of power to hold a steady temperature. Suits a ceramic heat emitter. Page {{P:thermostats}}.
PVCPolyvinyl chloride: a rigid, waterproof plastic used for enclosure panels. Holds humidity far better than glass. Pages {{P:enclosure}}, {{P:budget}}.
QuarantineKeeping a new snake fully apart from other reptiles, in a separate room with separate tools, for 3 to 6 months. Page {{P:arrival}}.
RanchingCollecting eggs and gravid females from the wild, raising the hatchlings briefly and exporting them. How most wild-sourced ball pythons now reach the trade. Page {{P:species}}.
RegurgitationA meal eaten and then brought back up. Stop feeding for a cycle and call the vet. Page {{P:parasites}}.
SalmonellaBacteria that healthy reptiles carry in the gut and shed in droppings without being ill. Spread from hands to mouth. Page {{P:cleaning}}.
Scale rotInfectious dermatitis of the belly scales, driven by damp, dirty substrate. Page {{P:respiratory}}.
StargazingThe head held up and back at an odd angle, as if staring at the sky. A nerve sign, the mark of IBD, and an emergency. Page {{P:ibd}}.
Units°F, degrees Fahrenheit, and °C, degrees Celsius, for temperature; in (inches), ft (feet) and cm (centimeters) for size; g (grams) for weight; % (percent) for humidity.
UrateThe white, chalky part of reptile waste, made from uric acid instead of liquid urine. Its color and texture are a hydration gauge. Page {{P:stool}}.
UVBUltraviolet B: the part of sunlight many reptiles use to make vitamin D3. Optional for ball pythons, which have been kept for decades without it. Page {{P:humidity}}.
UVIUltraviolet index: a measure of UVB strength at one spot. 0.7 to 1.0 across the gradient and 1.1 to 3.0 at the closest point, if you add UVB. Page {{P:humidity}}.
VCAVeterinary Centers of America, now VCA Animal Hospitals: an animal hospital chain whose pet care articles are among this package's sources.
Wobble syndromeA heritable nerve disorder linked to the spider morph: head wobbling, poor coordination, and trouble striking or constricting prey. Page {{P:species}}.
