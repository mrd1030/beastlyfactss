Glossary, A to M

21 CFR 1240.62Title 21 of the Code of Federal Regulations (CFR), section 1240.62: the federal four-inch rule. It restricts selling turtles under 4 in of shell, not owning them, and exempts private keepers. Page {{P:legal2}}.
AbscessA pocket of infection. In a tortoise it is a hard lump of thick, dry pus, classically at the ear behind the eye or in the mouth. A vet case. Page {{P:shell}}.
BrumationA reptile's winter slowdown into near-dormancy. Optional indoors for this species, held at 35 to 50°F (2 to 10°C) for no more than 10 weeks in a small tortoise and 14 in a large one. Page {{P:brumation}}.
Captive-bredHatched from parents kept by people rather than taken from the wild. The better buy: wild-caught tortoises arrive stressed and carrying parasites. Page {{P:species}}.
CarotenoidsOrange and yellow plant pigments a tortoise turns into vitamin A, found in dark leafy greens, weeds, squash, carrot and sweet potato. Page {{P:diet}}.
Ceramic heaterA screw-in heat source that gives off no light, so it can run at night when a room will not hold 60°F (16°C). Page {{P:temperature}}.
CITESThe Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species, the treaty that controls moving listed animals across borders. This species is on its Appendix II, traded on export quotas. Page {{P:legal2}}.
Cloaca, cloacolithThe cloaca is the single opening that droppings, urates and eggs leave by. A cloacolith is a stone of urates lodged in it: straining with noise, and an emergency. Page {{P:vitamina}}.
CFR, CMRThe Code of Federal Regulations (CFR), the official book of United States federal rules, and the Code of Massachusetts Regulations (CMR), that state's equivalent. Pages {{P:legal}}, {{P:legal2}}.
Dimming thermostatA controller that lowers the power to a heat bulb instead of switching it off and on. The right kind for a halogen basking bulb. Page {{P:equipment}}.
DystociaEgg binding: a female unable to pass her eggs. Told apart from normal laying by whether she stays bright and alert. Page {{P:binding}}.
FDAThe Food and Drug Administration, the United States agency behind the four-inch rule. Page {{P:legal2}}.
Fecal testA vet's microscope check of a fresh stool sample for parasites. On arrival, symptoms or not, and at every yearly exam. Page {{P:parasites}}.
HerpesvirusTortoise herpesvirus: contagious, incurable, carried for life by survivors, and able to hide in a healthy-looking tortoise for months. The reason for a six-month tested quarantine. Page {{P:respiratory}}.
Hexamita parvaA single-celled flagellate parasite of the kidneys and urinary tract that notably affects this genus. Watery, jelly-like urine is the sign. Page {{P:parasites}}.
Infrared temperature gunA handheld thermometer that reads a surface from a distance. The way to check the basking spot. Page {{P:temperature}}.
IUCNThe International Union for Conservation of Nature, which keeps the Red List of threatened species. It assesses this tortoise as Vulnerable. Page {{P:profile}}.
K (kelvin)The scale for the color of light. A 6500 K lamp gives white daylight. Page {{P:uvb}}.
MBDMetabolic bone disease: a soft, deformed shell and weak limbs from too little UVB or calcium. Among the commonest conditions in pet tortoises. Page {{P:mbd}}.
Moist hideA hide lined with damp peat moss on the cool side, giving a humid retreat in a dry enclosure. Page {{P:substrate}}.
MycoplasmaA bacterium behind upper respiratory disease in tortoises, found even in tortoises with no symptoms. Page {{P:respiratory}}.
Reference

Glossary, N to Z

OxalateA compound in spinach, chard and beet greens that binds calcium in the gut and can promote bladder stones. Why those greens stay off the rotation. Page {{P:never}}.
PCRPolymerase chain reaction: a lab test that finds a germ's genetic material in a swab, used to screen a quarantined tortoise for herpesvirus. Page {{P:arrival}}.
PlastronThe lower shell. Concave in an adult male, flat in a female. Page {{P:eggs}}.
ProbeThe thermostat's sensor, placed where the tortoise basks and held with a probe clip or suction cup, never tape. Page {{P:equipment}}.
ProtozoaTiny single-celled life forms. Some are gut parasites common in tortoises, and Hexamita is one that attacks the kidneys. Page {{P:parasites}}.
Pulse proportional thermostatA controller that sends short pulses of power to hold a steady temperature. Suits a ceramic heater. Page {{P:equipment}}.
PyramidingScutes that rise into raised cones as the shell grows. Not completely understood, linked to temperature, humidity and diet during growth, and permanent. Page {{P:mbd}}.
QuarantineKeeping a new tortoise fully apart, in a separate room with its own equipment, for at least six months with vet testing. Page {{P:arrival}}.
SalmonellaBacteria that healthy reptiles carry in the gut and shed in droppings without being ill. Spread hands to mouth. Page {{P:handling}}.
ScuteOne of the plates covering the shell. New growth appears at each scute's edge. Page {{P:growth}}.
SerologyA blood test for antibodies, the body's response to an infection. Repeated weeks apart in quarantine, because antibodies take time to appear. Page {{P:arrival}}.
Shell rotA bacterial or fungal shell infection: soft, discolored or foul-smelling patches, often worse in a damp enclosure. Page {{P:shell}}.
T5 HOA fluorescent tube five-eighths of an inch across (T5) in its high-output (HO) version. The UVB source for this species; coil and compact bulbs are not equivalent. Page {{P:uvb}}.
Tortoise tableAn open-topped enclosure with solid walls, preferred over a glass aquarium for ventilation and because the tortoise cannot see through it. Page {{P:enclosure}}.
Units°F, degrees Fahrenheit, and °C, degrees Celsius, for temperature; in (inches), ft (feet), sq ft (square feet), m (meters), cm (centimeters) and mm (millimeters) for size; lb (pounds) and g (grams) for weight; min (minutes); % (percent) for humidity, UVB tube strength and weight loss.
UratesThe white to cream paste a reptile passes instead of liquid urine. Smooth and creamy means hydrated; gritty or yellow means dry. Page {{P:stool}}.
UVBUltraviolet B: the part of sunlight a tortoise needs on its skin to make vitamin D3 and absorb calcium. Window glass blocks it, and tubes lose it with age. Page {{P:uvb}}.
UVIUltraviolet index: the strength of the UVB at one spot, read with a handheld meter. 3.0 to 4.0 at the basking area for this species. Page {{P:uvb}}.
VCAVeterinary Centers of America, now VCA Animal Hospitals: an animal hospital chain whose pet care articles are among this package's sources.
Vitamin A deficiencyA shortage from an iceberg lettuce or poor commercial diet: swollen eyelids, ear abscesses, respiratory infection. Page {{P:vitamina}}.
Vitamin D3The vitamin a tortoise makes in its skin under UVB and needs to absorb calcium. It builds up in the body, so a supplement is only given when a vet asks. Page {{P:supplements}}.
