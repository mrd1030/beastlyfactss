Power outages, heat waves, travel & transport

A tortoise depends on an outlet for the one thing it cannot make: body heat.

When the power goes out

A healthy adult tolerates one cool night, and this species tolerates 60°F (16°C). The risk is duration: a day or two below 60°F outside brumation leaves a reptile open to infection and off its food.

Do

  • Keep a battery or wind-up thermometer in the enclosure.
  • Drape thick towels over the table, leaving a vent gap.
  • Move the tortoise in a ventilated tub to the warmest room.
  • A wrapped hot water bottle or hand warmer beside it, never touching it.
  • Offer water and a few greens only, until heat is reliably back.
  • Past a day or two below 60°F, board it with a vet or a friend with power.

Don't

  • No candles, gas stoves or open flame near the enclosure.
  • No heat pack against the skin: a cold tortoise cannot move away in time.
  • No hot water for a cold, unresponsive tortoise. Lukewarm only, once it is alert.
Outage kit Hand warmers or a hot water bottle, two towels, a battery thermometer, a ventilated tub, vet numbers on paper.

In a heat wave

Read the enclosure and any pen on the hottest afternoon, keep the table out of direct sun, and make outdoor shade fixed. A tortoise drooling or frothing goes into shade at once and to a vet the same day.

Transport and vet trips

A ventilated tub with a towel to grip, in a dark bag, no loose substrate or water dish. Warm the car first; below 65°F (18°C) on a trip over 30 minutes, a wrapped hand warmer beside the tub. Check it every 20 to 30 minutes at a stop, and never leave it in a parked car. On a move, the new enclosure runs before the tortoise arrives.

The forty-year version of this page A tortoise outlives plans. Name a guardian who has agreed, and a second behind them, and contact a tortoise rescue in advance. A pet cannot inherit money, since the law treats it as property, but a pet trust can fund its care, and all 50 states and the District of Columbia have pet trust laws: a question for an estate attorney. Leave a care file with the animal, and rehome through a rescue, never a free listing.
