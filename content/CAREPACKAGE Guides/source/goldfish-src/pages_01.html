How to use this package

Three ways to get value from it, depending on where you are right now.

Units used throughout Every temperature and size is given twice. Temperatures appear in degrees Fahrenheit (°F) with degrees Celsius (°C) beside them. Lengths appear in inches (in) and feet (ft) with centimeters (cm) beside them, and the smallest in millimeters (mm). Weight is in pounds (lb). Tank volume appears in gallons (gal) with liters (L) beside it, small doses in milliliters (ml), and filter flow in gallons per hour (gph). Water test results are written in ppm (parts per million) or mg/L (milligrams per liter), two ways of writing the same strength of a dissolved substance in water. Prices are in United States dollars.

If you haven't set up yet

Read pages {{P:tank}} to {{P:substrate}} in order before buying anything. The tank and the filter shape the next decade, and both are cheaper to get right the first time. Buy the tank before the fish, and plan on four to six weeks of fishless cycling (growing the bacteria that process waste) before a goldfish goes in. Then use the budget and shopping list on pages {{P:budget}} to {{P:budget2}} to buy once.

If your goldfish is already set up

Audit what you have against the Setup Checklist on page {{P:checklist}}, and go to the Red Flags page ({{P:redflags}}) if anything seems off. Test the water first. Most goldfish problems that look like disease are a water reading.

If you already have a fish in an uncycled tank

This is the most common situation, and it is fixable. Go to the fish-in cycle on page {{P:cycling}}. Do not tear the tank down or start dosing treatments: daily testing and water changes carry you through it.

Cost overview

Initial setup

$310 to $615 for the equipment, quarantine tank and first food, plus under $10 for the fish itself, more for a fancy variety, so about $310 to $625 all in. Every line is on page {{P:budget}}.

Monthly ongoing

$10 to $30 a month to top up pellets, occasional gel or frozen food, and dechlorinator and other water treatments.

The cheapest animal in the store, one of the costliest fish to house The fish costs next to nothing. The money is in the tank and the filter, and the expensive mistake is buying the cheap bowl first and the correct tank six months later, alongside a sick fish.

Fun facts worth knowing

  • Goldfish form associative memories that last months and can be trained to push levers for food and navigate mazes, which makes the three-second memory line backwards.
  • A goldfish has no true stomach, so food passes along the gut: small portions suit it, and one large meal does not.
  • Goldfish and koi can be crossed, mostly by artificial spawning, and the first-generation hybrid males examined were sterile.

The goldfish, varieties, size & lifespan

What you are buying, how big it gets, and how long it stays.

One species, a thousand years of breeding

Every pet goldfish is Carassius auratus, bred in China from a wild carp relative for color mutations noted as early as the Jin dynasty (266 to 420). Moving the fish indoors in the Ming dynasty let breeders keep fancy-tailed mutations too fragile for an outdoor pond. The result is dozens of varieties, from the streamlined common and comet to the round-bodied oranda and ranchu.

Size: it does not fit the tank

Goldfish do not stay small to match their container. A small tank stunts them: poor water, chronic ammonia and cramped space suppress outward growth while the organs and skeleton keep developing, linked to shortened lives and organ failure. In outdoor ponds with excellent water, goldfish regularly reach 12 in (30 cm) or more. The small goldfish most people picture is a stunted one.

Lifespan

Ten to 15 years is normal in a well-kept tank and 20 to 30 or more in a spacious pond. The oldest on record, a male named Tish, lived from 1956 to 1999, 43 years. That makes a goldfish a decade-long commitment.

Company

Goldfish are social and generally do better with their own kind than alone. Keep fancy with fancy and single-tail with single-tail, so that body shape and swim speed match and competition at feeding time stays low. A tropical tank is the wrong home for it: a goldfish needs cool water, and guppies and anything slower at the food are the pairings that fail.

Goldfish or koi? They are different species. A koi has two pairs of whisker-like barbels at the corners of its mouth, and a goldfish has none. A goldfish can carry koi herpesvirus without showing signs and pass it to koi, so a goldfish added to a koi pond is quarantined like a new koi.
