First 30 days

Before day 1

Tank cycled and confirmed: a full ammonia dose reads zero within 24 hours, nitrite zero, nitrate present
Quarantine tank ready: bare, with a sponge filter seeded from a mature filter, and an air pump
Fish vet found and written on the emergency card ({{P:emergency}})

Day 1

Float the sealed bag 20 to 30 minutes, then net the fish across and discard the bag water
Match the temperature first; go slower if the bag and tank differ by more than 10°F (6°C)
The newcomer goes into quarantine, not the display tank

Week 1

Check daily for clamped fins, spots, labored gills, flashing, appetite loss and cloudy eyes
Feed normally, once a day for an adult, and pull out whatever is left after a couple of minutes
Test ammonia and nitrite in the quarantine tank, whose filter is small and new

Weeks 2 to 3

Weekly 25 to 50% change in the quarantine tank and the display, testing first
Start the Owner Log with the weekly readings ({{P:ownerlog}})
Rinse mechanical media in removed tank water only

Week 4 and beyond

Day 30 is the quarantine floor, not the target: a fish you especially do not want to lose stays 30 to 60 days
Review the log. Nitrate under 20 ppm before each change means the schedule fits; a drifting nitrate means feed less, change more, or stock fewer fish
Before the fish joins an established tank, confirm it has shown no new signs and the quarantine tank is clear

Daily, weekly & seasonal routine

Daily

Feed the measured portion and pull out what is left after a couple of minutes
Check the filter is running and the surface is moving
Look at every fish: fins open, breathing steady, no spots, all accounted for

Weekly

Test ammonia, nitrite, nitrate and pH on the same day, and log them
Change 25 to 50 percent of the water, siphoning the substrate
Check the temperature

Monthly

Rinse mechanical media in removed tank water, never under the tap
Replace carbon if you use it, and only part of the biological media at a time
Review the log for slow trends, not just single readings

Seasonal

Summer: watch for the water climbing past 75°F (24°C) and add surface agitation
Pond only: scale feeding down with temperature and stop below 39°F (4°C)
Spring: expect chasing and a dip in appetite in a mature mixed group
Nitrate sets the size of the change Under 20 ppm before the change, keep the schedule. 20 to 40 ppm, change 40 to 50 percent weekly or add a midweek change, and feed less. Over 40 ppm, change 50 percent now and retest in two days.
Check every fish, every day A change in behavior shows up before a mark does. A fish that is hiding, isolating or resting somewhere unusual is worth a closer look the same day.

Power outages, heat waves, travel & transport

For a fish, a power outage is an oxygen problem first and a temperature problem second.

When the pumps stop, the surface stops moving and gas exchange stops with it. Dissolved oxygen starts falling within a couple of hours, sooner in a heavily stocked or warm tank. A goldfish tolerates cold better than a tropical fish, but goldfish are often kept at high stocking densities, so the oxygen math matters just as much.

Do

  • Aerate right away. A battery-powered air pump and air stone is the cleanest fix.
  • With no pump, scoop water out with a cup or pitcher and pour it back from a few inches up for a minute or two. Repeat roughly every 10 to 15 minutes in a small tank and every 20 to 30 minutes in a larger one.
  • Unplug the filter deliberately, so it does not restart on its own.

Don't

  • Don't feed until the filter is back and stable. Uneaten food consumes oxygen.
  • Don't restart a filter that sat off for hours without rinsing the media in old tank water or at least looking inside.
  • Don't add a space heater, heat lamp or open flame near the tank.

Transport and vet trips

  • A bag beats a rigid bucket for most short trips: it holds the fish in the dark, which is calming, and flexes instead of sloshing. Use a tub or bucket with a tight lid for a longer trip.
  • Leave real headspace above the water. For a long trip, a battery pump or pure oxygen is standard. Keep it out of direct sun.
  • Move the fish last, once the new tank's water is dechlorinated and close to the old temperature, and get it out of the bag soon.
  • Pack the filter media in a sealed bag or tub of the tank's own water, never tap water, so the colony survives.
