Section 02 · Housing & Environment

Cleaning & hygiene

Three cleaning clocks for the enclosure, and the hand-washing habits that protect the people in the house.

The cleaning routine

WhenWhat
DailyRemove droppings, urates (the white, chalky part of a reptile's droppings), uneaten food and shed skin as soon as you see them. Empty, wash and refill the water dish with fresh dechlorinated water
WeeklyReplace soiled loose substrate in sections, and wipe down glass, walls and decor with a reptile-safe cleaner
MonthlyMove the gecko to a secure temporary tub. Take everything out, scrub it with detergent and hot water, disinfect the enclosure, decor and dishes, rinse, dry, and refill with new substrate

A bioactive enclosure is the exception: it skips the monthly strip-down, because the living substrate is the point. Pick up the droppings and food you can see, and let the cleanup crew handle the rest.

Disinfecting, properly

Cleaning removes the dirt; disinfecting kills what is left, and it only works on a surface that has already been scrubbed. Household bleach at a 1:32 dilution, half a cup of bleach per gallon of water, is cheap and effective. It needs 10 minutes of wet contact, so keep the surface wet for the full time rather than wiping it straight off. Then rinse with plenty of clean water until no bleach smell remains, and let everything dry and air out completely, a full day, before the gecko goes back in.

Salmonella: the germ healthy geckos carry

Salmonella is a bacterium that causes diarrhea, fever and stomach cramps in people. Healthy-looking reptiles carry it in the gut and shed it in their droppings with no sign of illness, on and off, so one negative test clears nothing. It is not only a turtle problem: a United States public health investigation into infections linked to pet geckos, closed in January 2026, counted 113 cases and 31 hospitalizations across 36 states. It spreads from hands and surfaces to mouth, not by bites.

The ruleIn practice
Wash after every contactSoap and running water after touching the gecko, its enclosure, its water, or anything that touched either
Keep it out of the kitchenNever on a counter or anywhere food is made or eaten
Never wash gear in a sink or tub people useNot the kitchen sink, the bathroom sink or the bathtub. Use a plastic tub kept only for the gecko; if a bathtub has to be used, clean it thoroughly afterward
No kissingAnd no holding the gecko close to your face
Children under 5Should not handle the gecko or touch its enclosure at all. Older children are supervised and wash every time
Who is at higher risk Children under 5, adults over 65, pregnant people and anyone with a weakened immune system. If anyone in the house gets diarrhea, fever and cramps, mention the gecko to the doctor, even if nobody remembers touching it.
Section 03 · Feeding

Diet & feeding by age

A powder for the base, and live insects that this gecko actually needs.

Gargoyle geckos look like their crested gecko cousins at the food dish, but they are more carnivorous, and that changes what a complete diet means. The base is a complete powdered gecko diet, a commercial powder mixed with water. On top of that, live insects are a required part of the diet, not optional enrichment as they are for a crested gecko. Wild gargoyles have been documented eating other lizards, young crested geckos included, alongside insects.

StageComplete diet powderLive insects
Hatchling and juvenile, 0 to 12 monthsFresh, daily1 to 2 times a week
Adult, from 12 to 18 months, at about 40 gFresh, every 2 to 3 days1 to 2 times a week

Weight on page {{P:weight}} corrects the schedule either way: a gecko gaining too fast gets insects less often, one losing gets them more.

How a feeding works

  • Evening. The gecko is awake and moving after dark, around the time of the main misting.
  • High up. Powder goes in a small cup or on a feeding ledge near the top. This is a climbing species, and many will not come down to a floor dish.
  • Insects released into the planting, so they have to be hunted. That is enrichment as well as food.
  • Clear up. Pull uneaten insects a few hours later, and take out made-up powder before it spoils.
  • Gravid females, carrying eggs, may stop eating for a week or more before laying. Page {{P:eggs}}.

Water

Most drinking is droplets licked off leaves after the evening mist, with a shallow dish of fresh dechlorinated water as a backup, refilled daily. Water is the time-critical one: no more than 2 to 3 days without water access, whatever the gecko's appetite. Food runs on a much longer clock, page {{P:avoid}}.

Weigh, don't guess A gram scale is the most useful feeding tool you can own for this species. Regular weighing is the most objective way to catch a real problem early, and a thinning tail, where this gecko stores fat, is the visible early sign of weight loss. Log the weight on page {{P:ownerlog}}.

Complete diet powder: mixing & rotation

The base of the diet. Get this right and the rest of the food pages are additions.
FoodTierNotes
Complete powdered gecko diet, the crested gecko formulasStapleA meal replacement mixed with water, carrying protein, fruit, calcium and vitamin D3 in one product
Three or more flavors or brands, rotatedStapleRotate 3 or more for variety and to head off a picky eater
Fruit puree alongside the powderOccasionalCalcium-rich, low-phosphorus fruits only, offered beside the powder, never instead of it.
Processed or sugary human foodNeverThe wrong nutrient profile for a gecko

Mixing and serving

  • Roughly 2 to 3 parts water to 1 part powder, stirred to a smooth, ketchup-like consistency.
  • Serve in a small cup or on a feeding ledge mounted high, where a climbing gecko feeds.
  • Mix fresh for each feeding. Dry powder keeps; made-up diet does not.
  • Remove uneaten made-up diet within 24 to 48 hours so it cannot go moldy.
  • Nothing gets stirred into the powder. It is already complete, calcium and vitamin D3 included, so supplements go on the insects instead.

How long a bag lasts

One 8 oz (227 g) bag of powdered diet lasts a single adult about two months and runs $20 to $30, so plan on six bags a year. With three flavors in rotation, buy smaller amounts of each, keep them sealed and dry, and note the opening date on the bag.

When a gecko leaves its powder

Pickiness is the first thing to rule out: rotate to a different flavor or brand before anything else. Look at the husbandry next, because a warm side that runs too cool slows digestion and drops appetite. Shedding, the breeding season and a recent move all cause normal dips. Only then treat it as a health question, using the thresholds on page {{P:avoid}}.

Powder first, insects second, both always A crested gecko keeper is told insects are an optional extra. For a gargoyle they are not, but they are still the second half of the diet, not the base. A gecko that fills up on insects and leaves its powder is missing the part formulated to be complete. Keep insects to the weekly schedule.

Gut-loading: the insect's last meal

Feeder insects are poor calcium sources, and the problem is the ratio: an unsupplemented cricket carries about 1 part calcium to 9 parts phosphorus, and phosphorus competes with calcium for absorption. Gut-loading means feeding the insects a high-calcium diet for 24 to 48 hours before they go to the gecko, so the calcium in their gut goes along with them. Use a commercial gut-load sold on its calcium content, or calcium-rich greens. Carrot and potato keep insects alive but do nothing for the ratio.

Dusting: two jars, used differently

SupplementHow
Plain calcium, without vitamin D3On most insect feedings. This is for the insects, which are calcium-poor
Calcium with vitamin D3Periodically, not at every feeding. The powder diet already supplies D3, and D3 builds up in the body
Into the powderNothing. The complete diet is balanced as sold

Dust just before feeding: shake the insects in a cup or bag with a pinch of powder until they look lightly floured, not caked. An insect left in a dusted container grooms most of it off. If the gecko is under treatment for bone disease, the vet sets the supplement routine.

Why gut-load and dust both Gut-loading improves what is inside the insect; dusting adds calcium to the outside. Neither replaces the other, and an insect that is neither is mostly phosphorus with legs.

Section 03 · Feeding

Weight, growth & body condition

Track this gecko in grams. Weight moves before anything else looks wrong.

A gram scale and a small lidded tub are the whole kit. Weigh at the same time of day, before a feeding, and write it on the owner log on page {{P:ownerlog}}. A small amount of natural up and down between weigh-ins is not alarming; a steady slide over several is.

Growth reference

StageWeightWhat changes
HatchlingUnder about 12 gA small grow-out enclosure, paper towel, powder daily. Too fragile and jumpy for routine handling
Growing juvenile18 to 25 gThe point where a male's bulge appears and sex can be confirmed. Page {{P:sexing}}
Reaching maturityAbout 35 to 40 gAround 12 to 18 months. A female is mature at around 40 g
AdultAbout 45 to 65 gFull size, 7 to 9 in (18 to 23 cm) including the tail, depending on sex and genetics. Adult feeding schedule

Maturity comes at around 35 to 40 g, and a fully grown adult runs 45 to 65 g. The spread is the real answer, not a target.

Body condition at a glance

Losing condition

A thinning tail first, because this gecko stores fat there. Then weight falling across several weigh-ins. Check that the powder is actually eaten, then the warm side, then parasites (page {{P:minor}}).

Holding steady

A full tail if it has one, weight stable in an adult or climbing steadily in a juvenile, and a firm grip.

Gaining too much

Usually too many fatty insects or pinky mice. Obesity in reptiles comes from too little exercise and too many calories, and can lead to fatty liver disease. Cut the treats, not the powder.

The calcium sacs

Geckos store calcium in a pair of small sacs. In this species they sit on the roof of the mouth, at the back of the palate, so they are something a vet checks rather than something you can watch from outside. Healthy sacs look plump and white; shrinking sacs are an early warning of a calcium problem, page {{P:mbd}}.

When a weight change is a vet visit Any steady loss across three or four weigh-ins, a juvenile that stops gaining, or a female still losing after she has laid. Bring the owner log: a trend line tells a vet far more than one number.
Section 04 · Handling & Behavior

Handling, the bite & the tail

Calm and slow as an adult. Skittish as a juvenile. Judge the species by the grown animal.

Adult gargoyle geckos are docile and slow-moving, one of the calmer climbing geckos to handle. Young ones are a different animal: skittish, jumpy, and quick to leap at the slightest disturbance. Adults settle considerably as they mature, so do not judge the species by a nervous juvenile.

Getting started

StepHow
WaitAbout two weeks after it comes home before any handling
SessionsStart at 5 minutes every other day, work up to about 15 minutes, and keep total daily handling near 20 minutes even once it is tame
WhereLow, over a bed, couch or close to the floor. A jumpy gecko leaps when startled, and a fall from height is a real injury risk
HowLet it walk onto your hand rather than grabbing it from a hide. Treadmilling, moving one hand in front of the other as it walks, keeps a jumpy one calm
WhenDuring the day, when it is naturally sleepier, tends to go more smoothly than an evening session
AfterBack into the enclosure, then wash your hands with soap and running water, page {{P:cleaning}}

Two defenses worth understanding

The bite. Rare, and almost always provoked. Gargoyle geckos have noticeably larger teeth than crested geckos, so an adult bite can draw blood where a crested gecko's usually cannot break skin. It is still a minor wound: wash it with soap and water.

The tail. Like most geckos, a gargoyle drops its tail if grabbed by it, badly startled or restrained roughly. This is called autotomy, a deliberate break at a built-in weak point. Never grab or hold the tail. Unlike a crested gecko, a gargoyle grows it back. The new tail is prehensile (able to grip), with cartilage rather than bone inside and somewhat uneven scales, and it works. A 2024 genome study confirmed the difference: crested geckos have lost the ability to regrow a tail, gargoyles have not.

Handling is not enrichment For a gecko that drops its tail when stressed, handling is something it tolerates. A gecko that leaps away, thrashes or bites every session is asking for shorter sessions or a break, and a new one gets none at all until it has settled and is eating.
