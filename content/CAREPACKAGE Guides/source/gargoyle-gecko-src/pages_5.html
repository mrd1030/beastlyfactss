Glossary, A to H

ArborealLiving off the ground, climbing. Why the enclosure is measured in height. Page {{P:enclosure}}.
ARAVThe Association of Reptile and Amphibian Veterinarians, which runs a find-a-vet directory. Page {{P:redflags}}.
AutotomyDropping the tail on purpose at a built-in weak point, as a defense. A gargoyle's tail grows back. Page {{P:fts}}.
BioactiveA living, planted substrate over a drainage layer, with a cleanup crew that breaks down waste. Page {{P:substrate}}.
Black soldier fly larvaeA feeder insect whose hard outer shell carries real calcium, unlike most feeders. Page {{P:insects}}.
BrumationA true winter dormancy some reptiles go through. Gargoyles show only a lighter seasonal slowdown. Page {{P:behavior}}.
Calcium sacsA gecko's calcium stores, described in this species at the back of the roof of the mouth. Shrinking sacs warn of a calcium problem. Page {{P:weight}}.
CDCThe Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, the US public health agency. Page {{P:cleaning}}.
Ceramic heat emitterA bulb-shaped heater that gives off heat and no light. Always on a thermostat. Page {{P:temperature}}.
CloacaThe shared chamber behind the vent that droppings, urates and eggs pass through. Page {{P:minor}}.
Complete powdered dietA commercial powder mixed with water that carries protein, fruit, calcium and vitamin D3 in one food. Page {{P:powder}}.
CryptosporidiosisA protozoal gut infection that causes wasting and has no reliably effective treatment. Page {{P:minor}}.
Dechlorinated waterTap water treated to remove its chlorine. Page {{P:humidity}}.
DysecdysisA bad or incomplete shed: old skin left behind, often on toes and the tail tip. Page {{P:shed}}.
DystociaEgg binding: a female unable to pass her eggs. Page {{P:eggs}}.
EcdysisShedding: how a reptile replaces its outer skin. Page {{P:behavior}}.
EctothermicRunning at the temperature of its surroundings, and warming or cooling by moving. Why a gradient matters. Page {{P:temperature}}.
ExoskeletonThe hard outer shell of an insect. Page {{P:insects}}.
Fecal examA microscope check of a fresh dropping for parasites. Page {{P:arrival}}.
Femoral poresA row of small pores in front of the vent, used by some breeders as an early, unreliable sexing clue. Page {{P:sexing}}.
Floppy tail syndrome (FTS)A tail that bends over the back, from sleeping upside down on bare glass. It can deform the hips. Page {{P:fts}}.
GoutUric acid crystals building up in joints or organs, linked to dehydration and too much protein. Page {{P:heat}}.
GravidCarrying eggs. A lone female can be gravid. Page {{P:eggs}}.
Gut-loadingFeeding insects a high-calcium diet for 24 to 48 hours before they go to the gecko. Page {{P:insects}}.
HemipenesThe paired male reproductive organs, which make the bulge behind a male's vent. Page {{P:sexing}}.
HygrometerA humidity meter. Read it an hour after the evening mist and the next afternoon. Page {{P:humidity}}.
Reference

Glossary, I to Z

ImpactionA gut blockage, linked to swallowing sand, wood chips or gravel with food. Page {{P:minor}}.
IUCNThe International Union for Conservation of Nature, whose Red List rates this species Least Concern. Page {{P:profile}}.
Jeweler's loupeA small magnifier, used by some breeders to look for femoral pores. Page {{P:sexing}}.
Metabolic bone disease (MBD)Soft, bending bones from too little usable calcium, vitamin D3 or UVB; formally nutritional secondary hyperparathyroidism. Page {{P:mbd}}.
NocturnalActive at night. Why misting, feeding and the daily health check happen in the evening. Page {{P:enrichment}}.
OviductThe egg tube in a female, one of the tissues that can prolapse. Page {{P:minor}}.
OxalatesPlant compounds that bind calcium, the reason starfruit and rhubarb are never fed. Page {{P:avoid}}.
PersinA plant toxin in avocado. Page {{P:avoid}}.
PrehensileAble to grip. A gargoyle's regrown tail is prehensile. Page {{P:handling}}.
ProlapseTissue pushed out through the vent that does not go back in. A same-day vet call. Page {{P:minor}}.
ProtozoaSingle-celled parasites, a common cause of loose droppings. Page {{P:minor}}.
QuarantineKeeping a new gecko in a separate room for 3 to 6 months before it shares air or tools with another reptile. Page {{P:arrival}}.
ReticulatedNet-patterned: the gray, black and white wild-type look, and the cheapest. Page {{P:arrival}}.
SalmonellaA bacterium healthy reptiles carry without illness, which causes diarrhea, fever and cramps in people. Page {{P:cleaning}}.
Springtails and isopodsTiny soil animals and woodlice used as a bioactive cleanup crew. Page {{P:substrate}}.
StomatitisMouth rot, a bacterial infection of the mouth and gums. Page {{P:minor}}.
SubadultA nearly grown juvenile. Page {{P:diet}}.
T5 HOA slim fluorescent tube, 5/8 inch across, in the high output (HO) format; the usual UVB source. A low rating keeps it gentle. Page {{P:uvb}}.
ThermostatA controller that switches or dims the heat source to hold a set temperature, so it cannot overheat the enclosure. Page {{P:temperature}}.
TreadmillingLetting a gecko walk from one hand onto the other placed in front of it, over and over. Page {{P:handling}}.
UratesThe white, chalky paste in a reptile's droppings: uric acid, the reptile version of urine. Yellow or gritty urates mean dehydration. Page {{P:heat}}.
UV index (UVI)The standard scale for UVB strength at a point. About 1.0 to 2.0 at this gecko's basking area. Page {{P:uvb}}.
UVBUltraviolet B, the band of ultraviolet light that lets a gecko make vitamin D3. Optional for this species, and worth having. Page {{P:uvb}}.
VentThe single rear opening that droppings, urates and eggs pass through. Page {{P:sexing}}.
Vitamin D3The vitamin a gecko needs to absorb and use calcium. It builds up in the body, so more is not safer. Page {{P:uvb}}.

On the twelve-month planner, M1 to M12 mean months one to twelve, starting from whichever month you begin.

Sources

Where the numbers in this package come from, so you can check them yourself.

Species references

  • ReptiFiles: Gargoyle Gecko Care Guide. Adult size of 8 to 10 in, adult weight of 45 to 65 g, maturity, lifespan, habitat and color.
  • ReptiFiles: Gargoyle Gecko Humidity and Temperatures. The 86°F (30°C) ceiling and 65°F (18°C) floor, the 50 to 70% average, the heavy evening and optional morning mist, and drinking droplets rather than from a dish.
  • ReptiFiles: Metabolic Bone Disease, Floppy Tail Syndrome, and Gargoyle Gecko Sexing. Signs and causes of both conditions, where the calcium sacs sit, tail amputation for severe floppy tail, and the 18 to 25 g sexing point.
  • Reptiles Magazine: Gargoyle Gecko Care Sheet, and Breeding Gargoyle Geckos. Weight at maturity, two-egg clutches, male breeding age, room-temperature incubation for 70 to 90 days, hatchling housing, and tail loss in breeding males.
  • The Bio Dude: Gargoyle Gecko Care Sheet and Maintenance. Feeding by age, gut-loading for 24 to 48 hours, the 24×24×24 in ideal, and the hatchling weight.
  • Bowling Green State University Herpetarium: New Caledonian Gargoyle Gecko. Maturity at around 40 g and 12 to 18 months, 4 to 5 clutches at 4.5 to 6 week intervals, about 6 laying years, and males fighting.

Veterinary and public health references

  • Merck Veterinary Manual: Management and Husbandry of Reptiles. The 3 to 6 month quarantine, ventilation and disease, fluorescent UVB within 12 in (30 cm), glass blocking UVB, and drinking from droplets.
  • Merck Veterinary Manual: Nutritional, Metabolic, and Endocrine Diseases of Reptiles. Bone disease causes, signs, diagnosis, treatment and outlook; gout; obesity and fatty liver.
  • Merck Veterinary Manual: Reproductive Diseases of Reptiles. Egg binding that develops over weeks or months, diagnosis, and surgery; prolapsed tissues.
  • Merck Veterinary Manual: Environmental Diseases and Traumatic Injuries of Reptiles. Causes of stuck shed.
  • Merck Veterinary Manual: Bacterial Diseases of Reptiles. Respiratory infection causes, signs, culture and spread to the blood; mouth rot.
  • Merck Veterinary Manual: Parasitic Diseases of Reptiles. Cryptosporidiosis in geckos, and lizard mites.
  • Merck Veterinary Manual, pet owner edition: Disorders and Diseases of Reptiles. Dehydration signs and fluids, mouth rot signs, and the causes of prolapse.
  • VCA Animal Hospitals (originally Veterinary Centers of America): Reptiles, Dystocia. Single females becoming gravid, the signs, causes, treatment and prevention of egg binding, and spaying.
  • Centers for Disease Control and Prevention: Salmonella outbreak linked to pet geckos, closed January 2026. Case, hospitalization and state counts, and the hygiene rules.
