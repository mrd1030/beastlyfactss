Emergency & quick targets

Call the reptile vet now if The air has passed 86°F (30°C) and the gecko is weak, limp or unresponsive. Open-mouth breathing, mucus or bubbles at the nose, clicking or wheezing. A soft jaw, kinked or wavy tail, curved limbs or tremors. A gravid female off food and weak, or straining. Any tissue showing at the vent. A toe gone dark or ringed tight with old shed.

First 30 days

The first month sets the habits. Quarantine, if you keep other reptiles, runs well past it.

Day 1

Move the gecko in a ventilated tub, then straight into the running enclosure
Weigh it in a lidded tub for a baseline, and start page {{P:ownerlog}}
Fill in the emergency card, page {{P:emergency}}
Start the equipment dates, page {{P:equiplog}}
Evening mist, powder in the dish, lights to timer, and leave it alone

Days 2 to 3

First vet exam within 72 hours, with a fecal exam
Offer powder on the normal schedule even if it goes untouched

Week 1

No handling. Read the hygrometer an hour after the evening mist and the next afternoon
Check both thermometers daily, and the highest reading of the day
Note where it sleeps: on cork or a branch, not upside down on the glass

Week 2

First insect feeding once it is eating powder: gut-loaded, dusted with plain calcium
Weigh again and compare with day 1
Settle the powder days and insect days, and write them on page {{P:emergency}}

Weeks 3 and 4

First short handling sessions, 5 minutes every other day, low over a bed, page {{P:handling}}
Check toes and tail tip after the first shed you notice
Adjust the humidity if it is not drying back toward 50%
Fill in the pet-sitter sheet, page {{P:sitter}}, before you need it
Act on the fecal result rather than filing it

After day 30

Quarantine continues for 3 to 6 months if another reptile lives in the house, page {{P:arrival}}
A quiet first month is a good first month A new gecko that hides by day, eats a little at night and holds its weight is doing everything right. Early food refusals are common while it adjusts. The things to watch for are the red flags on page {{P:redflags}}, not a lack of activity.

Daily

Heavy mist in the evening as the gecko wakes
Empty, wash and refill the water dish
Remove droppings, urates, shed skin and leftover food
Read both thermometers and the hygrometer
Juveniles: fresh powder

Every 2 to 3 days

Adults: fresh powder, uneaten diet out within 24 to 48 hours

Weekly

Insects: adults once, juveniles once or twice, gut-loaded and dusted
Weigh, and log it
Replace soiled substrate in sections; wipe the glass
Check toes and tail tip after any shed

Monthly

Full strip-down and disinfect, unless bioactive
Check every branch and cork piece is anchored
Look at where it sleeps: no hanging on bare glass

Replace the UVB tube on the maker's schedule, even if it still lights

Yearly

Wellness exam with a fecal test
Supplements past their date replaced

Seasonal

Summer: enclosure out of the sun, and a plan to keep it under 86°F (30°C)
Winter: night low against 65°F (18°C), and weigh through any slowdown
What a good week looks like The basking spot at 82 to 85°F (28 to 29°C) and the cool end at 70 to 75°F (21 to 24°C), with nothing ever over 86°F (30°C). The hygrometer high after the evening mist and back toward 50% by the next evening. A gecko that sleeps tucked into cork by day, climbs to its ledge at night, and holds its weight.
If you only keep one habit Weigh it. A gram scale often shows a problem before the gecko looks unwell, and a column of steady numbers is the best thing you can hand a vet.

Heat waves, power outages, travel & transport

The two edges are 65°F (18°C) and 86°F (30°C). For this gecko the hot edge is the closer one.

When it gets too hot

  • Turn the heat source off first. In a heat wave the room is the heater.
  • Get the enclosure out of any sun and away from exterior walls, to the coolest room in the house.
  • More air: open the screen top fully, and run a fan across the room, not into the enclosure.
  • Mist the surfaces so there are droplets to drink, and keep the dish full.
  • Air past 86°F (30°C) with a gecko that is weak or limp: coolest room, ventilated tub, and call the vet.

When the power goes out

  • A battery thermometer where the gecko sits. Act if it is heading below 65°F (18°C).
  • Drape thick towels over the enclosure, leaving a vent gap, to hold in the heat that is left.
  • Or move the gecko in a ventilated tub to the warmest room, usually one without exterior walls.
  • A wrapped hand warmer or hot water bottle beside the tub, never touching the gecko.
  • No candles, gas stove or open flame near it. Hold off on insects until warmth is back.
  • More than a day or two below 65°F (18°C): board it with a vet or a friend who has power.

One tier stricter than a desert reptile, because misting is a daily job for this gecko. A summer trip also needs the room plan: whoever visits should know the 86°F (30°C) limit.

Transport and vet trips

  • A ventilated plastic tub with paper towel to grip, no loose substrate and no water dish to slosh, inside a dark bag or box.
  • In cold weather warm the car first, and on a trip over about 30 minutes below 65°F (18°C) add a wrapped hand warmer beside the tub, not touching it. In hot weather cool the car first and keep the tub out of the sun.
  • Check the tub every 20 to 30 minutes at a stop. Never leave the gecko in a parked car, hot or cold.
