Three ways to get value from it, depending on where you are right now.

If you have not bought a bird yet

Read page {{P:decision}} first, before anything else in this book, and answer it honestly. If your cockatoo is already home

Start with page {{P:training}}, because it carries the single most useful research finding in this book: in a study of feather plucking in sulphur-crested cockatoos, structured daily training sessions outperformed medication, socialization and feeding enrichment. If the bird screams, plucks or has become a one-person animal that lunges at everyone else, pages {{P:bonding}} through {{P:screaming}} are the ones to work through, and they work better before the behavior is entrenched than after. Why this package exists Cockatoos are sold on the cuddling. A hand-raised baby cockatoo is one of the most affectionate animals anybody will ever meet, and almost nothing in a shop tells the buyer what that same bird needs at eight years old, or what happens when it does not get it. This book is written to close that gap: what the day actually looks like, what the noise actually is, what the dust does to a house, and what happens to the bird when you are seventy. Every figure is cross-checked against veterinary sources, listed on page {{P:sources}}, and every temperature and dimension is given in both imperial and metric.
Not a substitute for An avian veterinarian, and for an entrenched behavior problem, a certified avian behavior consultant. This is a husbandry reference for day-to-day care. This species has the highest concentration of serious behavioral problems of any commonly kept parrot, and the professionals who work with them exist for a reason. Find both before you need them and write the numbers on page {{P:emergency}}.

Cost overview

Getting started

The bird is $700 to $3,000 for a common species, with umbrellas at $1,000 to $3,000 and Moluccans $1,400 to $3,500. A rescue fee is $300 to $800 and adoptable birds are not rare. Add cage, stand, toys, air purifier and a first vet visit and a realistic total is $2,100 to $6,900. Page {{P:budget}}.

Every year

Food is the small line. Destructible toys are the big one, because a cockatoo destroying them is the system working. Budget $1,160 to $2,380 a year including an annual exam. Across a 40-year life that is $46,000 to $95,000, before a single emergency.

The reputation is earned, and it is on the record A parrot behavior consultant who specializes in this problem reports that clients with cockatoos make up more than half of her entire consulting practice. Rescues routinely describe cockatoos as the bird they take in most. That is not a rumour about a difficult species, it is a measured pattern, and page {{P:decision}} explains what causes it.
Section 01

Is a cockatoo right for you?

The only page in this series written to change some readers' minds. Answer it before you buy, not after.

Cockatoos are extremely social, extremely intelligent, extremely loud, extremely destructive and extremely long-lived, and the combination produces more behavioral problems than any other commonly kept parrot. The bird is not defective. Screaming and plucking are, in large part, a direct readout of unmet needs. But it means the difference between a wonderful cockatoo and a tragic one is almost entirely what the household can provide, every day, for decades.

Nine questions. Answer them out loud.

QuestionWhy it decides
Can I give this bird 4 to 6 hours of real engagement most days?Not company in the same room. Interaction. This is the figure behind the surrenders
Will that still be true in ten years, through a job change, a baby, or an illness?The commonest surrender story is a life that changed, not an owner who stopped caring
Can my household tolerate screaming that carries through walls?Dawn and dusk contact calling is normal behavior, not a fault to be trained out. Page {{P:screaming}}
Do I own the building, or will a neighbor complain?Noise is the second commonest reason a cockatoo is rehomed. Renting with one is genuinely hard
Is anyone here asthmatic or immunocompromised?Cockatoos produce very heavy feather dust. Page {{P:dust}} is not optional reading
Can I afford $1,100 to $2,400 a year, plus emergencies, indefinitely?Chronic conditions in this species are expensive and common. Page {{P:budget}}
Am I willing to have my woodwork, furniture and skirting boards destroyed?Destruction is normal behavior. A bird with nothing sanctioned to destroy picks your house, or itself
Do I have a plan for the bird if it outlives me?A 40-year animal needs a named person and money set aside. Page {{P:succession}}
Have I met an adult cockatoo, not a baby?Babies are uniformly sweet. The animal you are buying is the one at eight years old
If more than two answers are "probably" Then the honest recommendation is a different bird. That is not a judgement about you. A cockatoo whose needs go unmet does not become a slightly worse pet, it becomes a screaming, self-mutilating animal in a spare room, and then it becomes a rescue's problem. The cockatiel, which is a small cockatoo and shares much of the charm, is a genuinely realistic alternative for a normal household. So is a rescue cockatoo you visit weekly rather than one you own.

Two things that make it work, and one that does not

  • A household, not a person. A bird that accepts several people has several sources of company and does not collapse when one of them goes to work. Building this deliberately from day one is page {{P:bonding}}, and it is the highest-leverage thing a new owner does
  • Structured time, not just presence. Ten or fifteen minutes of training twice a day beats an afternoon on your shoulder while you work. Page {{P:training}}
  • Not more toys. They matter, and they are on page {{P:foraging}}, but the tested winner for plucking here was attention. No product substitutes for it
Consider adoption seriously Rescue fees run $300 to $800 against $1,000 to $3,500 for a baby, and adoptable cockatoos are not rare precisely because of everything on this page. An adult bird also shows you who it actually is, which a hand-raised baby cannot. A good rescue is honest about a bird's history, takes it back if it does not work, and is far more useful to you at year three than a shop will be.

Section 02 · Housing & Environment

Cage size, bar gauge, locks & placement

The most expensive object you will buy, and the one most often bought twice.

The commonly cited minimum for a large cockatoo is 36 in wide by 24 in deep by 48 in tall (91×61×122 cm), with many avian vets recommending closer to 48×48×60 in (122×122×152 cm) as genuinely comfortable. Bigger is always better here, and a cage top that opens into a play stand is the best arrangement there is, because it turns the cage into a base rather than a container.

A cage is a baseline, not the answer. No cage you can fit in a house is large enough for a bird this size to exercise properly, which is why the out-of-cage hours on page {{P:furnishings}} are the part that actually matters.

Bar gauge, spacing and locks Bar spacing 3/4 to 1 in (19 to 25 mm) for a large cockatoo, on genuinely heavy-gauge bar. A cockatoo's beak bends thin cage wire, and a bird that opens the cage while you are at work is loose in a house of hazards for eight hours.

Assume the latch will be defeated. Cockatoos are notorious escape artists with both the intelligence and the strength to work out standard fastenings, and cage-specific padlocks or spring clips on every door and feeder hatch are standard practice rather than paranoia.

Never galvanized or zinc-coated. A cockatoo chews the bars, and that coating is a zinc source. Stainless steel is the gold standard, rust-proof, non-toxic and able to survive this beak for decades. Heavy-gauge powder-coated steel is the affordable alternative and is acceptable. Page {{P:minor}} on what metal poisoning does.

Section 02 · Housing & Environment

Perches, play stands & out-of-cage space

The hours outside the cage are the ones that decide how this bird turns out.

Perches

Varied diameters from 3/4 to 2 in (19 to 51 mm), in several materials, at several heights. A cage of identical dowels loads the same points of a large bird's foot continuously, and a heavy bird develops pressure sores faster than a small one does. Natural hardwood branch is the best surface there is and it is free: apple, willow, eucalyptus and manzanita, unsprayed, scrubbed and dried.

Expect to replace them. A cockatoo reduces a perch to splinters, and that is the perch working as intended rather than a waste of money. Budget perches as a consumable on page {{P:budget}}.

Out-of-cage space, which is the real requirement

A cockatoo needs hours out of the cage daily, in a proofed room, with things to climb and chew and somewhere to be that is not your shoulder. Three arrangements, and most households end up with all three:

SetupWhat it does
A cage-top play areaThe cheapest useful hour of the day. The bird is out, in the room, occupied, and it goes back down without a chase
A free-standing play standPuts the bird somewhere in the room that is not the cage and not you. Essential for the independence work on page {{P:bonding}}
A proofed roomWhere actual exercise happens. Windows covered, fans off, cables and skirting protected, other pets out. Page {{P:hazards}}
Destruction is the point, not the problem Cockatoos destroy things. It is normal behavior, not damage, and a bird with nothing sanctioned to destroy will pick your furniture, the cage, or itself. Provide untreated softwood, cardboard, palm and yucca, paper, and cheap consumable toys in constant supply, and treat the whole category as an ongoing running cost. The good outcome is that everything you provide gets wrecked. A toy that survives a month has failed.

Dishes, water and the floor

  • Stainless steel dishes, ideally on swing-out feeders you can service without opening the main door. None of them under a perch
  • Water changed at least daily, and again whenever it is fouled, which with a bird that dunks food is often twice
  • Plain paper on the tray, changed daily, so you can read the droppings. Page {{P:droppings}}
  • Hard floor under the cage, or a washable mat. Dust, shredded wood, dropped food and droppings go a surprising distance
What to leave out No grit: cockatoos hull seed before swallowing and grit causes obstruction. No happy huts, tents, nest boxes or any dark enclosed cavity, which are a direct hormonal trigger: page {{P:eggs}}. Nothing galvanized, and nothing with zinc-plated clips or quick links. No frayed rope or loose fabric. And no toy a beak this strong can reduce to a swallowable fragment of metal or hard plastic.
Buy the stand at the same time as the cage Owners routinely buy the cage first and the stand a year later, after the bird has decided the only acceptable place to be is on a person. Having somewhere else that is genuinely good to stand, from week one, is most of the independence problem solved before it starts.
Section 02 · Housing & Environment

Light, sleep & the 10 to 12 hour rule

In this species, sleep is not a comfort question. It is the cheapest treatment for screaming there is.

A cockatoo needs 10 to 12 hours of genuine darkness and quiet every night. Not a dim room with the television on in the corner. Sleep deprivation in this species measurably worsens screaming and other behavioral issues, and it is one of the more common invisible causes of behavioral problems in pet parrots generally. It is also the first thing to check and the cheapest thing to fix.

Before you spend money on a behavior problem, count the hours A bird living in a family room that stays lit until midnight and wakes with the house at six is getting six hours. That bird will be irritable, loud and prone to plucking, and no toy, no supplement and no consultation fixes it while the sleep debt stands. If the main room cannot go dark and quiet on schedule, the answer is a separate sleep cage in a quiet room, moved to at the same time every night. This is standard practice with large parrots, not an extravagance.

Day length is also the hormone lever

Long days signal a breeding season. In a hen that means the laying cycle on page {{P:eggs}}; in a cock it means territorial aggression, displaying, and a sharp rise in biting. The routine figure is 10 to 12 hours of darkness. The intervention figure, used under veterinary advice to break a laying cycle, is to limit light to 8 hours a day, which is 16 hours of darkness. Both are on page {{P:eggs}}.

SettingNumber
Darkness, normal routine10 to 12 hours, uninterrupted, at the same times each night
Light, to break a laying cycle8 hours a day. An intervention, under vet advice, not a permanent setting
Room temperature70 to 80°F (21 to 27°C)
DaylightFull-spectrum or UV lighting is commonly recommended for indoor parrots, on a timer. Window glass filters most UVB, so a sunny window is a heat trap rather than a light source

The routine, not just the hours

Cockatoos do better on a predictable day than an unpredictable one, and this is doubly true for the anxiety-driven behavior on page {{P:bonding}}. A bird that knows when the lights go out, when you leave, when you come back and when the training session happens has far less to be anxious about than one for whom attention arrives at random. Fix the times before you fix anything else.

Cover partially, not completely A cover on three sides and part of the top gives security while leaving the bird able to see where it is. Blacking a cage out entirely suits some birds and panics others, and there is no way to know which yours is except by trying it and watching. If a bird thrashes in the dark, uncover a side and add a dim night light.
Section 02 · Housing & Environment

Feather dust, air quality & your own lungs

The page nobody mentions in the shop, and the one that decides whether the household copes.

Cockatoos are powder-down birds. Specialized feathers break down continuously into a fine keratin dust that the bird uses to condition its plumage, and a cockatoo produces a great deal of it. In practice that means a visible film on every horizontal surface in the room within a day, dust in electronics, and a haze in a sunbeam. It is not a sign of poor husbandry. It is what the bird is.

Bird fancier's lung Prolonged exposure to avian proteins in feather dust and dried droppings can cause hypersensitivity pneumonitis, known as bird fancier's lung. It is documented most often after exposure to pigeons and to psittacines including cockatoos, and it presents as breathlessness, a dry cough, fever, chest tightness, fatigue and weight loss. The serious form is progressive lung fibrosis, and the treatment is steroids plus removing the exposure, which for a pet owner can mean rehoming the bird.

This is rare, and it is not a reason not to keep a cockatoo. It is a reason to control the dust properly from day one rather than after symptoms start, and a reason to tell your doctor you keep a parrot if you develop an unexplained cough. Anyone in the house who is asthmatic, immunocompromised, or has existing lung disease should weigh this species specifically.

Controlling it, in order of effect

MeasureWhat it does
A HEPA air purifier in the bird's roomThe single most effective step, and a genuinely practical part of the setup rather than a nice-to-have. Size it for the room and run it continuously. Change the filter on schedule, which is a real running cost
Bathing the bird two or three times a weekA wet bird sheds far less dust, and most cockatoos enjoy a shower or a heavy misting. This is the cheapest control there is
Damp-wiping, never dry dustingA dry cloth or a feather duster puts the dust back into the air. Damp cloth, damp mop, and a vacuum with a sealed HEPA filter
Hard flooring, not carpetCarpet under a cockatoo cage becomes a dust reservoir you cannot clean
Not the bedroomEight hours a night of concentrated exposure is exactly the pattern that causes problems in people
VentilationFresh air, without putting the cage on a draft. An open window in another room and an internal door ajar works better than pointing a fan at the bird

The dust is also a disease route

PBFD is shed in high concentration in feather dander and dust, which is precisely why a quarantine has to be a separate room, ideally not on the same air, and not a corner of the same one. Page {{P:quarantine}}. It is also why a glossy, flaking or unusually shiny beak matters as a sign in this family: the beak looks that way because the powder down that normally dulls it has failed. Page {{P:pbfd}}.

Buy the purifier with the cage Not after the first month, when the dust has already been through the house. It belongs in the setup budget on page {{P:budget}} alongside the cage and the stand, and it is the piece of equipment cockatoo owners most consistently say they should have bought sooner.
Section 02 · Housing & Environment

Household hazards & bird-proofing

A bird's lungs make ordinary household air lethal, and this beak reaches things a small parrot cannot.
PTFE: the one that kills with no warning Nonstick cookware releases polytetrafluoroethylene gas when overheated to about 536°F (280°C), which an empty or forgotten pan reaches in minutes. The gas causes direct caustic damage to the lung, and the Merck Veterinary Manual notes that acute death is often the only clinical sign. The fumes travel, so a bird in another room is not safe.

Cookware is not the only source. Merck also lists irons and ironing board covers, some self-cleaning ovens, heating elements in some reverse-cycle heat pumps, and some heat lamps. If a bird lives in the house, replace nonstick pans with stainless steel or cast iron, never run an oven's self-clean cycle, and keep the cage out of the kitchen entirely.

And the hazard that runs the other way

A large cockatoo can inflict a bite that needs stitches, and it can destroy woodwork, a laptop or a set of blinds in the time it takes to answer the door. Bird-proofing for this species means protecting the room from the bird as much as the bird from the room. Page {{P:handling}}.

The pre-flight check Windows and doors shut. Blinds partly drawn. Ceiling fan off. Hob off and pans away. Toilet lids down and sinks empty. Other pets out of the room. Candles out. Cables covered. Do it in the same order every time until it is automatic, and do it even on the day you are only letting the bird out for five minutes.
