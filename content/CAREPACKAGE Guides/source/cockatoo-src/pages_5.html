Weigh and log it. Page {{P:ownerlog}}

Seasonally, and once a year

WhenWhat
Lengthening daysHormonal season. Expect regurgitating, cavity hunting, territorial biting and more noise. Tighten the photoperiod, remove every cavity, stop all body petting, keep the training running. Pages {{P:light}} and {{P:eggs}}

WinterDrier air, more heating, more dust in a closed house. Bathe more often, run the purifier harder, and remember an oven's self-clean cycle is a PTFE source

Power outages, travel & transport

For a bird, the danger in an outage is not the cold. It is what people light to fix the cold.
The rule that inverts from every other pet A reptile in a power cut is a temperature problem. A cockatoo in a power cut is a fumes problem. Candles, kerosene and propane heaters, generators running near a window, a gas hob left on for warmth, and a wood stove that smokes are all far more dangerous to a bird than a cold night is. If the power goes out, the first thing you decide is what you are not going to light in the bird's air.

If the power goes out

  • Keep feeding. Food and water stay available whatever else is happening
  • Move the cage to the warmest interior room, away from windows and exterior doors, and cover three sides with blankets to hold warmth while leaving airflow at the front
  • Body heat beats fire. A carrier under a blanket in an occupied room is safer than any flame
  • Ventilate anything you do burn. If a heater has to run, it runs in a different room with the door closed and a window cracked, and the bird is not in that room
  • The purifier being off is not an emergency, but the dust builds fast. Open a window in another room and damp-wipe more often until the power is back. Page {{P:dust}}

In the car

RuleWhy
A carrier, not the cageA cage is a box of perches and toys to be thrown around in. A carrier with one low perch is safer, and for most birds the smaller space is calming rather than distressing
A carrier this beak cannot openCheck the door latch specifically, and add a clip. A cockatoo loose in a moving car is a genuinely dangerous situation for everyone in it
Cover itLess visual input, much less stress. Leave one side open for airflow
Belt it in, on the floor or the seatNever the boot, never a lap, never the front seat with an airbag
Water at stops, not in the carrierA full dish soaks the bird, and a soaked bird chills
Never leave the bird in a parked carNot for five minutes. A closed car in the sun kills a bird faster than it kills a dog
No air freshener, no smoking, no vapingA car is a very small volume of air. Page {{P:hazards}}
Train voluntary carrier entry, now Leave the carrier open in the room, put nuts in it, and reward the bird for walking in. Page {{P:training}}. A cockatoo that enters its carrier for a reward can be moved in ninety seconds during an evacuation or a 10pm emergency. One that has never seen it is a fight in a stressful moment, with a beak that can take a finger.

Owner log

Weight is the vital sign a heavily feathered bird cannot hide. A trend beats a single reading every time.
DateWeight (g)EatingDroppingsNotes: feather condition, screaming, molt, training
How to use this page Weigh weekly once the baseline is set, at the same time of day, before the first feed, and daily through a diet conversion, a molt, or a change in how food is delivered. Write the number even when it is boring, because the boring numbers are what make one interesting number readable. Act on a drop of more than about 10% from the bird's own baseline, or any steady decline across three weighings. Take this page to the vet, and keep it with the care file on page {{P:succession}}.

Glossary

Terms used in this book, in the sense they are used here.
Bird fancier's lungHypersensitivity pneumonitis in people, caused by prolonged exposure to avian proteins in feather dust and dried droppings. Page {{P:dust}}
Blood featherA growing feather with a live blood supply in the shaft. Dark and full at the base, and it bleeds freely if broken or cut. Pages {{P:clipping}} and {{P:minor}}
ChlamydiosisInfection with Chlamydia psittaci. Called psittacosis in people. Page {{P:minor}}
Chronic egg layingRepeated clutches out of season, driven by the environment rather than by a mate. Page {{P:eggs}}
CITESThe international convention governing trade in endangered species. It regulates trade, not domestic ownership. Page {{P:legal}}
Contact callA loud call used to locate flock members out of sight. Most cockatoo screaming is this. Page {{P:screaming}}
CorticosteroneThe main avian stress hormone, used in the plucking study as an objective measure of whether a treatment worked. Page {{P:training}}
Eye pinningRapid dilation and contraction of the pupil, signalling high arousal. Excitement or aggression depending on what else the bird is doing. Page {{P:handling}}
ForagingWorking for food rather than eating it from a bowl. Wild parrots spend up to six hours a day doing it. Page {{P:foraging}}
Hepatic lipidosisFatty liver disease, usually from a seed-heavy diet. Page {{P:liver}}
Hypovitaminosis AVitamin A deficiency. The commonest nutritional disease in pet birds. Page {{P:liver}}
KeelThe ridge of breastbone down the front of the bird, used to judge body condition by feel. Page {{P:sexing}}
LipomaA benign fatty tumor, common in overweight cockatoos. Page {{P:liver}}
MoltOrderly, symmetrical replacement of the plumage. Page {{P:plucking}}
PBFDPsittacine beak and feather disease. An incurable circovirus first described in Australian cockatoos. Page {{P:pbfd}}
PhotoperiodThe length of the light part of the day. The main lever on hormonal behavior. Page {{P:light}}
Pin featherA new feather still in its waxy keratin sheath. Normal during a molt, and sore to touch. Page {{P:plucking}}
PolyuriaExcess urine, with the fecal part still formed. Not the same as diarrhea. Page {{P:droppings}}
Powder downSpecialized feathers that break down into a fine dust used in grooming. Cockatoos produce a great deal of it. Page {{P:dust}}
PTFEPolytetrafluoroethylene, the nonstick coating whose fumes kill birds when overheated. Page {{P:hazards}}
RegurgitationDeliberate bringing up of food as courtship, aimed at a mate, a toy or a person. Not vomiting. Page {{P:eggs}}
StationA trained cue to go to a named perch and stay. The behaviour that gets a bird off your shoulder politely. Page {{P:training}}
Target trainingTeaching the bird to touch its beak to a stick. The foundation for everything else, and it keeps hands away from the beak. Page {{P:training}}
UratesThe chalky white part of a dropping, uric acid rather than urine. Page {{P:droppings}}
ZoonoticPassing from animals to people. Psittacosis is. Page {{P:minor}}

Where the sources disagree

Five figures where credible references do not match, and what this book prints instead.
FigureThe disagreement, and the choice
Life spanLafeberVet's veterinary information sheet gives 30 to 45 years generally, with Moluccans up to 70. Consumer sources commonly give 40 to 70-plus, and umbrellas at 50 to 70. This book prints the veterinary range with the documented upper figure alongside it, because the planning consequence on page {{P:succession}} is the same either way: assume the bird outlives you
Umbrella weightLafeberVet gives 400 to 700 g for an umbrella. Other references give 1.1 to 1.7 lb, which is roughly 500 to 770 g. The ranges overlap and neither is wrong; the practical answer on page {{P:sexing}} is that your own bird's stable baseline matters far more than the published range
Diet splitSome care references give 60 to 70% pellets for large parrots. VCA's cockatoo feeding page gives 75 to 80%, with fresh food at no more than 20 to 40%, and that is what is printed here
Cage size36×24×48 in is the commonly cited minimum and 48×48×60 in the frequently recommended target. Neither was picked: page {{P:cage}} prints the first as a floor and the second as what to buy, and says plainly that no cage that fits in a house is large enough on its own
CostPublished setup figures of $250 to $1,300 omit the play stand, the air purifier, a gram scale, a carrier and a real first veterinary visit with a PCR panel. Page {{P:budget}} itemizes all of them and lands at $2,105 to $6,930, with the lines summing to the totals
