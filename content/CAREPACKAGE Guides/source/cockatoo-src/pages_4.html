Section 05 · Health & Common Issues

Reading droppings & hydration

The cheapest daily health check you own, and the reason the tray is lined with plain paper.

A bird dropping has three components, and telling them apart is the whole skill. The feces is the solid, formed, coiled part. The urates are the chalky white part, uric acid crystals that let a bird excrete nitrogen using almost no water. The urine is the clear liquid halo around the outside. Each can change independently, and which one changed tells you where the problem is.

What you seeWhat it usually means
Green feces, formed, in a seed-fed birdNormal. Diet colours the fecal part, and seed-eating birds pass green feces
Brown feces, formed, in a pellet-fed birdNormal. Expect the colour to change during a diet conversion and do not panic at it
Red, pink or purpleAlmost always berries or beetroot, and it lasts a day. If the bird has eaten nothing coloured, it is blood, and it is a same-day call
Lime green or yellow-greenA liver pointer, and a classic sign of chlamydiosis. Page {{P:minor}}. Vet
Black or tarryDigested blood from higher in the gut. Vet, today
Yellow or green uratesThe white part should be white. Coloured urates point at the liver. Page {{P:liver}}
Watery, but the feces still formedPolyuria, excess urine, not diarrhea. Usually a watery meal, sometimes stress. Persisting with minimal fruit over the last 24 hours raises kidney disease
Unformed feces, no solid partTrue diarrhea, much less common than owners think and much more serious. Vet
Undigested food in the droppingFood passing through unabsorbed. Always abnormal. Vet
Far fewer droppings than yesterdayThe bird is not eating, or is obstructed. Both matter. Page {{P:redflags}}
The 24-hour rule Learn your own bird's normal droppings, because once you know them anything else is abnormal by definition. A single odd dropping is not an emergency; you changed the food, or it ate a blueberry. Droppings that stay abnormal for more than about 24 hours are a vet visit. Photograph the paper before you throw it away and take the photo with you.

Hydration

A dehydrated bird has dry, tacky mouth tissue, dull sunken eyes, skin over the keel that stays tented when gently lifted, and urates that go thick and yellow. A large parrot on a pellet diet drinks noticeably more than one eating a lot of fresh food, so a change in water consumption is only meaningful against what that bird normally does. Change the water daily as a minimum, and again whenever it is fouled, which with a bird that dunks food is often twice.

Line the tray so you can read it Plain paper, changed daily, with the grate out if the bird will not stand on the tray. Corncob, sand and wood chip all hide droppings, absorb them, and grow mold underneath, which turns the most informative surface in the cage into the least. It also makes it much harder to notice the day the droppings stop.

Quarantine & adding a second bird

Skipping quarantine is how PBFD spreads. It is also the only control there is.

A new bird can carry PBFD, polyomavirus or chlamydia with no visible sign at all, shedding all three in droppings, crop secretions and feather dust. Quarantine is not about whether the new bird looks well. It looks well precisely because that is how these diseases work, and PBFD's incubation runs from weeks to years.

30 days minimum, and test Public health guidance for birds exposed to other birds at shows, sales or events is to quarantine at least 30 days and test before returning or adding them to a group. Take that as the floor. In a household with an existing bird, 45 to 60 days is the safer figure, because it covers a slow shedder and gives time to repeat a test. There is no version of this that takes a weekend.

How to actually run it

RuleWhy
A separate room, ideally not on the same air handlingThe rule that matters most for a cockatoo: feather dust is the transmission route and this family produces more of it than anything else kept. A different floor beats a different corner. Page {{P:dust}}
Separate dishes, tools and cleaning clothsCross-contamination is usually a bucket or a sponge, not the birds
Established birds first, new bird lastThe whole daily routine for the resident bird before you go near the new one, every day, without exceptions
Wash hands and change your shirtFeather dust sits on clothing. This is the step people skip
A vet exam and a PCR panel in week onePBFD, polyomavirus and chlamydia. This is what the 30 days is for; quarantine without testing is just waiting
Weigh the new bird dailyYou are looking for the thing it is hiding. Page {{P:sexing}}

Then the introduction

  1. Cages in sight of each other, several feet apart, for at least a week. Let them see and call with no contact
  2. Move them closer gradually, watching for interest rather than lunging at the bars
  3. Neutral ground first. The first physical meeting happens on a play stand or a table, never inside either cage. A cockatoo defends its cage seriously and a bite from this beak lands differently between birds than between budgies
  4. Short supervised sessions, extended as they get boring. Normal negotiation is beak fencing, chasing and noise; a real fight is one bird pinning another or gripping a foot. Separate for that, not the noise
  5. Separate cages permanently is the default with large cockatoos. Two birds who live beside each other and call back and forth get most of the social benefit with none of the risk
A second cockatoo is not a solution to the first one's needs Owners of an over-bonded, screaming cockatoo frequently consider a second bird as a fix. It rarely is. You now have two birds needing 4 to 6 hours each, two cages, two vet bills, and a real possibility that they dislike each other or that one is injured. The interventions on pages {{P:training}}, {{P:foraging}} and {{P:bonding}} address the actual problem. Get a second cockatoo because you want one and can serve one, not as a treatment.
Quarantine even if it is your only bird A 30-day settling period in a quiet room, with a vet exam at the start of it, is good for a single new cockatoo too. It catches problems while your expectations are still forming, gives the bird the low-pressure start page {{P:bonding}} asks for, and stops the intense first-weeks over-bonding that causes so much trouble later.
Section 06 · The Long View

Legal status, CITES & state rules

Broadly legal, with real exceptions that depend on which cockatoo you own.

In the great majority of US states a pet cockatoo raises no legal question at all. Idaho and Colorado clear pet birds as a category outright; Oklahoma's noncontrolled exotic-species exemption names cockatoos specifically; New Hampshire and Montana name the parrot order or family as unregulated. Where a state does restrict a parrot, it is almost always the monk parakeet, named in California, Connecticut, New Jersey and Virginia among others, and every cockatoo is outside those lists.

CITES does not make owning a cockatoo illegal CITES governs international trade, not domestic ownership, so an Appendix I listing does not by itself make a captive-bred bird illegal to keep. It matters here for two reasons: a state can write its own rule that keys off the listing, and moving a bird across an international border is a serious permitting exercise rather than a formality.

Section 06 · The Long View

The sixty-year bird

Succession and rehoming, written down now, while nothing has gone wrong.

The veterinary figure for cockatoo life span is 30 to 45 years, with Moluccans documented up to about 70. A cockatoo bought by a forty-year-old will very plausibly outlive them, and one bought by a sixty-year-old almost certainly will. That is a planning problem, and it is the most neglected part of owning this species.

The default outcome, if you do nothing The bird goes to whoever is sorting out your affairs, at the worst possible week, having just lost the only person it was bonded to. Rescues are full, and cockatoos are the bird they take in most. An unplanned cockatoo gets passed through several homes, and each move makes the next one harder.

What a plan actually contains

ElementDetail
A named person, who has agreedNot "my daughter will probably take it": an actual conversation, an actual yes, ideally someone who has handled the bird. Name a backup, because one name is not a plan across forty years
A named organisation as the fallbackA parrot sanctuary or species rescue, contacted in advance and asked whether they run a placement or bequest programme. Many do, and their requirements usually include documentation you have to keep from now on
Money attached to the birdA cockatoo costs $1,160 to $2,380 a year, so inheriting one without funds is inheriting a bill, which is why so many bequeathed parrots end up in rescues. A pet trust, a life-insurance arrangement or a specific bequest all work; a verbal promise does not
It in writing, in the willPets are property in most jurisdictions, so the bird passes with the estate unless the will says otherwise. Name the bird, the person, the fallback, and attach the funds
A care file that goes with the birdThis book, pages {{P:ownerlog}} and {{P:equiplog}} filled in, the vet's details, hatch date, band or chip number, any CITES paperwork, and what this bird likes, fears and has been trained to do

If you need to rehome now

Rehoming is not a failure, and doing it well is a real kindness. Doing it badly is how a bird reaches its fifth home by twelve.

  • A reputable rescue or sanctuary first, never a classified ad. They screen, they know what a cockatoo needs, and they take the bird back if a placement fails
  • Be honest about screaming, plucking, biting and bonding history. A hidden problem produces another failed placement, and the bird pays for it
  • Send the whole care file, logs and vet history included
  • Never free to a good home. A bird advertised free attracts exactly the people who have not costed the next forty years
  • Warn the new home to expect a transition. A rehomed cockatoo commonly screams and refuses food and handling for weeks. That is grief, not a character flaw

Toy replacement is not optional and not a rounding error Cockatoos destroy wooden toys extremely fast, and constant destructible enrichment is a welfare need rather than a luxury. Intense one-on-one time in the first weeks cements the anxious dependency that produces screaming and plucking later. Be present, be predictable, share the bird around the household. What normal settling looks like A new cockatoo may be quiet and watchful for days, eat less, refuse to step up, or be immediately and overwhelmingly affectionate. All are normal.
