Glossary, A to H

AutotomyCaudal autotomy: a lizard dropping its own tail when grabbed or frightened. A leopard gecko's tail regrows in about 30 days, shorter and thicker. Pages {{P:handling}}, {{P:shedding}}.
BioactiveA planted enclosure with a living substrate and a cleanup crew of tiny invertebrates that break down waste. An option for experienced keepers. Page {{P:substrate}}.
BrumationA reptile's natural winter slowdown: less activity and appetite, cued by cooler temperatures and shorter days, without weight loss. Page {{P:brumation}}.
Captive-bredHatched from parents kept by people rather than caught in the wild. Essentially every leopard gecko sold today is captive-bred. Pages {{P:species}}, {{P:legal}}.
Ceramic heat emitterA screw-in heater that gives off heat with no visible light, so it can run at night on its own thermostat. Page {{P:thermostats}}.
CITESThe Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna and Flora: the treaty that controls international trade in listed animals and plants. The leopard gecko is not on its most restricted list, Appendix I. Page {{P:legal}}.
CoccidiaSingle-celled gut parasites, found with a fecal test, often with no outward sign. Page {{P:respiratory}}.
CrepuscularMost active at dusk and dawn, which is why evening is the time to feed and handle. Page {{P:profile}}.
CryptosporidiosisA contagious parasite infection with no cure, called stick tail disease for the wasting it causes. Diagnosed with a fecal PCR test. Page {{P:crypto}}.
D3, vitamin D3The form of vitamin D a reptile makes in its skin under UVB, or takes in from a dust, and needs to absorb calcium. Pages {{P:uvb}}, {{P:supplements}}.
Deep heat projectorAn overhead heater with no visible light that sends its heat down in a more focused beam than a ceramic heat emitter. Runs on a dimming thermostat. Page {{P:thermostats}}.
Dimming thermostatA thermostat that lowers the power to a heater the way a light dimmer does, instead of switching it fully off and on. Also called proportional. Page {{P:thermostats}}.
DysecdysisIncomplete or "stuck" shedding, most often on the toes, the tail tip and around the eyes. Page {{P:shedding}}.
DystociaEgg binding: a female unable to pass her eggs. A same-day emergency. Page {{P:binding}}.
EcdysisThe normal shed cycle, from dull, dry pre-shed skin to the old skin coming off. Page {{P:brumation}}.
Fecal testA vet's check of a stool sample for parasites, under the microscope or by PCR. Bring a fresh sample in a sealed bag. Pages {{P:arrival}}, {{P:respiratory}}.
Ferguson ZoneOne of four groups that match a species' natural sun exposure to a UVB strength. Leopard geckos are Zone 1, the crepuscular and shade-dwelling group. Page {{P:uvb}}.
GravidCarrying eggs. A female can be gravid without ever meeting a male. Page {{P:eggs}}.
Gut-loadingFeeding feeder insects a calcium-rich diet for 24 to 72 hours before offering them, so the calcium in their gut passes to the gecko. Page {{P:gutload}}.
Hemipenal bulgesThe two bumps at the base of a male's tail where the hemipenes, its paired reproductive organs, sit. Page {{P:eggs}}.
Humid hideA closed hide with a damp lining, 70 to 80% humidity inside, in an enclosure otherwise kept at 30 to 40%. It prevents most shedding problems. Page {{P:substrate}}.
Reference

Glossary, I to Z

ImpactionA blockage in the digestive tract, usually from swallowed loose substrate or prey too large to pass. Page {{P:impaction}}.
Infrared thermometerA handheld gun that reads the temperature of a surface from a distance. The way to check the warm hide floor. Page {{P:temperature}}.
Lay boxA covered container of damp sphagnum moss, big enough to turn around in, where a female digs and lays. Page {{P:eggs}}.
MBDMetabolic bone disease: a weakened, deformed skeleton from too little calcium, vitamin D3 or heat. Common in leopard geckos and largely preventable. Page {{P:mbd}}.
MorphA captive-bred line that looks different from the natural, wild-type gecko in color or pattern. Over 100 are recognized. Page {{P:species}}.
Obligate insectivoreAn animal that must eat insects and cannot live on plant food. A leopard gecko is one. Page {{P:diet}}.
On/off thermostatA thermostat that cuts power fully at the set temperature and restores it once the reading drops. The usual match for a heat mat. Page {{P:thermostats}}.
OxalatesCompounds, oxalic acid among them, in spinach, beet greens and Swiss chard that bind calcium so it can't be absorbed. Why those greens make a poor gut-load. Page {{P:gutload}}.
PCRPolymerase chain reaction: a lab test that finds a germ's genetic material in a sample. How a vet tests stool for cryptosporidiosis. Pages {{P:arrival}}, {{P:crypto}}.
Preanal poresA V-shaped row of waxy pores just in front of the vent, enlarged in a maturing male. Page {{P:eggs}}.
ProbeThe thermostat's sensor. On the warm hide floor, held with a clip or suction cup, never tape. Page {{P:thermostats}}.
ProlapseTissue pushed out of the vent. Keep it moist and see a vet immediately. Page {{P:impaction}}.
Pulse proportional thermostatA thermostat that sends short pulses of power to hold a steady temperature. Suits a ceramic heat emitter. Page {{P:thermostats}}.
PVCPolyvinyl chloride: a rigid plastic used for enclosure panels. Holds heat that a glass tank loses. Page {{P:enclosure}}.
QuarantineKeeping a new reptile fully apart from others, in a separate room with separate tools, for 3 to 6 months. Page {{P:arrival}}.
SalmonellaBacteria that healthy reptiles carry in the gut and shed in droppings without being ill. Spread from hands and surfaces to mouths. Page {{P:cleaning}}.
Stick tailThe common name for cryptosporidiosis, after the tail that wastes down to a stick. Page {{P:crypto}}.
T5 HOA fluorescent tube 5/8 inch across (T5), in the high-output (HO) version. The distances in this package are for the standard-output 7% tube; an HO tube uses its maker’s chart. Coil bulbs are not equivalent. Page {{P:uvb}}.
Thermal gradientThe warm-to-cool range across the enclosure that lets a gecko choose its own temperature. Page {{P:temperature}}.
Units°F, degrees Fahrenheit, and °C, degrees Celsius, for temperature; in (inches), cm (centimeters) and mm (millimeters) for size; g (grams) for weight; % (percent) for humidity.
UrateThe white, chalky part of reptile waste, made from uric acid instead of liquid urine. Its color and texture are a hydration gauge. Page {{P:stool}}.
UVBUltraviolet B: the part of sunlight a reptile's skin uses to make vitamin D3. Optional for a leopard gecko, and beneficial at low output. Page {{P:uvb}}.
UVIUltraviolet index: a measure of UVB strength at one spot, read with a handheld meter. 0.6 to 1.4 at the basking spot for this species. Page {{P:uvb}}.
VCAVeterinary Centers of America, now VCA Animal Hospitals: an animal hospital chain whose pet care articles are among this package's sources.
