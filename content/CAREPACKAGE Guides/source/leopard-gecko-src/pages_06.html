That is $360 to $1,020 a year, before the yearly wellness exam at $85 to $105. Buying feeders in bulk, or keeping a small dubia roach colony, is the biggest lever on that number.

Plan for, don't count on avoiding A sick or emergency visit runs $100 to $800 or more, and diagnostics or surgery climb from there. A small emergency fund set aside matters as much as any item on this list.
Where not to cut corners The thermostat and the enclosure size. The thermostat is what stops the mat from overheating and burning the gecko; save on decor before you save there.

First 30 days

Day 1

Weigh the gecko in grams and photograph it and the setup for a baseline
Read the warm hide floor with the infrared thermometer and the cool side with a probe, not "feels warm"
First exam with a reptile vet within 72 hours, with a fresh stool sample and the cryptosporidiosis PCR test asked for by name (page {{P:arrival}})
If another reptile lives here, set up the separate quarantine room and its own tools
Fill in the emergency card (page {{P:emergency}})
Start the equipment dates (page {{P:equiplog}})

Week 1

No handling. Fresh water daily, food offered in the evening, uneaten insects removed
A hand resting flat in the enclosure for a few minutes each evening, no touching (page {{P:handling}})
Confirm it uses the warm hide. If it never does, re-read the floor and check the hide is snug

Weeks 2 to 3

Once it stops fleeing and eats normally, 5-minute handling sessions every other day
After the first shed, check toes, tail tip and eyes (page {{P:shedding}})
Start the Owner Log with a weekly weigh-in (page {{P:ownerlog}})

Week 4

Review the weight and the tail: steady gain for a young gecko, stable weight for an adult (page {{P:growth}})
Settle the feeding interval on body condition rather than the calendar (page {{P:diet}})
Set a phone reminder for the UVB replacement date and a monthly thermostat check
Do the first monthly deep clean (page {{P:cleaning}})
Introduce the first enrichment items (page {{P:enrichlog}})

Days 31 to 90

Book any follow-up the vet recommended, and act on the fecal result rather than filing it
Keep any quarantine running the full 3 to 6 months before the gecko shares a room, tools or hands with another reptile
Look back at the Owner Log for any slow drift in weight, appetite or tail thickness
Fill in the pet-sitter sheet before you need it (page {{P:sitter}})
Check the sex again as it matures, and add a lay box for a female (page {{P:eggs}})

Daily, weekly & seasonal routine

Daily

Read the second probe on the warm hide floor against the thermostat display; they should agree within a degree or two, and a widening gap or a climbing reading is the warning
Fresh water, and check the humid hide is still damp
Feed in the evening on the schedule for its age (page {{P:diet}})
Dust supplements on the right days (page {{P:supplements}})
Remove uneaten insects
Spot-clean droppings, and look at them before you do (page {{P:stool}})
Quick look: tail thickness, eyes, toes, posture
Wash your hands after every contact

Weekly

Weigh in grams and log it (page {{P:ownerlog}})
Read the warm hide floor with the infrared thermometer, and the hygrometer
Wipe down glass, walls, hides and decor
Replace the humid hide lining if it has started to break down

Monthly

Deep clean: everything out, scrubbed, disinfected, rinsed, dried, fresh substrate (page {{P:cleaning}})
Confirm the thermostat still cuts in, the probe hasn't moved, and the timer is on schedule
Review the Owner Log for slow trends, not just single readings
Move or swap one furnishing, not the whole layout

Seasonal

Fall: expect appetite to drop; weigh at least every couple of weeks through any brumation (page {{P:brumation}})
Winter: check the room's night low, and add a ceramic heat emitter on a thermostat if it falls below 65°F (18°C)
Adult females: a lay box ready whenever she looks gravid (page {{P:eggs}})
Spring and fall: move the light timer to 14 hours for summer and back to 12 for winter

Yearly

Wellness exam and a fecal test, even for a gecko that seems perfectly healthy (page {{P:redflags}})
Replace the UVB tube by its rated life, 12 months at the latest
Replace opened powders by the date on the jar
If you only keep one habit Keep the weekly weigh-in. Most of what goes wrong in a leopard gecko shows up as a weight change or a thinning tail before anything else, and in an animal this small a slide of a few grams is invisible by eye and obvious on a scale.

Power outages, heat, travel & transport

Normal night low about 70°F (21°C); act below 65°F (18°C). A small gecko loses heat faster than a heavy-bodied snake.

When the power goes out

Do

  • Keep the gecko at or above 65°F (18°C). A battery or wind-up thermometer in the enclosure gives you the real number.
  • Drape thick towels or a blanket over the enclosure, leaving a vent gap, to hold in what heat is left.
  • Move the enclosure, or the gecko in a ventilated tub, to the warmest room.
  • Put a wrapped hot-water bottle or a hand warmer beside the gecko, never touching it.
  • Stop feeding insects. Offer water only until heat is reliably back.
  • Past a day or two below 65°F (18°C), board it with a vet, a friend with power, or a reptile shop.

Don't

  • Don't use candles, gas stoves, or open flame near the enclosure.
  • Don't put a heat pack in contact with the gecko. A cold, sluggish animal can't move away fast enough and will burn.
  • Don't feed "to keep its strength up." Undigested food in a cold gut is a bacterial and impaction risk.
  • Don't warm a cold gecko in hot water; lukewarm only, and only once it's alert.
Outage kit Two or three hand warmers or a hot-water bottle, two thick towels, a battery thermometer, a ventilated tub, and your vet numbers written on paper.

In the heat

The cool side has to stay at 70 to 77°F (21 to 25°C), and a thermostat cannot cool the room. Gaping, pacing away from the warm side or flattening into the coolest spot means too hot; darkened skin or limpness is an emergency.

Transport and vet trips

  • A ventilated plastic tub with a towel to grip, in a dark, room-temperature bag or box. No loose substrate, no water dish.
  • Warm the car first in cold weather; for a trip over about 30 minutes below 65°F (18°C), a wrapped hand warmer beside the tub. Check the tub every 20 to 30 minutes at a stop.
  • Skip the meal before a trip. Never leave the gecko in a parked car, hot or cold, even briefly.
  • Moving house: the enclosure runs at the new place before the gecko arrives.

Why bother logging A steady weight trend is the best early warning you have, and in this species doubly so: a gecko that is brumating holds its weight and its tail, and a gecko that is ill does not, and the log shows the difference weeks before the gecko does.
