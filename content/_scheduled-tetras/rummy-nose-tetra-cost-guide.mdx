# How Much Does Rummy-Nose Tetra Ownership Cost?

<AffiliateDisclosure />

The rummy-nose tetra (Petitella bleheri, formerly Hemigrammus bleheri) is a slender schooling fish from the tannin-stained blackwater rivers of the Negro and Meta basins in South America. Adults reach about 1.5 to 2 inches (4 to 5 cm) and carry a bright red nose and head. It is kept in a school of 10 or more, and the long tank that school needs costs more than the fish themselves.

## The Fish: %%animal:rummy-nose-tetra%% for a School of 12

Online sellers ask about $4 to $6.50 a fish before shipping, often in packs of 6, 9 or 12. Ten is the smallest school to buy, and 12 is the number priced here: a school of 12 comes to %%animal:rummy-nose-tetra%%, with live-fish shipping charged on top. Buying in person can cost more, with one store listing them at $8.99 a fish.

Both the per-fish price and the school size sit above those of the [ember tetra](/blog/ember-tetra-cost-guide/), so the fish line for a rummy-nose tank lands well above an ember school's.

<FunFact>
The rummy-nose is one of the Amazon's biggest aquarium exports. Among the ornamental fish shipped out of Brazil's Amazonas state, it ranks second, behind only the cardinal tetra, at roughly 6% of the 20 to 30 million fish the state sends out each year.
</FunFact>

## Upfront Setup: %%setup:rummy-nose-tetra%%

<CostTable guide="rummy-nose-tetra" section="necessities" />

The tank is the largest line, and its length is what to shop by. Rummy-noses are active fish that swim back and forth the length of the tank, so buy a tank at least 36 inches (90 cm) long for a school of 10. That length comes as a 30 gallon (36 x 12 inches) or a 40 gallon breeder (36 x 18 inches). A 20 gallon long measures only 30 inches, and the [rummy-nose tank setup guide](/blog/rummy-nose-tetra-tank-setup-guide/) lays out how to keep the full 36 inches open for swimming.

The heater is a necessity for a tropical fish held at 73 to 79°F (23 to 26°C), and it needs the wattage to warm 30 to 40 gallons. Filtration can stay gentle, which a sponge filter rated for the tank's volume covers. Live plants are not part of the rummy-nose's home rivers, but the fish settle well in a planted tank, and three 10 lb bags of fine sand cover the 36-inch floor.

The liquid test kit pays for itself first by showing when the tank has finished cycling, which a kit reading settles and a calendar does not, as the [aquarium cycling guide](/blog/aquarium-cycling-guide/) explains. It also tracks pH, and this species prefers soft, acidic water: a pH of 5.5 to 7.0 and a hardness of 2 to 12 dGH. Hardness needs a separate GH test, and [what raises or lowers GH and KH](/blog/freshwater-ph-gh-kh-guide/) decides whether a given tap is worth softening. The kit has one more job with this fish: the red on the head fades when a rummy-nose is stressed or kept in poor water, so a washed-out school is the cue to test.

## The Extras: A Spare Tank for Quarantine

<CostTable guide="rummy-nose-tetra" section="extras" />

New fish spend their first weeks in a separate tank, and the [fish quarantine guide](/blog/fish-quarantine-and-treatment-guide/) sets out how to run the bare one priced above. Rummy-noses sometimes arrive carrying ich, and because 79°F is the top of their range, an outbreak is cleared with [salt or medication](/blog/aquarium-ich-treatment-guide/) and the heater goes no higher than 79°F, so a bottle of treatment is the likely extra spend.

## Ongoing Costs: Low

Rummy-noses are omnivores with small mouths. The staple is a small flake, granule or micro pellet, and the first supply in the table runs %%price:fish-food-micro-pellets%%. Small live and frozen foods such as baby brine shrimp, daphnia and mosquito larvae add a modest recurring cost, and a varied menu like this brings out the best color. The school eats small portions at least once a day, and the [rummy-nose feeding guide](/blog/rummy-nose-tetra-feeding-guide/) sets the schedule and the portion.

Water conditioner at %%price:water-conditioner%% a bottle, power for the heater and filter, and test kit refills make up the rest. A planted rummy-nose tank needs a partial water change every week, and the [water change schedule](/blog/aquarium-water-changes-guide/) a tank follows decides how fast a bottle of conditioner runs out.

## Vet Costs

Seldom a line item. A rummy-nose school stays healthy on stable water and, when needed, over-the-counter treatment. When fish fall ill, [the signs that separate a water problem from a disease](/blog/spotting-a-sick-fish-guide/) decide whether the next spend is a water change, a medication or a visit to a vet who sees fish.

## Lifespan

Rummy-nose tetras live about 5 years in a well-kept tank. That is a long run for a fish under 2 inches, and the 36-inch tank, heater, filter and test kit bought on day one serve the school for all of it.
