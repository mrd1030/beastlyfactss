# Black Skirt Tetra Feeding: Diet, Food Size, and How Often to Feed

<AffiliateDisclosure />

The black skirt tetra (Gymnocorymbus ternetzi), also sold as the black widow tetra, is an easy fish to please at mealtime. It is an omnivore, it eats the same dried food as most of a community tank, and it does not gorge itself. What it asks of a keeper is a little live or frozen food now and then and a portion sized to the school.

## What Black Skirts Eat in the Wild

In the slow creeks of the Paraguay River basin, a black skirt lives mostly as a small predator. Worms, little crustaceans and insects make up the bulk of its diet. It is not a selective feeder, though, and it swallows modest amounts of algae and plant matter along with its prey. That mix, mostly animal food with a small share of plants, is the pattern a tank diet copies.

## The Staple Diet

The black skirts sold in the trade are all bred in captivity, and that long history in tanks has left them easy to feed. A good flake or granule food makes a sound daily staple, and a slow-sinking <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B004PBB5TK?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Hikari Tropical Semi-Floating Micro Pellets, 0.77 oz">micro-pellet</AffiliateLink> works in the same role. Omnivorous fish have taken to captivity better than most, and this species shows why: there is very little it refuses.

<KeyTakeaway>
Captive-bred black skirts accept ordinary community fish food. Getting the menu right is easy; the part that takes attention is how much goes into the tank.
</KeyTakeaway>

## Food Size

A black skirt reaches 2 to 2.5 inches (5 to 6 cm) in a home tank, and up to 3 inches at most. That is big enough to take flake and granules as a staple without the fine crushing an [ember tetra](/blog/ember-tetra-feeding-guide/) at 0.8 inch needs. Live and frozen extras should still be small items, the size of daphnia or bloodworm, since those match the small prey the fish catches in the wild.

## Live and Frozen Foods

Small live and frozen foods belong in the routine on a regular basis, alongside the dried staple. Daphnia and bloodworm are the standard choices, and both are small invertebrates of the kind that make up the wild diet.

<FunFact>
Researchers studying hunger in fish have used the black widow tetra as their reference omnivore. A 2019 study of how fasting changes the genes that control appetite compared it with two other characins that feed in very different ways.
</FunFact>

## How Often to Feed, and How Much

Feed adult black skirts once a day. One meal daily is plenty for most pet fish, and the school should be fed on at least five days of the week. A maintenance ration is small: about 0.5 to 1% of the fish's body weight a day, which for a school of 12 two-inch fish is a light pinch.

Overfeeding is the commonest mistake keepers make, and black skirts make it easy to spot. They are not greedy eaters and do not overeat, so food the school does not want simply drifts down and settles. The next meal should be smaller. Leftovers that sit in the tank degrade the water for every fish in it, adding to the waste the [nitrogen cycle in an established tank](/blog/aquarium-cycling-guide/) has to process. Around other species, black skirts are not known to compete hard for food, so a school seldom crowds its tankmates out of a meal.

Appetite also follows the thermometer. When Kuhn, Azari and Volkoff held black skirts for two weeks at four temperatures from 68 to 90°F (20 to 32°C), the fish ate more as the water got warmer. A school in a tank kept near the cool end of the [black skirt's 68 to 79°F range](/blog/black-skirt-tetra-tank-setup-guide/) can be expected to take less food than one near the top, and the portion should shrink to match.

## Feeding Fry

Adult black skirts eat their own eggs whenever they get the chance, so they come out of the spawning tank the moment eggs are seen. The eggs hatch in 18 to 36 hours, and the fry swim free a few days later. Start them on an infusoria-type food for the first few days, then move to microworm or baby brine shrimp (Artemia nauplii) once they are large enough to take them. Growing fish need their food in small meals given more often than the adults' single daily feed.

A black skirt will eat nearly anything sized for it, which is why the keeper's real decision is the portion, not the food. The [black skirt tetra encyclopedia profile](/encyclopedia/animal/black-skirt-tetra/) has the natural history.
