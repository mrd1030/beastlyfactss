# Common Glowlight Tetra Health Issues (and Why the Ich Heat Cure Stops at 82°F)

<VetDisclaimer />
<AffiliateDisclosure />

The glowlight tetra is a fish of about 1.6 inches from the Essequibo River in Guyana, and no disease belongs to it in particular. The problems recorded for it are the short list every small community tetra shares: fin rot, white spot, parasitic infestations and general bacterial infections. Where it differs from some relatives is temperature. The top of its range sits below the heat that kills ich, and that changes how the disease is treated.

## Signs That Need Action Today

A head count at feeding time is the daily check in a school of 10. These are the signs that cannot wait for tomorrow.

**Test ammonia and nitrite first:**

- Fish gasping at the surface or breathing fast.
- Two or more fish showing the same sign at once.
- A fish found dead. Take the body out of the tank before anything else.

**Start treatment today:**

- White spots like grains of salt on the body or fins.
- Fins that look shortened, ragged or reddened along the edges, worse than the day before.
- Fast breathing that continues once the water tests clean, since ich can infect the gills.

**Watch closely and check again tomorrow:**

- Flashing: rubbing against plants, glass or substrate.
- A fish off its food or hanging listless.
- Fins held clamped against the body.
- Color fading to dull.

The last group are early signs that fit several problems, ich among them, so a fish showing them gets a close look every day until they pass. Gasping and a lost appetite are as often the water as a parasite, which is why [telling a water problem from an illness](/blog/spotting-a-sick-fish-guide/) starts at the test kit. A fish with an open ulcer, or illness moving through several fish in a tank, is past home observation and needs an aquatic veterinarian.

## Water Quality Comes First

Glowlights do best at 75 to 82°F, pH 5.5 to 7.5 and 2 to 15 dGH, and the stripe is more colorful in acidic water. Holding the tank steady inside those ranges matters more than hitting one number. A liquid test kit used [before each water change](/blog/aquarium-water-changes-guide/) catches a drift early, and in soft water [KH is what holds pH still](/blog/freshwater-ph-gh-kh-guide/).

Food is the other half. A glowlight takes micropellets or small flakes, and light portions keep uneaten food from fouling the water, as the [glowlight tetra feeding guide](/blog/glowlight-tetra-feeding-guide/) lays out.

## Ich: Warm to the Ceiling, Then Treat

Ich, or white spot disease, is highly contagious and can kill, and it usually reaches a tank through infected water or a newly added fish. The spots tend to show long after the infection starts, so the watch list above is often the first warning.

Most strains of ich cannot survive water above 86°F, and for a fish that lives comfortably that warm, holding the tank there clears an outbreak. The [cardinal tetra](/blog/cardinal-tetra-health-issues-guide/) tolerates water reaching 86°F in short spells, so for it the heat method is possible only with strong aeration and the fish watched closely. The glowlight's range ends at 82°F, four degrees short, so the heat method is not for it. Heat still helps up to the ceiling, because the parasite multiplies faster in warm water and a faster cycle brings each new generation into reach of the treatment sooner. Raise the tank slowly to 82°F, using a reliable <AffiliateLink href="https://amzn.to/4eQY1L5" product="Submersible aquarium heater">aquarium heater</AffiliateLink>, with an air stone running for the extra oxygen warm water needs, and let one of these do the killing:

- **Aquarium salt**, never table salt, at about a tablespoon per 5 gallons, added gradually. It suits scaled fish like this one for the length of the course only, and water changes take it back out once the infection clears.
- **An ich medication**, dosed to the label or to a vet's directions. Heat and salt are weaker than a chemical treatment, because resistant strains can develop.

Keep treating until every sign of illness has gone. The [ich treatment guide](/blog/aquarium-ich-treatment-guide/) sets out the full course for fish whose ceiling falls short of 86°F. Shrimp sharing the tank come out before any medication goes in.

## Fin Rot and Other Bacterial Infections

Fin rot, in early and late stages, is on the species' recorded list. It begins as fraying at the fin edge and works inward. Treatment starts with correcting the water that let it take hold. A fish still losing fin after that needs an antibacterial medication, given in a [hospital tank](/blog/fish-quarantine-and-treatment-guide/) away from the display.

General bacterial infections and parasitic infestations by protozoa or worms complete the list. Sores and red patches on the body are the cue to get a diagnosis before choosing a medication.

## Neon Tetra Disease

Neon tetra disease is caused by a microsporidian parasite, *Pleistophora hyphessobryconis*, named for its type host, the [neon tetra](/blog/neon-tetra-health-issues-guide/). It has turned up in a broad range of aquarium fish across several families, so the name does not fence it in. A dead fish comes out of the tank the moment it is found, whatever killed it.

## Quarantine Every New Fish

New fish are the usual route for ich into an established tank, so every new glowlight spends [at least 30 days in quarantine](/blog/fish-quarantine-and-treatment-guide/) before it meets the school. A glowlight that is eating, showing a bright stripe and moving with its school in steady water is a healthy one. Most of what goes wrong with the species begins in that water or arrives with the next fish.
