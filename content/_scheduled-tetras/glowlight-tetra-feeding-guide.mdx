# Glowlight Tetra Feeding: Diet, Live Foods, and How Often to Feed

<AffiliateDisclosure />

A glowlight tetra is about 1.6 inches (4 cm) long and will take nearly anything small enough to swallow. That makes feeding simple, and it also means the keeper, not the fish, has to set the limit on how much goes in.

## What Glowlights Eat in the Wild

Wild glowlights are omnivores. Their diet is worms, small crustaceans and plant material, which is the reason a mix of foods suits them better than a single product. The captive menu below follows from that: a dried staple, with small live and frozen prey added around it.

## The Staple Diet

Build the daily diet on a quality dried food of a suitable size. A slow-sinking <AffiliateLink href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B004PBB5TK?tag=beastlyfacts-20" product="Hikari Tropical Semi-Floating Micro Pellets, 0.77 oz">micro pellet</AffiliateLink> or a small flake does the job, and either one is eaten readily by a school this size.

<KeyTakeaway>
Glowlight tetras eat almost anything, so size and portion are the only rules. Use micro pellets or small flake as the base, add small live and frozen foods for variety, and feed only what the school clears.
</KeyTakeaway>

## Live and Frozen Foods

Small live and frozen foods are the best way to widen the diet. Bloodworm, daphnia and brine shrimp are the usual choices, and freshly hatched baby brine shrimp or freeze-dried tubifex make an occasional treat. Offer them as part of the regular rotation instead of the whole meal, so the dried staple still carries the day-to-day nutrition.

<FunFact>
The genus name Hemigrammus joins the Greek words for "half" and "letter". The name points to the lateral line of the genus's type species, which stops partway along the body instead of running to the tail.
</FunFact>

## How Often to Feed, and How Much

Feed once or twice a day, and keep each feeding light. The portion is what the school clears while it is still feeding hard; anything left drifting after that is too much, and it fouls the water.

Judge a portion by watching the fish eat. If flakes or pellets reach the substrate untouched, the next pinch is smaller. Uneaten food rots into ammonia, the waste that the [nitrogen cycle](/blog/aquarium-cycling-guide/) in a mature tank has to keep clearing, and that is how an easy eater ends up in a fouled tank.

Watching also shows whether every fish is getting a share. In a mixed tank, check that bolder tankmates are not taking most of each feeding. Companions that pass [the compatibility checks for sharing a tank](/blog/aquarium-stocking-and-tankmates-guide/) keep feeding time fair for a fish this size.

## Feeding Fry

Glowlights scatter their eggs, and the parents will eat them if left in the tank, so fry are raised apart from the adults. The eggs hatch quickly, and the fry live on their yolk sacs for the first few days. Cultured infusoria is the first food, followed by baby brine shrimp and crushed dry food as they grow.

An adult glowlight asks little more than that: a small dried food, a rotation of live and frozen extras, and a keeper who stops before the water suffers. The [glowlight tetra encyclopedia profile](/encyclopedia/animal/glowlight-tetra/) has the natural history.
