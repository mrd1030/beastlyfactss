## The five patterns that appear in nearly every set

**1. The cost table does not sum to its own heading. Nine of ten.** Only the
blue-tongue skink reconciles exactly, which is the species batch E redid the
math on, so the fix works and was simply never applied anywhere else. Three
shapes: rows that do not reach the stated total (iguana $985 against a $700
floor, hognose $385 against a $500 ceiling, rat $584 against $550); the animal
priced inside a total the prose says excludes it (conure, tree frog, slider);
and a table missing items the same site's buy list says to purchase (hermit
crab, guppy, rat, skink). Every reader checked this arithmetic unprompted and
every one said it cost the site trust in the numbers they could not check.

**2. Source narration is still live in all ten sets.** Not one came back clean.
Three forms: the named source in the sentence; the unnamed form ("most sources
recommend", "sources give a real range"); and a third the rules do not yet
name, the page apologizing for its own numbers. The conure feeding guide's
"Those figures are daily targets, loose enough that they will not add to a tidy
hundred against the pellet share" and the skink feeding guide's "present it as a
range and let body condition be the actual guide" are both that third kind, and
the second reads like an unedited note to the writer. Worst placements: the
guppy hub's emergency card and the slider hub's Diet row.

**3. A maintenance cadence is missing from all ten.** No cleaning schedule, no
water change percentage, no substrate replacement interval, no dish change
frequency. Guppy and slider are the sharpest cases: both tell the reader to run
a cycled biological filter and neither says how much water to change. This is a
corpus-wide content gap, the same shape as the missing price-check line already
logged in READMEFIRST.

**4. The hub eats the deep dive instead of routing to it.** Eight of ten.
Readers quoted identical sentences on hub and article for iguana, conure, rat,
slider, hognose, skink, tree frog and box turtle. The tree frog reader: the hub
"did not summarize that page, it ate it." Two consequences: the deep dive has
nothing left to give on the click, and any error in the copied sentence now
exists twice.

**5. Deep dives cross-link outward more than inward.** The cost guide almost
never links to the tank setup guide it prices (iguana, conure, slider, box
turtle, skink, hermit crab). The health guide almost never links to the feeding
guide carrying its prevention numbers (iguana, conure, slider, box turtle, tree
frog, hermit crab). Meanwhile a conure cost guide offers koi, red-eared slider
and emperor scorpion, and an iguana handling guide links to a savannah monitor
and a tegu before any iguana page. Readers read that as traffic routing.

## Findings that need real research, not an edit

1. **Hognose legal claim.** The table says Georgia and West Virginia ban the
   native Eastern and Southern hognose. The article and the hub then conclude
   hognose ownership itself is off the table in those two states. The trade
   sells Western. If the reader is right, the site tells people a legal pet is
   illegal in two states.
2. **Box turtle legal count.** Body says four states answer with a cap. Its own
   table shows ten. North Carolina reads "Not regulated by the state" in the
   table and appears in the FAQ's list of states that restrict wild collection.
3. **Box turtle hub fun fact is false.** "They are the only turtles that can
   completely seal themselves inside their shell." The set's own handling guide
   says "very few other turtle species," which is correct.
4. **Green iguana 70% first-year mortality.** Load-bearing on four pages,
   sourced to Wikipedia citing de Vosjoli 1992.

## Two structural findings worth a decision

**Handling guides that do not teach handling.** Green iguana (C-), hognose
(C+), guppy (C). Three of ten, all flagged independently: the title promises
technique and the page delivers a risk inventory. The iguana reader listed what
was absent: pickup technique, body support, session length, acclimation
timeline, claw trimming.

**The encyclopedia is the weakest page type in the corpus.** Six of ten graded
C+ or below, with the same complaint each time: the History paragraph is the
only part that earns its place and everything above it duplicates the hub.
Several readers also found the encyclopedia carrying the one figure that
contradicts the deep dives (conure lifespan and time commitment, slider adult
size, hognose lifespan, iguana enclosure "the size of a wardrobe").

**All three legal guides had table defects.** Box turtle mixes possession caps
under a "Wild Collection" heading. Slider changes its organizing axis halfway
down, categories for five rows then state names. Hognose has rows that answer
for the wrong species. A fixed shape would fix all three: one row per state,
columns that are questions a reader asks.

# Green Iguana care set: a buyer's review

I am about to get a green iguana. I read these eight pages the way the navigation presents them, hub first. Below is what I could act on, what I could not, and where the pages argue with each other.

## 1. One line per page

**00 Care guide hub.** Finished it; it is a competent router and the emergency card and buy list are the two things I would print, but almost every sentence is lifted verbatim from a deep dive, and nothing on it points me at the feeding guide. **B-**

**01 Encyclopedia.** Finished it; the CITES and import-trade history is the only genuinely new material in the whole set and I would not have found it elsewhere, but one sentence in the overview badly undersells the enclosure. **B**

**02 Cost guide.** Finished it and took notes; the tables are itemized, dated and attributed to named retailers, which is more than most sites do, but the headline range, the table total and the annual figure do not reconcile with each other. **B**

**03 Handling guide.** Finished it in two minutes and got no handling instruction out of it; it is a risk inventory and a surrender essay with a title that promises technique. **C-**

**04 Health issues guide.** Finished it; the symptom lists are the reason I would keep the emergency card, but the page contains almost no numbers and never links to the two pages of its own that carry the prevention. **C**

**05 Tank setup guide.** Finished it and could start building from it; the most actionable page in the set, though it leaves a hole where night heat should be and offers a 55 gallon tank while arguing against short enclosures. **B**

**06 Feeding guide.** Finished it carefully; the oxalate versus goitrogen split is the single most useful passage anywhere in this set, and it is the only page with a sources block, but the greens list and its own FAQ recommend different salads and it never says how often to feed. **B+**

**07 Enrichment guide.** Finished it and enjoyed it; real field research, a priority order I can execute, and one dangling sentence that points at nothing. **A-**

## 2. Do the hub and the encyclopedia earn their place

The encyclopedia does. Origin, habitat, the CITES Appendix II date, the import volumes, the note that the wild lifespan is about 8 years and that 15 to 20 is a captive figure: none of that appears on any deep dive, and the wild-lifespan correction is the kind of thing that makes me trust a site.

The hub mostly does not. It is a router in shape, but in substance it is a clipboard of sentences copied from the articles it links to. Compare the hub's handling bullet, "Approaching from directly above triggers a strong defensive response, it reads as an aerial predator attack to the iguana, approach from the side instead," with the handling guide's "Approaching from directly above triggers a strong defensive response too, it reads as an aerial predator attack to the iguana, approach from the side instead." That is not a summary, that is the same sentence. The parietal eye fun fact is duplicated the same way on both pages. A hub should tell me which article answers which question and hand me the one number I need before I click; this one pre-empts the articles instead.

**Where the hub disagrees with a deep dive.** The worst one is a size claim it inherits from the cost guide. The hub's own bullet says "A single adult needs a minimum of 10 feet long by 5 feet wide by 6 feet tall... No commercial enclosure is large enough, this has to be a custom, walk-in build." Its FAQ then says "a made to order 6x3x6 foot box $3,740," offering as a purchase an enclosure that fails the minimum stated four hundred words above it. The cost guide body actually explains this ("nothing ready made reaches the ReptiFiles number, which is walk-in territory"), but neither FAQ carries the caveat. Change the hub FAQ to: "The adult enclosure comes a year or two later. Nothing sold ready made meets the 10x5x6 minimum. A made to order 6x3x6 box is $3,740 and still falls short, so the realistic options are a walk-in build at several hundred dollars in materials or a custom builder from $1,000 up."

Second, the bulb count. The hub's buy list says "Halogen basking bulbs, 75 watt, two or three." The tank setup guide says heat comes "via a cluster of halogen bulbs, roughly six bulbs to adequately cover an adult's basking branch." The buy list is presumably the juvenile kit, but it never says so, and it sits directly under "A plan and a budget for the walk-in adult enclosure." Change the buy list line to: "Halogen basking bulbs, 75 watt, two or three for a juvenile enclosure; budget roughly six for an adult basking branch."

Third, the encyclopedia against the tank setup guide. Encyclopedia: "needs a custom enclosure the size of a wardrobe, and lives twenty years in captivity." Tank setup: "A single adult needs a minimum of 10 feet long by 5 feet wide by 6 feet tall." A wardrobe is not a 300 cubic foot walk-in, and the whole thesis of this set is that people underestimate the space. Change the encyclopedia to: "needs a custom walk-in enclosure roughly ten feet long and six feet tall, closer to a small room than a piece of furniture, and lives twenty years in captivity."

## 3. The set as a whole

Could I set one up? Nearly. I have basking, cool end, night floor, humidity, substrate depth and type, UVB type and replacement interval, and the adult footprint. What I do not have: **a night heat source.** The guide tells me nights must stay in the low 70s and that heat comes from halogen bulbs, which are lights. Nothing anywhere tells me what runs at night, and the buy list has no ceramic emitter or radiant panel on it. I also have no UVB mounting distance or UV index target for a six foot tall enclosure, no ventilation guidance for a humid walk-in, and **no cleaning schedule at all**: nowhere in eight pages does anyone tell me how often to spot clean or replace four inches of coco husk held at 70% humidity.

Could I buy? For the juvenile, yes, item by item. For the adult, no. The set tells me a walk-in costs several hundred dollars in materials and never tells me what those materials are, how many UVB fixtures a ten foot enclosure needs, or what six halogen bulbs and a thermostat rated for them cost. The buy list also includes branches, substrate and a soaking tub that the cost guide explicitly excludes from its own total and never prices anywhere.

Could I feed? Almost. I know the ratios, the greens, the traps and the supplement schedule. I do not know **how often to feed**, at any age. The closest the set gets is "The general reptile figure is 1 to 4% of body weight daily on a dry-matter basis," which is not something a person with a bowl of collards can execute. Nor is there anything on chop size for a hatchling, on a water dish as distinct from the soaking tub, or on how often to soak.

Could I keep it healthy? I can recognize the emergencies. I cannot prevent them from the health page alone, because that page carries no Ca:P ratio, no temperature, no UVB spec, and no link to the two pages that do.

**Questions raised on one page and answered on another without saying so:**

- The health guide says MBD is "Caused by low dietary calcium relative to phosphorus, insufficient vitamin D3, or inadequate UVB exposure" and stops. The feeding guide has the 2:1 minimum ratio and the age-by-age dusting schedule. No link between them.
- The handling guide says owners are unprepared "by the time that adult needs a walk-in enclosure." The cost guide prices that walk-in. The handling guide links to a savannah monitor and a tegu instead.
- The enrichment guide's whole argument is that space is the problem and everything else is secondary. The tank setup guide has the space numbers. The enrichment guide does not link to it, which brings me to a broken sentence below.
- The set never mentions outdoor housing, yet the sidebar carries "Outdoor Reptile Housing: Pens, Predators, and What Actually Kills Them," which is the obvious partial answer for a six foot arboreal herbivore in a warm climate. No iguana page says so.

## 4. Overlap and conflict across the deep dives

What I read twice or more: the 6 to 7 foot adult size (hub, encyclopedia, handling body, handling FAQ, enrichment); the 70% first-year mortality figure (hub, health fun fact, cost body, cost FAQ); the 10x5x6 minimum (hub bullet, hub FAQ, tank body, tank FAQ, cost body); "Adults are territorial" (hub, handling, enrichment body, enrichment FAQ). The parietal eye appears twice in near-identical wording.

Conflicts:

**Animal protein.** Health guide and hub: "no insects, no dog or cat food, no eggs, ever." Feeding guide: "There is a published tolerance, at under 5% of an adult iguana's diet and up to 10% for young growing animals." The feeding guide then argues for zero, which is defensible, but a reader who meets "ever" first and the tolerance second concludes the site is guessing. Add to the health guide: "The feeding guide covers the small published tolerance and why the practical recommendation is still zero."

**Juvenile housing.** Tank setup: "A juvenile can start temporarily in something like a 4x2x4 foot enclosure or a 40 to 55 gallon tank." Same page, four paragraphs later: "A wide, short enclosure undersells this species just as much as a small one does." A 55 gallon tank is about thirteen inches shorter than it is wide. Change to: "A juvenile can start temporarily in a 4x2x4 foot enclosure. A 40 to 55 gallon tank will physically hold a hatchling but gives an arboreal animal almost no height, so treat it as a stopgap of weeks, not months."

**Greens list against its own FAQ.** Body: "The calcium-rich greens are collard, beet, mustard and turnip greens, alfalfa hay, bok choy, kale, parsley, Swiss chard, romaine, kohlrabi, escarole, chicory and dandelion." FAQ: "Build the greens on collard, mustard, turnip and dandelion greens, escarole and romaine." The FAQ is right and the body list quietly includes three oxalate risks and two goitrogens without marking them. Split the body list: "Base the salad on collard and turnip greens, dandelion, escarole, romaine, chicory and kohlrabi. Rotate in, never base on: kale and mustard greens (goitrogenic), Swiss chard, beet greens and spinach (oxalates)."

**Bulb count**, as above: cost table "two or three" against tank setup "roughly six bulbs."

## 5. The tables

The juvenile table sums to **$985 low and $1,006 high** ($549 + $99 + $27/$48 + $130 + $180). The prose says "roughly $985 to $1,000." Close enough to be forgivable, but I would write "$985 to $1,006" since the whole point of the table is that it is exact.

The real problem is the heading above it: **"Upfront setup: $700 to $2,500 or more."** The page's own itemized minimum is $985 before branches, substrate and the soaking tub. A $700 floor is not reachable from any combination of these rows. Change the heading and its first line to: "Upfront setup: $1,000 to $2,500 or more. The itemized juvenile kit below comes to $985 before branches, substrate and a soaking tub, so treat $1,200 as a realistic floor."

**"All-in annual ownership lands around $2,140 once every category is counted"** contradicts the same page's "roughly $60 to $150 a month," which is $720 to $1,800 a year. The FAQ hedges it ("One cost aggregator models..."), the body states it flat. Change the body to: "Month to month that is $60 to $150. One cost aggregator, counting vet care and enclosure replacement across the year, models an all-in figure near $2,140, which is what the monthly number looks like once the irregular costs are averaged in."

Vague and placeholder cells I would change:

- `Walk-in build, materials only | several hundred dollars` → `$400 to $900 in plywood, welded mesh and hardware (Reptile.Guide estimate)`. A "Cost Range" column should not contain a phrase.
- `Custom builder | $1,000 and up` → `$1,000 to $3,500, quoted per build`. "And up" in a table is not a range.
- `Halogen basking bulbs, 75 watt, two or three | $27 - $48` → `$13 to $16 each, $27 to $48 for two or three`. As written the unit price silently changes between the low and high figure.
- Nothing in the second table carries the enclosure's internal height against the stated minimum. I would add a column, "Meets 10x5x6 minimum," reading No, No, No, Yes, Yes.

Cells that are missing entirely: **there is no vet table.** "Budget for an annual exam at minimum" is the whole of the numeric guidance on a page whose job is numbers. Add a row set: annual exam $60 to $150, fecal screen $25 to $50, radiographs for a suspected MBD case $200 to $500, egg-binding surgery $500 to $1,500.

## 6. The one link each page most needed

- **Hub.** "Diet: Strictly plant-based: no insects, no dog or cat food, no eggs, ever." It links to the health guide. It should link to **Green Iguana Feeding: Building the Salad and the Protein Mistake**, which is otherwise unreachable from any first-week bullet.
- **Encyclopedia.** "needs a custom enclosure the size of a wardrobe" should link to **Green Iguana Enclosure Setup**.
- **Cost guide.** "Budget for an annual exam at minimum, and know that emergency care for this species tends toward the expensive end" should link to **Common Green Iguana Health Issues**, so I can see what I am budgeting against.
- **Handling guide.** "Buying a $30 baby without budgeting for what a 6-foot adult actually needs." should link to **How Much Does a Green Iguana Really Cost?** This page links to two other species and never once to its own.
- **Health issues guide.** "Caused by low dietary calcium relative to phosphorus, insufficient vitamin D3, or inadequate UVB exposure." should link to **Green Iguana Feeding** for the ratio and the schedule.
- **Tank setup guide.** "Chronically low humidity is directly linked to dehydration and kidney disease." should link to **Common Green Iguana Health Issues**.
- **Feeding guide.** Well linked already. If one more, "an iguana that gets the powder and no usable UVB still ends up short" should point at **Green Iguana Enclosure Setup** for the T5 HO spec, not only at the generic UVB guide.
- **Enrichment guide.** "Read it before the rest of this." This sentence has **no antecedent and no link**. It follows a paragraph about needing a custom enclosure or a room, so it must point to **Green Iguana Enclosure Setup**. Change to: "Read the enclosure setup guide before the rest of this page."

## 7. Trust

What made me doubt them:

- The 70% first-year mortality figure appears four times and traces to "Wikipedia's entry on green iguanas in captivity, citing de Vosjoli (1992)." A thirty-plus year old figure, cited through Wikipedia, load-bearing across four pages. I would either find the primary source or write "a widely repeated 1992 estimate."
- The encyclopedia says iguana imports were "roughly a third of all reptiles brought into the United States in 1997" and, in the next sentence, that from 1996 to 2012 the species "made up close to half of the country's entire live reptile trade." A window containing 1997 cannot average to half if 1997 was a third, unless the two figures count different things. Say which.
- The hub uses an n=8 study as a general rule: "Do not skip parasite screening, given what the captive welfare assessment found: eight iguanas observed over 22 days." The enrichment guide honestly calls it "A tiny sample." The hub drops the hedge.
- The handling guide does not teach handling. No pickup technique, no body support, no session length, no taming timeline, no claw trimming despite opening with claws.

The sentence that most convinced me a keeper wrote this: **"A structure that shifts under a six-foot lizard teaches the animal that height is unsafe, which is the opposite of the point."** Nobody writes that from a source list. Runner-up, same page: "a basking platform sized for a whole adult iguana rather than the front half of one."

Sentences where the page talks about itself rather than the animal:

- Cost guide: "The 4x2x4 is a working choice for a growing juvenile, the usual first box in the hobby, and not a number from a vet reference." Cut the last clause.
- Cost guide: "That is what 'no commercial tank is large enough' means above: you can buy a box, but not one that meets the minimum." Keep the second half, cut the self-reference.
- Health guide: "If preventable, husbandry-driven health issues are a new concept, our Savannah Monitor health issues guide covers the exact same pattern in a similarly demanding large lizard." This exists to carry a link.
- Sidebar copy: "The forage list this site was missing."
- Enrichment: "Thermal choice is the item that ranks first in almost every reptile enrichment study that has bothered to test it." Cut "that has bothered to test it."

## 8. Set grade: B-

Page for page this is above what the category normally offers. The feeding guide's oxalate and goitrogen split, the enrichment guide's Burghardt material with an honest note that growth-rate correlation is not a controlled experiment, the encyclopedia's correction that 15 to 20 years is captive and the wild figure is about 8, and the cost guide's dated, attributed tables are all better than average. What drags it to B- is that these are eight pages, not a set. The hub duplicates instead of routing and never points at feeding. The health page is a hollow center that lists symptoms and outsources every prevention number without linking to where it lives. The numbers do not close: $700 against $985, $2,140 against $1,800, two bulbs against six, a 6x3x6 box sold under a 10x5x6 minimum. And the handling guide does not do the thing its title says. A careful reader can assemble a working plan from this, but only by holding five tabs open and adjudicating the conflicts themselves, which is the job the site was supposed to do.

**Prioritized fixes**

1. **Cost guide and hub FAQ.** "a made to order 6x3x6 foot box $3,740" → "a made to order 6x3x6 box at $3,740, which still falls short of the 10x5x6 minimum; realistically a walk-in build at several hundred dollars in materials or a custom builder from $1,000."
2. **Cost guide heading.** "Upfront setup: $700 to $2,500 or more" → "Upfront setup: $1,000 to $2,500 or more," with a line noting the itemized kit is $985 before branches, substrate and tub.
3. **Tank setup guide.** Add a night-heat paragraph. Nothing in the set says what holds the low 70s after the halogens go off. Specify a ceramic heat emitter or radiant panel on a separate thermostat, and add it to the hub buy list.
4. **Health issues guide.** Under MBD, after "Caused by low dietary calcium relative to phosphorus, insufficient vitamin D3, or inadequate UVB exposure," add "The feeding guide has the 2:1 minimum calcium to phosphorus ratio and the dusting schedule by age; the enclosure setup guide has the T5 HO UVB spec and the 12 month replacement interval," each as a link.
5. **Feeding guide.** Split the greens list into a base list and a rotate-in list, wording as given in section 4, and add a feeding frequency line: "Feed juveniles daily, adults daily to every other day, offering fresh salad in the morning when the basking site is already up to temperature."
6. **Encyclopedia.** "the size of a wardrobe" → "a custom walk-in roughly ten feet long and six feet tall, closer to a small room than a piece of furniture."
7. **Enrichment guide.** "Read it before the rest of this." → "Read the enclosure setup guide before the rest of this page," linked.
8. **Handling guide.** Add the missing technique section: how to lift with the body supported and the tail unrestrained, how long a first session should run, an acclimation timeline in weeks, and claw trimming. Then add the missing sentence the whole page implies: "Because so many adults are surrendered, a rescue is often the better place to get one, and the animal comes with a known age and history."
9. **Cost guide.** Add the vet cost rows listed in section 5, and price the branches, substrate and soaking tub that the buy list tells me to purchase.
10. **Hub.** Replace the verbatim-copied bullets with one-line pointers. For example, the handling bullet becomes "Approach from the side, never from above, and never restrain by the tail. The handling guide has the rest."

## 9. Not covered anywhere

Checked against the shared class guides in the Health and More list. These remain unanswered:

- A night heat source that maintains the low 70s once the halogen cluster is off.
- Enclosure cleaning schedule: spot clean interval and full substrate replacement interval for a humid walk-in.
- Feeding frequency by age, in meals per day or per week.
- UVB mounting distance, UV index target, and how many fixtures a 10x5x6 enclosure needs.
- How to actually pick up and hold an iguana, plus a taming or acclimation timeline.
- Claw trimming.
- Sexing, the age males mature, and how to manage the breeding-season window beyond expecting it.
- Vet costs in dollars, and how to find a qualified reptile vet.
- A materials list or build method for the walk-in adult enclosure.
- Legality: whether green iguanas are restricted in any US state, which is conspicuous given the encyclopedia's note on established Florida populations.
- Rescue and adoption as an acquisition route, despite the surrender rate being a stated theme.
- Weight and growth tracking as a routine health measure.

# Green Cheek Conure page set: a buyer's review

## 1. One line per page

**00 Care Guide Hub, grade C.** I finished it, and I can act on almost every number in the first-week list, but roughly two thirds of its sentences are copied verbatim out of the deep dives, so finishing it means reading the same prose twice.

**01 Encyclopedia, grade C minus.** I finished it in a minute, the history section is the only thing here I could not get elsewhere, and one sentence in the overview is flatly contradicted by the rest of the set.

**02 Cost guide, grade C.** I finished it and I can budget from the prose, but both tables are broken enough that I stopped trusting the arithmetic and fell back on the paragraphs.

**03 Handling guide, grade B minus.** I finished it, I can act on the warning signs and the sexing answer, and the one thing I actually needed, how to teach a step-up, is named but never taught.

**04 Health guide, grade B.** I finished it, the emergency list and the husbandry pattern at the end are directly actionable, and the causal framing is the clearest writing in the set outside the enrichment page.

**05 Cage setup guide, grade B minus.** I finished it and I could buy and furnish a cage from it tonight, but the light schedule does not close arithmetically and the fun fact box is about color genetics, not cages.

**06 Feeding guide, grade B plus.** I finished it, it is the only page with a sources block, and the diet percentages do not sum while the page tells me not to worry about that.

**07 Enrichment guide, grade A minus.** I finished it fast, every line is a thing I can do this week, and the priority order is the single most useful list in the set.

## 2. Do the hub and the encyclopedia earn their place?

The hub mostly does not. It is presented as a router, and a router is what I wanted, but it routes by reprinting. "Out-of-cage time, several hours of supervised free time daily, isn't optional, it's essential for both physical and mental health." is the identical sentence on the hub and in the cage guide. Same for the bar spacing sentence, the temperature paragraph, and the cage size paragraph. A hub that quotes its children in full is a hub I have to read twice. I would cut every first-week bullet to a number plus a clause plus the link: "Bar spacing: 1/2 to 5/8 inch. Why, and what else the cage needs: Cage Setup."

The hub also has a routing bug that matters more than the duplication. Its "Diet" bullet links to the health guide, its "Pellet conversion" bullet links to the shared conversion guide, and its "Feeding style" bullet links to the enrichment guide. Not one first-week bullet links to "Conure Feeding: Why the Seed Dish Is the Problem," which is the page that actually answers what to put in the bowl. The species feeding guide appears only in the "Where to go next" list and the sidebar.

The encyclopedia does earn its place, but only in the History block. Massena and Souance in 1854, the yellow-sided bird sold as a separate species until breeders showed it was a sex-linked opaline, Steve Garvin crossing cinnamon and yellow-sided to make the pineapple: that is real, specific, and available nowhere else in the set. The Overview block, by contrast, contains the worst sentence on the site.

Hub and deep dive disagreements:

**Vet cost.** Hub: "a routine wellness exam commonly runs $80 to $160." Cost guide table: "| Annual avian vet check | $70 - $120 |". The cost guide's own prose says $80 to $160. The table is the odd one out.

# Reading the Fancy Rat set as a buyer

## 1. One line per page

## 2. Does the hub earn its place, and does it disagree with the deep dives?

The hub earns it as a router, not as reading. Almost every first-week bullet is copied word for word from its source article. "A tank, even a large one, traps ammonia far more effectively than an open wire cage does" is the setup guide's sentence with the contraction expanded. That is a defensible design for a page you land on first, but it means the hub adds no information, only sequence, and the sequence is genuinely good: cage before size before spacing before temperature before bedding, then company, then food, then handling, then money.

The encyclopedia earns its place differently. It repeats only two things from the care set, adult size and lifespan, and both match. Everything else is natural history the care pages do not carry. It does zero work for someone about to buy, and it does not pretend to.

Disagreements between the hub and the deep dives:

**Vet cost.** Hub: "$60 to $110 for a routine exotic-vet wellness exam." Cost guide, same figure, then immediately: "expect that figure to roughly double once you're accounting for a full pair rather than a single rat." The hub is the page that insists you buy two rats, and it quotes the single-rat vet number without the doubling. Change the hub bullet to "$120 to $220 a year for routine exotic-vet wellness exams for a pair."

**Water bottles.** Hub buy list: "Food dishes and more than one water bottle." Cost guide table: "Food dishes and a water bottle | $10 - $18." One bottle is priced, two are recommended. Change the table row to "Food dishes and two water bottles | $16 - $28."

**Head tilt urgency.** Hub emergency card is headed "call the vet now if you see any of these" and lists head tilt, then ends "The last three are a reason to book a routine visit." Health guide FAQ: "Labored or noisy breathing, a new lump anywhere on the body, a head tilt, sudden lethargy or hunched posture, and any sign of pain or sudden behavior change all warrant prompt veterinary attention." Those cannot both be right. More on this below.

One smaller defect: the Adult size bullet is the only line in the entire first-week list with no source link attached. It should link to the encyclopedia page, which is where those numbers live.

## 3. Could I actually keep a rat after reading all of this?

Mostly. I could choose and buy a cage, check the bar spacing, set the room temperature, pick bedding, pick a wheel of the right diameter, buy the right food, know the toxic list, pick a rat up correctly, and recognize the disease most likely to kill it. That is a lot, and it is more than most care sites deliver.

What is missing, in rough order of how badly I would have wanted it:

- **How to introduce two rats.** Every page insists on a pair or a trio. No page says whether pet store cage-mates arrive bonded, how to introduce two unfamiliar rats, or how long that takes. This is the first thing that happens after the purchase the whole set is built around.
- **What happens when one of a pair dies.** With a two to three year lifespan, this is close to certain, and it lands the owner back at "never one" with an aging survivor. Nothing addresses it.
- **A portion number.** The feeding guide is subtitled "Diet, Portions" and says "weighing out a measured daily portion is a meaningful habit rather than a fussy one" without ever giving a weight. I have a scale and no target.
- **A cage size for two rats.** Every page repeats 24x24x24 inches as the minimum for one, and every page says that minimum "applies before adding a second or third rat, not after." Not one of them gives the number for two. The Critter Nation is named as the answer four times and its dimensions are never printed.
- **A cleaning routine.** "Changed at least twice a week" appears on four pages. Nothing says whether that is a full strip or a spot clean, what to use, or what a daily pass looks like. For a set whose central thesis is that ammonia shortens the animal's life, the actual cleaning procedure is the missing step.
- **Money for the two most likely medical events.** "Emergency and surgical costs run higher: mammary tumor removal is one of the more common surgical costs in this species" carries no figure. And the health guide recommends "Spaying a female rat before 7 months of age" while the cost guide never mentions spaying at all.
- **Where to get them, and sexing.** Rescue, breeder and pet store are priced but not compared, and nothing warns that mis-sexed store rats produce litters.

Questions raised on one page whose answer is on another, without the text saying so:

- The setup guide says scatter feeding "turns mealtime into foraging enrichment." The enrichment guide has a whole foraging category and the line "Throwing the ration across the cage instead of putting it in a bowl converts a two minute event into a foraging session." Neither points at the other.
- The health guide's bumblefoot paragraph argues "for solid-floored shelves and a soft hammock to rest in instead." The setup guide specifies solid ramped shelves and hammocks. No link.
- The handling guide says rats "can learn to come when called, respond to their own name, and perform simple tricks." The enrichment guide has the actual method: "a clicker and target stick is the whole kit." No link.
- The feeding guide warns that "A high-calorie diet also raises the risk of the pituitary tumors older rats develop" and does link to health. That one is done right, and shows the others could have been.

## 4. Overlap and conflict across the deep dives

The real conflict is substrate depth, three-way:

- Setup guide and hub: "at least 2 to 3 inches deep to support burrowing."
- Cost guide: "a 4 inch layer in a 36x18 inch indoor enclosure."
- Enrichment guide: "cypress mulch deep enough to disappear into... do not use a shallow scattering of substrate and call it deep, because the behavior depends on being able to get under it."

An adult is 4.5 to 7 inches long. It cannot disappear into 2 inches. Change the setup guide and hub to "at least 4 inches, deeper if the turtle can still be seen when it burrows down," which is the depth the cost guide already budgets for.

# White's Tree Frog page set: a buyer's review

## 1. One line per page

**00, Care Guide Hub.** Would finish it, and it is the only page I would print: every first-week line carries a real number and a link to where it came from, and I could shop off it. Grade **A-**.

**01, Encyclopedia.** Would finish it in ninety seconds, and the 1790 John White naming story is worth the ninety seconds, but nothing here changes what I do on Saturday. Grade **B**.

**02, Cost Guide.** Would finish it, and I can act on the annual table, but the upfront table does not add up to the number in its own heading and its FAQ contradicts its own body. Grade **C+**.

**03, Handling Guide.** Would finish it, and the plain-water-no-soap rule plus the wetted nitrile alternative is directly actionable, but the two most precise numbers on the page arrive with nothing behind them. Grade **B**.

**04, Health Issues.** Would finish it, obesity section is genuinely useful, but it names quarantine as the whole chytrid defense and never tells me how long quarantine runs. Grade **B-**.

**05, Tank Setup.** Would finish it, and then realize I had already read all of it on the hub, and that it still has not told me how to ventilate the box its own thesis is about. Grade **C**.

**06, Feeding.** Would finish it twice. The ridge test is the single most usable thing in the set and the clinical description of an obese frog is the most convincing writing on the site. Grade **A-**.

**07, Enrichment.** Would finish it, and it is the best-written page here, honest about resting a species recommendation on a study of a different species. Grade **A-**.

## 2. Do the hub and the encyclopedia earn their place?

The hub earns it as a router and then overshoots into being a replacement. Its first-week block is excellent structurally: one number, one sentence of why, one link. But compare the hub's UVB line, "A low-output T5 bulb in the 5 to 7% range works well, positioned so the basking branch sits at least 6 inches below the fixture, and the bulb gets replaced every 9 to 12 months on a schedule," with the tank setup guide's UVB section, "A low-output T5 bulb in the 5 to 7% range works well, positioned so the basking branch sits at least 6 inches below the fixture." The hub says strictly more. That pattern repeats for size, temperature, humidity, substrate and water. By the time I clicked through to the tank setup guide, it had one fact left to give me: that a low-wattage bulb or side-mounted mat is what supplies the heat. The hub did not summarize that page, it ate it.

The encyclopedia does earn its place, cleanly, because none of its content appears anywhere else: the 1790 description as *Rana caerulea*, the blue preserved specimens, the type specimen destroyed in the Hunterian bombing. That is a page I would send someone. Two objections. It carries no in-body links at all, so it is a dead end. And "have become the most popular pet frog worldwide" is a ranking claim with nothing behind it, which I would either source or downgrade to "are among the most commonly kept pet frogs."

**Hub versus deep dive, direct disagreement.** One, and it matters. The hub's obesity check reads: "Fat deposits build up visibly over the tympanum (the external eardrum) and in the armpit area." The feeding guide's ridge test reads: "**No visible ridges.** The frog is likely underweight" and "**Ridges present and defined.** This is the shape to aim for." A first-time keeper looking at the same anatomy is told by the hub that visible structure over the eardrum is fat, and by the feeding guide that visible defined structure over the eardrum is the goal. I would rewrite the hub line to: "Above the eardrum you should see defined ridges. Ridges that bulge, sag, or fold over, plus fat filling the armpits, mean the frog is overweight. No visible ridges at all means underweight."

That collision is worsened by a naming problem across the whole set. The same region gets called "the ridges that sit just above the frog's eardrum" (feeding), "over the tympanum" (health, hub), "the crests above the eyes" (feeding, same page), "a large, overhanging parotoid gland above the eye" (encyclopedia), "parotoid glands and lipid ridges" (hub), and "a moderate fat roll over the shoulders" (health). Pick one term, define it once, use it everywhere.

## 3. Could I actually set up, buy, feed and keep one?

Buy: yes. Feed: yes, with confidence. Set up: mostly, with a gap I would have to fill elsewhere. Keep long term: not quite.

What I have: enclosure dimensions, a temperature gradient with a night figure, a humidity target with an explicit cycling instruction, substrate options, UVB spec and replacement interval, a feeder list, an adult and juvenile feeding schedule, a supplement frequency, a body condition test, a handling protocol, a group housing rule, a five-item emergency list, and a budget.

What is missing after reading everything:

- **Ventilation as a specification.** Four separate pages blame poor ventilation for red-leg and bacterial infection. No page says what to do about it. The tank setup guide goes straight from "Constant, unbroken high humidity is directly linked to bacterial and red-leg issues" to a substrate heading. I need a sentence: screen top, cross-flow, front vents, whatever the answer is.
- **A cleaning schedule.** The health guide indicts "an infrequently cleaned water dish" and never says what frequent means. Daily? Every other day?
- **Photoperiod.** No page gives hours of light per day. The linked UVB guide may cover it, but that link appears in exactly one sentence on one page and is not in either sidebar list.
- **A thermostat and a failure plan.** A "low-wattage bulb or a side-mounted heat mat" with no thermostat is how people cook amphibians. No page mentions one, and the cost tables have no line for one.
- **Misting method.** "Once or twice daily" implies I am home twice a day forever. Hand mister, auto mister, fogger, none discussed.
- **Named plants.** "Broad-leafed live plants" and a $25 to $45 line item, and not one species named. Pothos? Bromeliads? I cannot buy from that.
- **Calling.** Nobody tells me a male will call, loudly, at night, indefinitely. For an animal marketed at beginners in a bedroom, that is the single most common regret and it is absent.
- **A quarantine enclosure.** The hub requires six to eight weeks of quarantine. The cost guide's setup table has no line for a second enclosure.

**Questions raised on one page whose answer sits on another, unsignposted:**

- The health guide says "Prevention through proper quarantine of new frogs, which is where the actual window sits, is the real defense here" and never gives a duration. The hub has it: "Six to eight weeks is typically adequate for a new amphibian." The health guide should carry that number inline.
