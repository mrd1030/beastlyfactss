# Rummy-Nose Tetra (Petitella bleheri, P. rhodostoma, P. georgiae): Research Notes

Researched 2026-10-31. Research only: no article copy here. Every claim below comes from a page that was opened and read during this session. Quotes are short extracts from those pages. Where a source page used an en dash in a range (for example 23 to 26°C, or a life-dates span), the range is written here with a hyphen or "to", because this file carries no em or en dashes. Quoted text keeps the source's own spelling (some sources are British: "colour", "behaviour").

Species key used throughout. "Rummy-nose tetra" is three look-alike fish. Figures are tagged by species wherever the source names one:
- [BLE] Petitella bleheri, formerly Hemigrammus bleheri. Firehead or brilliant rummy-nose tetra. The fish most often sold as "rummy-nose".
- [RHO] Petitella rhodostoma (or rhodostomus, see 1.3), formerly Hemigrammus rhodostomus. The "true" rummy-nose.
- [GEO] Petitella georgiae. False rummy-nose.
- [ALL] the source treats the three together, or says "rummy-nose" without a species.

## 1. Identity and naming

1.1 Three species under one trade name
- Practical Fishkeeping: "The 'true' Rummy-nose is Petitella rhodostomus", "most of those seen in the shops are P. bleheri", "sometimes also referred to as the Firehead tetra", "A third fish, Petitella georgiae, is known as the False rummy-nose." (S24)
- Seriously Fish [BLE]: "This is the species most commonly sold as 'rummy-nose tetra' in aquatic outlets." (S21). Seriously Fish [RHO]: "Most fish sold under the name are the firehead tetra, H. bleheri". (S22)
- Fishkeeping News: P. georgiae is "a much rarer import than what is now P.bleheri". (S29)
- Aquarium Co-Op covers all three and says "The care requirements for these three species are very similar because they all come from blackwater rivers". (S31)
- Reading for writers: the fish a reader buys is almost certainly [BLE]. Build the care guides on [BLE] figures and note [RHO] and [GEO] where they differ.

1.2 Genus change (all three now Petitella)
- Bittencourt et al. (2020): "the sister-taxon relationship of the rummy-nose tetras Hemigrammus bleheri and Petitella georgiae was strongly supported by molecular and morphological data. Therefore, we propose to transfer the rummy-nose tetras H. bleheri and H. rhodostomus to the genus Petitella." (S1) https://www.ni.bio.br/?p=3638
- Same paper: "Petitella georgiae is likely to be a species complex comprised of at least two species." Basis given in the discussion: "The striking p-distance divergence (9.1%) between the Peruvian P. georgiae and our samples from the Purus River". (S1)
- Fishkeeping News reports the change and adds that a fourth Petitella species may already be in the hobby depending on collection site (paraphrase). (S29)
- Seriously Fish: "Bittencourt et al. (2020) proposed moving it to Petitella." (S21, S22)

1.5 Name meanings (ETYFish, S17) https://etyfish.org/acestrorhamphidae/
- Petitella: "in honor of French zoologist-anatomist Georges Petit (1892-1973)", with "-ella, Latin diminutive connoting endearment".
- rhodostoma: "rose-mouthed, from rhódon (ῥόδον), rose, and stóma (στόμα), mouth, referring to red nose and mouth area".
- bleheri: "in honor of German explorer and ornamental fish wholesaler and supplier Heiko Bleher (1944-2025)", "who collected topotype specimens".
- georgiae: "in honor of Géry's wife, Georgie, or Georgette".

1.6 Common names
- ITIS: [BLE] "red-nose tetra", "rummy-nose tetra" (S11); [RHO] "rednosed tetra" (S12); [GEO] "false red nose tetra", "false rummy-nosed tetra" (S13).
- FishBase: [BLE] "Firehead tetra" (S14); [RHO] "Rummy-nose tetra" (S15); [GEO] "False rummynose tetra" (S16).
- Brazilian trade name for [BLE]: "rodostomo" (S3).
- Golden form [BLE]: "A selectively-bred "golden" variant has also been produced by commercial breeders" (S21).
- Caveat on the research papers (my reading, not a source claim): S5 to S9 all name their fish H. rhodostomus and bought them from commercial suppliers ("purchased from Amazonie Labège", S5; "bought from a professional supplier", S9). Given 1.1, some lab stock may have been [BLE]. Write the research facts as "rummy-nose tetras" rather than pinning them to one species.

1.7 Telling them apart (Seriously Fish notes, S21; these notes predate the genus move and still use Hemigrammus vs Petitella as the split)
- "H. bleheri is the only one of the 3 in which the red extends beyond the gill covers."
- Caudal peduncle: all three have a dark blotch at the top; "If the bottom blotch is absent, you are probably looking at a Petitella." (Under the new taxonomy all three are Petitella, so read this as "absent means [GEO]".)
- Lateral line from the central tail band: "This is quite broad in P. georgiae, narrow in H. rhodostomus and almost non-exisitent in H. bleheri." (source's typo kept)
- The red head alone is not conclusive because "if the fish are not in good condition the red colour can fade considerably." (S21)

## 2. Origin and wild habitat

- Distribution, FishBase: [BLE] "South America: Negro and Meta River basins." (S14). [RHO] "South America: lower Amazon River basin and Orinoco River basin." (S15). [GEO] "South America: upper Amazon River basin in Peru; Purus, Negro, and Madeira River basins." (S16)
- Seriously Fish: [BLE] "Rio Meta and Rio Negro in Colombia and Brazil." (S21). [RHO] "Rio Orinoco and the lower Amazon basin in Venezuela and Brazil." (S22). [GEO] "Native to the Rio Purus in Peru and Brazil, as well as the Rio Negro and Rio Madeira in Brazil." (S23)
- Water type. [BLE] and [RHO]: "Blackwater rivers and tributaries. The water in these biotopes is typically stained brown with tannins from decaying organic material and is very acidic as a result." (S22; S21 says the same). [GEO]: "Mainly found in clear water tributaries and small rivers." (S23). Fishkeeper: "The natural conditions of this species are very soft & acidic blackwaters." (S30)
- Plants: Seriously Fish [BLE]: "Aquatic plants are not a feature of this species' natural waters." (S21). Fishkeeper: "Aquatic plants are rare in their natural environment". (S30)
- Fishers' knowledge, Barcelos, middle Rio Negro [BLE]: fishers say "streams and the igapós, are inhabited by fish species belonging to the piabas", rodostomo among them. Rodostomo is listed among species that make the "arribação", "an event that consists of the displacement of some species" with seasonal water changes. Fisher citations for its behavior: 36 for living in shoals, 32 for migratory movement, 8 resident, 4 solitary (paraphrase of Table 4). (S3) https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8244192/
- Seasonal cycle [BLE], wild: "Females mature with the flood pulse (December to May)", with spawning peaking "at the end of the flood (March to May)." (S2)
- Measured wild water chemistry: not obtained. The Küchler, Miekeley and Forsberg (2000) Rio Negro chemistry paper (DOI 10.1590/S0103-50532000000300015) was located but no copy could be opened (see Gaps).

## 5. Tank

5.1 Minimum tank size and footprint
- Seriously Fish (all three): "its active swimming nature requires a tank length of 36 inches/90 cm minimum." Base dimensions "not recorded". (S21 to S23)
- FishBase: "minimum aquarium size 80 cm" for [RHO] and [GEO], with "groups of 5 or more individuals". (S15, S16)
- Aquarium Co-Op: "a 20-gallon tank or bigger is more suitable", because they "prefer a longer tank to swim back and forth". (S31)
- Aquarium Source: "should not be kept in a tank smaller than 20 gallons." (S32)
- Retailer spec (not a source): Aquatic Arts lists a 10 gallon minimum (see 12).
- DISAGREEMENT: 80 cm (FishBase) vs 90 cm/36 in (Seriously Fish). A 20 gallon long is 30 in; a 36 in tank is a 30 to 40 gallon footprint. Co-Op and Aquarium Source say 20 gallons. Footprint length is the point every source makes; writers should give the length.

5.2 Group size and shoaling research
- Husbandry minimums: Seriously Fish "groups numbering 10 or preferably more" (S21 to S23); Fishkeeper "particularly striking when kept as a shoal of twenty or more" (S30); Aquarium Co-Op "Get at least 8-12 tetras and you won't regret it." (S31); Aquarium Source "groups of 6 or more" (FAQ says 6 to 10) (S32); FishBase "groups of 5 or more individuals" (S15, S16).
- Seriously Fish [BLE]: "They are one of the more tightly shoaling small tetras". (S21)
- Practical Fishkeeping: "Rummy-noses are fairly reliable at sticking reasonably close to each other but don't expect 'Red Arrows' type synchrony!" (S24)
- Research (none sets an aquarium minimum, but all show the school is the animal's normal state):
  - Species chosen for schooling research: "This species was chosen because it exhibits a strong schooling behavior." Swimming style: "a burst-and-coast type of swimming characterized by sequences of sudden increase in speed followed by a mostly passive gliding period." Finding: "both attraction and alignment behaviors control the reaction of fish to a neighbor". (S5)
  - Group size effect on group decisions: tested groups of "n ∈ {1, 2, 4, 5, 8, 10, 20}"; "U-turns are mostly initiated by fish located at the front of the school"; groups of 10 make "one U-turn every 20 min", which "is two orders of magnitude larger that in groups of two fish (one U-turn every 0.2 min)". (S6)
  - Who each fish watches: "each fish pays attention to only a small subset of its neighbors, typically one or two". (S7, reporting earlier work)
  - Lateral line [BLE]: fish with the whole lateral system disabled by antibiotics could not keep a shoal; they recovered in about a month (paraphrase). "the lateral system is thus more crucial to shoaling behaviour than previously believed." (S4). Practical Fishkeeping report of the same paper: "This indicates that the laterosensory system is more crucial to schooling than previously supposed." (S26)
  - Vision: "vision is both necessary and sufficient for schooling in H. rhodostomus". (S8). "vision is necessary to achieve cohesive collective motion for free swimming fish schools." (S9)
  - DISAGREEMENT (mild) on senses: S4 found the lateral line essential for [BLE]; S8 found vision sufficient when the lateral line was compromised, though treated fish swam about 79% farther from neighbors. Both say the lateral line aids cohesion.

5.3 Temperature (DISAGREEMENT, and a conflict with existing site content: see 8.2)

5.6 Lighting
- Seriously Fish [BLE]: "Use fairly dim lighting." (S21)
- Fishkeeper: "No special requirements". (S30)
- Research: groups of five "required a minimum level of illuminance (greater than 1.5 lx)" to school (S8). In schools of about 50 tested from "0 to 900 lux", at insufficient light "fish are unable to give rise to coherent and cohesive group dynamics", and as light rises "the school structure is successively characterized by a polarized state followed by a highly regular and stable rotational configuration" (S9). Photoperiods used by labs: 12:12 (S5) and 14:10 light:dark (S9).
- Reading: "dim" in the aquarium sense is far above 1.5 lux, so the two do not conflict; the research only shows a school needs some light to function.

## 6. Diet

- Wild diet [BLE], from stomach contents of 177 fish: "The species is omnivorous, consuming a large part of the food of autochthonous origin." Microcrustaceans matter more in the flood; filamentous algae, chironomids and plant fragments more in the dry season; stomachs fuller in the flood (paraphrase). (S2) https://institutodepesca.org/index.php/bip/article/view/1196
- FishBase [GEO]: "Feeds on worms, small crustaceans and plant matter." (S16)
- Seriously Fish [BLE] and [RHO]: "Omnivorous and will accept just about anything offered." "Feed a mixture of dried flakes and granules and small live and frozen foods." (S21, S22). [BLE]: "It does have a small mouth, though, so correspondingly sized foods are best." "A varied diet such as this is essential for the best colour development." (S21). [GEO]: "Any food offered must be small enough for its tiny mouth." (S23)
- Fishkeeper: "Flake, micropellets, frozen foods such as mosquito larvae, brineshrimp and daphnia." (S30)
- Aquarium Co-Op: foods "such as baby brine shrimp, nano pellets, and daphnia", plus krill and salmon-based foods for color (paraphrase). (S31)
- Feeding frequency: Aquarium Source: "You should aim to feed your fish two small meals a day." (S32, lower tier). Lab feeding: "fed ad libitum with fish flakes" (S5); fine pellets "from an automated feeder once a day" (S9). No stronger source on frequency.

## 7. Behavior and tankmates

- Temperament: "It's a very peaceful species that won't compete well with very boisterous or much larger tankmates." (S21, S22; S23 same for [GEO]). Fishkeeper: "extremely peaceful". (S30)
- Good companions, Seriously Fish [BLE] (paraphrase): other Hemigrammus or Hyphessobrycon tetras, pencilfish, Apistogramma, Corydoras, small loricariids; in a general community, smaller rasboras, barbs, anabantoids and West African dwarf cichlids such as Pelvicachromis. (S21)
- Aquarium Co-Op: "They get along with any peaceful community fish that are similar-sized, such as other tetras, rasboras, and corydoras." (S31)
- Aquarium Source: "The best tank mate for Rummy Nose Tetras is more Rummy Nose Tetras." (S32)
- Discus: "Many discus enthusiasts keep a shoal of rummy noses in their show tanks, too." (S21). Fishkeeper: they "mix well with discus in large aquaria." (S30). See 8.2 for the temperature conflict this creates.
- Angelfish (rummy-noses as the victims): Neale Monks: "Rummynose tetras are notably sensitive to their environment"; with a territorial angel, rummies "'gone to ground'". His fix: "put the peaceful fish in first, like the tetras", let them settle "preferably with the lights out", then "After another half hour or so, you can add the Angel." (S25)
- Fin nipping: no established source says rummy-noses nip fins, and none says they do not. Seriously Fish, Fishkeeper and Aquarium Co-Op do not mention it. Only low-tier blogs and forums in search results disagree on it; none was used. Record as no source found. Aquarium Co-Op lists "bullying" among stressors that fade the nose (paraphrase). (S31)
- Swimming: "a burst-and-coast type of swimming" (S5). Co-Op: they need length to swim "back and forth" (S31).

## 8. Health

8.1 Common problems and sensitivity
- Aquarium Co-Op: "Because of their sensitivity to sources of stress, many people do not recommend them for new fishkeepers." New fish "sometimes arrive with ich (white spot disease)"; "Make sure to quarantine them in a separate hospital tank" and "consider proactively treating them with broad-spectrum medications." (S31)
- Aquarium Source: "Two of the most common to affect Rummy Nose Tetras are Ich and Dropsy." (S32, lower tier)
- No species-specific disease literature found.

8.2 The red nose as a condition signal
- Aquarium Co-Op: "they often get called the "canary in the coal mine" of aquariums because their noses lose their color when stressed." (S31)
- Fishkeeper [BLE]: in alkaline water nitrate becomes more of a problem, which "may cause a significant loss of colour as a first symptom of stress." (S30)
- Seriously Fish [BLE] and [RHO]: "if the fish are not in good condition the red colour can fade considerably." "Its colours will become faded if kept in alkaline conditions." (S21, S22)
- Wikipedia's "mine canary" line tying pallor to ammonia, nitrite and nitrate thresholds is uncited. (S33). Do not use.
- Scope for writers: sources support "the red fades when the fish is stressed or conditions are poor". No source supports a specific chemical threshold or a claim that it fades before a test kit reads a problem. No controlled study found.

8.3 Ich and the 86°F heat treatment
- VCA: "Most strains of ich cannot survive in water temperatures above 86ºF" and "Not all fish can stand the heat, though." "The speed of replication depends on the temperature of the water: they replicate faster in warmer water." (S20) https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/fish-white-spot-disease-ich
- Rummy-nose ceilings: 79°F [BLE] and [GEO], 81°F [RHO] (Seriously Fish, S21 to S23); 79°F (FishBase, S14, S16); 79°F (Practical Fishkeeping 25-26°C, S24); 82°F (Fishkeeper, S30); 84°F (Aquarium Co-Op and Aquarium Source, S31, S32). Breeding advice raises water to "the 80s°F" (S31).
- No critical thermal maximum or lethal-temperature study for any of the three species was found.
- Plain reading for the owner: 86°F sits 2°F above the highest husbandry ceiling found and 7°F above Seriously Fish and FishBase's [BLE] ceiling. No source documents that rummy-noses tolerate 86°F. As with the ember tetra, the heat method cannot be stated as safe for this species from the sources found.
- CONFLICT WITH EXISTING SITE CONTENT: content/guides/discus-handling-guide.mdx line 76 says discus "need sustained 82 to 86°F water" and names rummynose tetras alongside cardinals as tankmates that "tolerate the warmth". The sources here put the [BLE] range at 73 to 79°F in Seriously Fish and FishBase and at most 84°F anywhere. Seriously Fish and Fishkeeper do say rummies are kept with discus (section 7), so the pairing is real in the hobby, but the warmth-tolerance framing is not supported. Owner to decide whether to revisit that sentence (it also feeds src/lib/data/guides/fish.js line 570).

8.4 Neon tetra disease (Pleistophora hyphessobryconis)
- Sanders et al. (2010): "The primary and type host is the neon tetra"; the parasite is reported "infecting some 20 species of freshwater fishes in four orders"; and "it is conceivable that some of these infections may have been caused by other related, undescribed species." The paper does not name any rummy-nose species; the only Hemigrammus mention is a 1942 reference title on Hemigrammus ocellifer. (S10) https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4155925
- Fish Pathogens database host list: "Characins, Cichlids, Cyprinids (i.e., danios), and all the carp-like fishes". No rummy-nose mention. (S19)
- Rummy-nose susceptibility specifically: no source found. Writers can say characins as a group are hosts, not that rummy-noses are documented hosts.

8.5 Quarantine
- Aquarium Co-Op: quarantine new rummies "in a separate hospital tank". (S31)
- Practical Fishkeeping: "Utilising a quarantine or hospital tank is always advisable no matter where you source your fish." (S27)
- VCA: "Ich is very contagious, so you should quarantine infected fish for at least 30 days" (sentence continues on the page). (S20). Note this wording is about infected fish; the ember notes recorded VCA's 30 days as applying to new fish. The site's cardinal guide already uses 30 days.

## 9. Handling: netting, acclimation, transport

Species-specific item first, then general small-fish guidance.
- Playing dead in the bag: "it is quite normal for them to "play dead" in the fish bag, but once you place the bag on a solid surface, they will easily right themselves again." (S31) https://www.aquariumcoop.com/blogs/aquarium/rummy-nose-tetra
- Add them before boisterous or territorial fish, lights out, as in Neale Monks's angelfish advice (S25, section 7).
- Lights: "the fish should be kept in fairly dark conditions, and not blinded by tank lighting, so switch these off!" (S27)
- Mixing water: "Repeat this every few minutes until the bag contains more water from your aquarium than the shop." "This could take anywhere from 30-60 minutes." (S27)
- Do not pour the bag in: "it is good practice not to pour the contents of the whole bag into your aquarium." (S27)
- Check for strays: "double check the bag, the floor and the net to ensure no fish have got stuck or jumped out along the way." (S27)
- Long trips: "Ammonia levels can increase due to fish waste and pH can drop due to increased CO2." (S27)
- Drip acclimation: "Drip acclimation is another common method used by aquarists often that have a little more experience." (S27)
- Bagging ratio: "Aim to have a one-to three ratio: one part water, three parts air." because "it's the air that contains the essential oxygen." (S28)
- Transport: "Place the fish bags into a dark container, or wrap them in a towel or some newspapers." (S28)
- Netting: "Don't be tempted to get a friend to hold a half-filled bag open beside you while you try to tip fish in from a net." (S28)
- Stress sensitivity context: "notably sensitive to their environment" (S25); "sensitivity to sources of stress" (S31).

## 10. Breeding basics

- Wild [BLE]: "this species has multiple spawning", oocytes "between 0.06 and 0.66 mm in diameter", fecundity "between 101 and 721 oocytes"; spawning peaks "at the end of the flood (March to May)". (S2)
- Fishers in Barcelos name the rising-water period as the main breeding season for ornamental species generally: "The period when the river rises (37.6%) is the period that they considered to be the main breeding season." (S3; not rodostomo-specific)
- Seriously Fish [RHO]: spawning water "soft and acidic in the range pH 5.5-6.5, gH 1-5"; "Contrary to much that has been written this species spawns exclusively in the evening"; eggs "will hatch in 24-36 hours", fry free swimming "a 3-4 days later" (source's wording). (S22)
- Seriously Fish [BLE]: "It can be spawned in a group, with half a dozen specimens of each sex being a good number." Pair method: "The pair should spawn the following morning." "the adults will eat the eggs given the chance." (S21)
- DISAGREEMENT on spawning time: Seriously Fish says [RHO] spawns "exclusively in the evening" (S22) but describes [BLE] pairs spawning "the following morning" (S21). Different species, so both may be right; do not generalize either to "rummy-noses".
- Seriously Fish [GEO]: "Can be quite tricky to breed." (S23)
- Fishkeeper [BLE]: rated "Difficult"; very soft acidic water near 28°C, peat filtration, spawning mops or Java moss, dark tank against fungus, adults removed after spawning; "Infusoria is the recommended first choice." (paraphrase plus quote) (S30)
- Aquarium Co-Op: "start with a big breeding group of at least six fish"; "Add a heater to raise the temperature to the 80s°F and a sponge filter with gentle flow that won't suck up the babies."; "Remove the adults after a few days, especially if you notice eggs or fry."; "Start the newborns with tiny foods like infusoria, live vinegar eels, and powdered fry food". (S31)
- Aquarium Source hatch: "After about 24 hours, the eggs will hatch." (S32, lower tier)
- Captive-bred supply exists: Aquatic Arts sells tank-bred [BLE]; Imperial Tropicals lists "Captive bred, Indonesia" and "Wild caught, Brazil" options (retailer listings, observations only, see 12).

## 11. "Did you know?" candidates

A. Physicists use them to study how schools decide. Rummy-nose tetras are a standard model species for collective-motion research, chosen "because it exhibits a strong schooling behavior" (S5). In a ring-shaped tank, "U-turns are mostly initiated by fish located at the front of the school", and the turn passes back through the group like "a chain of falling dominoes". Bigger groups change their minds far less: groups of 10 made "one U-turn every 20 min", against one every 0.2 minutes for pairs (S6). Each fish watches "typically one or two" neighbors (S7).
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5783427 ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5936730 ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7098660

B. Turn the lights down far enough and the school falls apart. Groups of five needed more than 1.5 lux to school (S8). In schools of about 50, very dim light left "fish are unable to give rise to coherent and cohesive group dynamics"; as light rose, the school lined up and then settled into a stable rotating mill (S9). And when researchers temporarily switched off the lateral line in firehead tetras, the fish could not hold a shoal until it regrew about a month later (S4).
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7661294 ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10232518/ ; https://orbi.uliege.be/handle/2268/31748

C. The Amazon's number two export. The wild firehead rummy-nose, "rodostomo" to Rio Negro fishers, "ranks second on the list of the species most exported from the state" of Amazonas, behind the cardinal tetra, at about 6% of 20 to 30 million fish a year (S3).
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8244192/

D. (Backup) "Rhodostoma" means "rose-mouthed", for the red nose and mouth (S17). https://etyfish.org/acestrorhamphidae/

E. (Backup) They play dead in the bag and right themselves once it is set on a solid surface (S31).
